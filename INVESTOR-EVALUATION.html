Six Patterns That Emerged Across the Ten Lenses

No single evaluation killed the business. But six recurring patterns showed up in three or more of the ten lenses, independently, with different mechanics each time. These are the structural shapes of the plan, not the contents.

Pattern 1 — Every revenue line is counted at the high end of its range

Unit economics counts 10% commission when the blended rate is 7.8%. Market sizing counts the global $1.57T when the relevant ceiling is $77B. GTM counts a 25% warm close rate when the central tendency is 15–22%. Pricing counts $14K Society average against a Knightsbridge anchor at $31K. The pattern is not dishonesty — it is a founder model that takes each line independently at the top of its range and never asks what happens when several of them simultaneously land mid-range. In aggregate, the plan is 30–45% optimistic on the Y5 line.

Pattern 2 — Founder personal cost is absent from the math

The unit-economics audit flags it directly. The capital lens names it as the largest analytic gap in v2.0. The founder/team lens shows it via burnout math. The operations lens shows it via the hours-per-week capacity model. The same hole shows up four ways: the founder's own salary-equivalent, opportunity cost, and personal burn are simply not in any line. Once added at $90–$120K/yr, Y1 and Y2 turn cash-negative and the cumulative deficit through Y2 is $116K — the actual capital requirement of the business.

Pattern 3 — The 24/7 SLA promise is the structural lie at the center of the plan

Six of the ten lenses independently identified that the 24/7 promise + named-human + 15-min Society SLA + solo founder is mathematically inconsistent. The promise costs nothing to make and everything to keep. The PHT VA at month 9 is the only mitigation budgeted, and the VA structurally cannot handle live IROPs, sub-$10K spending decisions, or relationship-coded supplier escalations. The promise must come down or the cost must go up. Both are recoverable. Pretending neither is necessary is not.

Pattern 5 — The market is real, but smaller than claimed, and the wedge is narrower than the plan addresses

Pattern 6 — The plan over-rotates to venture framing for a business that is structurally services

The capital lens, the moat lens, and the unit-economics lens all reach this conclusion through different methods. Gross margin sits in the 50–70% band (services), not 75–90% (software). The marginal cost of a new customer is significant. Scaling is via more advisors, not more code. Founder dependency is high. Exit multiples are 1–4× revenue, not 6–15×. The realistic Y5 outcome is a $400–$800K-net solo practice that compounds to a $3–$8M strategic acquisition in Y7–Y9. That is a strong personal outcome. It is not a venture outcome. The single highest-impact framing change is to stop trying to make it venture-shaped.

The Unifying Thesis

Five of the six patterns dissolve if the founder accepts the lifestyle-practice framing, sequences the moat-builders honestly, and removes the venture-shaped overlay from the planning documents. The sixth (the 24/7 SLA promise) is independent and requires its own operational fix.

1.1 Why the Headline $871K Y5 Number Doesn’t Survive Inspection

The v2-facts table forecasts $871K of Y5 revenue against a 60 Light / 40 Office / 20 Society member mix. Under unit-economic inspection, four corrections move the number down by roughly $96K to $775K. Each correction is mechanical, defensible, and reproducible from public sources.

The first correction is the Virtuoso commission methodology. The v2-facts doc states "Virtuoso clips 2% of commissions before the Fora split." This is only partially true and is the most-misunderstood line in the entire model. On Virtuoso preferred hotel rates, the gross commission paid by the hotel is typically 10%; of that, the advisor receives 8% and Virtuoso receives 2%. So the "10% commission" the founder is modeling is already net of the Virtuoso clip if the booking is Virtuoso-routed. On non-Virtuoso preferred rates (FHR, Bonvoy STARS, Hyatt Privé direct), the 2% clip does not apply because the booking is routed through a different program. The realistic mix is approximately 60% Virtuoso-routed at 8% net, 30% other preferred at 10% net, 10% non-commissionable. Blended effective rate: 7.8%, not 10%.

The second correction is the dormancy term. The v2-facts model has no dormancy assumption. At Y3+ when the cohort matures, empirically 12–20% of Light members are functionally dormant — they paid the subscription, used the desk twice, then drifted. Net effect on commission revenue: deflate by 15–22% from Y3 onward.

The third correction is the ADR mix. The model uses $500 ADR flat. The realistic Light blended ADR is closer to $420 (SMB founders book mostly business hotels at $300–$450). The realistic Office blended is closer to $700 (PE/VC/RIA partners book $650–$800). Society is closer to $900 vs. the modeled $750. The ADR error is directional but offsetting; Light is overstated, Office and Society are understated. Net Y5 effect on the v2-facts table is +$15K to +$40K if Society launches.

The fourth correction is design-fee leakage on small trips. 8.4 Permanent Establishment Risk

Under Canada-US Tax Treaty Article V, a PE arises if the founder has a fixed place of business in the US OR under the services PE rule (Article V(9)) if physically present in the US providing services for >183 days in any 12-month period, OR if more than 50% of gross active business revenue is derived from US services performed during 183+ days.

The ">50% of gross active business revenue from US services" test is the dangerous one. 100% of revenue is US-sourced. The 183-day test is the saving grace.

Practical guidance: Track US travel days obsessively. Cap founder US days at 120 per calendar year, 150 per 12-month rolling window — a 33-day safety buffer. Atlas Firm "embedded" service models (founder spends 2 days/week in NYC client office) are the exposure point.

Unit Economics Deep Dive

The Per-Member Sensitivity at Y5 Scale

At Y5 base case (120 members), a 5% increase in commission revenue per member equals roughly $17K/yr of additional revenue. A 5% increase in subscription pricing across the board equals $22K/yr. A 5% improvement in retention compounds far larger — a member retained from Y3 through Y5 instead of churning in Y3 is worth approximately $8–$15K of lifetime revenue depending on tier. Retention is the highest-leverage line item the founder controls.

The Bimodal Cohort Distribution

The v2-facts model uses average member behavior. Reality is bimodal: 30–40% of members are heavy users (25–35 nights/yr) generating 60% of commission, 60–70% are light users (5–12 nights/yr) generating 25%, and 10–15% are functionally dormant. The implication is that the average buyer behaves nothing like the average. Modeling the average produces a misleading picture; modeling the bimodal distribution produces a more credible picture and identifies the cohort segment (heavy users) that the founder should over-invest in retaining.

The Personal-Travel Multiplier

The v2-facts assumption is that 39% of Light members have meaningful personal travel intent at signup, but only 30–50% of those actually book personal travel through Latitude in Y1. By Y3, the personal-travel conversion rate should rise to 60–75% as trust is established. Personal travel commission per Light member rises from $140–$350 in Y1 to $300–$600 in Y3 — a meaningful per-member revenue lever that does not require new acquisition.

The Working-Capital Curve

Commission revenue lags booking by 30–90 days. At Y5 cohort size with $267K of commission revenue, the working-capital line is roughly $40–$60K of receivables outstanding at any moment. Building a $50K Y5 working-capital reserve is not a defensive line item; it is the cost of the booking-to-collection lag, and it scales linearly with commission revenue. The founder should plan for this from Day 1 even though it does not bite until Y3.

Market Sizing Deep Dive

Why the SAM Filters Are More Brutal Than They Look

The four SAM filters — T&E band, no-travel-manager, principal-buyer, cultural-fit — each look defensible at first glance. But they multiply. A buyer who clears the T&E gate (0.75 for founders) still has to clear the no-TM gate (0.95), the principal-buyer gate (0.90), and the cultural-fit gate (0.40). Multiplying: 0.75 × 0.95 × 0.90 × 0.40 = 0.257, or 25.7% of the base population. That is the headline conversion rate from "lives in the segment" to "could buy at v2.0 pricing." It's why the corrected SAM is so much smaller than the gross population number suggests.

Concentration vs. Distribution

63% of the SAM lives in six metros. That concentration is both an opportunity and a constraint. The opportunity: founder outreach can be six-cluster rather than fifty-city, dramatically reducing total addressable founder-time. The constraint: each cluster has its own cultural register, its own peer-introduction patterns, its own conference circuit. A founder who builds NYC distribution in Y1 has not built SF distribution; the two clusters operate as separate markets even though they're nominally in the same SAM.

The "Universe of Plausible Society Buyers"

Family offices in the US below institutional scale ($50M–$250M family-office AUM) number roughly 3,000–4,000. Of those, maybe 200–400 have the cultural fit and travel volume to pay $14–$30K/yr for a travel membership. The Y5 plan calls for 20 Society members — 5–10% of plausible buyers. Knightsbridge Circle has fewer than 100 members globally after 12 years; capping Society at 12–15 (not 30) is the right move given the structural rate-limit.

The Cross-Border Cultural Premium

Toronto founders selling to US buyers carry a small but measurable cultural premium in the founder/VC/RIA segments: "Canadian-founded" reads as European-adjacent, more reserved, less hard-sell. The premium is bounded (15% of the SAM is meaningfully friction-resistant to a Canadian operator), but in the segments where it lands, it lands as a small advantage rather than a friction. The plan should not over-claim this, but should not ignore it either.

Founder Risk Deep Dive

The 18-22 Member Breakpoint Explained

The breakpoint is not arbitrary. At 18–22 active members with the v2-facts mix, the founder is running roughly 1,650 service hours/year plus ~600 sales hours, ~400 content hours, ~300 admin hours, ~200 learning hours. Total: 3,150 hours/year, or 60.5 hours/week. Sustainable for 12–16 weeks, then sleep deficit compounds, error rate rises, SLA breaks become visible to members.

The PHT VA at month 9 buys back roughly 300 hours/year of overnight admin (the VA cannot touch live IROPs, sub-$10K spending decisions, or relationship-coded supplier escalations). 300 hours equals roughly 8 hours/week, which extends the founder's sustainable ceiling from 20 members to 23–24. The VA does not solve the breakpoint problem; it postpones it by 2–3 members.

The Second Principal Advisor Math

The hire the plan most underestimates. Corporate-grade travel advisors with 5+ years experience, willing to associate in a 2-person shop, are vanishingly rare. Realistic recruiting timeline is 6–9 months. The founder must start the search at month 9 to have a Y2-onboarded hire by month 18. The all-in cost ($110–$140K including commission share and benefits) is justified only if Society launches; without Society, the second advisor is a $130K expense against a Y3 revenue line that doesn't support it.

The implication is that Society launch timing and the second-advisor hire timing are linked: defer Society and the advisor hire deferral becomes economically reasonable; accelerate Society and the advisor hire becomes existential.

The Burnout Detection Protocol

Burnout is invisible until it isn't. The founder needs a measurement protocol: weekly self-reported hours, monthly NPS to the spouse (literal NPS, "would you recommend this partner to a friend"), quarterly check-in with a peer founder or therapist. These look excessive; they are not. The literature is clear that founders who measure burnout signals catch them 60–90 days earlier than founders who don't, and 60 days is the difference between "course-correct" and "shut down."

The Concurrent IROP Math

The IROP playbook handles serial incidents. The realistic IROP load at 20 members in flight: P(simultaneous Tier 2) = roughly 1−e−λt where λ is the per-trip Tier-2 rate and t is the overlap window. At empirical rates (1 Tier-2 per 80 trips, 3-hour resolution window), at 20 in-flight trips the daily P(concurrent Tier-2) is approximately 6–8%. Over 30 days: roughly 2–3 concurrent events expected. By Y3, Notion's permission model becomes a security risk — accidental exposure of one member's profile to another is a real failure mode. Glossary

Appendix E — Glossary of Industry Terms

Travel Advisory

  • ADR — Average Daily Rate. The per-night hotel rate, typically expressed as a blended average across a portfolio of bookings.
  • ARC/IATA — Airlines Reporting Corporation / International Air Transport Association. Accreditations required to ticket air travel.
  • BAR — Best Available Rate. The lowest publicly available hotel rate.
  • Commission — The percentage of a booking that the hotel or supplier pays the advisor/host. Typically 8–10% on hotels, $0 on most domestic air.
  • FAM trip — Familiarization trip. Discounted or complimentary travel offered to advisors to learn a property or destination.
  • FHR — Fine Hotels & Resorts. The Amex Platinum hotel program with member benefits.
  • GDS — Global Distribution System. Sabre and Amadeus are the dominant systems for booking air, hotel, and car.
  • Host agency — A licensed travel agency that hosts independent contractors who book under the agency's ARC/IATA accreditation. Fora, Brownell, Cadence, SmartFlyer are examples.
  • IROP — Irregular Operations. Flight cancellations, schedule changes, hotel walks, and other disruptions requiring rebooking.
  • NDC — New Distribution Capability. Airline content distributed outside the GDS; now ~30% of premium fare inventory.
  • PNR — Passenger Name Record. The reservation record in a GDS.
  • Privé / STARS / Impresario — Consortium hotel programs offering member amenities (upgrade, F&B credit, late checkout) at participating properties.
  • SLA — Service Level Agreement. The promised response or resolution time.
  • SOT — Seller of Travel. State-level registration required in CA, FL, HI, WA, IA for entities selling travel.
  • TICO — Travel Industry Council of Ontario. The Ontario regulator for travel sales.
  • Virtuoso — A luxury travel consortium of ~20,000 advisors with negotiated supplier amenities.

Financial & Capital

  • CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost. Total cost (cash + time) to acquire one paying customer.
  • CCPC — Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation. Ontario tax structure with small business deduction.
  • F&F — Friends & Family. Early-stage capital from personal network, typically structured as a note.
  • GBV — Gross Booked Volume. Total dollar value of travel booked through the advisor, not the commission.
  • HST/GST — Harmonized/Goods and Services Tax. Canadian VAT-equivalent. Zero-rated on exports of services to non-Canadian residents.
  • PE — Permanent Establishment. Tax-treaty concept triggering source-country tax obligations.
  • SAFE — Simple Agreement for Future Equity. Convertible instrument popular in VC seed rounds.
  • SBD — Small Business Deduction. Canadian CCPC tax preference at ~12.2% on first $500K active business income.
  • TAM/SAM/SOM — Total Addressable Market / Serviceable Addressable Market / Serviceable Obtainable Market.
  • UDAP — Unfair and Deceptive Acts and Practices. State consumer protection statutes.

Latitude 43 Specific

  • Atlas — The Latitude 43 membership instrument (Atlas Light, Atlas Office, Atlas Family Office).
  • EA Companion Seat — Complimentary Light-tier seat included with Atlas Office for the principal's executive assistant.
  • The First 30 — 90-day no-fault exit program for new members.
  • First Trip Guarantee — Service credit capped at $1,000 of labor on one named trip per member (formerly "First Loss Insurance").
  • The Desk — The Latitude 43 service line; the institutional noun used in product surfaces.
  • The 43rd Parallel — Recommended renamed content brand (currently "Latitude Journal").
  • Latitude Society — The invite-only top tier; deferred to H2 2027, recommended cap 12–15 members.
