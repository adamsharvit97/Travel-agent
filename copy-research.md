## 2. Patterns observed across all five

### a. **Verbs of motion + discovery**
"Going," "wander," "set jetting," "uncovered," "hidden," "discover," "find." Movement, not booking.

### b. **Curiosity as the opening note**
- *"Curious and wondrous travel destinations"* (Atlas Obscura)
- *"Hidden wonders where you least expect them"* (Atlas Obscura)
- *"Rare, real, and wonderful"* (PRIOR)
- *"Invite the unexpected"* (Going)

They lead with the *feeling*, not the service. The booking is implied; the wondering is sold.

### c. **Editorial credibility as the moat**
- PRIOR: "Travel Designers" + "Editors"
- Indagare: "First-hand intel that's been vetted"
- Atlas Obscura: "Always lead with whatever is the absolutely most interesting thing"
- Black Tomato: works *with* EON Productions (the actual Bond producers)

The pattern: name the editorial layer explicitly. Don't say "we curate" — say "our editor spent three weeks there."

### d. **Anti-template positioning**
- Black Tomato: "Not templates, not off-the-shelf"
- PRIOR: "Meaningful journeys… original stories"
- Indagare: rejects algorithmic recs in favor of "vetted" intel

They define themselves *against* TripAdvisor and Booking.com — and they name it.

### e. **Specificity over adjectives**
Black Tomato on Yellowstone: *"Lamar Valley cattle drives, stargazing, waterfalls, geysers, rodeo and cookouts."* Not "amazing experiences." **Five concrete nouns.**

Going on flights: *"The most exciting flight opportunities find you."* Not "discounts." A reframe.

### f. **Confident, slightly wry voice**
- Black Tomato: *"Swapping salubrious saloons and questionable lodgings for sumptuous hotels."*
- Going: *"Now we hope you'll invite the unexpected with us, together, as Going."*

A wink. A slightly-too-clever turn of phrase. Never corporate.

### g. **Direct second-person address**
"You'll," "we hope you'll," "for travelers who." It reads like a letter from a friend who happens to have access.

### h. **Short headline, slightly longer subhead**
Apple/Aesop structure. The headline is the hook (4–8 words). The subhead earns the click (1–2 sentences). Body copy can breathe.

## 3. Specific lines worth borrowing the *shape* of

**On positioning:**
- *"A new world of travel."* (PRIOR — short, suggestive, no verb-noun structure)
- *"How you travel matters."* (Indagare — 4 words, value statement)
- *"Invite the unexpected."* (Going — verb-led, two words)

> **Shape to use:** 3–5 word headline that's a *proposition*, not a description. No "the best travel agency for…"

**On editorial framing:**
- *"Rare, real, and wonderful."* (PRIOR)
- *"Curious and wondrous."* (Atlas Obscura)
- *"Hidden wonders where you least expect them."* (Atlas Obscura)

> **Shape to use:** triplets of adjectives or short noun phrases. Concrete and slightly surprising.

**On the trip itself:**
- *"Swapping salubrious saloons and questionable lodgings for sumptuous hotels."* (Black Tomato)
- *"Behind the scenes of some of TV's most awe-inspiring locations."* (Black Tomato)

> **Shape to use:** lead with the unexpected detail. Don't open with the destination — open with the moment.

## 4. Synthesized new copy — Onset, refined voice

The new voice = **Atlas Obscura's curiosity + Going's wryness + PRIOR's editorial framing + Black Tomato's set-jetting specificity**, all spoken at the volume of Aesop — quietly confident, never shouting.

### Brand essence
A travel company for people whose trips begin in a screen. Built on the premise that the best travel ideas of the last decade have come from *Succession*, *The White Lotus*, an F1 race, a Frank Ocean show in Athens — and that the people booking those trips deserve more than a Reddit thread.

### Possible names (if you want something quieter than "Onset")
- **Onset** — keep. Has "on set" + "onset of something." Works.
- **Reel** — film reel + the verb. Tight, mysterious.
- **Frame** — cinematic; what you see, where you sit.
- **Cue** — theatrical; the moment something begins.
- **Marker** — the slate that opens a scene.
- **Reverie** — what a film leaves you in.
- **Open** — the opening shot; opening to the world.

For the refined site I'm keeping **Onset** — it's the most ownable.

## 5. Voice rules — what to do and not do

**Do:**
- Lead with specifics (proper nouns, hotels, scenes).
- Triplets and small lists, not adjective stacks.
- Mention the moment, not the deal.
- Use "you," speak directly, sound like a person.
- Allow one slightly clever sentence per section.

**Don't:**
- Use "bespoke," "curated," "tailored," "seamless," "unforgettable," "journey."
- Use exclamation points anywhere.
- Stack 3+ adjectives in a row ("luxurious, personalized, unforgettable").
- Promise "the best."
- Talk about yourself before you've talked about the trip.

## 6. Design implications — "simple elegance"

The first Onset mockup leaned magazine-cover loud (red/green/yellow, condensed display, marquee strip). The voice above wants the opposite:

- **Palette:** warm off-white paper (#F7F4ED), ink (#111), single accent (deep terracotta or muted teal). No more than three colors.
- **Type:** one editorial serif (Söhne Halbfett, GT Sectra, or Cormorant) for display; one humanist sans (Inter) for body. No condensed sans display.
- **Photography:** large editorial single images, not collage tiles. (In the mockup, replaced with warm gradient placeholders.)
- **Whitespace:** Aesop-grade. Sections breathe. One idea per screen.
- **Motion:** none. No marquee, no autoplay. Reading-paced.
- **Hierarchy:** one h1, one accent line per section, body in 17–18px.
