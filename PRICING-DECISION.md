# The Pricing Decision

> **SUPERSEDED — June 2026.** This document predates the pricing pivots recorded in `business-plan-changelog.md`. Current pricing is **v3.1 Model B Unlimited**: Atlas Light Unlimited **$189/mo** and Atlas Office Unlimited **$349/mo**, all trip design included; Latitude Society invite-only, H2 2027. See `business-plan.md` §2. Kept for the decision history only — do not quote prices from this file.

The single biggest strategic question that has emerged from the research: where does Atlas price?

The competitive research argues for one structure ($3,600 / $12,000 / $36,000 — Indagare-adjacent). The adjacent-boutique-services research argues for another ($15,000 / $35,000 / $75,000 — Knightsbridge-adjacent). These are not minor adjustments. They are different businesses. Pick one before week one.

---

## Three viable paths

### Path A — Indagare-adjacent (current default on the site)

**Tiers:** Atlas Principal $3,600/yr · Atlas Office $12,000/yr · Atlas Firm $36,000/yr

**Positioning:** A premium-but-accessible members' travel office. Anchored to Indagare's $2,850 ceiling, ceiling at the bottom of the lifestyle-concierge band.

**ICP:** Seed-to-Series-A founders, RIAs with $100M–$500M AUM, individual VC partners (not GP-level), solo consultants, family office principals at smaller MFOs.

**Member count to base case:** ~18–20 by Y5

**Revenue at base case:** $171,370 net take-home Y5

**Required service level:** 24/7 SLA, named principal, but service ratio of ~1:15 members per principal at scale

**Acquisition motion:** Content-led (Latitude Journal), LinkedIn-led, alumni and EA-network warm intros, conference presence at Future Proof / Upfront Summit. Faster volume, lower per-member ACV.

**Comparable references:** Indagare ($2,850/yr), Bell & Bly's per-trip equivalents ($800/wk × ~4-5 weeks of travel = ~$3.2K/yr), the absent middle in the Knightsbridge-Indagare band.

---

### Path B — Boutique-services-adjacent (the upmarket pivot)

**Tiers:** Latitude Member $15,000/yr · Latitude Partner $35,000/yr · Latitude Firm $75,000/yr

**Positioning:** "The boutique travel advisory for principals." Lockstep retainer like Cravath or Centerview. Wachtell / Spencer Stuart register applied to travel.

**ICP:** Senior partners at established VC/PE funds, RIA principals with $500M+ AUM, founders post-Series B with $300K+ personal T&E, family office principals at Bessemer/Rockefeller-tier MFOs.

**Member count to base case:** ~8–12 by Y5 (lower count, higher per-member)

**Revenue at base case:** $250K–$450K net take-home Y5 (model not yet built; ranges from raising tiers ~3x)

**Required service level:** 60-min SLA, named principal, ratio of 1:6–8 members per principal — much higher touch

**Acquisition motion:** CoI (centers-of-influence) driven — referrals from accountants, wealth managers, lawyers serving the same ICP. Founder-led, slow, relationship-only. No content for first year. No conferences. The "we don't market" register of Wachtell.

**Comparable references:** Knightsbridge Circle (£25K standard), Sienna Charles ($50K–$150K), single-family-office advisory fees, Cravath-style billable rates.

---

### Path C — Hybrid two-track (the optionality play)

**Tiers in two tracks:**

*Atlas (volume):* Atlas Principal $3,600/yr · Atlas Office $12,000/yr — for the broad mid-market

*Latitude Society (premium):* Single tier $24,000/yr — for the upmarket principal who wants the 1:6 ratio, invite-only, capped at 30 total members

**Positioning:** Two products under one brand. Atlas is the public, priced, scalable product. The Society is the by-invitation product, mentioned on the site but not sold openly.

**ICP:** Atlas serves Path A's buyer. The Society serves Path B's buyer.

**Member count to base case:** 15 Atlas + 8 Society by Y5

**Revenue at base case:** ~$300K net take-home Y5

**Required service level:** Two tiers of SLA — Atlas at 60-min, Society at 15-min and "no busy signals ever"

**Acquisition motion:** Atlas drives content and acquisition; the Society sits below as the upmarket option that members are quietly invited into after 12 months of demonstrated fit.

**Comparable references:** Soho House Cities-Without-Houses + Soho Mews; Equinox club + E by Equinox; the ladder-up structure private aviation uses (Jet Card → Fractional → Whole).

## The decision framework

Three questions to resolve.

### Question 1: How big is the founder's warm network at the top of the market?

If you have 20+ warm introductions you can make in the next 60 days to senior partners at $1B+ funds, family office principals, or RIA founders with $500M+ AUM — **Path B** is viable.

If your warm network is broader but shallower — a hundred founders, EAs, VC associates — **Path A** wins because volume compounds faster.

If you genuinely have both — high-touch founders + a small group of marquee names — **Path C** is built for you.

### Question 2: What kind of business does the founder actually want?

**Path A** is closer to a SaaS scale-out. More members, more LTV per content piece, more leverage from systems. Year 5 founder is running a 4–5 person firm.

**Path B** is closer to a single-shingle wealth advisor or a boutique law partner. Fewer relationships, deeper relationships, no employees ever, year 5 founder is doing exactly what they're doing now but with twenty principals.

**Path C** is the most operationally complex. Two products, two service levels, two acquisition motions. Year 5 founder has more income and more headache.

### Question 3: What's the founder's risk tolerance for year one?

**Path A** loses if no one signs at $3,600 (price isn't the issue, distribution is). **Recoverable.**

**Path B** loses if no senior partner takes a chance on a brand-new firm at $15K (price IS the issue — you're charging Knightsbridge prices without Knightsbridge tenure). **Harder to recover.** You'd have to drop to Path A anyway and the messaging is now "we cut prices in year one," which is a brand wound.

**Path C** loses if you spread yourself thin across two products and neither dominates. **Most likely to flop sideways.**

## What the research actually argues

The competitive teardowns (ALTOUR, Cadence, Brownell, Bell & Bly, SmartFlyer, Indagare) argue **for Path A** because:
- The gap they leave is the $3,000–$5,000 band, accessible to a broader buyer
- No competitor has dominated this band; Indagare leads but is leisure-skewed
- Bell & Bly's per-trip pricing leaves an annual-membership opening at exactly $3,600

The lifestyle-membership research (Knightsbridge, Soho House, Equinox, Velocity Black) argues **for Path A or Path C** because:
- Knightsbridge's £25K floor is too high for any new brand to clear in year one
- Velocity Black's $3,100/yr point validates the Principal price band
- Equinox's tiered service-depth model proves the multi-tier structure

The boutique-services research (Pictet, Spencer Stuart, Centerview, Wachtell) argues **for Path B** because:
- Retainer-and-restraint pricing is the proven boutique model
- The 1:6 ratio matters more than the price
- Marketing absence is itself the moat

The card-concierge research argues **for Path A** because:
- The biggest objection ("I already have Amex") is at the $695-$15,000 price band
- We win that objection by being a real human at marginally above the same price
- At Path B prices, the objection becomes "I already use Knightsbridge" — a worse fight

---

## The recommendation

**Default to Path A for year one. Build the option to evolve to Path C by year two.**

Specifically:

1. Launch with the public $3,600 / $12,000 / $36,000 tiers as designed
2. Cap Atlas Firm at 5 members in year one — anything more dilutes the founder's bandwidth
3. Build the unnamed Latitude Society concept internally: keep the door open for an invite-only $24,000 tier you can introduce in year two, when the brand has earned the right to that ask
4. Re-price Atlas Principal to $4,800 after the first ten paying members close — the early adopters get grandfathered at $3,600, future members pay full
5. Re-evaluate the entire pricing question at month 18, with 12 months of conversion data, ten members of churn data, and supplier-program traction in hand

**Cost of getting this wrong by going to Path B prematurely:** you spend 18 months chasing a buyer who won't take a chance on a year-old firm at Knightsbridge prices, and pivot down to Path A having damaged the brand twice.

**Cost of staying at Path A:** you leave money on the table from prospects who would have paid Path B prices — but you can recover that in year two with the Society tier.

This is the safer Y1 path that preserves both upside paths. Hold the price discipline. Do not lift it until the case studies justify it.

---

*This decision is the most important one ahead of launch. The website, deck, business plan, sales kit, and competitive positioning all currently assume Path A. Changing paths in week one means rewriting all of them. Worth doing if you decide on a different path — but make the call now, not month four.*
