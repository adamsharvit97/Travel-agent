# Evaluation: Latitude 43 Business Plan v3.0 (Model B Unlimited)

**Evaluator framing:** McKinsey-trained early-stage operator evaluating a solo lifestyle services business. Not VC underwriting; not consumer SaaS underwriting. Operator-grade evaluation, honest about both the math and the founder's path.

**Verdict up front:** B+. The plan is structurally sound, makes one large defensible bet (Model B Unlimited), and is honest about its lifestyle ceiling. It has three specific weaknesses worth fixing before Year 1 closes — none structural, all operational. Detail follows.

---

## Lens 1: The pricing decision (Grade: A−)

The Model A → Model B pivot is the right call **for this founder, in this segment, at this stage** — but the plan should be more transparent about which case it doesn't survive.

### What's right
- The buyer math in §4 ($16–46K realized value vs $4,188 annual fee, 4×–11× value-to-price) is the strongest single argument in the document. It changes the sales question from "is this affordable?" to "is this enough work to be worth showing up?" That's the right question.
- The "everything included" anchor solves a real, observed friction (the unanswerable *"what's the all-in?"* question under Model A). 30 sims is enough data for a 1-person shop to act on, not enough for a Series A board, and the plan correctly treats it as the former.
- Killing the design fee escalator for members is a non-trivial simplification. It removes 4 of the 6 SKUs the founder would otherwise have to explain in every sales call. Cognitive load reduction is a real operating advantage for a solo.

### What's questionable
- **The labor-margin risk is acknowledged but undersized in the projections.** §5.3 says "bear case 5% of members go labor-negative, ~$15–25K dollar impact at Y5." The bear case in §6 doesn't actually price this in — bear Y5 is $540K, which uses a smaller member count, not labor-negative members. The plan should add a second bear scenario where the mix is normal but 10–15% of Office members are labor-negative. That's the realistic bad case, and it's worth $40–70K of revenue offset at Y5 scale.
- **The 3-active-traveler cap on Office is doing a lot of work, and it's been redefined twice across versions** (v2.0 said 5 named/3 active; v3.0 maintains this). It needs to be in the MSA, in the welcome email, in the onboarding script, and in the quarterly review template. The plan mentions it once. Without enforcement infrastructure, the cap is aspirational.
- **The non-member design fee in §2.4 ($500 floor + escalator) is largely orphaned.** It exists to qualify in. But the plan doesn't specify the conversion mechanic — who proactively asks the non-member to convert, when, and against what trigger. Without it, $500–$2,000 design projects become one-off revenue without a feeder mechanic. The 30–40% conversion claim is asserted, not engineered.

### What might be wrong
- **$189 might still be the wrong Light price.** The data showed $189 holds against $99 because the freemium-shopper gets filtered out. But the plan never seriously stress-tests $229 or $249 Light. If $189 → $229 doesn't move close rate materially, that's $40/month × 60 members × 12 = $28,800 of foregone Y5 revenue. Worth a Round 8 sim block.

## Lens 2: Unit economics (Grade: B)

The numbers are mostly defensible, but three assumptions are doing too much work.

### Assumption stress-tests

**Hotel commission rate (7.8% effective).** The 7.8% derives from 10% gross × (1 − 2% Virtuoso clip) × 80% Fora share at scale (or 70% Y1). This is approximately right for Virtuoso preferred hotels. It is too high for the actual booking mix:
- Marriott Bonvoy preferred negotiated rates: often 7–10%
- Non-Virtuoso boutique properties: 5–8%
- Hyatt Privé: 10%
- Mid-tier business hotels (Hilton, Marriott non-luxury): 5–7%
- The blended rate is probably **6.5–7.2% Y1**, not 7.8%. Tightening this drops Light Y1 commission from $702 to ~$580, Office from $2,106 to ~$1,740. Total Y1 revenue lift of ~7% disappears.

**18 nights/year per traveler.** This is reasonable for a Series A/B founder. It is high for an RIA principal (10–14) and low for a PE operating partner (24–30). The plan should run unit economics by segment, not by tier, to reflect this.

**Personal travel commission ($350–$700 Light, $1,400–$2,200 Office).** These are reasonable but assume the member actually books their personal travel through Latitude. The "free design" hook helps, but the conversion is not automatic — most members continue to book personal travel direct on Expedia/Booking. The plan should track personal-travel attach rate as a Year 1 KPI, and treat the $350–$2,200 as "stretch" rather than "base."

**Net effect of stress-tests on Y5 base case:**
- Tighter commission rate: -$45K
- Personal travel attach at 50% rather than 100%: -$28K
- **Realistic Y5 base case: ~$795K, not $870K**

That's still a credible number. But the plan should publish this version, not the $870K.

### What's right

- 100% subscription retention is a real and material correction from earlier versions. The Y5 subscription line of $592K is the single most predictable revenue line in the plan, and it's properly modeled.
- The Virtuoso 2% clip is correctly handled as a pre-split deduction, not a post-split deduction. This was wrong in v1.0 and right here.
- The Society contribution to Y5 (~$440K of $870K) is correctly flagged as the slow-fill, relationship-bound segment. The plan correctly defers Society to H2 2027 rather than trying to fill it in Y1–Y2 against capacity that doesn't exist yet.

## Lens 3: Go-to-market realism (Grade: C+)

This is the section that needs the most work. The plan describes a GTM motion that's possible but not yet engineered.

### The cold email math doesn't survive contact with reality

The plan states:
- 100 outreaches/day × 250 working days = 25,000 outreaches/year
- 1–3% reply rate = 250–750 replies
- 30–50% reply→call = 75–375 calls
- 15–25% call→conditional yes = 11–94 conditional yeses
- 40–60% conditional yes→close = 4–56 closes

**The plan's Y1 target is 22 Light + 8 Office = 30 closes.** This is achievable at the optimistic end of the funnel (56 closes) and impossible at the realistic end (4–11 closes). The plan does not say how it will hit the higher number.

The plan's defensive answer is that warm/referral closes carry the year. But §10.1 lists exactly 1 warm anchor + 1 brother-in-law network + ~6 dinners. A generous read is 8–15 warm-introduced prospects in Y1, of which 4–7 close. Plus 4–11 cold closes. That's **8–18 Y1 closes, not 30.**

**Recommendation:** revise Y1 target to 12–18 total members (8 Light + 4–10 Office), explicitly bear case. Push the 30-member milestone to end of Y2. This drops Y1 revenue from $115K to ~$60–80K. That's the honest number.

### The newsletter strategy is asserted, not engineered

The Desk Diary (2x/week) + 43rd Parallel (monthly) = ~108 newsletter pieces in Year 1. At ~500 + ~2,500 words each = ~75,000 words of original content. For a solo founder also running a US-daytime desk and a separate primary income. **This is not realistic.** Either the cadence drops to 1x/week Desk Diary + quarterly 43rd Parallel, or a writer is hired (and not in the cost model), or the founder eats into desk time.

**Recommendation:** start with Desk Diary 1x/week + 43rd Parallel quarterly. Scale up if subscriber growth justifies.

### The 100/day cold volume

100 targeted, well-researched outreaches per day is roughly 3 hours of solo work. Across 250 working days, that's 750 hours/year. The plan doesn't budget founder time against this. The founder cannot do 750 hours of cold prospecting + 24/7 desk coverage + writing + relationship work + primary income. Something gives.

**Recommendation:** cut cold volume to 30–50/day, hire a VA for list-building and personalization at month 3 (not month 4–6 — earlier than the desk VA).

## Lens 4: Operations and the 60-min 24/7 SLA (Grade: B−)

The SLA is the product. It is also the single largest operational risk. The plan handles this honestly in §11.2 (PHT VA hire month 4–6), but understates two specifics.

### The PHT VA is on the critical path

The plan says PHT VA covers 11pm–7am ET. That's an 8-hour window per night × 7 nights = 56 hours/week of overnight coverage. A single VA at 40 hours/week cannot cover this; the plan implicitly requires 2 VAs or a part-time backup. The cost model in §14.2 ($4–6K/yr operating cost) doesn't include VA salary at ~$1,500–2,500/month × 2 VAs = $36–60K/yr. **That single line item changes the burn profile materially.**

**Recommendation:** specify VA staffing model and cost in §14. Y1 plan should either (a) start with founder-only 24/7 coverage and 60-min SLA flex clause in the MSA for first 6 months, or (b) budget $30K+ for VA coverage from month 4.

### Founder coverage 7am–11pm ET, every day, for 16 hours

This is not sustainable. The plan should specify:
- Backup principal coverage windows (vacation, illness, family)
- SLA flex clauses in MSA (e.g., "60-min target SLA, 90-min hard SLA")
- Off-hours response model for non-urgent inquiries (acknowledge in 60 min, resolve next morning)

The 60-min SLA should be the **promise** but the operational reality is "60 min for urgent, same-day for routine." That nuance is missing.

### What's right

- The desk on text/SMS as primary channel is correct. Members hate apps and portals.
- The standing instructions model is a real productivity unlock for both sides.
- The IROP playbook and scenarios book are real, documented, and in the repo. Most early-stage advisors have neither.

## Lens 5: Competitive defensibility (Grade: B+)

The competitive thesis in §13 is mostly right. One blind spot.

### The thesis holds

Of the 8 named competitors, exactly one (Bell & Bly) has the "all design included + 24/7 + relationship" stack. Bell & Bly does not publicly advertise 24/7 SLA. The plan's wedge is structurally rare and defensible against named competitors.

### The blind spot

**The new entrant is not Bell & Bly. It's an EA/COS-startup hybrid that has not appeared on the competitive map.** Several venture-backed companies are building "Chief of Staff as a Service" products (Athena, Double, Persona-style services). At scale they will add travel as a feature, not a product. They will not be better than Latitude on the principal-relationship axis, but they will be cheaper and broader. The plan should name this category explicitly and articulate why Latitude wins on travel even when the EA-CoS bundle is included.

**Recommendation:** add the EA-CoS-as-service category to §13. Wedge: "they will book your trip. We will redesign it when it breaks."

### The Toronto-from-the-US arbitrage

The plan handles TICO Path A correctly. But the "Toronto-based founder serving US principals" framing is itself a competitive moat that the plan understates:
- Cost-of-living arbitrage: founder runs profitably at burn levels that would be unsurvivable in NYC/SF
- Tax arbitrage: Ontario CCPC + US treaty position is structurally cleaner than a US C-corp for a single founder
- Identity: "Canadian operator serving US principals" is its own positioning advantage (cf. Shopify, Wealthsimple)

This deserves a paragraph in §14 or §13.

## Lens 6: Founder fit and capital plan (Grade: A)

The strongest section of the plan. The lifestyle framing is honest, the zero-capital plan is real, and the founder's primary-income backstop is acknowledged.

### What's right

- **Zero outside capital.** Genuinely zero. The founder owns 100% of the equity, 100% of the upside, and 100% of the downside. This is the right structure for a 1-person services business.
- **Primary income from Renmac covers personal burn.** This is the single most important fact in the business plan and the plan handles it correctly: Y1–Y2 founder draw is $0, growing only if revenue justifies.
- **7-year outcome distribution is realistic.** 40% lifestyle / 25% acqui-hire / 15% strategic / 5% venture-shaped / 15% failure is approximately correct for a solo professional services business. The plan does not pretend this is a venture outcome.

### What's mildly missing

- **The founder's primary income risk is mentioned (Risk #10) but not stress-tested.** What happens if the Renmac role ends in Year 2 at a $80K Latitude run-rate? The plan should specify the Latitude revenue threshold ($150K? $200K?) at which the founder could survive on Latitude alone, and how to accelerate to that threshold if primary income changes.
- **Spouse/family acceptance is not addressed.** A 24/7 SLA from a solo founder requires a household that accepts it. This is not in the plan. It should be — even one sentence acknowledging that the operating model assumes spousal/family alignment with 16-hour daily coverage.

## Lens 7: Risk register (Grade: B)

The 15-row risk register is the best one this plan has had. Three additions/corrections.

### Missing risks

- **R16: Single-channel concentration on text/SMS.** If the SMS service degrades (Twilio, etc.) or if a member's phone number changes mid-trip, the entire SLA fails. Mitigation: email + a documented secondary contact protocol.
- **R17: Founder burnout in months 6–18.** The single largest risk in any 1-person services business, and absent from the register. Mitigation: documented vacation coverage, mandatory 1-week off in months 6 and 12, backup principal trained.
- **R18: Newsletter/content fatigue.** Mentioned as R15 (newsletter fails to grow) but the founder-fatigue version is different. Mitigation: cadence reduction protocol, "skip a week" allowance, batch-writing days.

### Correctly weighted

- The Fora program change risk (R4) is correctly medium/medium. Diversifying host eligibility is the right hedge.
- The labor-negative member risk (R2) is correctly medium/medium and tied to specific mitigations.
- The bear case capital risk (R13) is correctly high probability but low severity — because the plan was built assuming bear case.

### Incorrectly weighted

- **R7 (FTG invoked at scale) is rated low probability.** This is wrong for Year 1. In Year 1, you're learning the operating model. Trip failures are more likely, not less. Realistic Y1 FTG invocation is 15–25% of new members, not <10%. Mitigation already covers it (named trip, bounded amount), but the probability rating should be high in Y1 and decreasing to low by Y3.

## Lens 8: What the plan should add before Y1 closes

Three additions, in priority order:

### Addition 1: A revenue-by-segment table

§3 lists 5 ICP segments. §5 gives unit economics by tier. §6 gives revenue by Year × tier. There is no table that shows revenue by segment × year. This matters because the segments have different LTV, different acquisition cost, and different probability of upgrade. The plan flies blind on segment mix until this is built.

### Addition 2: A Y1 12-month operating timeline

The plan describes Y1 outcomes but not Y1 milestones. A month-by-month operating timeline (when does Atlas Office #1 close? when does PHT VA hire? when does the newsletter launch?) is the difference between a plan and a guess. This should fit on one page.

### Addition 3: The "labor-negative member" trigger and response

Tie this to a specific operational mechanic. Recommended: at the end of each calendar quarter, the founder runs a "members hours" review. Any member whose Q-over-Q labor hours exceed a threshold (3.0× the implied hours in the unit economics) triggers one of three actions: (a) Society upgrade conversation, (b) coverage adjustment with the member, (c) friendly off-board. This needs to be a documented mechanic, not a vibe.

## Overall grade and verdict

**Grade: B+**

The plan is operationally honest, structurally sound, and bets the right one large bet (Model B Unlimited). It is not yet executable as written — the GTM math is too optimistic, the SLA staffing cost is undermodeled, and the labor-margin risk is acknowledged but not engineered against. None of those are fatal. All can be fixed in a v3.1 revision in roughly 1–2 working days.

**The Year 1 number to use for personal planning is $60–80K of revenue, not $115K.** The $115K is achievable in the optimistic case; the $60–80K is the planning case. Anything above that is upside.

**The Year 5 number to use for personal planning is $795K, not $870K.** The $870K is achievable in a clean operating year with full personal-travel attach; the $795K is the realistic case after stress-testing commission rate and attach assumptions.

**Recommended next moves, in order:**
1. Add a 12-month Y1 operating timeline (one page)
2. Add revenue-by-segment table to §3 or §5
3. Stress-test commission rate to 6.5% and republish Y5 base
4. Specify PHT VA staffing model and cost in §8 and §14
5. Cut planned cold email volume from 100/day to 30–50/day, hire list-building VA at month 3
6. Reduce newsletter cadence to weekly + quarterly (not 2x/week + monthly) for Y1
7. Add R16/R17/R18 to risk register
8. Document the labor-negative member trigger and response

None of this changes the strategic bet. All of it makes the bet executable.
