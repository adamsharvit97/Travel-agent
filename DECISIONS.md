# Open Decisions — Latitude 43 Launch

Every unresolved decision blocking launch. For each: the decision, the recommendation, the cost of getting it wrong. Decide these in one focused sitting and most of the work to date snaps into place.

---

## Brand & identity

### 1. Founder identity disclosure on the site
**Decision:** Do you appear on the site with name, photo, and bio — or stay anonymous behind "the Principal" until you have a few anchor members?

**Recommendation: Appear with name and bio.** Premium aviation brands make founders visible off the home page (interviews, board seats, conference talks) and quiet on it (no founder photo above the fold), but the About page must name the person. The current `[FOUNDER NAME]` placeholder is the single biggest piece of damage on the existing site per the brand-register research.

**Cost of getting it wrong:** A faceless luxury service brand reads as a shell. Prospects asking "who am I going to be working with at 2am" need an answer they can Google.

---

### 2. The exact tagline above the fold
**Decision:** Which line carries the home page?

**Candidates (in priority order):**
- *"A travel office for people whose time costs more than their tickets."*
- *"The Travel Office."* (definite-article frame, two words)
- *"The 43rd Parallel. Where business travel becomes a service."*

**Recommendation: Test the second.** *"The Travel Office."* asserts a category of one in two words — the most reproducible premium-aviation pattern (per Amalfi's "The Charter Provider," Sentient's "The Jet Card," NetJets' "The Promise of Always"). The longer line moves to viewport two as the explanatory sub-headline.

---

### 3. Visible footer phone number
**Decision:** Display a real phone number in the footer / contact page, or only an email / form?

**Recommendation: Display a number.** Every premium aviation brand (NetJets, VistaJet, Amalfi, Jet Linx) shows a real phone number prominently. A travel office that won't take a call signals it doesn't take calls. Set up a Toronto-based business line via Google Voice, JustCall, or OpenPhone; route after-hours to mobile.

## Pricing

See `business-plan.md` §2 and `business-plan-changelog.md`. Kept for the record.

### 4. Atlas Principal floor — $3,600 vs. $4,800 vs. $6,000
**Decision:** Where does Atlas Principal land?

**Recommendation: Hold at $3,600 for launch.** Indagare's Custom Planner at $2,850 is the public market floor for advisor-with-membership; we sit $750 above that and add a 24/7 SLA. Going to $4,800 risks looking like a stretch on day one; going to $6,000 prices out the solo principals (Series A founders, RIA principals) we most want as early references. **After 10 paying members, raise to $4,800.**

**Cost of getting it wrong:** Underpricing burns through founder energy on members who can't afford the service we promise. Overpricing makes the early case studies impossible to land.

---

### 5. Pilot pricing for first 2–3 anchor clients
**Decision:** Discount the first members or hold price?

**Recommendation: $1,800/yr for the first 2–3 named anchors only, contract specifies snap-back to full price year 2.** This buys you testimonials, case studies, and reference calls. Cap at 3 spots, time-box the offer, and write it into the contract so renewal at full price is the default.

---

### 6. Planning fee model for non-members
**Decision:** Charge non-members per trip, refuse non-members entirely, or do a hybrid?

**Recommendation: Hybrid. $250–$500 planning fee per complex trip plus $50 air ticketing.** Use the non-member channel as a top-of-funnel for membership conversions. The pitch on the second trip: "your second planning fee equals two months of Atlas Principal — let's just enroll you."

## Operations

### 7. Tern vs. Travefy vs. TripCreator as CRM
**Decision:** Which CRM/itinerary tool?

**Recommendation: Tern.** Fastest-growing among new advisors, modern UX, native integrations, AI-enabled. Travefy is mature but UX-tired. Sign up before week 2 of operations.

---

### 8. ARC accreditation now vs. later
**Decision:** Pursue your own ARC accreditation in year one, or stay under Fora's?

**Recommendation: Stay under Fora.** ARC is expensive, bonded, administratively heavy. Industry rule: justify it at ~$2M in air volume. You will not hit that in year one. Revisit at month 18.

---

### 9. E&O insurance — Fora umbrella or independent
**Decision:** Rely on Fora's umbrella or buy your own?

**Recommendation: Fora umbrella for year one, supplemental from year two.** Confirm in writing what Fora's policy actually covers before launch. Add Berkshire Hathaway E&O or similar by month 12.

---

### 10. The 24/7 coverage plan for year one
**Decision:** Cover 24/7 yourself for the first 6 months, or hire a VA in Manila/Cebu immediately for overnight ET?

**Recommendation: Cover yourself for the first 90 days, then hire a Philippine VA.** This is the right ramp: the first 90 days you need to feel every IROP yourself to write the playbook. By month 4, you should have a VA on the night desk. Budget $1,500/mo, scaling to $3K/mo by month 12.

**Cost of getting it wrong:** Burnout. The 2am promise breaks the founder within six months without a partner.

## Geographic & legal

### 11. Toronto entity structure
**Decision:** Operate as a sole proprietor, Ontario LLC equivalent (Inc.), or PC?

**Recommendation: Federally incorporate as a CCPC (Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation).** This gives you small-business tax deduction (SBD) on the first $500K, limits liability, and is the standard structure cross-border CPAs work with. Cost: ~$1,200 with a registered agent. Do this before billing your first US client.

---

### 12. US bank account / Stripe entity
**Decision:** Operate the US business through a US LLC, or invoice from the Canadian corp in USD?

**Recommendation: Start with the Canadian corp invoicing in USD via Stripe and Wise multicurrency.** Form a Delaware LLC only if/when a US institutional client requires US contracting (PE firms sometimes do). Consult a cross-border CPA before month three to lock the structure.

---

### 13. TICO and Canadian marketing
**Decision:** Ever solicit Canadian clients, or stay strictly US-targeted?

**Recommendation: Strictly US-targeted for year one.** TICO licensing creates more compliance burden than the small Canadian market justifies for a year-one operator. Revisit once you have 25+ US members and want a fifth.

## Marketing & GTM

### 14. The Latitude Journal — LinkedIn vs. Substack vs. both
**Decision:** Where does the Journal live?

**Recommendation: LinkedIn primary, Substack mirror.** LinkedIn is where your ICP (chiefs of staff, EAs, COOs, GPs) actually reads. Substack is the long-term archive and email list. Cross-post each issue. Newsletter signup on the site goes to a Substack list, not Mailchimp.

---

### 15. First conference investment in Q1
**Decision:** Which conference does the founder attend with the budget for booth/sponsorship?

**Top candidates (mapped to ICP density):**
- **Future Proof** (RIA conference, Huntington Beach, September) — the highest-density RIA audience in the country
- **Upfront Summit** (LA, March) — VC partners + founders
- **ACG / Association for Corporate Growth** — PE deal teams
- **SaaStr Annual** — Series A–C founders
- **YPO regional events** — founders + family offices

**Recommendation: Future Proof if your warm network is RIA-heavy, Upfront if it's VC/founder.** Pick one and dominate it; do not split budget.

---

### 16. Press timing
**Decision:** Pitch trade press immediately on launch, or wait until 5–10 members in?

**Recommendation: Wait until five paying members.** Pitching with zero members reads as PR for vapor. Pitching at five members with named (anonymized) case studies reads as a launching firm. Target Travel Weekly, Skift, BTN, Business Insider's "next big things" beat.

---

### 17. Referral program structure
**Decision:** 1-month Atlas credit per referral, 2-month, or a flat $500?

**Recommendation: 2-month Atlas credit per converted referral, capped at 6 months total credit per member per year.** A 2-month credit ($600 value at Principal tier) is meaningful enough to motivate but not so large it cheapens the program. Cap protects against gaming.

## Tech and tools

### 18. The Slack-based client communication channel
**Decision:** Use Slack Connect (which requires the client's company has Slack), iMessage/SMS, WhatsApp, or email primary?

**Recommendation: WhatsApp Business + email primary; Slack Connect on request for Firm-tier members only.** WhatsApp is the international default for US founders and most VC partners. Slack Connect requires the client's company to be on Slack and is bureaucratic for cross-org channels.

---

### 19. The member portal — build now or use Notion / a static page?
**Decision:** Build a real members-only portal in v1, or fake it with a Notion page until volume justifies?

**Recommendation: A polished Notion workspace for each member at launch.** Notion can be branded, password-protected (via Tools like Super.so), and looks premium. Build a portal proper at month 12 when you have 15+ members and the duplication cost justifies it.

---

### 20. Site host — Vercel, Netlify, or static
**Decision:** Where does the website live?

**Recommendation: Vercel with a custom domain (latitude43.travel or latitude43.co — confirm availability).** Free tier handles your traffic for the first 18 months. Connect GitHub for continuous deploy. Buy the `.travel` TLD; it's expensive but reads like an actual member of the industry.

## What you do not need to decide yet

These will declare themselves over the first six months and don't need to be solved in week one:

- Whether to ever leave Fora for direct ARC accreditation
- Whether to ever add a second principal advisor as an employee
- Which vertical (PE, VC, founder, RIA) to most aggressively niche down to
- Whether to offer leisure travel inside Atlas or as a separate optional service
- Whether to ever raise outside capital
- Whether to ever do MICE / events
- Whether to ever build proprietary software

Hold these open. Optionality is cheap; premature commitment is expensive.

---

## The single most important decision

If you can only resolve one thing this week, resolve **#1 and #2**: your name + bio go on the About page, and the home page hero changes to *"The Travel Office."* Everything else can be iterated. Those two move the brand from "polished placeholder" to "real firm with a real principal" — which is what your first ten members will need to see before signing.
