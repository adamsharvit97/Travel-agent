# Research Tracks

Six parallel tracks. Each agent gets its track's questions verbatim plus the destination, dates, and trip purpose. Tell agents to return findings with source URLs and publication dates — claims without sources get dropped from the report.

## Track 1: Business geography

- Which districts hold the corporate HQs, financial center, and main offices? Name them.
- Where do business travelers actually stay, and why (proximity, transit, evening options)? Distinguish "where tourists stay" from "where business travelers stay" — they often differ.
- Which areas look central on a map but are wrong for business stays (dead at night, far from offices, traffic-cut-off)?
- If the trip is industry-specific (tech, finance, pharma, energy), where does that industry cluster?

Search patterns: `"where to stay" <city> business district`, `<city> financial district hotels`, `<city> CBD vs <neighborhood> staying`, recent Reddit/FlyerTalk threads (`site:reddit.com/r/businesstravel <city>`).

## Track 2: Hotels

For each tier (luxury, upscale, value) find 3–5 candidates and answer per hotel:

- Exact location and what it's walkable to
- Business credentials: executive/club lounge, 24h gym, meeting rooms, reliable desk-and-chair rooms (not just aesthetic), early breakfast hours, late checkout culture
- Chain affiliation and loyalty program (Marriott Bonvoy, Hilton Honors, World of Hyatt, IHG One, Accor ALL) — corporate travelers care
- Recency: last renovation, recent openings nearby that beat it, any "tired property" signals in reviews from the last 12 months
- What business travelers specifically say in reviews (filter for "business trip" review tags)

Cross-check every shortlisted hotel against two independent sources. Prefer named evidence ("renovated lobby and rooms in 2024") over star counts.

Search patterns: `best business hotels <city> <year>`, `<hotel name> renovation`, `<hotel name> club lounge review`, `site:flyertalk.com <city> business hotel`.

## Track 3: Venues & events

- Main convention/exhibition center(s): name, district, nearest hotels, transit access
- Hotels with the largest conference/banquet capacity (where corporate events actually happen)
- Recurring citywide events that sell out hotels or spike rates (trade fairs, festivals, summits) — list with approximate annual timing
- If the user named a conference: its exact venue, official hotel blocks, and the realistic walking-distance hotel set

Search patterns: `<city> convention center hotels nearby`, `<city> largest trade fairs calendar`, `<conference name> <year> venue hotel block`.

## Track 4: Logistics

- Airports serving the city; which one business travelers prefer and why
- Airport→business district transfer options as a comparison set: mode, duration, cost, frequency, early/late availability, where ride-hail pickup actually is
- Getting around during the trip: transit card/app to get, whether ride-hail works (Uber? local equivalent?), taxi reliability, walkability of the business core
- Visa/entry requirements for common passports (US/EU/UK at minimum), and any registration or arrival-card apps
- Typical traffic patterns that affect meeting scheduling (rush-hour windows to avoid)

## Track 5: Working & entertaining

- Client-dinner restaurants: 4–6 picks that are impressive but bookable, with cuisine, district, price band, and reservation lead time
- Breakfast/coffee meeting spots near the main business district
- Coworking and day-office options (e.g., WeWork, Regus, hotel day rooms), plus hotel lobbies that work for ad-hoc meetings
- Connectivity: best eSIM/SIM option, mobile coverage quality, hotel/café wifi reliability, any VPN or service-blocking issues
- Late-night and weekend options for downtime that are appropriate to mention in a business report (gyms, running routes, one or two cultural highlights)

## Track 6: Ground truth

- Safety: realistic assessment for the business districts at night, common scams targeting travelers, areas to avoid
- Business etiquette: greetings, punctuality norms, card exchange, dress code, gift culture, negotiation pace — only what differs from generic Western practice
- Money: cards vs cash reality, tipping norms (restaurants, taxis, hotel staff), ATM advice
- Practical: power plug type and voltage, time zone and jet-lag direction from the user's origin, emergency numbers, pharmacy/medical access, tap water
- Climate for the travel dates (or by season if dates unknown) and what that means for dress code

## Source standards (all tracks)

- Anything price- or state-dependent (rates, renovations, visa rules, transit fares): source from the last 18 months
- Prefer primary sources (hotel sites, convention center sites, government visa pages) for facts; traveler forums (FlyerTalk, r/businesstravel) for judgment
- When sources conflict, report the conflict rather than picking silently
