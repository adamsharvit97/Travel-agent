## SECTION 2: HEAD OF OPERATIONS EVALUATION

I've run a 24/7 desk for a major TMC and two ultra-HNW concierges. Here's the honest read.

**SLA: Met, with margin.** First response 1m 22s against a 60-min SLA is a 97.7% margin. That's not just compliance — that's the kind of response time that creates a permanent emotional memory. Members will tell this story for years.

**Resolution time: 31 minutes from text to confirmed rebook.** Industry benchmark for IROP at premium TMC tier is 45-90 min. She beat it. The reason she beat it: the CRM was pre-loaded with trip context, payment authorization, and meeting stakes. **The founder's Sunday voice memo is the unsung MVP of this incident.** Without it, Joana would have spent 15 extra minutes asking diagnostic questions Daniel was too stressed to answer cleanly.

**What Joana did well — formalize these immediately:**

1. **The 90-second acknowledgment with a committed timeline.** "3 options in under 10 minutes." That's not just SLA — it's expectation management. The member stops refreshing their phone.
2. **Three options, ranked, with a recommendation.** Most desks dump options and ask "what do you want?" That offloads cognitive work onto a person at their worst moment. Joana told him what she'd do. That's the move.
3. **The proxy text to Sarah Chen.** This is the single highest-leverage moment of the night. The member's *counterparty* now has a Latitude touchpoint. Brookfield Place's Sarah Chen now knows Daniel has an institution behind him. That's free B2B marketing inside a Series B raise.
4. **The honest framing of Option A.** "You will be 5-15 min late." Not "you'll make it!" That's a desk that respects the member's intelligence.
5. **Logged the incident with structured fields.** The founder can audit, the data is queryable, this becomes operational memory.

**What broke or almost broke:**

1. **The DL1086 seat hold was a near-miss waste of motion.** Joana took a 20-min hold on DL1086 at 3:06am while she was still building Option A. If Daniel had picked B, fine. But he didn't, and Marcus at Delta now has a wasted hold. Not catastrophic, but at scale this burns advisor-desk goodwill. **Better:** present options first, hold inventory only after member directional signal.
2. **The corporate AmEx float for booking speed.** She put $612 on the LAT43 card to avoid a re-auth friction step. That's fine at $612. It is not fine at $4,200 for a same-day J-class ticket. There is no documented threshold. **This is a written-policy gap.**
3. **The "I'm here until 6pm your time" line.** Daniel doesn't know what time it is in Cebu. He doesn't know what happens at 6pm her time. **This needs to read: "I'm here until 6am EST, then Ari [founder] picks up direct."** Continuity, not abandonment.
4. **No backup human if Joana herself had an issue at 2:48am.** Single point of failure. If Joana had been in the bathroom, the 90-second SLA dies. There is no documented secondary coverage.

**Should this have gone to the founder earlier?**

No. The founder's job at 3am is to sleep so he can be sharp at 7am for the personal check-in call. Joana correctly identified that she had: (a) authorization, (b) inventory, (c) a clear member preference signal (logged stakes), and (d) no escalation trigger (no medical, no legal, no >$2K spend, no member distress beyond normal stress). She solved and notified. That's exactly right.

**If** the founder had been woken at 2:48am, he would have done what Joana did — slower, less rested, and without adding value. The model worked.

**Three improvements to the IROP protocol:**

1. **Written escalation matrix.** Founder must be woken if: member health/safety, spend >$2,500, member explicitly asks for owner, supplier dispute requires founder relationship leverage, or any incident involving a Concierge Atlas tier member (when that tier launches). Everything else: notify async, founder responds when up.
2. **A "stakes log" pre-trip ritual.** The Sunday voice memo from founder was the secret weapon. Formalize it: every Atlas Office trip gets a 60-second founder-recorded voice note in the CRM 48 hours before departure flagging stakes, sensitivities, and any pre-authorized escalations. This is the most valuable 5 min/week the founder can spend.
3. **Counterparty notification consent at onboarding.** The Sarah Chen text was brilliant but technically improvised. At onboarding, members should pre-authorize Latitude to proxy-text counterparties during IROPs. Get the consent once, use it forever.

**The scalability question — 5 Daniels at once:**

**Trigger to hire VA #2:** 35 paying Atlas members, or the first month with 2+ concurrent IROP events. Whichever comes first.

**The verdict — is $249/mo economically sound when crisis-handling is the dominant work mode?**

1. Joana's loaded cost ($8/hr) makes a 45-minute IROP cost $6 in direct labor.

**At 30 Atlas members, the unit economics are pristine.** Gross margin per member is ~78% after VA labor, tooling, and founder time. The membership is economically sound. The risk is not pricing — it's concurrency, satisfaction-measurement, and a written escalation matrix that doesn't exist yet. Fix those three things in the next 30 days or the next IROP will be the one that breaks the model in public.
