## 4. WHERE THE PLAYBOOK BROKE

**The reframe got delivered too late and to a defensive audience.** Lines 49–53 in the playbook are written as if the prospect has already opened up. Marcus did not. He sandbagged the diagnostic ("nothing catastrophic," "I haven't had a real problem in three years") and the founder had to push the reframe in around minute eight after Marcus had already disclosed Jessica's weakness. The playbook assumes diagnostic compliance. With a dismissive prospect, the diagnostic is itself a fight — and there's no guidance for that.

**The Centurion FAQ answer is technically sharp but emotionally flat.** The playbook (line 100) leads with facts. Marcus's response — *"That's partially true... but Virtuoso rates, FHR — I get all of that through Centurion already"* — exposes that knowing Centurion has degraded is not the same as caring. The founder had to improvise the "FHR is automated, nobody's calling the GM" line on the fly. That move is not in the playbook and it should be the headline answer.

**No answer for "I haven't had a real problem in three years."** This is Marcus's objection #3 and the playbook has no scripted response. The founder pivoted to the Aman story but a sharper operator would have said *"The fact that the only story you can tell me is one where it took four hours and you don't remember the name of the person who fixed it — that's the problem you're describing without realizing it."* The playbook should arm the founder with that line.

**Pricing presentation backfired.** Line 71: *"Based on what you described, you're in [Tier]."* The founder did this correctly — anchored Marcus to Principal at $3,600. But Marcus had already pre-loaded the $36K Firm objection from public materials. The playbook does not anticipate that prospects will price-anchor themselves to the *top* tier before the call. With Centurion cardholders specifically, the founder should lead with *"You're a Principal, not a Firm — three hundred a month"* in the opening reframe, not at minute fifteen.

**No answer for "The Points Guy says boutique travel is a vanity play."** Marcus's Brian Kelly invocation is exactly the kind of authority-figure objection a Centurion cardholder will deploy, and the playbook is silent on it. The founder improvised a decent answer (published SLA + membership cap = accountability) but it should be canon.

**The Firm tier exists publicly and is a liability with this segment.** A solo VC partner reading the website sees $36K and pre-decides the conversation is not for him. The playbook never addresses how to neutralize the optics of the top tier with solo principals before they self-select out.

## 5. THREE SPECIFIC IMPROVEMENTS

### Improvement 1: Rewrite the Centurion FAQ to lead with the structural failure, not the historical timeline

**Existing playbook (line 100):**
> *"Two structural differences. First, Amex Platinum is one of two outsourced call centers — Ten Lifestyle Group and Aspire. Centurion is also a pod. The Centurion 'dedicated' model was downgraded around 2022 according to cardholders paying fifteen thousand a year. Second, email concierge was eliminated in 2021 and off-list reservations in 2022. The product is materially worse than it was three years ago, even if you're still paying the same fee. We are a named human, salaried, fifteen members maximum, on a published 60-minute SLA."*

**Proposed rewrite:**
> *"Centurion's FHR booking is automated — you go through amextravel.com, you get the rate, you get the amenity, but nobody is calling the GM. The hotel knows your card; they don't know you. That's why your last upgrade dispute took four hours instead of forty minutes. Specifics: email concierge eliminated 2021, off-list reservations eliminated 2022, the 'dedicated' lifestyle manager model converted to pod-based the same year — Ten Lifestyle Group and Aspire are the call centers behind it. You've felt the rotation. Four reps in two years isn't 'dedicated.' We are a named human, fifteen members maximum, 60-minute published SLA. The card gives you the rate. We give you the relationship."*

This leads with the experiential consequence ("nobody is calling the GM") and uses the dates as evidence, not as the headline. It also pre-empts "but I get Virtuoso through Centurion" — the rebuttal Marcus reached for first.

### Improvement 2: Add a scripted answer to "I haven't had a real problem in [N] years"

The playbook has no entry for this and it's the most common high-status objection. Add to the FAQ section:

**Proposed addition:**
> *"That's the right question, and I'll give you the honest answer. The people who tell me they haven't had a real problem in three years usually mean one of two things. Either they actually haven't — in which case they're a bad fit for us and I'll tell them that — or they've absorbed the failures as background noise. The Aman moves you from a Garden Suite to a Deluxe and it takes four hours and you can't remember the name of the person who fixed it. That's a 'problem,' it just doesn't feel like one because you got through it. The Latitude question isn't 'are your trips collapsing.' It's 'are you spending four hours absorbing failures that should take forty minutes.' Most of my members don't realize how much time they were absorbing until the second quarter with us."*

This converts Marcus's defensive stance ("no problems") into an admission ("I'm absorbing failures") without making him feel cornered. The Aman story would have closed the loop if this script existed.

### Improvement 3: Reframe pricing in the opening for solo-principal prospects

**Existing playbook (line 22 opening + line 71 pricing):**
> *"Thanks for the time. Quick housekeeping..."* then later *"There are three tiers. Principal is thirty-six hundred a year..."*

**Proposed rewrite — add a sentence to the opening for solo principals:**
> *"Thanks for the time. Quick housekeeping — I usually keep these to twenty minutes. Before we start: I know you've probably seen three tiers on the site. For someone in your seat — solo principal — we're talking three hundred a month, not the firm tier. I want to take that off the table before it gets in the way of the conversation. Sound right?"*

This neutralizes the $36K anchor in the first thirty seconds, before Marcus can deploy it as objection #2. With Centurion cardholders specifically, the $15K Centurion fee should be framed inside the opening as the comparison ($3,600 vs $15,000), not at minute fifteen when the founder is already on the back foot.
