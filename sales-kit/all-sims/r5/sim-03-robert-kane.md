**ROBERT:** Right. And I can quantify Lauren — she's $95K all-in, ninety minutes is about $70. The risk to the prospect is the real number and it's not quantifiable until I lose one. ## 4. PLAYBOOK BREAKS (where the script failed Maya)

- **The hero ROI line is a liability with a data-driven buyer.** Playbook: *"at your spend level — call it eighty thousand a year — we typically capture three to nine thousand in upgrades, credits, and rebooked savings."* Robert called this out immediately: *"Not 'three to nine thousand in upgrades.' I've heard that pitch."* The word "typically" with no n, no case study, no methodology is exactly the kind of unsupported claim a numbers-driven buyer punishes. Maya saved it by abandoning the script and admitting she has 11 members and only 6 months of data. **The playbook offers no fallback when "typically" gets challenged.**

- **No case-study scaffolding exists.** Playbook says *"After 20 calls, patterns emerge"* and *"By call 30, you should know your conversion rate."* That's a system for *after* you've sold. It gives the founder nothing to bring *into* call #12. Maya had to invent on the spot: "Boston principal, six months, $4,200 captured." That number could be right, could be a stretch, could have been challenged with one more question ("rack value or realized?") and she'd have been exposed.

- **The "you're not buying ROI" deflection is wrong for this persona.** Playbook: *"you're not buying ROI. You're buying never thinking about it again. The same way you don't calculate the ROI of your accountant."* Robert *does* calculate the ROI of his accountant — he's an RIA, that's his job. If Maya had used this line, the call would have ended. The playbook treats the ROI dodge as universal; for finance-buyer personas it's a tripwire.

- **No 90-day measurement framework anywhere in the playbook.** Robert asked: *"How do I measure success in 90 days?"* This is the most common evaluator question from any sophisticated B2B buyer and the playbook has zero language for it. Maya invented four metrics on the spot. They were good metrics. They should not have been invented live.

- **No contractual-recourse language beyond a cancel clause.** Robert asked: *"What's the contractual recourse if you don't deliver?"* Cancel ≠ recourse. Maya improvised the 90-day money-back. The playbook treats cancel as the answer; for a buyer who's been burned, cancel is the floor, not the ceiling.

- **Tier-anchoring math is hand-wavy.** Playbook: *"VC partner at a $400M fund: $150–300K. RIA with 200 clients and a quarterly conference: $80–150K."* These are guesses dressed as bands. Robert's $80-120K T&E happened to be in-band, but Maya's Office-tier ROI math ("$12,000 to $18,000 across the firm") had no source. A buyer who pushed one more layer would have unwound it.

## 5. THREE IMPROVEMENTS (concrete edits)

### Improvement 1 — Add a "Numbers Pack" section to the playbook, replacing the "three to nine thousand" line.

Replace the Reframe-script line *"at your spend level, that's $4,000 to $9,000 a year of upgrades and credits sitting unused"* with a sourced, tiered table the founder maintains and updates monthly. Example structure:

> **Realized-benefit benchmarks (as of [month])**
> Source: n = [X] active members, [Y] months of data, methodology: rack-value of captured upgrades minus 40% utilization discount, plus itemized credits redeemed, plus rebooked-IROP delta.
> - Principal tier, 20-30 trips/yr: $2,400-$4,100 realized, $4,000-$6,800 rack
> - Office tier, firm spend $400K+: $9,200-$14,000 realized
> - Caveat: data is [N] months in. We will not quote figures we cannot source.

And add a script line: *"I'll tell you what the data says and how thin it is. We've been operating [N] months with [X] members. Here's what I can defend."* Honesty as a feature, scripted.

### Improvement 2 — Add a "Sophisticated Buyer Track" branching from the Diagnostic.

Currently the playbook has one path. Add a decision point after question 2: *if the prospect uses the word "ROI," "data," "metrics," or "framework" in their first two answers, branch to the Sophisticated Buyer Track*, which includes:
- A pre-built 90-day measurement framework (4 metrics, hours/dollars/IROP-time/qualitative), with targets keyed to T&E band.
- A 90-day money-back clause as the default, not the exception, with the exact contract language.
- An explicit ban on the "you don't calculate the ROI of your accountant" line.
- A reference-call protocol: which member, which day, what they're authorized to disclose.

### Improvement 3 — Build the reference roster into a named asset, not a vibe.

The playbook says *"if you want to talk to my first two reference members before signing, I'd rather you do that than not."* That's a sentence. It should be a system. Add a section called **The Reference Bench**:

> Maintain a living list of 3-5 members per segment (RIA principal, VC partner, founder-operator) who have explicitly opted into reference calls, with: their cap (2/quarter is typical), what they will and won't say, the specific dollar/hour figures they have agreed can be shared, and a 48-hour SLA for setting up the call.
>
> If you don't have a reference for a segment, say so on the call: *"You'd be my first RIA principal at your AUM band. That's a risk and a benefit — first-member pricing and direct line to me. Here's what that looks like."*

Without this, every founder on every call reinvents the reference offer under pressure. With it, the offer is calibrated, repeatable, and survives scrutiny from buyers like Robert who will absolutely make the call.

---

**Harsh bottom line:** Maya closed (conditionally) because she abandoned the playbook the moment Robert pushed on numbers. The playbook's ROI scaffolding is built for buyers who want to be reassured, not buyers who want to be convinced. Against a Robert Kane, the script is a liability after minute eight; the founder's instincts saved the deal. Fix the Numbers Pack, the Sophisticated Buyer Track, and the Reference Bench, and the next Robert closes on the call instead of conditionally three weeks out.
