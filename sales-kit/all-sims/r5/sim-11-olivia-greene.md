## PLAYBOOK BREAKS — where the script could not handle her

The playbook **does not have a section for the burned-before buyer.** The FAQ assumes objections are intellectual ("how are you different from Amex"), not emotional ("the last person had the same badge and I lost eleven grand"). Three specific breaks:

1. **No script for the Cruise Planners ghost.** When Olivia opened with "you're under Fora, the last one was Cruise Planners, walk me through why you're not the 2026 version" — the playbook has nothing. The founder improvised the "host vs. operating model" distinction. It landed, barely, but Olivia called it "wordplay" at 01:55. A scripted operator without the founder's improvisation would have lost the call there.

2. **The 90-day no-fault exit and monthly billing are not in the playbook.** They had to be invented on the call. These are the two concessions that moved her from "walking at minute four" to "calling references." The playbook's cancellation answer ("three months notice, prorated") is *not enough* for someone who's been burned. The burned-before buyer needs a structural escape hatch built into the entry, not the exit.

3. **The playbook has no protocol for "prove it on something small before I sign."** Olivia asked for an unpaid hotel-move as a trust test. That's a real pattern with this buyer category and the founder had no rehearsed answer — he agreed reflexively. That could go badly (precedent of free work, scope creep, expectation that every prospect gets a freebie). It needs to be a deliberate, productized offer with rules, not a per-call concession.

## THREE IMPROVEMENTS — for the "burned before" prospect category

### 1. Add a "Burned Before" diagnostic branch to the opening

After question 2 in the Diagnostic ("worst trip in the last twelve months"), add a follow-up: **"Have you ever worked with a travel advisor before? What happened?"** If the answer is "yes and it went badly," the entire call structure changes. The Reframe section needs an alternate path: don't pitch the rewards math first. Pitch the *operating model* first — named principal, capped membership, published SLA, regulatory backing, commission disclosure — because the burned-before buyer's primary purchase is *not being burned again*, not capturing upgrades. The playbook currently leads with the wrong value prop for this segment.

### 2. Productize the "Trust Hatch" — 90-day no-fault exit, monthly billing, one free pre-sign task

The founder invented three things on this call to save it: 90-day no-fault refund, monthly Stripe billing, and the unpaid hotel-move test. These should be a documented, named offering — call it the **First 30 Program** — available to any prospect who explicitly cites a prior bad experience, gated behind a single qualifying question. Without productizing it: (a) the founder will offer it inconsistently, (b) it'll feel like an ad-hoc concession (which reads as desperation), (c) the free pre-sign task will become unbounded scope. With productizing it: it becomes a confident, on-brand differentiator. Add it to the website. Make it the headline for inbound leads.

### 3. Add a "Commission disclosure + reference call" section to the FAQ

The two questions that moved Olivia the most weren't in the playbook FAQ: *"how are you actually paid"* and *"can I talk to existing members."* Both should have rehearsed, sharp, repeatable answers — including the specific commission percentage range (10–16%), the fact that disclosure is per-booking in quarterly reports, and a standing offer of two reference calls with paying members (with those members' pre-consent). The current FAQ handles "what if you disappear" but not "how do I know you're not financially conflicted" or "let me talk to someone who isn't you." These are *category-of-one* questions for the burned-before buyer and they have to be answered before she's asked. If she has to ask, she's already half-suspicious; if you volunteer them at minute eight, you're the only firm she's talked to that did.
