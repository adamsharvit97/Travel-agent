## 5. THREE IMPROVEMENTS

### Improvement 1: Add a dedicated AI objection-handling section with the "Three Things ChatGPT Cannot Do" framework.

Insert into "The hard questions" between the Amex and Navan answers:

> **"How are you different from ChatGPT / my custom GPT?"**
> *"Three things the model structurally cannot do. One, it cannot see live inventory — it generates plausible text, not verified availability, which is why it hallucinates rooms and rates. Two, it cannot access consortium inventory — Virtuoso, Four Seasons Preferred Partner, Marriott STARS, Hyatt Privé rates are contractually gated to credentialed advisors and the contracts prohibit redistribution. Three, it cannot leverage a relationship — when I call a GM at 11pm, I'm calling because we've sent them eleven travelers in eighteen months, and that trust is not transferable to a language model. ChatGPT is a search and scaffolding tool; we operate in the licensed-inventory and named-relationship layer above it. Most of our members use both."*

This must be the most-rehearsed paragraph in the playbook by call 10. Every DIY founder in 2026 will ask this.

### Improvement 2: Build a "show, don't tell" demo asset for the call.

The playbook is verbal-only. Against a DIY prospect, words are not enough — they've heard every sales line. Build a 90-second live demo the founder can offer mid-call:

> *"Give me a hotel you're planning to book in the next 60 days. I'll pull the public rate on Google, the Amex FHR rate, and the consortium rate, side by side, on screen-share. Three minutes."*

The visible price-and-benefit delta destroys the "Amex Plat gives me Virtuoso rates" objection in a way that no sentence can. Add a section to the playbook: **"The Live Comparison" — when and how to offer it.** Pair with a pre-built spreadsheet template.

### Improvement 3: Add a "2am scenarios" library — three named, concrete failure stories per persona.

The playbook tells the founder to diagnose pain ("What's the worst trip in the last 12 months?") but provides no inventory of *founder-side* stories to deploy if the prospect plays it cool and won't admit failure (Sam's exact posture). Create a section:

> **For the founder/operator prospect, three stories on the shelf:**
> 1. *Fog at LHR, last flight to JFK cancelled, member at Connaught lobby at 11pm. We called the duty manager and held the room a second night while we rebooked through CDG-JFK on AF. ChatGPT would tell you to "contact your airline."*
> 2. *Hotel walk during Cannes Lions week — every room in the city gone. We had a held inventory block with the Martinez via FSPP. Took 14 minutes. The model has no held inventory.*
> 3. *Member's daughter passport stolen in Lisbon Sunday night. We had the US embassy duty officer's direct line through a Fora supplier network. The model would tell you to "visit the embassy Monday morning."*

These are the silver bullets for a prospect who refuses to surface their own failure. The founder names a story, the prospect's Florence memory surfaces involuntarily. **The playbook currently relies on the prospect being honest about their failures. Sales reality: half of them won't be. Build stories the founder can fire first.**
