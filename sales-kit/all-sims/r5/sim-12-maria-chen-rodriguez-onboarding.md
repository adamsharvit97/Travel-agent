## SECTION 2: SALES COACH EVALUATION

**Outcome: Strong start, with one structural risk I'll name.**

David did the thing most founders fail at on onboarding calls — he treated Patricia as the customer, not the gatekeeper. The line "you're the operator, I'm the supplier, Maria's the principal" is the entire game in 11 words. Patricia visibly shifted at that point ("That lands. Keep going."). That's the moment the call stopped being a sales risk and started being a working relationship.

**What worked, specifically:**

The opener — "what would make this call worth it for you" asked to *both* people independently — surfaced Patricia's actual fear ("If this becomes another tool I have to manage, it's a net loss") in the first ninety seconds. You cannot address an objection you haven't drawn out. Most founders would have run the deck and discovered that objection in week six when Patricia stopped responding to emails.

The $2,500 approval threshold negotiation was clean. David proposed $1,500, Maria said "two-five," he said "done" with no hedging. That moment told Maria: *this guy makes decisions at the speed I make decisions.* Founders who say "let me think about it and follow up" lose principals like Maria in the first month.

The Patricia-corrects-Maria moment ("She's also Delta Platinum but doesn't fly them domestic" and "she wants a desk, not a console table") is gold and David handled it perfectly — he didn't redirect to Maria, he wrote it down, he acknowledged Patricia knows more than Maria does on operational detail. That's the foundation of Patricia's buy-in. She is now publicly the expert in the room on Maria. She will defend that position by defending Latitude.

The passport spreadsheet handoff — "send me the spreadsheet, I'll put renewal reminders in our system, you stop tracking" — is the single highest-leverage moment in the call. He took a tedious recurring task off Patricia's plate on the spot. That's worth more than the dashboard demo would have been.

**What broke, specifically:**

David never explicitly addressed the EA Companion Seat as a *benefit* to Patricia. He mentioned it operationally ("Patricia's profile is built alongside yours"), but he missed the chance to say "Patricia, when you travel with Maria, you fly on us — same cabin, same hotel tier, same priority." Patricia is the renewer here and David undersold the one thing in the package that's hers.

The Naples trip handling was solid but he over-recommended Four Seasons without a fallback. "I'd put you there. It signals you read the room." is confident but slightly cocky. The right move is to offer two and let Maria pick — Maria is a managing partner of an RIA, she does not enjoy being told what's right. She'll forgive it on a $1,500 booking, she won't on a $40K trip.

He skipped the dashboard walkthrough entirely. Patricia asked twice about her workflow and never actually saw the tool. That's a week-two callback risk — "hey can you show me how the dashboard works" — which David should have closed in the call.

He didn't set a written confirmation. The 30-day check-in is scheduled but there's no recap email promised, no shared doc with the profile they just built. That profile is the deliverable from this call and it lives only in his notes right now.

**Three improvements for the onboarding playbook:**

1. **Send the profile back within 24 hours as a shared doc Patricia can edit.** The profile they built verbally has to become an artifact Patricia owns. That doc becomes Patricia's evidence to Maria that Latitude is real.
2. **Walk the dashboard live, even if it takes 8 minutes.** Patricia is a power user. She needs to touch it on the call, not read about it Tuesday.
3. **Name the EA seat as Patricia's benefit explicitly.** "Three trips a year, you fly the way Maria flies. That's yours." Make it personal, not procedural.

**The most critical moment in any onboarding call** is when the gatekeeper (EA, COO, chief of staff) decides whether to defend you or undermine you in the next internal conversation about you. David hit it. Patricia's "I'll have questions after I see the first itinerary. That's where I'll know" is a Patricia who is willing to be convinced. Six weeks ago she was willing to torpedo this. That's the win.

**Society upgrade probability over 24 months: MEDIUM-LOW (20-25%).**

Effect: Patricia tells one peer in her EA network within two weeks. That's the highest-ROI move in the first 30 days and no founder remembers to do it because they're focused on the principal.

Maria is not the customer to win. Patricia is. The transcript shows David knows this intellectually. The next 30 days are about whether he acts on it.
