# Latitude 43 — Sales Sim R4 — Cold Lead #1: Marcus Lindqvist

## TRANSCRIPT

**Zoom connects. Marcus is in a glass-walled office, AirPods in, has a coffee.**

**Marcus:** Hey — Marcus. You're, uh, Latitude something?

**Founder:** Latitude 43. Hey Marcus, thanks for the fifteen.

**Marcus:** Yeah, listen, I want to be upfront with you because I hate wasting people's time. We're not in the market for this right now. I replied because the email was actually well-written and I was curious what you do, but I'm not gonna pretend I'm going to buy anything today.

*(Beat. Marcus smiles, genuinely friendly. Not hostile — just clear.)*

**Founder:** Appreciate you saying that upfront. Genuinely. Can I ask — when you say "not in the market," is that "we already have someone," or "travel just isn't a problem worth solving right now"?

**Marcus:** Second one. We have an EA pool that books everything, and honestly the founders here travel less than you'd think. Maybe four trips a year each. It works. It's not broken.

**Founder:** Got it. That's a fair answer. Do you mind if I ask one or two things just so I'm not flying blind, and then I'll know whether to even bother you again later?

**Marcus:** Sure, go.

**Founder:** When the EA pool books a trip — say, you, Singapore, two weeks out — does it get done in twenty minutes or does it eat half a day somewhere in the org?

**Marcus:** *(pauses)* Honestly? Probably eats a chunk of someone's day. But that someone isn't me, so I don't feel it. Sarah handles mine. She's good.

**Founder:** Yeah. That's the thing — most COOs I talk to who say "we're fine" are fine because someone downstream is absorbing the cost. Which is a totally valid setup. I'm not going to try to talk you out of it.

**Marcus:** *(laughs)* I appreciate that. I was bracing for the reframe.

**Founder:** *(laughs)* I mean, the reframe exists. I could run it. But you already told me where you are, and I'd rather not insult you by pretending I didn't hear it.

**Marcus:** Okay. I respect that. So what do you actually want out of this call, then?

**Founder:** Two things. One — I want to understand whether Latitude is ever going to be relevant to a company like yours, or whether you're a structurally bad fit and I should just stop emailing fintech COOs at your stage. Two — if it could be relevant, I want to know what would have to change for you to think about it.

**Marcus:** Fair. Okay. Honestly — the structural answer is, we're at 90 people, we have admin infrastructure, and adding another vendor for something that isn't bleeding doesn't pencil. We're optimizing for fewer tools, not more.

**Founder:** Understood. Last question, and then I'll let you have your nine minutes back. *What would have to be true* for you to be in the market in, say, six months?

**Marcus:** *(actually thinks about this)* Hm. Probably one of two things. Either Sarah quits — she's the linchpin and we'd be exposed — or we hit a point where the founders are traveling so much that the EA pool can't keep up. We're talking about an EU expansion that might do that. But it's a "might."

**Founder:** That's useful. So the trigger is either a key-person exit on the admin side, or a step-function increase in founder travel because of EU.

**Marcus:** Yeah. That's accurate.

**Founder:** Okay. Would you be open to me checking back in six months — not to pitch, just to ask whether either of those things moved? If neither has, I'll go away. If one has, we can have a different conversation.

**Marcus:** Yeah, that's fine. Put it on your calendar. November-ish.

**Founder:** Done. One last thing — totally optional. If the EU expansion thing accelerates between now and then and you find yourself thinking "huh, this is starting to hurt," is it okay to email me directly instead of waiting?

**Marcus:** Sure. I'll probably forget your name by then but yeah.

**Founder:** *(laughs)* I'll remind you. Marcus, thanks for being straight with me. This was a more useful call than ninety percent of the ones where someone strings me along.

**Marcus:** Likewise. Good luck.

*(Call ends at 11 minutes. Four minutes early.)*

## OUTCOME

**Polite no with a clean 6-month re-engagement opt-in.** No sale. No materials sent (which would have been a kill). Founder identified two specific triggers (Sarah exit, EU expansion) that he can reference in the November follow-up. Marcus left the call with a positive impression — not because he was sold, but because he wasn't sold-to.

This is the modal cold-call outcome. Probably 50-60% of cold calls should look like this if the founder is doing it right.

## WHAT WORKED

1. **Accepted the "not in the market" frame within thirty seconds.** No "let me ask you something" jiu-jitsu. No "I hear you, but —". Just: okay, I heard you, let me check one thing.
2. **Named the reframe instead of running it.** "The reframe exists. I could run it. But you told me where you are." This is disarming — it signals competence (he knows the move) and respect (he chose not to use it). Marcus visibly relaxed.
3. **The diagnostic question was singular and surgical.** Not "walk me through your travel program." Just: "does a Singapore trip take 20 min or half a day?" One question, real data.
4. **The future-trigger question did the heavy lifting.** "What would have to be true for you to be in the market in 6 months?" got Marcus to do the work of identifying his own buying triggers — Sarah and EU expansion. That's the gold of this call.
5. **The follow-up ask was specific and low-cost.** Not "can I check in?" (vague, easy to deflect). Instead: "check back in six months, ask whether either of those two things moved, if not I'll go away." Marcus could say yes without committing to anything.
6. **Left four minutes early.** Counterintuitively a credibility move. Signals the founder respects time and doesn't need to fill the slot.

## WHAT BROKE

Not much actually broke this call — because the founder didn't fight the river. But worth flagging:

- **The "Sarah handles mine, she's good" line was a genuine close-out signal** and a worse founder would have tried to puncture it ("but what if Sarah leaves?"). That would have felt like a threat and would have killed the relationship. The founder correctly let it sit and reframed via the future-trigger question instead, which got Marcus to *volunteer* the Sarah-leaving risk himself.
- **No discovery of company-level pain.** The founder learned about Marcus's personal travel (handled by Sarah) but not about the EA pool's actual capacity, the founders' frustrations, or whether anyone has complained. In a warmer call that'd be a miss. In a cold call where the prospect declared "not in market" in sentence one, pushing for that data would have burned trust. Correct trade.

## ONE IMPROVEMENT

**Log the specific triggers in a CRM field and set a Nov 2026 reminder with the exact language to use:** "Hey Marcus — checking in like we agreed. Two questions: is Sarah still with you, and did the EU expansion happen? If both are 'same as before,' I'll go away for another six months." The November call's effectiveness depends entirely on whether the founder remembers the specifics. A generic "just checking in" follow-up undoes everything earned on this call.

The play here isn't to close Marcus. It's to be the one vendor who, when Sarah finally does quit in 2027, Marcus actually emails back.
