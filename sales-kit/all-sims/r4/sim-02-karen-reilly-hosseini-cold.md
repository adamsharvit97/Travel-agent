# Latitude 43 — Cold Sim R4: Karen Reilly-Hosseini (GP, $700M Fund)

## 1. Transcript

**[Zoom. Karen joins at 2:00:00 sharp. Camera off. Founder camera on.]**

**Karen:** I'm here. Five minutes. Go.

**Founder:** Thank you, Karen. I'll be tight. I'm David, founder of Latitude 43 — a Toronto-based travel office hosted under Fora. Before I pitch I just want to say the line from your GSB panel about "the cost of partner time is the cost no LP sees on the K-1" is the reason I sent the email. That framing is the whole reason this business exists. I'll keep this to ninety seconds and then ask you one question.

**Karen:** *(off-mic, to someone)* — yeah, tell him I'll call at four. *(back)* Okay. Ninety seconds.

**Founder:** Latitude 43 is built for GPs, RIA principals, and family office principals who travel a lot and don't want to run their own trips or manage a corporate travel desk that wasn't designed for how you actually move. Three things we do. One: we plan and book — flights, hotels, ground, the whole stack — so a partner doesn't lose a Sunday rebuilding an itinerary their EA half-assembled. Two: 24/7 desk, sixty-minute SLA, real humans in Toronto, so when your Lufthansa connection dies in Munich at 3 a.m. somebody is solving it before you've finished swearing. **[Call ends at 4:34. Karen had 26 seconds back on her calendar.]**

## 2. Outcome

**Soft-positive maybe.** Not a close. But the ending flipped a 95% ghost into something better:

- Karen reclaimed the inbox from her assistant — "send to me directly" is a real signal. Assistants are graveyards; principal inboxes are not.
- The materials will get opened. Probably skimmed, not read. Maybe a 25-30% chance of a reply (vs. the modeled 5%).
- A 15-min follow-up is not booked, but the door is wider than it was at 1:59.

## 3. What Worked

- **Ninety-second pitch, no diagnostic.** Founder correctly read that Karen would not answer "what's your worst trip" — she'd have hung up at the question mark. Pitch-first was the only viable shape.
- **The GSB callback was specific, not flattering.** He quoted a line and tied it to the thesis. That's research, not sycophancy. Karen is pattern-matched on flattery and tunes it out; she is not pattern-matched on someone actually engaging with her ideas.
- **Society-not-Atlas framing.** Telling a GP at a $700M fund "you'd be Society, not the $249 tier" is status-correct without grovelling. It says *I know who you are.*
- **The 4:30 exit.** This is the move. Ending early on a five-minute call is so rare that it physically broke Karen's attention pattern — she looked up at the camera. That's the moment "send to me directly" became possible. Respect for time is the only currency a cold GP actually values.
- **"I won't chase you."** Removes the threat of a nurture sequence. Cold prospects assume you'll spam them; saying you won't is a small trust deposit.

## 4. What Broke

- **Nothing catastrophic — but no close, and that's correct.** The playbook's Force-a-Decision and diagnostic both stayed in the holster. Under cold conditions with a hostile-time prospect, deploying either would have triggered the hard no.
- **"24/7 desk, 60-min SLA" got an "mm."** Operational specs do not move GPs on a first cold call. They're table stakes; she assumes any vendor claims them. Save for the materials.
- **The pricing line is still slightly long for ninety seconds.** Could be trimmed — Karen doesn't need to hear about Atlas at all if she's a Society candidate.

## 5. One Improvement for This Archetype (Cold, High-Status, Time-Hostile)

**Cut the operational pillars from the cold-call pitch entirely.** For a GP, the ninety seconds should be: (1) the thesis callback, (2) who it's for in one sentence, (3) the Society tier as a positioning anchor, (4) the question. The 60-min SLA and the rewards routing belong in the one-pager, not the call. Save thirty seconds, end at 4:00 instead of 4:30, and the goodwill compounds further. The pitch should sound less like a pitch and more like a peer briefing a peer on a thing they built.
