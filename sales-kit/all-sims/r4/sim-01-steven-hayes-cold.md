# Sales Sim R4 — Steven Hayes, GP at $1.1B VC Fund (COLD)

## Outcome

**Conditional handoff to Marlene** with a quarter-long pilot pre-committed if Marlene endorses. Probability-weighted: ~45% this becomes a $747 Atlas Office quarter, ~30% Marlene says no after evaluating, ~25% Steven forgets and Marlene never books. Genuinely the best plausible outcome on a cold call with this persona. Not a close — a *qualified pass to the actual buyer*.

## What worked

- **No diagnostic.** The founder didn't ask "tell me about your travel" or "what's your worst trip." Steven volunteered the Heathrow story unprompted because the pitch had already named the shape of the pain.
- **Acknowledging Marlene without scapegoating.** "She didn't go rogue. She thought it was worth checking, which is the job." That line bought ~30 seconds of patience.
- **Pre-empting the Amex objection.** Steven was loading the "I already have this" gun. Founder fired first.
- **The "vitamin vs painkiller" disqualification.** Offering to NOT sell was the single highest-trust move in the call. It reframed the founder from vendor to advisor in one sentence.
- **The Marlene-handoff as default option.** Not framed as a fallback — framed as "what people in your seat actually do." That's social proof masquerading as a logistics suggestion.

## What broke

- **First 20 seconds were still slightly too verbose.** "Throat-clearing" was itself throat-clearing. On a 2-minute clock with a chewing GP, the opener should be: "Private travel desk. $249/mo. We take the 2am calls Marlene shouldn't have to. That's the pitch — want the 90-second version or not?" Force the consent before pitching.
- **"Subscription, not commission" is jargon to Steven.** He doesn't know commission travel is a thing. Skip it.
- **No price anchor against his actual spend.** He travels 20x/year. $249/mo is ~$150 per trip. Naming that ratio would have landed harder than the monthly number.
- **No name-drop of a peer-GP client.** Cold GPs respond to "two of your LPs use us" more than to any feature. Founder had nothing to offer here — either because no permission, or because the roster isn't there yet. That's a roster problem, not a script problem.

## One improvement for the irritated-principal archetype

**Front-load the EA handoff offer.** Don't wait until the prospect is wavering. Inside the first 60 seconds, say: "Honestly, I usually don't pitch principals — I pitch the EA. Marlene's the one who'd use this. Want me to just talk to her and report back?" This collapses the entire call into a 90-second transaction, respects the principal's time, and routes to the actual decision-influencer without the principal having to ask. The Marlene-as-buyer realization should be a *founder-led* move, not a Steven-led concession. Right now it reads like the founder won — but it'd be cleaner if Steven never had to feel cornered into suggesting it.
