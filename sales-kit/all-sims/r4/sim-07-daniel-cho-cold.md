## Outcome

**Soft no, clean exit, possible future warm lead for Society tier.** Not a sale. Not a ghost either — the close-the-file email pulled a real answer out of him. He named the actual objection ("timing isn't right") which he would not have surfaced on the call. The Society interest is genuine but is also a face-saving move; treat it as a 20% real signal, not a pipeline entry.

This is the realistic best-case for the polite-ghoster archetype. The founder didn't get the sale, but he got the truth, and he got it within 14 days instead of 90 days of "circling back."

## What worked

1. **The diagnostic exit when it died.** Daniel had no worst-trip story. Founder didn't push it — he said "that's useful data" and pivoted. Forcing the diagnostic on someone with no friction story would have wasted the rest of the call.
2. **The Force-a-Decision close, run twice.** First pass got "send materials." Founder didn't accept it. Second pass — "what specifically would make you decide today?" — exposed that Daniel had no answer. That's the diagnostic for ghost-risk. The founder *knew* before he hung up that this was going to ghost.
3. **The close-the-file email.** The single highest-ROI artifact in the cold playbook. It gave Daniel a graceful out, and graceful outs convert polite-ghosters into honest-no'ers about 30-40% of the time. The "Society" mention is a bonus — file it, don't chase it.
4. **No needy follow-up after the close-file.** Founder didn't reply to "keep me on the list" with "great, let me send you more info!" He let it sit. That preserves the dignity of the no.

---

## What broke

1. **The diagnostic died at question 2 — as predicted.** Cold prospects without a pain story can't be diagnosed into one. The founder needs a cold-specific alternative: instead of "worst trip," try "what made you take this call?" — that question forces them to articulate a reason, and the polite-ghoster usually can't, which surfaces the no faster.
2. **"Tokyo is already booked" was a missed kill-switch.** Founder kept going. The honest move there is: "Okay, then there's nothing for me to help you with for the next three weeks. Want to talk again after Tokyo, when you actually need something?" That defers the decision to a moment when there's real demand — which there won't be, but at least the no comes from reality, not from a hypothetical.
3. **The on-call "soft yes" still cost a week.** Founder knew it was a ghost and still sent the one-pager and the bump. Both were wasted motion. A more disciplined version: skip the bump. Go straight from one-pager to close-the-file at day 10. Saves the founder a follow-up cycle and arrives at the same answer.

## One improvement for the polite-ghoster archetype

**Replace "what would make you decide today" with "is there anything I could send you that would change this from a maybe to a yes?"**

The first question requires the prospect to construct a yes-condition. The polite-ghoster can't, so they retreat into vagueness ("just need to think about it") and the call ends ambiguously.

The second question asks them to *commission* the artifact that would close them. If they can name it ("a case study from another studio owner"), you have a real next step. If they can't — and the polite-ghoster cannot — you've earned the right to say, on the call: "I don't think this is a fit right now, and that's okay. Let's not put it in the follow-up queue."

That's the cold-lead version of the close-the-file email, but delivered live, which saves two weeks and one wasted one-pager. The polite-ghoster will agree (they agree with everything) — but this time, the agreement is to *not* pursue it. Which is the only kind of yes they're capable of giving.
