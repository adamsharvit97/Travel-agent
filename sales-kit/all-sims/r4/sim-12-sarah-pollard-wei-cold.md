## OUTCOME

**Conditional yes, pending Bell & Bly Thursday call. Decision Monday.** Realistic close probability: 65-70%. She has a deal-breaker list, the founder named four items only Latitude provides, and she committed to a date.

## WHAT WORKED

- **Skipped the brand pitch when offered.** She wanted the grid; founder gave her the grid.
- **Honest competitor respect.** Said FROSCH is excellent, said the Fora advisor wins on price math for low-volume travelers. That bought permission to differentiate elsewhere.
- **Commission disclosure as the lead differentiator.** It was on her spreadsheet as an open question nobody had answered. He answered it unprompted and in mechanics, not adjectives.
- **EA Companion Seat landed because he asked first** whether she had an EA. Conditional differentiator only works after the qualifier.
- **Held $99 cleanly.** Reframed the discount as a signal problem ("I've told every other member the price is negotiable"), then immediately gave her something of real value (First Loss on Tokyo) so the "no" didn't feel like a wall.
- **Named the trip.** "Tokyo in July" turned First Loss from a marketing phrase into a contract clause.
- **Coached her on the Bell & Bly question.** Gave her a test only Latitude passes. Weaponized her shopping process.

## WHAT BROKE

- **Almost nothing — but** the founder leaned on the "desk vs. person" frame three times. Sarah caught it the second time ("SmartFlyer would say the same"). One more repetition and it would've felt like a script.
- **No discovery on her actual travel pattern.** He guessed at three-trips-a-year. She volunteered husband + Tokyo, but he could've pulled annual trip count, disruption history, points balance. Cold-call efficiency cost him texture.

## ONE IMPROVEMENT

For the *actively-shopping comparison-shopper* archetype: **bring your own competitive grid to the call.** Open with "I assume you have a spreadsheet — want me to fill in my row in real time?" That move flips her from interrogator to collaborator, and forces the comparison into your frame instead of hers. She's going to grid you anyway; own the grid.
