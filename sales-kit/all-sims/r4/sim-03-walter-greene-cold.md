## OUTCOME

**Send-materials with an authorized buyer handoff.** Not a close. Not a soft maybe. A genuine ~35% — better than the brief's 15% baseline because the founder got two things on tape: (1) Walter named the EA pain unprompted ("she spent four hours on a Delta rebook"), and (2) Walter pre-authorized Diane to spend up to $3K without coming back to him. The deal now lives or dies with Diane reading the one-pager. The founder has the budget cleared and the buyer named.

## WHAT WORKED

- **Price in sentence two.** Walter asked "how much" — he got "$249" before he got the brand story. That bought the next 90 seconds.
- **Naming the real buyer out loud.** "The pitch is for her, honestly. You're the budget holder, she's the user." Walter visibly relaxed after this — he no longer had to pretend to care personally.
- **The four-hour Delta rebook moment.** Founder didn't push for it; Walter offered it because the pitch had already framed the EA as the protagonist. Reflecting it back as "$2,400/yr on one symptom" math made the price feel like a discount, not a spend.
- **The forced binary at the end** — "send materials or stop emailing" — gave Walter a dignified exit and produced the budget pre-authorization, which is the actual asset from this call.

## WHAT BROKE

- **Nothing catastrophic, but the founder almost ate clock on the Fora/IATA credibility line.** Walter didn't care. Cold CFOs don't reward institutional name-drops in the first 90 seconds — they reward price and mechanism. That line should move to the materials, not the call.
- **The "we don't keep your points" beat landed flat.** For a CFO who doesn't book his own travel, loyalty is invisible. Save it for the EA call.

## ONE IMPROVEMENT FOR THIS ARCHETYPE

For the irritated-CFO-with-a-complaining-EA archetype, **lead with "this call is really for your EA, and I need 90 seconds of your budget authority"** in the first breath. Walter would have given the meeting back two minutes faster, and the founder would have walked out with the same pre-authorization without having to fight for airtime. The CFO isn't the buyer; he's the unlock. Treat him like the unlock.
