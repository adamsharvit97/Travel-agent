## Outcome
**Closed with concession (downgrade retention).** $2,988/yr → $1,188/yr. Net ARR loss $1,800 + $249 credit. Saved relationship, kept LTV, opened EA channel.

## What worked
- **Opening with Rome before Greg did.** Disarmed the biggest churn driver in the first 60 seconds. The "settling a debt from Y1" framing decoupled the credit from the renewal ask — made the apology feel real, not transactional.
- **Specific Y1 numbers, including the weak ones.** $14,200 captured, 11 hours saved, "Not spectacular. Fine." The honesty about middling value made the strong numbers credible.
- **"Right-size, not downgrade."** Reframed the cheaper tier as product-fit, not retreat. "I'd rather have you at $99 for three years than $249 for one" — confessional register, lesson 25.
- **EA reframe as "the seam, not surveillance."** Solved the underused-trips objection structurally instead of arguing Greg should use the Desk more.

## What broke
- **Pricing math under-rehearsed.** The $400 design-fee estimate on Light was improvised mid-call. Founder hadn't pre-computed Greg's actual trip-mix exposure. Could have been off by $500 either way and Greg wouldn't have caught it — but in a more numerate buyer (CFO, PE) this lands as sloppy.
- **No multi-year hold attempted.** Lesson 9 says never discount without a hold. The founder gave up $1,800/yr with no ask for a 2-year commit at Light. Should have floated: "$99/mo on a 24-month, or $109 month-to-month."
- **Maya onboarding promised but no calendar link in-call.** Lesson 22 — book the EA's onboarding before ending the founder's call. Founder said "end of week," which is a soft commitment that decays.
- **No First Loss Insurance offered for Y2.** Rome just happened. The natural follow-on was "and I'm putting First Loss on your next international trip, named today." Missed it.

## One improvement
**Build a renewal-call pre-read packet.** Before any Y2 conversation, the founder should arrive with: trip-by-trip Y1 ledger, captured-value dollar figure, hours-saved log, and a pre-modeled right-size scenario with design-fee exposure computed against actual trip data. Walking into a renewal call without the math pre-built means improvising the most important number in the conversation — and the only reason it worked here is that Greg wasn't a numerate buyer. Against a CFO or PE partner, this same call loses on the pricing improv alone.
