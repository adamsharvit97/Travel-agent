## OUTCOME
**Conditional yes** — closed design-fee project on Naples trip (June 18-20), with First Loss Insurance named, and an open path to Atlas Office Q3 contingent on trip execution. Not the full Office close, but the right close for this persona on this call.

## WHAT WORKED
1. **The napkin quote.** "I paid four thousand dollars to fly to a conference where the most valuable thing was a party I didn't get to." That's the line that flipped her from "remind me" to "okay you have my attention." Specific. Verbatim. Unfakeable.
2. **Naming Bree.** Got the name wrong first (Brittany), got corrected, kept moving. Better than not trying — it proved the founder was reaching for real memory, not reading a CRM.
3. **"Absorbed as background noise"** on the "IMPACT is always rough" objection. The playbook line landed exactly as designed — she went quiet, then did the $20M math herself.
4. **"Layer above" on the TMC objection** with concrete contrast: Concur compliance vs. daughter's peanut allergy. She didn't push back.

## WHAT BROKE
1. **Got the assistant's name wrong.** Brittany vs. Bree. On a re-anchor call, every detail matters. A 39-year-old founder noticed. Should've left a buffer ("your assistant — was it Brittany?") instead of asserting.
2. **Didn't disqualify up.** $250M AUM, 30 trips/yr, RIA principal — she's a Society candidate, not an Office candidate. Founder defaulted to Office. The "$249 is the wrong product for you" flip would've raised her status and likely closed harder.
3. **No reference offered until prompted.** Should have name-dropped the RIA-principal reference unprompted in the opening 90 seconds, not as a trust-rebuttal late in the call.
4. **Underused the rewards pillar.** A Naples client offsite with three couples is exactly where Four Seasons Preferred Partner credits ($100 F&B/room/night, room upgrades) become a visible, dollar-quantifiable win. Founder mentioned the pillar but didn't apply it to the named trip.

## ONE IMPROVEMENT
**Open with the trip, not the pitch.** This persona type — busy, vaguely-remembers-you, hostile to generic follow-up — responds to "I want to talk about Naples in June" faster than to "let me re-anchor our Future Proof conversation." Lead with the napkin quote (yes), but pivot to a *named upcoming trip* within 90 seconds. The structure should be: anchor moment → named future trip → product fits the trip. Not: anchor moment → product pitch → trip as proof. The trip is the wedge, the product is the consequence.
