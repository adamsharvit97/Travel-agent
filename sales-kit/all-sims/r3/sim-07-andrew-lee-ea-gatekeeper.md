## Outcome

**Conditional yes.** Decision deferred 48 hours with explicit deadline, named owner per workstream, written materials promised, and a Friday open-the-desk date contingent on Thursday outcome. Probability of close: ~70%.

## What worked

1. **Opening rotation** — *"I want a different answer from each of you"* — forced each persona to surface their own concern instead of letting Andrew speak for the room.
2. **Naming the three calls happening in one window** — labeling Andrew's as a *people question*, Sandra's as *integration*, Eric's as *math* — gave structure and prevented mush.
3. **"The member is you. The user, most days, is Priya"** landed on Andrew's threat-to-EA objection without lecturing him about delegation.
4. **Refusing to invent a Navan integration** built credibility with Sandra in one sentence. Honest "no" beats fake "yes" with a COO.
5. **Force-a-decision close with named owners and a deadline** — Andrew decides, Sandra checks with Priya, Eric runs the math, Thursday noon. No "circle back."

## What broke

1. **Eric's "fee you invented" jab** almost worked. The response was decent but reactive. Should have pre-empted by publishing the design fee schedule before he asked.
2. **The First Loss Insurance offer arrived late** — Sandra had to ask for the safety net rather than being given it inside the first ten minutes per the playbook.
3. **No reference bench deployed** beyond a vague "two EAs this week." Should have named segments — *"one Series B founder's EA in SF, one COO at a 60-person fintech"* — to make it concrete.
4. **Didn't address why Sandra and Eric were even on the call.** A founder hiring a travel desk shouldn't need a committee. Naming that gently — *"this is unusual at this price point"* — was done in line one but not pressed. There's signal there about how Andrew makes decisions, and it was left on the floor.

## One improvement

**Open with the math memo, not the people answer.** Eric is the gatekeeper. Andrew has already decided emotionally — he wouldn't have brought the other two if he hadn't. The fastest path to yes is neutralizing the CFO in the first five minutes by handing him the unit economics on screen, *then* doing the rotation. Right now Eric had to wait through Andrew and Sandra to get to his question, and a skeptical CFO who waits gets more skeptical, not less. Lead with the wallet in the room, not the heart.
