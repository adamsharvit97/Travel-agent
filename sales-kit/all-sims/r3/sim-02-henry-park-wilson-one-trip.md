## Outcome
**Closed with concession** — Atlas Light + member-rate design fee. Founder accepted lower upfront ($1,786 vs $3,375) in exchange for recurring $99/mo and the option value of future bookings. This is the correct outcome.

## What worked
1. **"Membership probably isn't your shape"** — early disqualification flipped Henry from defensive to curious. Removed the pressure he was bracing for.
2. **Naming the Instagram ad disclosure unprompted** — "Latitude doesn't run paid social, that was Fora." Bought trust cheaply.
3. **Running the math out loud, twice** — $3,375 vs $2,387 net of cancellation. Henry asked him to repeat it, which is the buying signal.
4. **Disqualifying up off $249** — "I'd be wrong to sell you up" is the line that closes skeptics. Confessional register, not triumphalist.

## What broke
1. **Design fee number landed harder than expected.** Henry said "two grand in my head." Founder should have pre-framed the fee range *before* asking trip details — not after. Anchored low by accident.
2. **Refund-the-fee promise was improvised.** Founder offered it without a written policy behind it. Fine as a one-off but it's a hole in the playbook for one-off projects — need a standing "Japan Guarantee"-style policy for trips over $30K.
3. **Didn't ask about the Park Hyatt points reservation.** Henry flagged it as wobbling and the founder skipped past. That's a $2K hotel decision and a relationship-building moment left on the floor.
4. **No reference offered.** For a $45K trip from an Instagram-cold lead, a 48-hour reference call from a past Japan member would have closed faster and harder. Bench wasn't deployed.

## One improvement
**Build a "One-Off Project Sheet" sent before the call** — pricing tiers, design fee scale with worked example, refund policy, what membership changes. Henry walked in anchored at $2K because nothing primed him otherwise. If he'd read the sheet, the $3,375 wouldn't have been a flinch moment, and the math conversation would have started 8 minutes earlier — leaving time to handle the Park Hyatt reservation and offer a reference. The playbook gap isn't the pitch; it's the pre-read.
