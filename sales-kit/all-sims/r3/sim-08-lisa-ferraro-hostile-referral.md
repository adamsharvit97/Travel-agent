## Outcome
**Conditional yes** — Atlas Light $99/mo, 90-day measurement clause, First Loss Insurance on trip one, onboarding Thursday 8am.

## What worked
1. **"Sarah's call shouldn't matter to your decision. Let's evaluate this on its own merits."** — The exact phrase the brief said would unlock her. Said in the first 30 seconds, before she could weaponize the referral.
2. **"I'm not pitching, I'm diagnosing. Three questions, then I tell you whether you should buy this or not."** — Bain-alumni framing. She recognized the structure and dropped her guard.
3. **The three-layer model — Booking / Recovery / Recognition.** Gave her a MECE frame she could pressure-test. A Bain partner buys frameworks; a vendor sells features.
4. **"I'd be wasting your money selling you Atlas Office."** Disqualifying up the price ladder (or down, here) flipped the dynamic from "vendor wants my money" to "advisor wants the right fit."

## What broke
1. **The "$2,500-$3,500 recoverable value" number was fragile.** She didn't push on it, but a sharper buyer would have asked which of her 60 Marriott nights are actually STARS-eligible (most aren't — STARS is portfolio-specific). The math hand-waved.
2. **"I'm new enough that you have no reason to believe me"** — honest but risky. With a different hostile buyer this reads as weakness, not candor. Lisa happened to reward it.
3. **No reference offered.** Brief calls out a Reference Bench, 2 per segment. With a Bain-grade skeptic, the move was: "I'd offer you two members on calls this week — not Sarah." Missed it.
4. **The "$1,200/hour" number came from her, not me.** Good outcome, but I led her to it instead of anchoring first. A Bain partner respects whoever sets the anchor.

## One improvement
**Pre-stage a non-Sarah reference inside the first five minutes.** The hostile-referral persona is structurally telling you the existing social proof is poisoned — you need to swap it out, not just neutralize it. The line is: *"Sarah's not the reference. I'll put you on with two members this week who match your profile — solo founder, 20+ trips, books direct today. You decide if they're credible."* That converts "ignore Sarah" into "here's better evidence," which is what a McKinsey/Bain brain actually wants. Neutralizing the bad referral is table stakes; replacing it is the win.
