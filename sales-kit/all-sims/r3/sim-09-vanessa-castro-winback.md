## Outcome
**Conditional yes** — re-joined Atlas Light with month one waived + First Loss Insurance named on call. Month-to-month, no annual lock.

## What worked
1. **Opening with Austin, by name, in the first ten seconds.** No throat-clearing, no "thanks for your past business." The acknowledgment had to be the first transaction.
2. **Surfacing the "not enough usage" lie without shaming.** *"I don't think that was the real reason."* — flat statement, no question mark, no accusation. Gave her room to confirm without losing face.
3. **The second failure framing** — *"Nobody called you. That's the second failure, and honestly the bigger one."* This was the one that actually mattered to her, and naming it before she did collapsed her remaining resistance.
4. **"We're not better than a good EA on a good day. We're better on a bad day."** Disarmed the EA-replacement objection without disparaging the alternative.

## What broke
1. **The 3.8% miss rate stat.** Unverified internal metric stated on a win-back call where trust is the entire game. If she'd asked "how do you measure that?" I'd have stumbled. Should have led with the process change and skipped the number.
2. **"We got smaller before we got bigger."** Too neat. Sounded rehearsed. A win-back persona's bullshit detector is high — anything that sounds like a tagline reads as marketing.
3. **The Atlas Office mention at the end** felt premature even with the six-month hedge. She's re-joining warily; the upsell flag plants a seed that this is a foot-in-the-door play.
4. **No mention of what happened to the agent who made the Austin error.** Sophisticated buyers want to know if the failure had consequences. I dodged it.

## One improvement
**Build a dedicated "Austin Protocol" for win-back calls: a written 4-line incident summary of what went wrong, sent to the prospect 24 hours before the call.** Vanessa walked in already knowing the founder knew. The call's first move shouldn't be acknowledging the failure — it should be discussing what to do about it. Acknowledgment-on-call is still a half-step of theater. Acknowledgment-on-paper-pre-call is the real upgrade, and it would have saved three minutes of preamble for a higher-leverage conversation about Devon and the SLA.
