# Sales Sim — Anaya Patel, Emerging-Manager GP

## 1. Transcript

**Founder (Ravi, Latitude 43):** Anaya, thanks for the 30 minutes. I'll keep it tight. Before I pitch anything — you took the meeting, which means something cracked open. What was it?

**Anaya:** Honestly? I'm in a car right now from SFO to Sand Hill because the United app rebooked me onto a connection through Denver without telling me. So your timing is good. But fair warning — I've heard the travel-concierge pitch four times. I don't have an LP-relations team, I don't have an events team, I don't have an EA. You see the same rate you'd see on the hotel site, frequently better because consortium rates include breakfast and a room credit you wouldn't get direct. The $99 is the access fee — text the Desk, get a named person, 60-minute first response day or night. If I'm honest with you: at 32 trips a year, you're a profitable member. ## 2. Outcome

**Conditional yes.** Hinged on First Loss Insurance delivery for the June 11 London/Edinburgh trip. Light tier ($99/mo), monthly billing. No discount given, no concession used.

---

## 3. What worked

1. **Fractional-EA-for-travel-only reframe in the first 60 seconds** — exactly the frame the brief said would close her. Removed the "should I hire a travel team" objection before she could finish forming it.
2. **Refusing to fabricate the breakfast-credit number on her actual trip** — "I won't fabricate a benchmark" preserved credibility with a sophisticated buyer who would have caught a made-up figure and walked.
3. **First Loss Insurance deployed with a specific named trip** — June 11, London/Edinburgh. Converted abstract skepticism into a concrete 48-hour deliverable. This is the move.
4. **Force-a-Decision close at the end** — yes/no/need-more-info. She committed verbally to a conditional yes instead of polite-ghosting into the void.

---

## 4. What broke

1. **The "Balmoral by default" moment was lucky, not engineered.** Ravi stumbled into it by asking a clarifying question. There's no scripted diagnostic that surfaces "default hotel I don't love" pain for solo GPs. Should be.
2. **The EA-cost comparison was clean on coverage and inventory but soft on the third leg.** "Performance management, the awkward firing" is true but feels HR-pitchy to a 35-year-old GP who has fired people before. Needs a sharper line — something like *"An EA is a person who will, statistically, leave in 22 months. We're a subscription that doesn't have a life event."*
3. **"Cute" landed but Ravi didn't pressure-test whether she was using humor to deflect or genuinely warming.** A more careful reader would have paused for a half-beat instead of immediately pivoting to logged-and-I'll-send.
4. **No mention of the post-trip receipt** (replacement for Reports). She's a solo operator who has to expense things to the fund — that's a small but real selling point that would have landed and was left on the table.

---

## 5. One improvement

**Build a "Solo Operator" diagnostic branch** — distinct from the EA-user/COS-user split. Emerging-manager GPs, solo RIAs, and one-person family offices are *both* the buyer and the user. The current playbook assumes a COS exists or doesn't, but the more interesting question for this segment is: *"What's the trip you've booked the same way three times in a row because researching it again isn't worth the hour?"* That question would have surfaced the Balmoral default in 20 seconds instead of stumbling into it at minute 18. Add it to the diagnostic.
