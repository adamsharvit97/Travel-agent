# Sales Sim — James Park (Founder, fast-casual chain, 28 locations)

---

## 2. Outcome

**Closed, full price.** Atlas Light $99/mo, First Loss Insurance attached to the Atlanta trip. Monthly billing, First 30 active.

---

## 3. What worked

- **"Operating tool, not concierge"** in the opening frame — neutralized the luxury allergy before he could deploy it. He never had to throw the "I'm a chef" line as a real objection; it came out as a half-joke.
- **Time math at his stated rate.** Fifty hours × $200 vs. $1,188/year. Concrete, his numbers, not a benchmark. He laughed and conceded — that's the tell.
- **EA rebuttal grounded in inventory and the 2am problem,** not in advisor mystique. "Your EA is asleep. We're not." Founder-to-founder, no condescension.
- **First Loss Insurance pinned to a named trip (Atlanta).** Moved the risk from him to us in one sentence. He stopped negotiating after that.
- **Force-a-Decision close at the end** — "yes, or yes-pending-form" — kept him from polite-ghosting after the call.

---

## 4. What broke

- **"Why so cheap" answer was okay but soft.** The commission-model explanation is fine, but the "filter" framing risks sounding like we don't value the fee. A founder like James respects pricing discipline. Better: "Ninety-nine is what it costs us to staff the desk per member. We're not in the membership-margin business, we're in the booking-volume business." Cleaner.
- **Two-hours-per-trip estimate was unverified.** He said "a couple hours a week" for all trips, not per trip. I extrapolated. He didn't catch it, but a sharper buyer would have. Should've asked: "How long does an average trip take you to book end to end?" and used his number.
- **Didn't name the principal.** The whole pitch is "one human, named." I said "we" and "the Desk" the entire call. James would have responded well to "Your principal will be Maya. She'll text you tomorrow." Missed.
- **Didn't acknowledge his wife.** He brought her up twice. That's not a throwaway. The pitch could have closed harder on "weekends back" as a non-dollar ROI. I left it on the table.

---

## 5. One improvement

**Add a "spouse line" to the founder track.** When a self-booking founder mentions their partner being annoyed about travel admin bleeding into weekends, do not skip past it. Name it back: *"The ninety-nine bucks isn't really for you. It's for Sunday night. You get Sunday night back."* For bootstrapped founders who refuse to spend on themselves, reframing the spend as protecting the marriage — not protecting the founder — is the unlock. James would have closed thirty seconds earlier.
