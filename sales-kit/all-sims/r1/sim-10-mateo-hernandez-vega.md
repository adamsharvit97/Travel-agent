## Outcome
**Closed full price** — Atlas Office at $2,988/yr, with a soft handshake on Society tier evaluation post-Cartagena (Q3).

## What worked
1. **Family-first reframe in opening 90 seconds** — naming Adriana as the real decision-maker, not Mateo, immediately ("the person who has to be happy in six months is not you"). Bought the whole room.
2. **Sit-underneath-Carmen framing** for the staff objection — didn't threaten Carmen's territory, gave her the trips she'd rather not own. Avoided the EA war.
3. **Put discretion in writing, with a fourth offer he didn't ask for** (quarterly access log). Overdelivered on the one objection where overdelivering signals seriousness.
4. **Holding Price on Society** — refused to sell the higher tier before Cartagena. Earned trust by leaving money on the table visibly.

## What broke
1. **Multilingual answer was honest but soft** — admitting the named principal is English-first was correct, but the Fora-pool backstop sounded slightly improvised. Should have had a pre-named Spanish-speaking backup principal ready to cite by name.
2. **Didn't deploy First Loss Insurance** on Cartagena even though it was the obvious named upcoming trip. Free win left on the table.
3. **"Small war" landed but "small war" is borrowed from his vocabulary** — risk of mirror-sounding sycophantic on a buyer this perceptive. He didn't flag it, but it was close.

## One improvement
**Pre-stage a named multilingual backup principal for any Latam/Iberian family-heavy buyer before the call.** "Your principal is Sarah; her Spanish-language counterpart on your file is María, based in Madrid, here's her bio" — said in the first ten minutes — would have closed the language objection cold instead of asking the buyer to trust an unnamed pool. For this persona band, the family-and-language axis is the deal; ship a roster, not a reassurance.
