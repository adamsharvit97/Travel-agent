## 2. Outcome

**Conditional yes** — Atlas Office signed pending Patricia's onboarding call. Italy trip scoped separately at design-fee rate (~$1,750–$2,300 member). No price concession given.

## 3. What worked

- **EA buyer/user split, deployed in first 90 seconds:** *"The member is you. The user, most days, is Patricia. Onboarding is with her, not you."* — neutralized the "Patricia does the booking" objection before it became an argument.
- **2am scenario draw-out on IMPACT:** *"Every RIA principal I talk to has one. Tell me anyway."* — got her to surface the LP meeting story herself instead of being told what her pain was.
- **Reframing cost from Patricia's time to Margaret's meeting:** *"The cost wasn't her time. It was your meeting."* — moved the value calculation from admin overhead to revenue protection, which is the right frame for a rainmaker.
- **Pre-empting Italy with real numbers:** quoting the $1,750–$2,300 design fee unprompted, before she could ask, signaled the trip was already understood as in-scope. This was the deal-saver — the brief said she walks if Italy is dismissed.

## 4. What broke

- **Charlotte local question got buried near the end.** The honest answer landed, but it should have come earlier — when she said "I'm in Charlotte, do you have local relationships," delaying the answer reads as dodging. Next time, address it inside the first ten minutes.
- **Husband question handled okay but not gracefully.** The "side channel vs. read-access" framing was correct but felt improvised. Should be a prepared line: *"He gets his own profile inside your account. Patricia sees what you want her to see."*
- **Didn't use the First Loss Insurance hook.** Margaret named a specific upcoming trip (October Italy). That's the textbook trigger for offering the productized free trip-fix. Missed it.
- **"$40-60K" range was slightly loose.** The voice guide bans "approximately" and prefers specific dollars. Should have committed to one number based on her city count and stuck to it: "Twelve pax, three cities, October — call it $52K, design fee at $1,850 member."

## 5. One improvement

**Build an "annual client trip" play as a named module in the playbook.** RIA principals, lawyers running client appreciation programs, and PE firms doing LP retreats all have one annual trip that is operationally radioactive and emotionally load-bearing. The Italy trip in this sim isn't an edge case — it's a wedge. The play should be: surface the trip in minute 8, quote the design fee inside minute 12, treat the membership as the consequence of solving the trip, not the headline product. Right now the playbook treats the membership as the lead and the complex trip as an upsell. For this buyer band — founder-led, client-relationship-driven, annual ritual — the order is inverted.
