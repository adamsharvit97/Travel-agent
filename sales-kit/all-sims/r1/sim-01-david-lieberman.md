## Outcome

**Conditional yes.** Booked a working session as Society founding-member candidate #4. No money committed. Expected close September at $1,000–$1,500/mo.

## What worked

1. **"I'm not going to pitch you our $249 product."** Disqualifying him *up* — naming that the headline tier was beneath him — flipped the dynamic from "salesman is selling" to "operator is choosing." This is the entire pivot for buyers above the band.
2. **Specific date for Centurion's decline ("2018").** No fabricated benchmarks, but a defensible structural claim about *why* Centurion degraded (Amex offshored, relationships moved to consortia). Gave him a story he could repeat to himself.
3. **"You'd churn in ninety days and tell two people I was a waste of time."** Reframed the honesty as commercial self-interest, not virtue. Luxury buyers smell virtue-signaling; they trust self-interest.
4. **The COS-as-single-point-of-failure framing.** Connected his stated reality (firm sale, COS distracted) to a future cost he hadn't priced. Made eighteen months the *reason to start*, not the reason to wait.

## What broke

1. **"I don't fully know yet" on what $1,000 buys.** Honest, but thin. For a $1.5B-AUM operator, "I don't know yet" reads as underbaked unless paired with a specific structural answer. The save (named principal, shadow principal, GM lines, quarterly review) should have led, not followed.
2. **No anchor against the $1,000 floor.** Centurion is $15K/yr; Society at $1K/mo is $12K/yr — *cheaper* than what he pays now. Should have said that out loud. The math defends the price; leaving it implicit risks "is this serious money or not."
3. **The Society cap (30 members, 6 per principal, 5 principals) is 30 — but the math is 30 exactly, which is suspicious-clean.** A sharper operator would push on it. Need a real answer for why it's not 27 or 34.
4. **"Working name is Society"** — fine in this call because he engaged, but if he'd been skeptical, naming an unlaunched product is a credibility risk. Need a fallback: *"we haven't named it yet, and we won't until the founding six tell us what it actually is."*

## One improvement

**Build a "Society Candidate" diagnostic that lives separately from the Atlas funnel.** The moment the founder hears (a) over 40 trips/year, (b) existing $10K+/yr spend on a status product, (c) a COS or EA in the seat, and (d) a near-term liquidity event — he should switch tracks within the first five minutes and never mention $249 except to disqualify it. Right now the playbook treats Office as the default and Society as an upsell. For this persona band, Office is the *insult* and Society is the *only product*. The disqualifying-up move ("$249 is the wrong product for you") needs to be a named play in the book, not an improvisation.
