## Outcome
**Conditional yes** — Atlas Office, full price, contingent on reference call + AACR/FDA trip language written into the agreement. Decision within 7 days. Probability ~75%.

## What worked
1. **No fabricated benchmarks.** Refusing to invent a "trip-save percentage" and instead splitting into priors / SLA / signal-monitoring earned credibility in the first 90 seconds. She said it explicitly: *"more honest answer than I expected."*
2. **Three structural reasons re: Marcus** (inventory, GM access, coverage) — clean enumeration, no emotional appeal, matched her register.
3. **"Service contract on a scientific instrument, not a concierge."** Neutralized the luxury objection in one sentence by accepting her frame instead of defending against it.
4. **First Loss Insurance pinned to AACR/FDA**, the actual trip that scares her. Turned an abstract guarantee into a falsifiable test.

## What broke
1. **ASCO 2024 anecdote was thin.** "Member oncologist" with a Marriott walk is plausible but unverifiable mid-call. She let it pass but visibly downgraded the moment ("Maybe. Move on."). Needed a sharper detail — flight number, dollar figure, or a stat about Marriott walk rates at Marquis during ASCO week.
2. **"38-minute median" on the overnight pool** — I quoted it as if it were tracked data. If Fora doesn't publish that at the pool level, this is a soft fabrication and violates the playbook rule. Risk: she asks for the source in writing and it falls apart.
3. **Didn't quantify Marcus's reclaimed hours.** Said "Tuesday afternoons" — qualitative. For this persona, should have offered a measurable number from another biotech-CEO member or declined to quote one.
4. **Pricing came too early.** Dropped $99/$249 in the opening 60 seconds before she'd asked. With a sophisticated buyer the price reveal should follow the value frame, not precede it.

## One improvement
**Build a Biotech/FDA-travel scenarios library with verifiable specifics** — three named cases (one AdCom prep, one ASCO/AACR hotel-walk, one JPM week sold-out rebook) with actual dollar figures, flight numbers, and consenting references on the bench. This persona will accept "I don't have that number" but will not accept a number that smells invented. The 38-minute median is the single most fixable risk in this transcript: either prove it from Fora data or replace it with "the SLA we hold ourselves to in the agreement, with refund triggers if we miss." Don't quote precision you can't defend.
