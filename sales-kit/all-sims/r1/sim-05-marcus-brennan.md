## Outcome

**Closed full price.** Atlas Office, $249/mo, monthly billing, 90-day evaluation window. Reference call with Greenwich RIA principal offered but Marcus declined ("Sharon can call her if she has questions").

## What Worked

1. **"I'm not going to tell you Centurion is bad"** in the first 30 seconds — neutralized the top-tier anchor before Marcus could deploy objection #1. This is the Round 1 fix landing exactly where it should.
2. **Named the three specific seams** (no continuity, weak escalation lever, no memory) instead of generic "we're better." Marcus volunteered the Carlyle story unprompted, which means he was already mapping his own pain onto the structure.
3. **"You don't need a coach, you need a back office"** — handled the 30-years objection without contradicting him. Reframed the value as labor arbitrage on a $2K/hour rate, which is a language Marcus already speaks.
4. **Design fee escalator math** — converting "$249 feels light" into "pays for itself on two deal sprints" turned the cheap-tier objection into a *features* conversation. This is the playbook fix the round needed.

## What Broke

1. **"Use Centurion for the easy stuff"** at the end — true and disarming, but a coach reviewing this would flag that I just told a prospect to keep paying a competitor. Should be reframed as "Centurion's fine as a backup card" rather than active routing.
2. **Didn't deploy First Loss Insurance.** Marcus named a specific upcoming pain (any deal sprint in the next quarter) and the playbook has a productized free trip fix for exactly this. Missed offer.
3. **"Our hour is worth a lot less"** — self-deprecation that worked with Marcus but is brand-off. The voice guide says confident-restrained. This was confident-cute. Acceptable once, not a pattern.
4. **No close on Sharon.** The EA buyer/user split (Round 1 lesson #4) says onboarding goes through the COS. I let Marcus end with "Sharon will fill it out" without booking Sharon's onboarding call. That's a 20% activation-risk leak.

## One Improvement

**Add a "post-yes immediate next step" beat to the playbook.** Right now the close treats "send the application" as the end state. For EA-supported buyers (Marcus, PE MDs, RIA principals), the application is the easy part — the hard part is getting 30 minutes with the COS/EA in the first 7 days to load preferences. Script the line: *"I'll send the application now. Separately, I'll put a 30-minute hold on Sharon's calendar Thursday or Friday — that's the conversation that makes the next deal sprint actually work. Which day?"* Force the calendar event before the call ends. Without it, half these closes activate slowly and churn at month three.
