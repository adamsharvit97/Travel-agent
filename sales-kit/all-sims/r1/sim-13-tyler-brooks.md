## Outcome

**Closed full price.** Atlas Light, $99/mo monthly billing, personal Amex.

## What worked

1. **"You eat it. Ninety-nine a month, your card, never crosses your firm's books."** — Directly validated the personal-payment frame in the first 90 seconds. Tyler would have walked if the founder had dodged this.
2. **"Four PE and IB associates in the membership right now"** — Named segments (megafund, middle-market, bulge bracket) without naming people. Neutralized the "am I weird for doing this" objection cleanly.
3. **"Partners have EAs. You have a Saturday and a phone. Different problem."** — Reframed the seniority objection as a category difference, not a status one. Tyler stopped justifying.
4. **Twelve-minutes-vs-60-SLA honesty** — When Tyler challenged the specific number, the founder admitted twelve was a one-off and held the floor on the 60-minute promise. Bought credibility for everything after.

## What broke

1. **The TMC corporate-rate objection got handled, but slowly.** Founder gave a three-part answer when one line would have sufficient: "TMC for firm trips, me for personal. They don't overlap." Tyler is a finance guy — he'd have respected the cleaner version.
2. **"Congratulations. Even more reason."** — Veered close to schmoozy. Tyler laughed, but with a different associate this lands as oily. Cut it or shorten to "Congratulations."
3. **Design fee disclosure was rushed at the end.** A $5K cap on a two-week honeymoon is a real number Tyler will remember later. Should have been delivered with a beat, not as one-of-three in a list.
4. **No First Loss Insurance offer.** Tyler is a sophisticated buyer with a specific upcoming trip (the rebooking pattern is recurring). Founder could have said "name your next trip — I'll cover the first rebooking free up to $1K labor" and locked in the close even harder. Missed.

## One improvement

**Add a "Parallel Track" script to the playbook for associate-tier buyers at TMC-contracted firms.** The pattern is: associate uses firm TMC for booked-in-advance diligence trips, uses personal card for last-minute rebookings and personal travel, expenses what's expensable. The playbook currently treats TMC firms as a disqualifier. They're not — they're a *segmentation*. The script: "The TMC owns the calendar. I own the chaos and the weekends." One line. Use it whenever the prospect mentions a corporate vendor in the first three minutes.
