## Outcome

**Closed with concession** — Aspen design-fee project booked at $5,000 cap (~$80-100K trip). Atlas Office membership deferred to post-Aspen decision. Confidentiality clause and NDA agreed as condition precedent.

## What worked

1. **"That's not a trip. That's a diplomatic summit with luggage."** — Named the actual problem in her language; got the first laugh; established that the founder understood the political layer, not just the logistics.
2. **Four-question diagnostic before pitching** — Especially Q4 ("whose phone rings in the lobby") — surfaced the real pain (11pm calls) without the founder having to assert it.
3. **Externalizing the word "no"** — Reframed the staff objection from "redundant headcount" to "outsourced political cost." This is the line that broke the staff objection cleanly.
4. **Structural confidentiality answer** — Five concrete mechanisms (NDA, naming convention, read-access scoping, no marketing, opt-in case-study clause) instead of the word "discreet." The opt-in-default-off marketing clause was the line that visibly moved her.

## What broke

1. **Almost lost her on the opening.** "Convince me, or don't" was a real test and the founder's first instinct was to ask about the trip — which worked, but barely. A weaker opener (any pitch language, any "what we do is...") would have ended the call in 90 seconds. Playbook needs a named **"or don't" opener** for ultra-senior buyers who pre-emptively decline.
2. **Did not quote Atlas Office structure cleanly the first time** — had to be re-prompted by Caroline. The rolling-roster idea for a family of 14 against a 5-traveler cap was improvised mid-call. Should be a productized variant: **Atlas Office Family** with explicit rolling-roster language.
3. **Patriarch's "hard line" question got answered but not used** — Caroline said "he wants to feel like he arranged it" and the founder didn't return to it. The close should have explicitly named how the engagement protects the patriarch's hosting frame. Missed reinforcement.
4. **Did not anchor against alternatives.** No mention of why this isn't a job for a high-end DMC, an event planner, or the family's existing concierge service. For a $1.4B AUM buyer, those are the real comps, not other travel advisors.

## One improvement

**Build an "Atlas Family Office" sub-tier** explicitly for single- and multi-family offices: rolling-roster membership (5 active travelers swappable monthly from a named pool of up to 20), pre-signed mutual NDA template, opt-out-by-default marketing clause as a contract standard not a negotiation, and a flat-fee event-design SKU separate from per-trip design fees for set-piece family gatherings (weddings, milestone birthdays, memorials). The current Atlas Office tier was designed for a 5-partner PE firm, not a 14-person family across three generations who don't speak to each other. The playbook is improvising this tier on every family-office call; productize it.
