# Outcome

**Warm — calendar hold secured for 15-minute follow-up.** No close attempted. No money committed. One-pager promised, follow-up booked with the EA (Diane). This is the correct outcome for this persona on this call.

# What worked

1. **The cold open disarmed him.** "I am not here to close you today" inside the first thirty seconds is the only sentence that buys a CFO's attention. He gave a one-word "Go" — that's the sign the frame worked.
2. **The 90-second script, delivered verbatim and timed.** Pillars, price, no adjectives. He didn't interrupt, which means he was listening, which means it was short enough.
3. **The "I'm not going to manufacture a pain point" line** when he admitted no horror story. This is the Round 1 lesson #2 inverted — instead of forcing the "you absorbed the failures" reframe, the founder *agreed* with him and shifted to cumulative annoyance + future-buyer framing. For a cynical CFO with genuinely thin pain, that's the right move. The reframe is for buyers in denial; Maslow wasn't in denial, he was honest.
4. **The board-meeting return-leg specific.** This is the one moment the founder went concrete and Maslow said "that's a real moment, yeah." That's the only emotional flicker in the call, and the founder didn't pounce on it — moved straight to the calendar ask.

# What broke

1. **"Send me materials" got a slightly long answer.** The founder pushed back instead of just saying "I'll send it." Maslow let it pass, but a tougher CFO would have read the pushback as a closer's tell. Better: "I'll send it. Separately — fifteen minutes next week?" Don't argue with the dodge; route around it.
2. **"What's the point?" was answered with a feature list (cancellation, walked hotel, Newark).** The founder slipped into pitch mode for two sentences. The correct move is to bounce the question back: "What's annoying enough about your current setup that your COO told you to take this call?" Let Maslow surface the pain.
3. **The personal-vs-company answer was good but slightly long.** "Your controller will" is the right close to that thread; everything before it was filler. CFOs decide expense classification in three seconds — don't explain the structure to them.
4. **No First Loss Insurance offered.** Could have been deployed as the calendar-hold sweetener: "On the follow-up, if you name a specific trip in the next 90 days, we'll handle it free as a trial, capped at our labor." Would have made the Friday call land harder. Not a fatal miss, but a missed lever.

# One improvement

**Build a "Cynic CFO" branch in the playbook with one rule: never answer a CFO's deflection with a counter-argument.** Maslow's three objections — send materials, what's the point, personal or company — are not real objections. They are tests of whether the founder will pitch. Every time the founder explained instead of redirecting to the calendar ask, he lost ground he had to win back. The playbook needs a literal flowchart for this persona: *Deflection → Acknowledge in one sentence → Calendar ask → Stop talking.* The current playbook has a 90-second script for time-poor buyers but no discipline for what happens in minutes 2 through 5, which is where most CFO calls actually die.
