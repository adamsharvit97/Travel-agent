## 2. Outcome

**Conditional yes** — committed to First Loss Insurance on the June 11 Austin trip. No paid membership yet. The trip is the trial. Real conversion decision happens around June 13.

---

## 3. What worked

- **Value in 25 seconds.** "Human desk that watches your flights in the background and rebooks you before you know it's broken" — concrete, no marketing puffery, anchored to the ORD story he'd already volunteered. Cleared the 60-second walk-away bar with margin.
- **"One prevented mistake = 6 months paid for."** Forced him to do the math out loud against his own missed-demo number. He laughed, which is the tell that the frame landed. The follow-up "the math gets uncomfortable for you, not me" was dry-humor-on-spec for the brand voice.
- **First Loss Insurance, deployed at the right moment** — right after the second objection ("what's the ROI") rather than panic-thrown at the first. Marcus's "wait, free?" is the exact reaction the product is designed to provoke.
- **Force-a-Decision close inside a 5-minute window.** "Yes, no, or need-more-information" at 4:20 left enough runway to actually close the conditional. Without it he would have said "let me think about it" and ghosted.

## 4. What broke

- **"Your COO is right and you know it"** is too familiar for a first call. Marcus laughed it off, but with a different founder that's a slap. Should have been "your COO has a point" — same content, no presumption.
- **The "spec, not the edge case" line landed but took too long to set up.** Burned ~25 seconds on the "too small" objection when there's a tighter version: *"Atlas Light was built for fourteen-trips-a-year domestic. You're the target, not the exception."* One sentence, move on.
- **Didn't name the principal.** Brand voice says "named principal." I said "the Desk" five times and never gave Marcus a human name. For a seed founder who's never used an advisor, the name is the trust anchor. Should have said "you'll work with [name], she runs the desk for members on your cadence."
- **Missed the COS/EA handoff opportunity.** Marcus mentioned his COO twice. The playbook has a clean line for this ("the member is you, the user most days is your COO") and I didn't deploy it. Would have made the membership feel less like *another thing for Marcus to manage* and more like *something his COO would actually use.*

## 5. One improvement

**Add a "Sub-$100 Founder" branch to the diagnostic tree.** Marcus is not a frugal Series A founder, not a PE MD, not a CFO — he's a sub-$200K-personal-comp seed founder for whom $99/mo is genuinely a budget line, not a rounding error. The current playbook treats $99 as obviously cheap. It isn't, to him. The branch should: (a) skip the ROI-in-time-saved frame entirely — he doesn't value his time at $400/hr yet, he values it in runway months; (b) lead with First Loss Insurance in the first 90 seconds, not as a save; (c) reframe membership decision as "decide after the free trip" by default, not as a close on the first call. Conversion target on this segment is the *second* call, not the first. Treating the first call as a qualified-lead-generator rather than a close would have left Marcus less defensive and the free-trip handoff cleaner.
