## 2. Outcome

**Conditional yes** — Atlas Office, monthly billing, contingent on David's onboarding call. High-conviction conditional; the gate is real but low-risk if the Desk handles David well.

## 3. What worked

- **"What isn't David doing this week because he's doing this"** — the sideways question landed exactly as the brief said it would. Four seconds of silence, then she surfaced the board-prep slip and the missed 1:1s herself. The founder never criticized David; Helena did the math.
- **EA buyer/user split, clean delivery** — "The member is you. The user, most days, is David. Onboarding is with him." No "10,000 hours" lecture. She physically softened on that line ("That's — yeah. Okay.").
- **Navan triage** — "We don't replace Navan. We sit next to it." Killed the objection before she finished asking it. Specific framing ("policy and expense engine for 240 people booking economy to SFO") earned the trust.
- **Force-a-Decision close** — yes/no/need-more-information at minute 14 forced a real answer instead of "let me think about it" drift into the board call.

## 4. What broke

- **Opening anchor ran slightly long** — the "sixty seconds on what we are" actually went about 35 seconds but included pricing before diagnostic. With a 15-min hard stop and a warm intro, the founder could have skipped the box-tour entirely and gone straight to "what trip broke." Lost ~30 seconds.
- **"What's a trip David booked that broke"** — too direct on first pass. Helena's instinct was to defend David ("nothing catastrophic"). Recovery worked ("try a smaller word") but a softer first-ask — "what's one that frustrated you" up front — would have saved the friction.
- **ROI calc was sloppy** — "150 hours of his time back" is fine, but "at what you're paying him fully loaded, that's not close" is exactly the kind of vague "typically" hand-wave the playbook bans. Should have been a real number or no number.
- **Didn't name First Loss Insurance until minute 13** — it should have been offered the moment New York came up, not bolted on at the end.

## 5. One improvement

**For the protective-of-EA founder persona, lead the diagnostic with the EA-workload question, not the trip-failure question.** Helena's loyalty to David made "what broke" a defensive prompt. The sideways question ("what isn't David doing this week") should be the *opening* diagnostic for this band, not the rebuttal. Once she's named the slipping work herself, the trip-failure question becomes safe because she's already in problem-solving mode, not protection mode. Reorder the persona-band fork: founders-with-tenured-EAs get the workload question first, the trip-failure question second.
