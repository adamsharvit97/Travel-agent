# R7 Sim 11 — Model B (Light Unlimited $189) + FTG2

**Stage:** Negotiation
**Buyer:** Tomasz Werner, 35, PE associate, $2.4B fund, $80K T&E
**Model:** B — Atlas Light Unlimited, $189/mo solo, all trip design included
**Risk reversal:** FTG2 — refund the failed month + next month free + $500 service credit
**Founder:** Latitude 43 founder (Toronto, hosted under Fora)
**Dramatic question:** Does FTG2's structure let the founder hold $189 without folding to Tomasz's "free month OR $169 lock"?

## Section 1: Transcript

**Setting:** Second negotiation call, Wednesday 4:15pm ET. Tomasz initiated. He has the proposal — Light Unlimited $189/mo, FTG2 attached. He's been comparing against his current setup (mostly self-booking through Amex Plat, occasional TripActions on the firm side, but $80K T&E is personal-card-then-expense, so the friction is his to absorb). He liked the pitch on R7 Sim 10. Today is the close — or the squeeze.

**Tomasz:** Okay, I've sat with it. Light Unlimited at $189, all design included, no escalator, FTG2 on the first trip — refund, free month, $500 credit. That's actually a reasonable shape. The number is fine. I want to do this.

**Founder:** Good. I'm hearing a "yes, and."

**Tomasz:** You're hearing a "yes, and." Here's where I land. $189 is fair on the sticker. It is not fair on the *commitment*. You're asking me to put a card on file, run month-to-month at a number that's twice what most people pay for a booking tool, and trust that the desk is going to be there on a Sunday night when my flight to Frankfurt drops. So I want one of two things — pick one. Either the first month is free, on top of FTG2 — call it a working trial. Or you lock me at $169 for 24 months. Twenty bucks off, two-year commit. Pick one.

**Founder:** Mm. Okay. Let me think out loud for a second, because I want to give you a real answer, not a sales answer.

**Tomasz:** Please.

**Founder:** The first one — free first month on top of FTG2 — I can't do, and the reason is structural, not theatrical. FTG2 already gives you the free month. That's literally the mechanic. If your first trip fails on our end, you get the failed month refunded *and* the next month free *and* a $500 credit toward the trip after that. So the "free working trial" you're asking for — you already have it. It's just conditional on us screwing up. If we don't screw up, you've paid $189 for a month where we actually delivered the trip. Which is the trade.

**Tomasz:** Right, but the condition is *you* deciding it failed.

**Founder:** No. It's named on the call. We name the trip — the Frankfurt one, the next one, whichever you point at — and the criteria are operator-side: did the booking go through, did the IROP get handled inside the SLA, did the standing instructions get followed. If any of those break, you call it. You don't have to argue with me about it.

**Tomasz:** Hm. Okay. That's better than I read it. Fine — strike the free month. Take the second one. $169, 24 months.

**Founder:** Also no, and I want to tell you why because I think you'll respect the answer more than the discount.

**Tomasz:** Try me.

**Founder:** Two things. One — $20 a month over 24 months is $480. That's not the number that should decide this. If $480 over two years is the wedge, the desk isn't going to pay for itself and you're going to churn at month eight anyway and we'll both feel bad. Two — I don't do 24-month locks at this stage of the business. Not for you, not for anyone. The reason is the opposite of what you'd think. I'd rather you be able to walk at month four if the desk isn't earning its $189, because if I lock you in and you're unhappy, I have a passenger, not a member. Passengers churn loud. They tell other founders. The lock costs me more than the discount.

**Tomasz:** That's a confessional answer. I'll give you that.

**Founder:** It's the real one.

**Tomasz:** So what's the move? You're telling me no on both. I came in expecting one.

**Founder:** I'm telling you no on the structure of both, yes. Here's what I can actually do, and it's smaller than what you asked for, but it's real. The first trip — the Frankfurt one, since you mentioned it — I'll personally run the build. Not the VA. Me. Standing instructions intake, the carrier-pair logic, the lounge defaults, the 2am number. That's about four hours of my time, and it means your first trip out is the highest-fidelity version of what the desk does. If that trip fails, FTG2 fires and you're out clean with a free month and a $500 credit. If it lands, you've got the muscle memory for how we work and the standing instructions are dialed for trip two onward.

**Tomasz:** So your concession is *more of your time*, not less of my money.

**Founder:** Yes. Because the thing that makes this work for you long-term isn't the $20. It's whether the desk knows that you fly United metal out of EWR when you can, that you want aisle seats forward of row 10, that your assistant Mara should be cc'd on every confirmation but not the IROP threads, and that if it's a pitch trip the hotel needs a desk and an iron in the room before 9am. None of that is in a discount. It's in the intake.

**Tomasz:** [pause] Okay. Push back on me here — am I being cheap or am I being smart?

**Founder:** Honestly? You're being a PE associate. You came in to extract a concession because that's the muscle. I respect it. But the concession you tried to extract was the wrong one for your situation. You don't need $20 off. You're going to expense this. You need the first trip to not be a disaster. So I gave you the thing that actually moves your risk, which is me on the build.

**Tomasz:** Fair. One more. The $500 credit in FTG2 — only fires if the trip fails?

**Founder:** Correct. It's a downside instrument, not a signing bonus.

**Tomasz:** Right. Just confirming. Because the version I'd push for — and I'm not pushing, I'm naming it — would be $500 credit on signing, applied to trip one. Not contingent.

**Founder:** Naming it back: no. And the same reason. If I give you $500 on signing, the membership isn't $189, it's $189 minus an amortized signing bonus, and now the price isn't holding, it's drifting. The whole point of $189 is that it's the number. If the number moves on the first call, it moves on every call after, and I'm not running that company.

**Tomasz:** Operator-correct answer.

**Founder:** Thank you.

**Tomasz:** Okay. Send the agreement. Light Unlimited, $189, FTG2 as written, you on the Frankfurt build. Month-to-month, I assume.

**Founder:** Month-to-month. Card on file. You can cancel inside the portal, you don't have to call me.

**Tomasz:** Good. I'll send you Mara's email and the Frankfurt dates by EOD. What do you need from me for the intake?

**Founder:** Forty-five minutes on Friday. I'll send a Calendly. Bring your last two trips' itineraries — the messy ones, not the clean ones. I want to see where it broke.

**Tomasz:** Done.

*[End of call. Founder holds $189 cleanly. No discount, no free month beyond FTG2. The concession is four hours of founder labor on trip one — a non-pricing concession that strengthens retention rather than diluting ARPU.]*

## Section 2: Sales Coach Evaluation

**Outcome:** Closed. $189/mo Light Unlimited, FTG2 intact as written, no pricing concession given. Founder traded labor for price-hold. Strong outcome — possibly the best read on this matrix cell.

**What worked, specifically:**

The founder's strongest move was the *structural* refusal of the free-month ask: *"FTG2 already gives you the free month. That's literally the mechanic."* This is the textbook play when a buyer asks for a concession that is already embedded in the offer. Don't fold, don't even negotiate — point at the existing structure and let the buyer self-correct. Tomasz did exactly that (*"Hm. Okay. That's better than I read it. Fine — strike the free month."*). One sentence, one objection dissolved. This is FTG2's job: it pre-empts the "give me a free trial" ask by being a contingent free month already.

The second strong move was the 24-month lock refusal framed as founder *interest*, not buyer favor: *"I'd rather you be able to walk at month four if the desk isn't earning its $189, because if I lock you in and you're unhappy, I have a passenger, not a member."* This reframes a "no" as alignment. Tomasz's response — *"That's a confessional answer"* — confirms the register (confessional > triumphalist, per the brand brief) landed. The founder didn't say "we don't do locks." He said "locks cost me more than they cost you," which is operator-correct.

The third move — and this is the one a less experienced rep would have missed — was offering a *non-pricing* concession that *increases* trip-one fidelity: founder personally runs the Frankfurt build. This costs about four hours of founder time, has zero ARPU impact, and dramatically reduces the probability FTG2 ever fires. It's a concession that *protects margin while feeling generous to the buyer*. Tomasz registered it correctly: *"So your concession is more of your time, not less of my money."*

The fourth — caught the $500-on-signing reframe before it landed. Tomasz tried a softer extraction (*"the version I'd push for"*) and the founder named it back immediately: *"If the number moves on the first call, it moves on every call after."* That's price discipline as a *practice*, not a tactic.

**What broke:**

Not much, but two micro-criticisms.

First — the founder's "you're being a PE associate" line is *almost* too clever. It worked here because Tomasz has self-awareness and PE muscle memory. With a different buyer (a founder, a partner) it would read as smug. The rep got away with it; the rep should not assume it generalizes.

Second — the offer of "forty-five minutes on Friday, bring your messy itineraries" is excellent but the founder didn't *book the time on the call*. A Calendly link gets ignored 30% of the time. Better: "Pulling up my calendar — Friday 2pm or 3:30pm Eastern, which?" Close the time the way you closed the price.

**Three improvements:**

1. **Book the intake on the call, not via Calendly.** Calendars cool faster than deals.
2. **Pre-arm the FTG2 reframe.** The founder *discovered* the "you already have the free month" answer in real time. It should be a rehearsed move — the moment a buyer asks for a free first month with FTG2 on the table, the response is automatic.
3. **Quantify the four-hour concession.** Don't just offer "I'll run the build." Say "I'll run the build — that's normally a $500-coded VA workflow, I'm doing it myself." Make the gift legible. (Tomasz is PE; he respects line-items.)

**Most important behavioral change for this rep:**

*Stop discovering your best lines in the moment.* The two killer moves — "FTG2 already is the free month" and "locks cost me more than the discount" — should be muscle, not improvisation. Drill them. Every Model B + FTG2 negotiation will see the same two asks (free month, multi-year lock at a discount). The answers should be reflexive. The improvisation budget belongs on the *non-pricing concession* (founder labor on trip one), which is buyer-specific and can't be scripted.

## Section 3: Business Evaluator

**Unit economics at this conversion:**

Tomasz signs at $189/mo, month-to-month, no discount. Annualized ARPU if he stays 12 months: $2,268. At a Toronto-based desk hosted under Fora, with PHT VA carrying US daytime and founder absorbing evenings/weekends/overnights, the variable cost per solo Light Unlimited member at moderate trip volume (Tomasz indicates Frankfurt + likely 8-12 trips/year based on PE associate travel norms) sits around $55-75/mo loaded VA cost, plus design labor now fully included (Model B's premise). Gross margin per member at $189 lands in the 55-65% band — meaningfully tighter than Model A's $99 booking-only tier (which has lower COGS) but with the offsetting benefit that Model B's design-included structure removes the per-trip escalator friction that historically drove churn at month 4-6.

The negotiation outcome *protected* this margin. Had the founder folded to "first month free on top of FTG2," ARPU would have effectively been $189 × 11/12 = $173.25 in year one — a 8.3% haircut on a sub-2,500 ACV member, which is not a number you give back at this stage. Had the founder folded to "$169 × 24," the haircut would have been $480 over two years against an annual contract value of $2,028 — a 10.6% per-year ARPU hit, and worse, a *precedent*. PE buyers talk. The first $169 deal becomes the floor for the next three PE buyers in Tomasz's network.

**Strategic positioning:**

The most important strategic move in the transcript is the founder's refusal to convert FTG2 from a *contingent* instrument into a *signing bonus* ($500 credit on signing vs. $500 credit only if trip fails). This is the difference between a risk-reversal instrument and a discount. FTG2 is designed as the former; the moment it becomes the latter, the desk's economics shift and the trust signal collapses. The founder's line — *"It's a downside instrument, not a signing bonus"* — is the right strategic frame and should be canonized in the playbook.

**Retention probability:**

Above baseline. Three reasons. (1) Tomasz personally negotiated and lost the price point — he's emotionally bought in to $189 being the right number, which is the opposite of how a discount-pried buyer feels at month four. (2) Founder is on the trip-one build, which means standing instructions get dialed properly the first time, which is the single highest predictor of retention past month six in this category. (3) FTG2 is intact, which means if trip one *does* fail, Tomasz gets caught by the structure rather than churning angry. Predicted 12-month retention probability: 70-75%, vs. ~55% baseline for a discount-pried PE associate.

**Referral value:**

High and *specifically* high. PE associates at $2.4B funds travel in a network of 40-60 peers with near-identical travel profiles and similar T&E budgets. A clean, on-spec experience with Tomasz is worth approximately 1.5-2.5 referred members over 18 months, weighted by his social proximity to other PE associate buyers. The founder's no-discount close *strengthens* referral value because the price point is uncontested — Tomasz will quote $189 to his peers, not "$189 but I got them down to $169."

**The hidden risk:**

Founder labor on trip one is a concession that *does not scale*. The founder can run six to eight trip-one builds personally per quarter before it cannibalizes the time required to run the company. If Model B + FTG2 closes at this rate, the founder needs to systematize the trip-one build (a senior VA running it under founder supervision) by the time member count crosses ~40. Otherwise the concession becomes a constraint, and the founder is buying retention with the wrong currency at scale. Flag for the playbook: trip-one founder-build is a Round 1-30 instrument, not a forever instrument.

## Section 4: Model + FTG Scorecard

**Did the price land or stick?** Landed cleanly. Tomasz opened with *"the number is fine"* and the negotiation was on terms, not price. No friction on $189 itself. Friction points: (a) the free-month ask, dissolved structurally by FTG2; (b) the 24-month lock ask, refused on operator grounds; (c) the $500-on-signing reframe, caught and refused before it landed. Net: price held without compression.

**Did the FTG mechanic move the buyer?** Decisive. FTG2 did three jobs in this call: (1) pre-empted the free-trial ask because the free month is already baked in contingent on failure; (2) gave the founder a structural — not rhetorical — reason to refuse the concession; (3) signaled enough trust that Tomasz stopped pushing on price and started asking about intake logistics. FTG2 is the right instrument for the negotiation stage with a PE buyer. It *out-generouses* the buyer's ask, which is rare and powerful.

**Would the buyer have bought under a different model?**
- *Model A ($99/$249, escalator):* Tomasz would have signed at $99 faster but churned at month 4-5 when the first $1,500 design escalator hit a complex Frankfurt itinerary. Lower CAC efficiency, worse retention. Net: worse.
- *Model C ($99/$249/$499):* Tomasz would have anchored on $99 Basic, refused to ladder up, and the relationship would have been booking-only — same churn risk as Model A.
- *Model B (this sim):* Right fit. The all-design-included structure removes the line-item friction PE buyers hate, and the $189 price is defensible because it's *all-in*.

**Net read:** Model B + FTG2 is the strongest pairing on the matrix for Negotiation-stage buyers who can absorb $189 and respect operator discipline. The FTG2 mechanic does specific anti-concession work that FTG1 and FTG3 cannot: it makes the free-month ask structurally redundant.
