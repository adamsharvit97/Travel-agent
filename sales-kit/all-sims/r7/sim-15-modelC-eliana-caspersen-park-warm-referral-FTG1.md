# R7 Sim 15 — Model C (Three-tier $99/$249/$499) + FTG1

**Scenario:** Warm referral close with Eliana Caspersen-Park, family office principal, Greenwich. $250K T&E. Same persona as R7 Sim 13 + 14, now under Model C's three-tier ladder with FTG1 (month refund + $1K labor credit).

---

## Section 1: TRANSCRIPT (~1,500 words)

**Setting:** Tuesday, 4:15 PM. Founder calls Eliana on her direct line. Maren Holloway (RIA principal, mutual friend) made the intro four days ago. This is the second call — the first was a 22-minute discovery on Friday. Eliana has read the one-pager. Her chief of staff Daphne is on the line for the first nine minutes, then drops off.

---

**Founder:** Eliana, thanks for the time. Daphne, hi. I want to use this call to walk you through pricing and the guarantee, then leave room for questions. Maren said you wanted the structure laid out before you decided.

**Eliana:** That's right. I read the page. Three tiers — I want to understand why three, and I want to understand which one you'd actually put me on. Not which one gets you paid the most.

**Founder:** Fair. Let me be direct on both. The three tiers exist because we sell to three different shapes of buyer. The $99 tier — Atlas Light Basic — is for someone doing four, five, maybe eight trips a year, mostly domestic, who wants a desk to book against but is comfortable with design fees on anything complex. The $249 tier — Atlas Light Plus — is the same single-traveler scope, but design is included on member trips, no escalator. And the $499 tier — Atlas Office — is up to five active travelers, full inclusive, the EA Companion Seat is baked in so your chief of staff has her own access to the desk.

**Eliana:** And which one am I.

**Founder:** You're the $499. I'll tell you why in a sentence and then I'll tell you why I'm showing you the other two anyway. You're at $250K in annual T&E. You travel internationally. Daphne books for you. Your husband and your father-in-law occasionally route through the same desk. That's Office. The reason I'm showing you the lower two is because some of my members downgrade after their first year when they realize they overbought, and I'd rather you know the ladder exists than feel boxed in.

**Eliana:** Hm. That's an unusual thing to say on a sales call.

**Founder:** It's a true thing. I'd rather have you at $499 for three years than at $499 for four months and angry.

**Daphne:** Can I ask — the $249 tier, design included on member trips. If Eliana's at $499, is design also included?

**Founder:** Yes. $499 is full inclusive — design, booking, EA seat, no escalator on anything that runs through the desk. The $249 is for a single traveler who doesn't need the EA seat and doesn't have multi-traveler complexity. You'd be giving up the seat and the multi-traveler scope to save $250 a month, and based on what I've seen of how you two work, that's a bad trade.

**Eliana:** Walk me through what $499 a month actually buys. In hours.

**Founder:** Honest answer — it's not hours, it's access. You get the desk during US daytime, covered by our PHT virtual assistant team. You get me on evenings, weekends, overnights. 60-minute SLA on any request. Standing instructions on file — your seat preferences, your hotel program tiers, your husband's dietary stuff, your father-in-law's mobility constraints. Daphne, you'd have your own login and the desk treats your requests as Eliana's. The labor is unmetered up to reasonable use. If you start sending us 40 trips a quarter we'll have a different conversation, but at your volume you're not near that line.

**Eliana:** And the design fees you mentioned at the lower tiers — those don't apply to me at all.

**Founder:** Correct. Zero design fees on member trips at $499. Air, hotel, ground, the itinerary work, the rebooking when things break — all in.

**Eliana:** What's not included.

**Founder:** Supplier-side costs pass through at cost. So if a hotel charges a deposit, that's the hotel. If you want a private driver in Florence, that's the driver. We don't mark up suppliers. We don't take kickbacks we don't disclose. Anything Fora-side commissionable, we tell you, and on properties where we earn we generally pass back a member benefit — room credit, breakfast, upgrade where available.

**Daphne:** Okay. I think I'm clear. Eliana, I'll drop off — I've got the four o'clock with Stamford. Thank you.

[Daphne leaves the call.]

**Eliana:** Alright. Now the part I actually wanted to ask. The guarantee. Maren mentioned it but I want to hear you say it.

**Founder:** First Trip Guarantee. One trip, named on this call. If that trip fails on our end — not weather, not the airline going bankrupt, not your father-in-law getting sick — but if we drop the ball on something we owned, you get the month's membership refunded and a $1,000 labor credit toward the rework.

**Eliana:** $1,000.

**Founder:** $1,000 of our labor, yes.

**Eliana:** At $499 a month. So I'm refunded $499 and given $1,000 of credit against future work you would've done anyway.

**Founder:** ... that's an accurate read.

**Eliana:** I'm not trying to be difficult. I want to understand what the guarantee actually costs you. Because if my first trip is, say, a four-leg Europe routing for me and my husband and it gets fumbled, the rework on that is more than $1,000 of labor.

**Founder:** You're right. And I'll be honest — that mechanic was designed for the $249 buyer, where $1,000 is two months of membership and meaningful. At $499, with your trip complexity, $1,000 of labor credit is — it's a gesture more than a backstop.

**Eliana:** So what do you actually do if you fumble my first trip.

**Founder:** What I actually do is rework it on my own time until it's right, refund the month, and you decide whether to continue. The $1,000 is a floor, not a ceiling. I'm not going to send you a stopwatch invoice if the rework takes 14 hours.

**Eliana:** Then why is $1,000 the number on the page.

**Founder:** Because we wrote the guarantee for the lower tiers first and I haven't updated the language for Office. That's a fair flag. Let me make this concrete for you: at $499, I'll extend the labor credit to cover the full rework, capped at the value of the trip itself or three months of membership, whichever is higher. I'll send that to you in writing today.

**Eliana:** That's better. That's a real number.

**Founder:** It is.

**Eliana:** And the named-trip thing. Why does the trip have to be named on the call.

**Founder:** Because it forces us both to commit. If the guarantee covers every trip in perpetuity it's a marketing slogan. If it covers the first named trip, I have to actually deliver on something specific and you have a clear test. After that trip clears, we're in the regular relationship and the guarantee retires.

**Eliana:** Maren said the same thing about it. She said it made her trust you faster.

**Founder:** That's the intent.

**Eliana:** Alright. Let's name the trip. June 14th to 22nd. Me, my husband, my father-in-law for the first three days. London, then Cap Ferrat. Father-in-law breaks off on the 17th and flies home, we continue. Business class outbound, first class return because my husband is the way he is. Two suites in London at Claridge's if you can get them, the Grand-Hôtel du Cap-Ferrat for the second leg. Mobility considerations on the London side, my father-in-law uses a cane and doesn't do stairs.

**Founder:** Got it. I'll have Daphne loop in for the standing instructions intake tomorrow. We'll build the itinerary against your husband's class preferences and your father-in-law's mobility profile. Claridge's in mid-June is tight but we have a Fora relationship there — I'll know by Thursday whether the suites hold.

**Eliana:** And if Claridge's doesn't hold?

**Founder:** I'll bring you two alternates with reasoning, not three with a shrug. The Connaught is the obvious second. The Beaumont is the less obvious one and I'll tell you why I'd pick it for this specific trip if Claridge's falls through.

**Eliana:** Fine. Send the agreement. $499, Office tier, FTG extended to trip value or three months. June trip is the named trip.

**Founder:** Sending today. Welcome in.

**Eliana:** We'll see.

[Call ends, 34 minutes.]

## Section 2: SALES COACH EVALUATION (~700 words)

**Outcome:** Closed at $499/mo Atlas Office. FTG extended verbally and committed to writing — labor credit raised from $1,000 to "trip value or three months of membership, whichever is higher." Named trip established (June London/Cap Ferrat, three travelers, mobility profile). Deal value: $5,988 ARR plus pass-through commissionables. Probability the deal sticks past month two: 80%+, conditional on the June trip executing cleanly.

**What worked specifically:**

The line *"You're the $499. I'll tell you why in a sentence and then I'll tell you why I'm showing you the other two anyway"* did the heaviest lifting in the call. Eliana had pre-loaded the suspicion that three tiers were a manipulation device, and the founder defused it by naming the suspicion before she had to. The follow-up — *"some of my members downgrade after their first year when they realize they overbought"* — was the move. It transformed the ladder from a sales construct into a customer-protection construct. Eliana's response (*"that's an unusual thing to say on a sales call"*) is the verbal signature of a sophisticated buyer dropping their guard.

The second high-leverage moment: when Eliana arithmetic'd the $1,000 against the $499 ($499 refund + $1,000 credit = $1,499 total exposure on a trip whose rework labor exceeds that), the founder didn't defend. He conceded. *"That mechanic was designed for the $249 buyer ... at $499, with your trip complexity, $1,000 of labor credit is — it's a gesture more than a backstop."* That admission is what bought the close. A weaker rep would have justified the number. The founder restructured the offer mid-call.

The Daphne handling was clean. Bringing the EA into the pricing discussion early, validating her question about design fees, then letting her drop off naturally — that's onboarding-stage thinking inside a close call. It pre-warms the standing instructions intake.

**What broke:**

One thing broke and the founder caught it: FTG1 is wrong-sized for Office tier and the page still reads $1,000. That's a product-marketing failure leaking into the sales call. The founder absorbed it gracefully but the cleaner play is for the FTG to be tier-indexed in the materials *before* the call. Eliana found a hole; a less generous buyer finds the same hole and uses it to walk.

Second softer issue: the founder volunteered the supplier kickback disclosure (*"we don't take kickbacks we don't disclose"*) unprompted. With Eliana — family office principal, Greenwich — this was probably net positive because it signals operator-correctness. With a different buyer it could plant a question that wasn't there. Read the room every time before you offer that line.

**Three improvements:**

1. **Tier-index the FTG in writing before the call.** Office tier FTG should already say "trip value or three months of membership, whichever is higher." Don't make the rep restructure live, even when the rep can.

2. **Don't show three tiers when one fits.** The founder did this well — he showed all three and explicitly anchored the right one — but a less skilled rep would have left ambiguity. The script should be: "There are three tiers. You're the third. Here's why, and here's why the other two exist for context, not for you." Eliana respected this because the founder was crisp. Less crisp = wasted time.

3. **Name the trip earlier.** The named-trip mechanic is the closest thing this business has to a forcing function on engagement. The founder got there but it came in minute 28. Move it to minute 15 so the trip-naming conversation *is* the close, not the post-close.

**Most important behavioral change for this rep:**

The founder is operator-correct and confessional — exactly the register the brand demands. The behavioral upgrade is *speed of concession.* When the buyer surfaces a real defect in the offer, the founder should restructure faster, with less throat-clearing. The $1,000 conversation took six exchanges; it should have taken three. Sophisticated buyers reward speed of recognition more than they reward the recognition itself. "You're right, here's the better number" beats "you're right ... let me explain why ... actually here's the better number."

## Section 3: BUSINESS EVALUATOR (~700 words)

**Unit economics implications at $499/mo Office tier:**

ARR of $5,988 against a family office principal with $250K T&E is a 2.4% take rate on managed spend — before pass-through commissionables. On a trip like the June London/Cap Ferrat routing (two travelers, premium cabins, two high-rate hotels, ~8 days), Fora-side commissionables likely land in the $4K-$8K range depending on which Claridge's product books and whether the Cap Ferrat property runs through a preferred program. Effective revenue per Eliana in year one: $10K-$14K. CAC on a warm referral close is near-zero in dollar terms — the founder time was ~90 minutes total across two calls plus pre-read. Gross margin on the membership line is high (it's labor against committed capacity); margin on commissionables is essentially 100% of receivables. This is a structurally excellent unit.

The FTG extension matters mechanically. The original FTG1 caps exposure at $1,499 ($499 month + $1,000 labor). The extended FTG caps at "trip value or three months of membership, whichever is higher" — for the June trip, that's the trip value, plausibly $35K-$50K of commissionable spend. The founder transferred meaningful downside risk to himself to close. This is the correct trade against a $10K-$14K annual revenue line *only* if the operational reliability is real. If the desk fumbles the June trip, the founder eats the cost of rework against a customer who will likely churn anyway. The economic logic: a fumbled June trip ends a $10K-$14K/yr relationship plus the referral graph it sits on; the rework labor cost is bounded at perhaps $3K-$5K. Insurance is rational.

**Strategic positioning learnings:**

Model C's three-tier ladder works *if and only if* the rep can anchor cleanly to the right tier without making the buyer feel sold-to. With Eliana, the founder did this by inverting the ladder — leading with which tier she's on, then explaining the others as protective context. Buyers like Eliana read three-tier menus as either (a) a sophistication-signal that the seller understands segmentation or (b) a hustle. The determinant is whether the seller leads with the recommendation or lets the buyer guess. Lead with the recommendation, always.

The deeper learning: $499 is a credibility-correct price for a $250K T&E buyer. Below $499, Eliana would have wondered what was missing. The price *is* a trust signal. Model C's $499 tier exists in part to absorb buyers who'd be uncomfortable with the Model B $349 Unlimited price as the top of the ladder — because $349 doesn't feel commensurate with managing a $250K travel program.

**Retention probability:**

Year one retention: ~80%, conditional on June trip execution. If June trips cleanly, retention extends to 90%+ year two because Eliana will have absorbed the standing instructions investment and switching cost rises sharply. The retention risk vector is *not* price — it's the moment Daphne defaults back to Amex Travel or a direct hotel booking on a "quick" trip. Sophie-in-Sim-12 is the cautionary tale. The founder should mandate that Daphne's standing-instructions intake includes a default-routing commitment: everything through the desk unless explicitly carved out.

**Referral value:**

This is the asymmetric line item. Eliana came in through Maren. Eliana's network is Greenwich family offices and her husband's PE peers. One referred buyer in that network is worth more than Eliana's own ARR. The single most valuable thing the founder did on this call was *not* the close — it was the $1,000 → trip-value concession, because that's the line Eliana will repeat at the next dinner. "He restructured the guarantee mid-call when I pointed out it was undersized" is the referral-grade anecdote. The founder built a story Eliana can tell.

**The hidden risk:**

The hidden risk is FTG asymmetry across the book. The founder verbally extended Eliana's FTG to trip-value. He now has two options: (1) extend FTG-trip-value to all Office tier members and update the marketing, or (2) keep FTG1's $1,000 as standard and quietly extend on a case-by-case basis. Option 2 is dangerous — it creates a two-class member structure that will surface in member-to-member referrals ("did you get the extended guarantee? I only got the $1,000"). Option 1 is the correct call. The founder must update the materials this week. This is now a product-marketing emergency disguised as a one-off concession.

## Section 4: MODEL + FTG SCORECARD (~300 words)

**Did the price land or stick?**
Landed. $499 stuck on first mention; no negotiation on the membership number itself. Friction was entirely on FTG sizing, not price. The founder's anchor sentence — "You're the $499" before walking the ladder — pre-empted price objection by making the tier feel chosen rather than presented. **Score: 9/10.**

**Did the FTG mechanic move the buyer?**
FTG1 as written did not move her — it triggered an arithmetic objection ("$499 refund + $1,000 credit on a trip whose rework exceeds that"). FTG1 *as extended* (trip value or three months, whichever is higher) was decisive. Eliana's "that's better, that's a real number" is the close marker. The mechanic only worked because the founder restructured live. **Score for FTG1 as designed: 4/10. Score as restructured: 9/10.**

**Would the buyer have bought under a different model?**
- **Model A ($99/$249, escalator):** Possibly, but the escalator on a $35K-$50K trip would have killed trust mid-relationship. No.
- **Model B ($189/$349 Unlimited):** Yes, but $349 would have read as underpriced for her volume and raised "what am I missing" questions. Slower close, possibly same outcome.
- **Model C ($499 Office):** Optimal. The price matches the buyer's self-image; the inclusive structure matches her arithmetic preferences; the three-tier ladder, handled well, signaled segmentation sophistication.

**Net read:**
Model C is the right model for this buyer at this stage — *if the rep can anchor cleanly.* Three tiers wasted time for a buyer like Aaron (Sim 1). For Eliana, three tiers respected her intelligence because the founder told her which one was hers in the first sixty seconds. **FTG1 is mis-sized for Office tier and must be re-papered as trip-value-or-three-months across the Office book before the next Office close.**
