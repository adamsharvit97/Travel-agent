## SECTION 1: TRANSCRIPT (~1,500 words)

**Setting:** Tuesday, 2:15 PM EST. Second call. Vanessa took the intro 11 days ago, asked for a working session before deciding. She's blocked 45 minutes. Founder (Arvit) on video. Vanessa is in her office, door closed, notepad open.

**Arvit:** Vanessa, thanks for the longer block. Last time we ran 22 minutes and I felt like we just got to the edge of the real conversation. So today I want to actually understand the firm's travel — not pitch.

**Vanessa:** That's the agreement. I told Mark I wasn't signing anything off a 30-minute call. We're a 19-person firm, $410M, and I've been burned by "founder-led" vendors twice in three years. So — fair warning, I'm in diligence mode.

**Arvit:** Good. That's the right mode. Walk me through the travel footprint. Not just yours. The whole firm.

**Vanessa:** Me primarily. I'm probably 28 trips a year — client visits, two industry conferences, board work for a foundation in DC, family logistics that bleed in. My partner Jim does maybe 8. We have three senior advisors who travel client-side, call it 6-10 trips each. And then the COO and our compliance head, occasional. Total firm T&E last year was $148K, will be closer to $165K this year.

**Arvit:** Who books today?

**Vanessa:** Mess. I book mine through my EA, Catherine. The senior advisors are on Concur because compliance liked the audit trail. Jim books his own through Amex Platinum Travel because he refuses to learn anything new. The COO uses a corporate Egencia seat we've had since 2019 that nobody likes but nobody's killed. So — four systems, zero coordination, and when something goes sideways at 11pm it's me getting the text.

**Arvit:** That last part. The 11pm text. Tell me about the last one.

**Vanessa:** *(pause)* February. Brent — one of my senior advisors — Atlanta to Boston, client meeting at 8am. Delta canceled his 6pm out of ATL. He's on Concur, Concur's after-hours line is a call center in Manila that took 40 minutes to pick up. He texts me at 10:47pm. I'm in bed. I spent an hour on my phone rebooking him through LaGuardia and an Uber to Boston. He made the meeting. The client renewed. But I lost a night and I was the travel agent for my own senior advisor.

**Arvit:** And that's the part I want to be honest about — that exact night is what we get paid to absorb. Not the booking. The 10:47pm. Before I tell you how we price it, can I ask: what would you have paid that night to not have that hour?

**Vanessa:** *(short laugh)* That night? Two hundred dollars. Easily.

**Arvit:** And how many of those nights a year does the firm absorb?

**Vanessa:** Across everyone? Six, eight. Probably more I don't hear about because the advisors handle it themselves and then complain at the partner meeting.

**Arvit:** Okay. So here's the shape of what I want to propose, and I want to walk it slow because the math matters more than the brochure. We have two membership tiers — both unlimited, meaning every trip we touch, all the design work, is included in the monthly. No per-trip design fees, no escalator, no surprise invoice in your inbox three weeks later.

**Vanessa:** Stop right there. "Unlimited" is the word that makes me nervous. Every "unlimited" SaaS I've bought either has a fair-use clause buried on page 14 or the service degrades as you actually use it. Which is it for you?

**Arvit:** Fair. Direct answer: there's no fair-use clause. The constraint is structural — Atlas Light is one named traveler, Atlas Office is up to five active travelers plus EA companion access. We cap the seats, not the trips. If you book me 40 trips a year on Light, I do 40 trips. The reason we can offer it is the seat cap; the reason we put a money-back guarantee on it is because if the math doesn't work for you, it doesn't work for us either.

**Vanessa:** Pricing.

**Arvit:** Atlas Light Unlimited, one traveler, $189 a month. Atlas Office Unlimited, up to five active travelers including EA companion seat, $349 a month. All design included on both. No escalator. No "first trip is free then we charge." That's it.

**Vanessa:** *(writing)* So if I put me on Light and the five advisors on Office — wait, am I one of the five on Office, or is Light separate?

**Arvit:** Good question. You'd be one of the five on Office. You don't need both. The reason I'd push you toward Office for the whole firm is: $349 a month, $4,188 a year, covers you, Jim, and three of the senior advisors. The COO and compliance head — light travelers — we can leave them on their existing setup or add them as observers. The math on the firm version is roughly $4,200 against what's currently a four-system mess that's costing you about $165K plus your sleep.

**Vanessa:** What about Catherine? My EA.

**Arvit:** Companion seat is included in Office. She gets her own login, can book on your behalf, sees everything. No additional cost.

**Vanessa:** *(long pause)* Okay. So the $349 number — and I'm going to be blunt — feels low. Which is its own problem. Last time I bought something at $349 a month for a 19-person firm it turned out to be the loss-leader and the real cost was implementation, integration, training, and add-ons. What's the actual all-in?

**Arvit:** $349 a month. Onboarding is included — we do a 90-minute working session with you and Catherine, build a profile doc, get your standing instructions on file. No setup fee. No per-seat fee inside the five. No design fee escalator because there is no design fee. The only number that's not in $349 is the trip itself — the flights, hotels, the actual cost of travel. We don't mark those up. You see what we pay.

**Vanessa:** And if I'm reading the contract right, what's the term?

**Arvit:** Month-to-month. Which brings me to the second thing I want to put on the table before you ask. We have a 30-day money-back guarantee on the membership. Doesn't matter why. You sign up, we onboard, you use us for 30 days — if at any point in that window you decide it wasn't worth it, we refund the month. **Vanessa:** *(silence, then)* That's unusual. Most vendors at this stage of a sale would be telling me about the annual discount.

It's "if you, Vanessa, in your judgment, don't feel this is worth $349 a month after 30 days, you tell us, we refund. You don't have to prove anything." If a trip fails on our end, that's a separate conversation and we'll fix it. But the money-back is yours unconditionally for 30 days.

**Arvit:** That's exactly the decision I want you to make.

**Vanessa:** One more. The senior advisors. If I put them on Office and Brent has another Tuesday night in February — that's covered? He texts who?

**Arvit:** He texts the desk number. Daytime is our PHT VA team, 60-minute SLA. Evenings, weekends, anything overnight — that's me. Founder phone. Brent's 10:47pm becomes my 10:47pm. You sleep.

**Vanessa:** *(nods slowly)* Send me the agreement and the guarantee in writing. I want to walk it past my COO Friday and I'll have an answer Monday.

**Arvit:** I'll have it in your inbox by 5pm today. One ask: when you talk to your COO, the question isn't "is $349 cheap." It's "what are six 10:47pms a year worth to this firm." That's the number I want her arguing with.

**Vanessa:** Noted.

## SECTION 2: SALES COACH EVALUATION (~700 words)

**Outcome:** Soft yes pending COO review. Vanessa committed to a Monday decision, requested paperwork, and verbally reframed the deal from "$349 evaluation" to "30-day pilot with clean exit." That reframe is the deal. Probability of close: 75-80%. Probability of Office tier specifically (vs. Light only): 70%+, which is materially higher than Sim 4 produced under Model A.

**What worked specifically:**

1. **The opening reframe.** "I want to actually understand the firm's travel — not pitch." This earned the diligence-mode buyer's trust within 30 seconds. Vanessa explicitly said "that's the agreement" — she felt met. Compare to Sim 4 Model A where the rep opened with pricing context and Vanessa stayed posture-up for the first 8 minutes.

2. **The 10:47pm anchor.** "What would you have paid that night to not have that hour?" extracted a $200 number from the buyer in her own voice. Once she said $200, the $349/month became the cost of 1.75 of her own stated nights. The rep didn't have to argue value — she'd already priced it. This is the single best moment in the transcript.

3. **The Unlimited frame opened the firm-wide conversation.** Critical R7 finding: Vanessa volunteered the advisors, Jim, the COO, and Catherine unprompted because there were no per-trip economics for her to model out in her head. Under Model A she would have been calculating "if Brent does 8 trips at $X design fee, plus the escalator..." Under Model B the math collapsed to one number. She moved straight to firm composition. That is the Unlimited model doing exactly what it's supposed to do.

4. **FTG3 delivery was timed correctly.** The rep waited until *after* Vanessa surfaced the "what's the actual all-in" objection before deploying the 30-day guarantee. If FTG3 had landed in minute 8 it would have read as a closing tactic. At minute 28, after the integration-friction concern surfaced, it read as risk-symmetry. Vanessa's response — "that actually changes how I'm thinking about this" — is the cleanest FTG3 land in any sim to date.

**What broke:**

1. **The "$349 feels low" objection was under-explored.** Vanessa flagged it explicitly — "low is its own problem" — and the rep answered the surface (no hidden fees) without addressing the *psychological* problem (a 19-person RIA principal expects to pay more for a real service; cheap signals junior). The rep should have leaned into the seat-cap economics or the deliberate small-book positioning. Missed depth.

2. **No mention of the 2027 Society deferral.** Vanessa is exactly the buyer who would value future community access. The rep left a future-value lever on the table that costs nothing to mention.

3. **The COO handoff.** "What are six 10:47pms a year worth" is a good closing line but it's the rep's frame, not Vanessa's. She'll re-narrate it to the COO and lose 40% of the energy. The rep should have offered a 15-minute call directly with the COO Friday.

**Three improvements:**

1. **Build the COO into the deal.** Offer a Friday 15-minute call. Don't let Vanessa carry your pitch.

2. **When the buyer says "the price feels low," answer the meta-objection, not just the line item.** Something like: "We price for the size of book we can actually serve well. Above 50 members we lose the founder-on-evenings model. The price is the constraint that keeps the service real." That neutralizes the cheap-signal concern.

3. **Earlier surfacing of the EA seat.** Catherine came up at minute 22. In an RIA firm where the EA is the actual operator of half the travel, Catherine should be a named beneficiary by minute 10.

**Most important behavioral change for this rep:** Slow down on price reveals. The rep gave the $189/$349 numbers cleanly but moved past Vanessa's reaction too quickly. In a 45-minute discovery call, the buyer needs *time* with the number. Sit in the silence. Let her do the math on her notepad. The Unlimited model's superpower is that the buyer can do the math themselves — but only if you give them the seconds to do it.

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS EVALUATOR (~700 words)

**Unit economics at this conversion.** Vanessa converts to Atlas Office Unlimited at $349/month = $4,188 ARR. Under Model A v2.0 she would have converted to Atlas Office at $249 plus an expected ~$3,000-4,500 in annual design fees across 28+ trips at her travel volume (member 50% off rate, escalator activating on 4-6 of those trips), netting roughly $5,988-7,488 ARR. So at first glance, Model B *loses* $1,800-3,300 of ARR on this account.

That framing is wrong. Three corrections:

*First,* Model A's design-fee revenue is collection-risky. Every escalator invoice is a friction event with a high-touch client. Vanessa would have disputed at least one in year one. Realized ARR under Model A is closer to $5,200-6,400 after disputes, write-offs, and the founder absorbing fees to preserve relationship.

*Second,* Model B converted her to *Office* not *Light*. Under Model A Sim 4 with the same persona, she signed Atlas Light at $99 and treated it as a personal-travel experiment. The firm-wide conversation never happened because per-trip design economics made it impossible to do napkin math on 19 people. Model B's all-inclusive framing made the firm-wide TAM legible in real-time. That's a $99 → $349 tier jump = 3.5x base revenue, before any design-fee comparison.

*Third,* the 30-day money-back guarantee compresses the sales cycle. Vanessa is deciding Monday. Under Model A Sim 4 she asked for a 30-day pilot which the rep declined because the design-fee economics made pilots unprofitable. Sales cycle was 6 weeks. Here it's 14 days. Velocity matters at this scale of book.

**Strategic positioning learnings.**

The Unlimited model *changes the conversation from per-trip economics to firm composition.* This is the most important finding of R7 so far. RIA principals don't think in trips, they think in headcount and book size. Pricing that maps to headcount (5 seats / $349) parses; pricing that maps to trips doesn't.

The "all design included" framing also removes a category of defending the rep has to do. Under Model A, every conversation about design eventually requires explaining the escalator — and the explanation always sounds defensive because *it is* defensive. The rep is being asked to justify a surprise. Under Model B, there are no surprises to defend. Saved approximately 8 minutes of call time and approximately 2-3 trust-erosion moments.

**Retention probability.** Materially higher under Model B than Model A. Reason: under Model A the design fees become the trigger for monthly P&L reviews — Vanessa would have stared at a $4,500 cumulative design-fee line at month 8 and started counting trips. Under Model B the membership is a clean line item that maps to her firm budget and disappears into operating overhead. The first time she'd seriously reconsider is the annual renewal conversation, by which point Brent has had his 10:47pm and the founder-on-evenings story is real.

Estimated 18-month retention: Model B ~82%, Model A ~64% for this persona.

**Referral value.** Vanessa is the highest-referral-leverage persona in the book. Charleston RIA community is tight, $410M is a credible badge, and she's exactly the kind of operator who recommends a vendor that solves a specific operational pain. The 30-day money-back makes referrals psychologically free for her — she can recommend without putting her own judgment on the line ("try it, you get your money back if it doesn't work"). Expected referrals over 24 months: 2-4 qualified intros.

**Hidden risk.** The Office tier covers 5 travelers, Vanessa's actual firm travel-active headcount is ~5-7 depending on how you count the COO and compliance head. If usage drifts to 6-7 travelers in year two, the rep faces an awkward upsell conversation that the original "all-inclusive, no surprises" pitch made harder to have. The Unlimited model creates an expectation of unlimited *everything*, including expansion. Mitigation: document the 5-seat structural cap in onboarding, frame additional seats as "adding a desk" not "upgrading." This is a year-two problem the founder needs to pre-empt at signing.

Secondary risk: the 30-day money-back is genuinely unconditional. At 5-8% take rate on the guarantee, the math still works. At 15%+ it doesn't. Worth instrumenting from day one.

## SECTION 4: MODEL + FTG SCORECARD (~300 words)

**Did the price land or stick?** Landed clean. $349 produced *one* friction moment — Vanessa's "feels low" reaction — which is the inverse of the usual price objection and is structurally easier to handle than "feels high." The all-inclusive framing meant the rep never had to defend a second invoice, never had to explain an escalator, and never had to apologize for a surprise. Specific friction point: minute 19, "what's the actual all-in" — neutralized in one exchange because there was nothing to disclose.

**Did the FTG mechanic move the buyer?** Decisively. Vanessa's literal words: "that actually changes how I'm thinking about this." FTG3 reframed the entire decision from "should I commit to $349/month" to "should I run a 30-day pilot." The deep-discovery format gave FTG3 room to land — in a 22-minute first call it would have read tactical; at minute 28 of a 45-minute working session, after the buyer surfaced her own integration-friction concern, it read as risk-symmetry. FTG3 is the right mechanic for this stage. It would have been weaker in a cold inbound, stronger here.

**Would the buyer have bought under a different model?** Under Model A she would have signed Atlas Light at $99 (Sim 4 outcome). Under Model C three-tier she likely would have anchored to the $249 Plus tier and stalled on the $499 Office decision for another month. Model B is the only variant that produced an Office-tier conversion at this stage.

**Net read.** Model B + FTG3 in deep discovery is the strongest combination tested to date for the RIA-principal persona. The Unlimited framing makes firm-wide TAM legible; FTG3 makes the commitment psychologically reversible. The 3.5x revenue jump from Sim 4's $99 outcome to this sim's $349 outcome is the single largest pricing-architecture lever observed in the round.
