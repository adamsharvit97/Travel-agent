**Buyer:** Theo Bardakian, 38. Atlas Light Plus ($249/mo, design included, no EA Companion Seat). 7 weeks in, second trip. ## SECTION 2 — SALES / OPERATIONS COACH EVALUATION

**Outcome:** IROP recovered (JFK flight, met meeting albeit with a downgraded second-meeting outcome that Theo himself reframes as a win). Plus-to-Office upgrade closed on a 90-day trial at June pro-rated. $600 voluntary refund issued despite FTG2 having technically expired. Founder converted a guarantee-expired second-trip crisis into a $250/mo expansion (annualized $3,000 ARR uplift) and a likely referral asset.

**What worked, specifically:**

1. *"Three real options"* (2:33am). Mike presented JFK / charter / later-Delta as a structured triage in under 45 seconds. The charter quote was held but not pushed — critical. A weaker operator either skips the charter (under-serves) or pushes it (over-serves and looks self-interested). Mike's framing — *"The charter is your fallback, not my upsell"* — is the line of the call.

2. *Voluntary refund despite expired FTG.* "The guarantee was the *floor.* It expired. But the way we operate doesn't expire." This is the highest-leverage sentence in the transcript. It reframes FTG2 from a contractual mechanic into a behavioral floor — which is exactly the position a confessional brand should occupy. The refund is $600. The trust it buys is worth $30K+ of LTV.

3. *Separation of refund from outcome.* "The refund is for the IROP, not the outcome of your pitch." This decouples Latitude's accountability from variables they don't control (Sequoia's decision). It's a Karpathy-grade definition of the unit of work.

4. *Tier conversation was deferred 60 hours.* Mike refused to have the upgrade conversation at 6:13am after Theo landed. Asking for Thursday/Friday was the right call. Selling on adrenaline post-rescue is the cheap move — it works, but it leaves a residue. Thursday at 4pm with Theo back in his office and the Sequoia outcome known produced a cleaner, more durable yes.

**What broke / what was suboptimal:**

1. *The charter quote ($24,400) was unnecessary information.* Theo never asked Mike to price the charter — Mike volunteered it. In hindsight, the charter price became the anchor that made $499/mo feel cheap on Thursday, which is a useful side effect, but at 2:33am that anchor was a distraction risk. If Theo had been more anxious, the $24K number could have ratcheted him into panic-buying the charter. Better play: hold the charter as an internal option, surface it only if JFK fails.

2. *FTG2 mechanics weren't used cleanly.* FTG2 is full refund + free month + $500 credit. Mike issued "this month and next month" ($600) but not the $500 service credit, and described the original guarantee inaccurately by collapsing it. Either honor FTG2 as written (even though technically expired) — refund + free month + $500 — or invent a new gesture and own it. The middle path is mushy.

3. *The Companion Seat sell was abstract.* "Ambient vs. reactive" is a smart frame, but Theo deserved one concrete example of a specific IROP where the Companion Seat triggered 18 minutes before the member knew. Mike has those stories. He should have used one.

**Three improvements:**

- **Build a Tuesday-2am playbook card** that scripts the first 90 seconds of any 2am IROP call: greeting, file pull, three options framed, no charter price unless asked.
- **Standardize the "expired-guarantee good-faith" gesture** as a documented FTG2.5 — refund + service credit, no improvisation per-call. This protects unit economics from founder-mood pricing.
- **Move the tier upgrade conversation off the rescue call entirely.** Mike did this 90% — but even the 6:13am text mention is too soon. Save it for Thursday cold.

**Most important behavioral change for this rep:** Mike already has the hardest skill — composure at 2:30am. The behavioral edge is *price discipline on the rescue call*. Don't volunteer dollar amounts. Don't hold up charter quotes as anchors. Solve the IROP, then sell on Thursday in daylight.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS EVALUATOR

**Unit economics at this conversion.** Theo enters at Plus ($249/mo, $2,988 ARR). Post-IROP he moves to Office ($499/mo, $5,988 ARR) on a 90-day trial with June credited at Plus rate. Net Year 1 ARR after the IROP refund ($600) and June credit ($250): approximately $5,138 — a ~72% revenue uplift on the same logo, generated by a Delta cancellation Latitude did not cause and was not contractually obligated to compensate for. Cost of the gesture: $600 refund + $250 June credit = $850 against a $3,000 ARR delta. **Payback on the gesture: under four months.** This is the Model C / FTG2 expansion math working as designed — the FTG2 generosity creates the trust that makes the tier upgrade conversation a layup rather than a fight.

**Strategic positioning learnings.**

Model C's three-tier ladder ($99/$249/$499) is doing two distinct jobs here that Model A and Model B can't do simultaneously:

1. *The $249 Plus tier is wide enough to capture solo-founder economics* without forcing them into the $499 Office tier before they're ready. Theo at sign-up was a single traveler doing 6-8 trips a year. Plus was correct.

2. *The $499 Office tier is high enough to feel like a meaningful upgrade — not a $100 bump but a doubling.* This matters at the IROP moment. The $250 monthly delta translates to *roughly the cost of one Uber Black per week,* which is a number a Series B founder can do in his head. A $349-vs-$189 ladder (Model B Unlimited) would have produced a $160/mo delta — too small to feel like a structural commitment, too small to anchor "ambient vs. reactive."

The brief's central question — *does Plus at $249 vs. Office at $499 create a perception that Latitude is "more vested" or "less vested" in the crisis?* — has a counterintuitive answer here. Plus actually creates a *more vested* perception during the IROP because Mike personally answers at 2:30am regardless of tier, and the *generosity gap* between what Plus pays and what Mike delivers is larger than the gap at the Office tier. The risk is that this gets read as "Latitude over-services Plus and Office is therefore overpriced." Mike navigates this on Thursday by reframing Office as *structural surface area*, not *better service* — which is the right move. Office isn't "more Mike," it's "Mike plus a Companion Seat already inside the calendar." That distinction holds the $499 price.

**Retention probability.** Very high. Theo has now experienced (a) a clean first trip, (b) a 2am rescue on a non-Latitude-caused IROP, (c) a voluntary refund that exceeded contract, and (d) a tier upgrade conversation that was structured to give him an exit if Office doesn't pay for itself. The 90-day Office trial is also a retention insurance policy — if Theo down-tiers to Plus in September, Latitude still wins the $250 delta for June-August and the strengthened relationship.

**Referral value.** Theo is a Series B founder in a peer network that is the exact ICP. The "Mike answered at 2:30am, refunded the month even though he didn't have to, and didn't try to sell me the charter" story is the *kind* of story that travels in founder DMs. Estimated referral value: 1.2-2.0 logos in the next 12 months, weighted at ~$4K ARR each. The narrative is more powerful because FTG2 had expired — the gesture wasn't a contractual obligation, which makes it a *story* rather than a *feature*.

**The hidden risk.** Latitude is building a service expectation it cannot scale without proportional founder bandwidth. Mike took this call personally at 2:30am because he's the founder and the brand is "operator-correct." At 50 members, this works. At 200 members, Mike sleeping is non-negotiable, and the 2am call goes to PHT VA coverage. The hidden risk is that *Theo's story* — Mike personally — becomes the *expected experience* for everyone who hears the referral. The org needs a documented "PHT-handles-IROP-with-founder-on-text-escalation" runbook by Round 7, or this exact moment becomes a scaling liability.

**Secondary hidden risk:** FTG2's "expired but honored" pattern, if repeated without documentation, will eventually be cited by a member who *didn't* receive the same treatment. Latitude needs a policy: *"After FTG expires, IROP-triggered refunds are at founder discretion up to $X."* Document the discretion or it becomes a fairness problem.

## SECTION 4 — MODEL + FTG SCORECARD

**Did the price land or stick?**
Plus at $249 landed cleanly at sign-up seven weeks ago and was never relitigated during the IROP. The $499 Office tier landed on Thursday because Mike anchored it against (a) the actual ambient-vs-reactive functional gap, and (b) implicitly the $24K charter quote from Tuesday morning. **Friction moment:** Theo's "the difference between two-forty-nine and four-ninety-nine is what — speed?" — that's the sentence where Office could have died. Mike's "speed and surface area" reframe saved it. Price stuck.

**Did the FTG mechanic move the buyer?**
Half-credit. FTG2 had *expired* at the moment of the IROP — Theo's first trip (Lisbon) succeeded — so the contractual mechanic was not in play. What moved the buyer was Mike's *voluntary echo* of FTG2 ("the guarantee was the floor, the way we operate doesn't expire"). The FTG2 spirit moved Theo; the FTG2 letter was irrelevant. **Decisive on the upgrade, not on the rescue.** The rescue would have happened either way. The Thursday upgrade conversation was made possible by the Tuesday refund.

**Would the buyer have bought under a different model?**
- *Model A v2.0* ($99/$249, no $499 ceiling): Theo would be at Office already at $249 with EA Companion Seat — the IROP would have triggered the ambient response. No upgrade conversation available. Latitude loses the $3,000 expansion. Net worse for Latitude, slightly better for Theo Tuesday morning.
- *Model B Unlimited* ($189/$349): Theo likely sits at Office Unlimited ($349) given his trip volume — similar to Model A outcome. The $160 delta between Light and Office Unlimited is too small to support an "ambient vs. reactive" upgrade narrative.
- *Model C* ($99/$249/$499): Best for Latitude. Plus is the correct starting tier for a solo founder; the $499 ceiling creates a real upgrade story with real margin.

**Net read:** Model C is the right model for Theo specifically because his usage pattern is *increasing post-Series B*. The three-tier ladder lets Latitude meet him at Plus and grow him to Office on his own evidence. FTG2's generosity didn't fire contractually but its *spirit* fired voluntarily — which is, paradoxically, the strongest possible FTG outcome: the member learns Latitude operates above the contract, not at it.

**Sim 9 grade: A-.** Lost a half-grade for the charter-quote anchor at 2:33am and the mushy execution of the voluntary FTG gesture ($600 instead of clean FTG2 = refund + free month + $500). Recovered by Thursday. Tier upgrade was textbook.
