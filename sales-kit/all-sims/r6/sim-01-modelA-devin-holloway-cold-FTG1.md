## Section 2 — Sales Coach Evaluation

**Outcome:** Soft yes pending Carla loop-in. Realistic for cold inbound at 25 minutes — Devin is a thinker, not an impulsive signer, and the founder correctly read that and didn't push for a card on the call. The named trip (Sonoma June offsite) is a strong commitment artifact even without signature, because Devin has now mentally pre-committed to evaluating Latitude 43 against a specific upcoming event rather than against an abstract "would I ever use this."

**What worked, with citations.**

The opening question — "what made you reply to a cold email" — is the strongest move on the call. It does three things at once: it surfaces the emotional trigger (the Sunday afternoon on hold), it positions the founder as someone interested in signal rather than spray-and-pray, and it gives the founder the language Devin will respond to ("forty minutes," "Sunday afternoon," "primed"). Every later beat in the call calls back to that vocabulary. The line "the cost wasn't really three hundred dollars, it was three hundred dollars plus a Sunday plus the open question of whether you got hosed" is the single best line in the transcript because it reframes price from a number into a felt cost, and it does so using Devin's own words.

The honesty move on commissions ("yes, on hotel bookings through Fora we do receive supplier commissions") is high-trust. Cold-stage buyers like Devin have been pitched by enough vendors that they're scanning for spin. Volunteering the awkward answer before being asked converts skepticism into trust faster than any feature claim could.

The FTG delivery — particularly the explanation of *why* it's structured as month + labor credit rather than full refund — is the second-best moment on the call. The founder didn't just state the mechanic; he defended its specific shape against the obvious alternative ("most people would have just said money-back, no questions asked"). That defense — "what you need isn't your money back, it's the trip to work" — is the kind of line that lands because it shows the founder has thought about the problem from the buyer's chair, not the seller's.

Asking Devin to name the trip on the call was the highest-EV move in the back half. It converts the guarantee from a marketing artifact into a personal commitment device. Devin saying "Sonoma" out loud is a small ritual of buying — he's now mentally simulating the trip running through Latitude 43.

**What broke.**

Two small things and one bigger thing.

Small one: The founder said "about a hundred members right now" as a credibility signal. For a sophisticated buyer like Devin, that number is either fine or slightly concerning — it's small enough that he might worry about bus factor. The founder didn't pre-empt the bus-factor concern (what happens if you get hit by a truck, what's the backup desk). It didn't come up because Devin didn't push, but with a more aggressive buyer it would have.

Small two: The phrase "we don't want that incentive" when comparing to percentage-of-trip advisors is slightly preachy. It's a borderline triumphalist register move in a brand voice that prefers confessional. A tighter version: "We tried that model. It made our recommendations worse."

Bigger one: The founder never quantified ROI in Devin's specific numbers. Devin volunteered $80K T&E equivalent context (implicit in the trip patterns) and three flight-change events last quarter. The founder could have done the math out loud — "three thousand a year on a forty-thousand-dollar travel spend is 7.5%, and you're trying to claw back maybe ten percent in time and avoided mis-bookings." That math would have made the $249 feel even smaller than it already did. Missing this is a missed opportunity to anchor price against a savings number rather than letting it sit as a standalone monthly.

**Three improvements.**

1. Add a 60-second ROI moment after the buyer signals price comfort. Don't lead with it — Devin had already accepted the number — but reinforce it so when he forwards to Carla, he has a defensible math story rather than just a vibe.
2. Pre-empt the bus-factor question with a one-liner about the Fora hosting structure and the PHT VA team. Frame the small size as a feature (operator attention) but reassure on continuity.
3. Replace "we don't want that incentive" with a confessional framing. The brand voice is operator-correct, not crusader.

**Most important behavioral change for this rep:** Earn the right to math. The founder has the relational and ethical instincts dialed in. What he leaves on the table consistently is the quantitative case — translating membership cost into avoided cost, in the buyer's own dollars, at the moment they signal openness. It's not a personality change. It's a single thirty-second module to insert at the price-comfort beat.
