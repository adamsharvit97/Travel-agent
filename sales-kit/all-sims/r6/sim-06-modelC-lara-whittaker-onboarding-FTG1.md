## SECTION 1: TRANSCRIPT

**Ruben:** Honestly? I don't know yet. I've never used a service like this. I've been booking Lara's travel for three years out of Concur and direct on the airline sites. So when she said "we're paying five hundred a month for someone to do what I already do," I didn't have a good answer.

**Lara:** And he's not being passive-aggressive. That's the actual question.

## SECTION 2: SALES COACH EVALUATION

**Outcome:** Strong onboarding. Buyer arrived with live price doubt ("watching"), EA arrived skeptical-to-neutral, both left aligned. Devon converted a latent churn risk in the first 90 seconds and used the rest of the call to build operating muscle. No new commitment was needed — the commitment had already been made — but Devon successfully relitigated the $499 decision in the buyer's favor, which is the real onboarding job most reps miss.

**What worked specifically:**

The single best move was Devon's response to Lara's opening shot. She said "I'm watching" and Devon didn't defend, didn't reassure, didn't soften — he turned to Ruben and asked for his read. That move did three things at once: it validated that the question was legitimate, it pulled the EA into the conversation as a peer rather than a note-taker, and it stalled Devon from giving the answer until he understood which version of the question was actually live. Cite the line: *"Fair question, and a fair time to ask it. Ruben, what was your read?"* That's a confessional-register move and it's the brand voice working correctly.

Second, the $250-delta explanation was disciplined. Devon didn't list features. He named one concrete thing per dollar — Ruben gets a seat, four more travelers without re-pricing, no design escalator. Three specific items, each tied to a real Whittaker Reed scenario Devon remembered from discovery (the Q3 senior advisor expansion). That's account memory deployed as proof of attention, which at $499/mo is doing real work.

Third, Devon's handling of the FTG was honest about its asymmetry. When Lara said *"the $1,000 labor credit feels small next to $499 a month,"* Devon agreed with her instead of defending the mechanic. That's the operator-correct register. He then re-framed the credit as "for the member who'd rather travel than litigate" — which gave Lara permission to discount the credit while still valuing the month refund. He held the package without holding every piece of the package equally.

Fourth, Ruben's "what if I get cut out" question was the highest-stakes moment of the call and Devon handled it perfectly. He affirmed the question's legitimacy in front of Lara, gave a real answer with a 90-day check-in implied, and acknowledged the failure mode. That's the move that buys Ruben's actual cooperation for the next 12 months. Without it, Ruben becomes a passive user, the EA Companion Seat goes unused, and the $499 tier feels like $249 plus tax.

**What broke:**

Devon undersold the labor credit's specific use case. He said *"we eat the rework hours instead of charging design time against your account"* — but he never quantified what "design time" would normally cost at the desk. A thousand dollars of labor credit means nothing if Lara doesn't know labor is priced at, say, $200/hour. That's five hours of rework, which on a botched SFO trip is the difference between a rebuilt itinerary and a half-fix. Devon left the math on the floor.

Second, the FTG one-shot framing — "after the first trip the guarantee converts into your ongoing SLA" — was elegant but Devon didn't pre-empt the obvious follow-up: *what's the SLA's teeth?* He pivoted to close before Lara could ask, which worked tactically but leaves a question hanging that will resurface on trip three.

Third, the standing instructions piece was rushed. Lara gave two preferences (no United, real gym) in a 15-second burst and Devon logged them but didn't probe. There are five more standing instructions buried in her travel history and Devon didn't excavate them. That's a missed onboarding moment that will cost the desk in month two.

**Three improvements:**

1. Quantify the labor credit in dollars-per-hour terms so the $1,000 becomes legible as time, not abstract money.
2. Pre-empt the post-FTG SLA question with one sentence: "And here's what the ongoing SLA enforces, with consequences."
3. Run standing instructions as a structured 10-minute segment, not a 90-second free-form. Have the form on screen.

**Most important behavioral change:** Devon already does the confessional register well. The growth edge is *quantification of soft promises.* Every time he says "meaningful," "real," "the right shape" — Lara is a managing partner of an RIA. She converts qualitative claims into numbers in her head whether you give her the numbers or not. Give her the numbers.

The risk to that math is concentration of usage in Lara herself. **Strategic positioning learnings:**

Model C's three-tier ladder works *if and only if* the top tier earns its delta inside the first 30 days. The $99 and $249 tiers can be sold on features. The $499 tier has to be sold on *fit* — multi-traveler households or firms, EA-paired workflows, and the absence of design escalator pain on complex trips. This call surfaced the central tension: at $499, the EA seat is the difference between value and resentment. If the EA doesn't use it, the price is indefensible. If the EA does use it, the price is underpriced relative to what a 22-person RIA spends on travel chaos.

The implication for Model C's go-to-market: the top tier should not be sold without the EA on a discovery call or kickoff. Selling it to a principal in isolation is selling future churn. Devon got Ruben on the call. Many reps wouldn't have insisted.

If the SFO trip goes smoothly, retention is in the high 70s but not bonded. Counter-intuitively, a small failure handled well is the highest-retention outcome.

**Referral value:**

High. Houston RIA community is dense and Lara sits on two advisory boards. A satisfied Whittaker Reed at $499/mo is worth 2-3 warm intros over 18 months, each with a 35-45% close rate at the same tier. Probabilistic referral LTV: $25,000-$45,000. This is the silent multiplier on the top tier that the $99 segment doesn't deliver. Model C's economics are referral-weighted in a way Models A and B aren't.

**Hidden risk:**

Ruben's "what if I get cut out" question was honest, but it surfaces a structural fragility. EAs at this seniority (3+ years tenured) have political capital with the principal. If Ruben quietly turns against the service — slow to open requests, defaults back to Concur, frames the $499 as wasteful at the next budget review — the account dies without ever opening a ticket about it. This is invisible churn. Devon's 90-day check-in language was good but informal. The structural fix is making the EA Companion Seat usage a metric Devon reviews monthly, and intervening if Ruben's request volume drops below threshold. Without that, the top tier of Model C has a hidden EA-defection mortality curve that won't show up until month 5-7 of cohort data.

The month refund felt meaningful to her ("matters more"). The $1,000 labor credit explicitly did *not* feel meaningful next to $499 — she said so directly. Devon's recovery — reframing the credit as for-the-member-who-wants-the-trip-fixed-not-refunded — partially rescued it but didn't make it decisive. **Score: 5.5/10.** FTG1's labor credit is undersized for the $499 tier; it was designed for $99-$249 economics. The math doesn't scale.

**Would the buyer have bought under a different model?** Probably yes under Model B Unlimited Atlas Office at $349/mo — Lara would have read it as a simpler value proposition ("everything included, lower price"). She might not have bought Model A Office at $249 because the design escalator would have re-surfaced in month two and felt like a bait-and-switch at a 22-person firm with growing trip complexity. Model C's $499 is defensible *only because* Lara has multi-traveler ambition and an EA. For a solo principal at the same AUM, $499 is overshoot.

**Net read:** Model C's top tier fits Whittaker Reed correctly but barely. The fit depends entirely on Ruben activating the EA seat. FTG1's $1K labor credit is the wrong-sized lever for a $499 buyer — at this price tier, FTG2 (full refund + free month + $500 service credit) would land harder because the dollar values become legible against the monthly. **Recommended pairing:** Model C top tier with FTG2, not FTG1. Current pairing is operator-defensible but suboptimal.
