Comparative Brief · The Five Differentiators

What changes when you choose Latitude 43.

Five structural differences, in the order they tend to matter once a buyer has signed.

I.The named human on the contract.

At Amex Centurion, Navan, Cadence, and Brownell, the human who picks up the phone is not the human named on the contract. The contract names a brand or a team. The shift picks up. At Latitude 43, the founder is on the contract, and the founder is the advisor. The relationship is one-to-one, capped at fifteen accounts before a second advisor is brought on — and even then, the founder stays on the contract.

II.The 60-minute SLA, in writing.

None of the four alternatives publishes a first-response SLA on its corporate site. Cadence's after-hours desk is reached via a code (SV23G). Brownell's 24/7 language lives in the Privacy Notice. Navan's response varies by tier. Amex's is bundled into the card relationship. Latitude 43's SLA is on the homepage: 60 minutes, in writing, day or night, day one to day 365 of the membership.

III.The published price.

Three tiers — Atlas Principal ($3,600), Atlas Office ($12,000), Atlas Firm ($36,000) — on the homepage. No "contact sales" gate. No per-transaction surprise. The B2B booking amenities, the override commissions, and the night-desk staffing are all inside the membership price. The buyer knows the all-in cost before the discovery call.

IV.The amenity-channel stack, applied by default.

Every hotel booking is routed through the right B2B channel — Virtuoso, FHR, STARS, FSPP, Rosewood Elite, Aman Privé, Peninsula Preferred Privileges, Mandarin Fan Club. The amenities are stacked on top of any loyalty status the traveler already holds. The price the traveler sees is the price the traveler would have seen on the hotel website. The amenity layer is the value the booking channel quietly adds.

V.The aligned incentives.

Cadence is owned by Certares, a private equity firm. Brownell's corporate side routes through the same Internova / Tzell consortium. Navan is on a VC clock. Amex is a public company. Each is optimized for the return profile of its capital. Latitude 43 is founder-owned. The economic unit is the long-term client relationship, not the LBO exit. The advisor's bonus is renewals, not transactions.

If you sign by month-end

We charge the first year on signature, no proration. We do an 90-minute onboarding call to capture preferences, document seat-and-room-and-meal preferences, and load the traveler profile into Sabre and the four major B2B booking channels. We commit to a first trip booked within seven days of signature. We send the first written quarterly review at day 90 — a one-page document covering trips booked, dollars saved against retail benchmark, disruptions managed, and amenities delivered. The agreement is renewable annually, terminable on 30 days' notice, no claw-back, no fine print.
