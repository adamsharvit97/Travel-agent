# The Discovery Playbook

How to run the first call with a prospect. Pre-call prep, the call itself (20–30 min structure), the close, the follow-up. Designed for the first 50 calls — after which it should evolve based on what is actually working.

---

## Before the call (10 minutes of prep)

For every discovery call, do this. No exceptions in the first 50.

1. **Open their LinkedIn.** Read the last 90 days. Find one specific thing — a panel they spoke at, a portfolio company they led, a job change. You will reference it within the first 90 seconds.
2. **Pull their fund / company / firm one-liner.** AUM, headcount, vintage, recent news. PitchBook for VCs, AdvisorPedia for RIAs, LinkedIn for everyone else.
3. **Decide the probable T&E band.** Founder of 12-person seed-stage: $40–80K. VC partner at a $400M fund: $150–300K. RIA with 200 clients and a quarterly conference: $80–150K. This determines which Atlas tier you'll anchor toward.
4. **Write your one note.** Single Post-it: "Why is this person on this call." If you can't answer it, cancel.

## The call structure (20–30 minutes)

### Opening (2 min)

> *"Thanks for the time. Quick housekeeping — I usually keep these to 20 minutes, then leave the last 10 for your questions. Fair? Good. Before we get into Latitude, I want to understand how you travel today and what's broken. Then I'll tell you what we are, and we can figure out together whether we should keep talking. Sound right?"*

Do not pitch in the opening. Do not say "I'm so excited" or "thanks for jumping on." Acknowledge time, set the structure, transfer agenda control.

### Diagnostic (8 min)

Ask these in order. Listen for 80% of this section. Take notes.

1. **"Walk me through a typical week of travel for you. How many trips, how complex, who books it today?"**
   - You're listening for: who controls the booking (them / EA / Concur / Navan / Amex Travel), how often they have to redo their own work, whether the EA is overloaded.

2. **"What's the worst trip you've had in the last twelve months? Don't have to name names — what went wrong?"**
   - This is the question. Whatever they say next is the pain point you sell to. Note the specific failure.

3. **"How often does something go wrong — cancel, delay, hotel walk, ground transport falls through — and what do you do when it does?"**
   - You're listening for: do they call Amex? Does the EA scramble? Do they personally fight it? How long does it take?

4. **"How do you handle points and status? Are you actively optimizing or just letting it happen?"**
   - Almost universally the answer is "I have an Amex Platinum and a Marriott Bonvoy and I have no idea what I'm doing with them." This is the rewards pillar handle.

5. **"What does your assistant do today that you wish was handled differently?"** (only if they have an EA)
   - You're listening for: EA is great at calendar, terrible at travel. EA is overloaded. EA quit last quarter. Any of these is your opening.

### Reframe (3 min)

Now you talk. Three short statements, slow.

> *"Two patterns I keep seeing. First, most operators in your seat aren't paying for travel — they're paying twice for it. They pay an airline, and they pay the time it takes to clean up what the airline broke. Second, the points and status thing — at your spend level, that's $4,000 to $9,000 a year of upgrades and credits sitting unused, because nobody on your team is trained to capture them."*

> *"Latitude 43 is a travel office. One human, on call, with the tools to make your week work. Not a booking platform. Not a card concierge. A small office with named members and a published response time."*

> *"What I want to test on this call is whether you'd actually benefit from this. If you wouldn't, I'd rather tell you that now than sell you a year of something you don't need."*

That last sentence is the most important in the entire call. It's the trust transfer. Use it every time.

### The product (4 min)

Three pillars. One sentence each. No bullets.

> *"What you get: rewards captured on every booking through the same hotel programs the luxury leisure houses use — Virtuoso, Four Seasons Preferred Partner, Marriott STARS, Hyatt Privé. These are upgrades, credits, breakfast, fourth-night-free benefits you can't access on Expedia or Concur."*

> *"You get convenience: one Slack channel, one email, one phone number. Tell us once that you sit in 6A on long-haul, that you want the Connaught in Mayfair, that your assistant is named Pat — we remember. Forever."*

> *"And you get 24-7. First response under sixty minutes, day or night. Not a queue. Not a code. The desk."*

### The price (2 min)

State it calmly. Make the prospect choose the tier.

> *"Two ways in. Light is one eighty-nine a month — one traveler, all trip design included, business and personal. Office is three forty-nine a month — up to five travelers and your EA, everything included. No per-trip fees, no surprise invoices, either way. Based on what you described, you're in [Tier]."*

If the first trip comes up, name the guarantee: *"If we screw up your first trip, you get the month back, the next month free, and a service credit on the next one."*

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

### Their questions (8–10 min)

Whatever comes up here is the real conversation. Common questions and how to handle them are in the FAQ below.

### Close (1 min)

Three possible closes, by signal strength:

**Strong signal ("I'm in" / "How do I sign up"):**
> *"Good. Two things. I'll send you an application — it's a short form, takes about ten minutes. Once it's back I'll get on a fifteen-minute call to build your travel profile, and we go live within forty-eight hours. Sound right?"*

**Medium signal ("This sounds interesting" / "Let me think about it"):**
> *"Understood. Let me send you a short brief with the comparison to what you're using today and a sample of what a member's quarterly report looks like. Sleep on it. I'll follow up next [day]."*

**Weak signal ("I'm probably fine with what I have"):**
> *"Fair enough. Let me ask one last thing — if your next IROP costs you four hours of your weekend, will you let me know? Sometimes people only realize the math after they've felt it again. No pressure either way."*

The weak-signal close keeps the door open and reframes the next disruption as a sales trigger.

## The hard questions and how to answer them

### "How are you different from my Amex Platinum concierge?"

> *"Two structural differences. First, Amex Platinum is one of two outsourced call centers — Ten Lifestyle Group and Aspire. Centurion is also a pod. The Centurion 'dedicated' model was downgraded around 2022 according to cardholders paying fifteen thousand a year. Second, email concierge was eliminated in 2021 and off-list reservations in 2022. The product is materially worse than it was three years ago, even if you're still paying the same fee. We are a named human, salaried, fifteen members maximum, on a published 60-minute SLA."*

### "How are you different from Navan / TravelPerk?"

> *"Different category. Navan is a software company. Their job is to give a finance team a dashboard. Their support is a chat queue, and their AI is great for confirming you're in seat 12A. Their AI does not call the Mandarin Oriental on your behalf. Their AI does not know that you fly out of Teterboro when you can. We don't compete with Navan; we operate in the layer above it. Some of our members use Navan for the company spend, and Latitude for the trips that matter."*

### "What if my EA already handles this?"

> *"Your EA is a generalist. Travel is a specialty that takes 10,000 hours to be good at. Most great EAs are honest that they're booking on Expedia and Marriott.com because that's what they were trained on. Two things happen when we partner with an EA: their travel hours drop by about half, and the quality of the trip goes up because we have access to upgrade programs they don't. We work with your EA, not around them. If anything they become a fan first."*

### "Isn't this a lot of money for someone who travels twenty times a year?"

> *"It's one eighty-nine a month, and that includes the trip design, not just the bookings. At your spend level — call it eighty thousand a year — we typically capture three to nine thousand in upgrades, credits, and rebooked savings. Even at the low end the membership pays for itself. But more honestly, you're not buying ROI. You're buying never thinking about it again. The same way you don't calculate the ROI of your accountant."*

### "What happens if I want to book my own trip?"

> *"Book it. We don't gate-keep. Just forward us the confirmation and we'll add it to your profile, attach any program benefits we can after the fact, and watch for IROPs. You're paying for the desk to be there when you want it, not to be required."*

### "How do I know you're not just going to disappear in six months?"

> *"Fair. Two answers. One, I'm under Fora Travel as a host, which means the regulatory and supplier infrastructure is theirs. If I disappear, your trips still happen — but I'm not planning to disappear. Two, this is what I do full-time. If you want to talk to my first two reference members before signing, I'd rather you do that than not."*

### "Can I cancel mid-year?"

> *"Yes. Three months notice, prorated refund. No clawback. The model only works if you'd renew, not if you feel trapped."*

### "Do you handle leisure travel?"

> *"Yes — for members, included. We are work-first, life-aware. Your anniversary in Lisbon, your kid's spring break, your Aman in Tokyo — same desk, same email, same human. We are not splitting your relationship across two advisors the way a leisure agency would."*

## After the call

### Within 24 hours
- Send the brief follow-up note (see [Cold Outreach](./cold-outreach.md) — there is a template at the bottom for post-call)
- Add them to the CRM (Tern) with: company, role, T&E band, pain identified, tier recommended, next touch date
- If signal was medium or strong: schedule the follow-up call now

### Within 72 hours
- Send the comparison sheet (see [`/sales-kit/comparison.html`](./comparison.html) once built)
- Send one specific value-add: a sample year-in-review, the one Journal post most relevant to their situation, an introduction to a hotel GM in a city they mentioned

### Within two weeks
- One more high-quality touch, not another email
- Then: silence. Do not chase. The right buyer comes back when they have their next bad trip.

## Track what's working

For every call, record three things in the CRM:

1. **The pain they named.** Verbatim. After 20 calls, patterns emerge — and your Journal content writes itself from real prospect pain.
2. **The objection that almost killed it.** What they hesitated on. After 20 calls, the top three objections inform every piece of marketing material.
3. **Closed / Open / Lost.** And if Lost — why? "Too expensive" is different from "I don't think I travel enough" is different from "My partner blocked it." Each has a different fix.

By call 30, you should know your conversion rate, your average ramp time from first call to signed application, and the two objections that kill 60% of your deals.

---

## A note on cadence

These first 50 discovery calls are your only education that matters. Do not delegate them. Do not template the answers to the point where you stop listening. Every call is information about the product, the market, and the brand voice you should be using. The Journal posts you write in month four are written from things prospects say in month two.

The script is a floor. The relationship is the work.
