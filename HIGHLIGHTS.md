# Latitude 43 — Research Highlights

The twenty most actionable findings from the research stack, pulled forward for fast scanning. Each is sourced to its full document for the deeper version.

## I. Direct competitor weaknesses we can attack today

### 1. ALTOUR's tech is licensed, not proprietary
"ALTOUR Intelligence" is a marketing wrapper. AI Book = 'ello (Tel Aviv startup). AI Predict = Lumo (Boston ML startup, also licenses to Acai Travel). Mobile app = mTrip. OBT = SAP Concur. There is no proprietary AI moat. Any competitor can license the same Lumo predictive engine in 60 days. ([competitor-altour.md](research/competitor-altour.md))

### 2. ALTOUR's founder is gone and seven senior people walked
Alexandre Chemla, founder, departed Dec 2023. Wynona Dyer, ALTOUR Air co-founder, retired July 2025. In Feb 2025 Internova sued former exec Anthony Lee Thomas for starting **NousTravel** — and admitted **seven senior employees** had resigned to follow him. Internova paid Thomas $193,427.78 in non-compete consideration they are now trying to claw back. The boutique they sell you is not the boutique that exists.

### 3. Cadence publishes Mon–Fri 8:30–5:30 PT on the homepage
For a "business travel" company, this is structurally damning. Their after-hours desk exists, but it requires a phone code (**SV23G**) and is buried on a sub-page. The fact that the code hasn't been renamed in years is the brand-staleness tell. ([competitor-cadence.md](research/competitor-cadence.md))

### 4. Brownell Corporate's copy still says "135 years"
At 138 years in 2025, that means the corporate microsite hasn't been refreshed since ~2022. The site also returns intermittent 503s. brownellcorporate.com is a Tzell routing layer with no named corporate advisors and no published SLAs. ([competitor-brownell.md](research/competitor-brownell.md))

### 5. The most dangerous direct competitor is Bell & Bly Travel
Sarah Groen (Stanford MBA, ex-Uber) targets exactly our buyer ("entrepreneurs, executives, families, and couples"). Their pricing is the weakest vector: **$800 per week of trip up to 5 people, +$600/additional week, +50% rush fee within 45 days**. We attack with annual membership economics that flatten this transactional model. ([competitor-closest-analogs.md](research/competitor-closest-analogs.md))

## II. The pricing intelligence that should set Atlas

### 6. Indagare's two tiers ($395 / $2,850) are the public market floor
$2,455 to upgrade from Self Planner to Custom Planner. Custom Planners must book hotels through Indagare. Minimum recommended budget $1,500/room/night. Service hours: M–F 9–6 ET — not 24/7. Our Atlas Principal at $3,600 lands $750 above their top tier — defensible if we deliver 24/7.

### 7. Knightsbridge Circle is £25K standard, £100K Elite
60–80 members total. 4–5 members per lifestyle manager. 95%+ renewal. This is the ultra-premium ceiling. The empty space between Indagare's $2,850 and Knightsbridge's £25K (~$31,500) is exactly where Atlas Office $12K and Atlas Firm $36K live. We are the only branded service in that band. ([adjacent-lifestyle-memberships.md](research/adjacent-lifestyle-memberships.md))

### 8. SmartFlyer publishes a $500 planning fee floor
The only top-tier luxury house to disclose. Founded 1990 by Michael Holtz. ~$1B in gross bookings, 250 advisors, $31.2M direct revenue, 60 staff. Transparent pricing is itself the positioning.

## III. The "I already have an Amex concierge" objection — destroyed

### 9. Visa Infinite and Mastercard concierge are the same vendor
Both run on **Ten Lifestyle Group (LON:TENG)**. The Visa/Mastercard "competition" at the concierge layer is fictional. Cardholders are dialing the same call-center pool under different brand skins. ([adjacent-card-concierges.md](research/adjacent-card-concierges.md))

### 10. Amex Platinum concierge has been quietly hollowed out
Email concierge **eliminated August 2021**. Off-list restaurant booking **eliminated July 2022**. Phone hold times reported "up to an hour or more." The $695/year service in 2026 is materially worse than the $550/year service in 2020. Most prospects believe they have the 2018 product.

### 11. Even Centurion has degraded to a "POD" model
Cardholders paying $15K/year (initiation $5K + $10K annual) report the dedicated 1:1 has shifted to pod-team routing with continuity gaps. Agents reportedly don't know Centurion's own benefits stack. Brian Kelly's famous "Ray" experience is the marketing exception, not the norm.

### 12. Brex/Ramp travel is OBT + third-party support
Both publish "24/7 chat" — backstopped by Priceline (Ramp) or Brex's own thin team. Real cardholder reports: a $1,500 Marriott hold stuck with Brex's response of *"not our fault, nothing we can do."* Neither is staffed for travel judgment.

## IV. Brand register — the Amalfi-adjacent move

### 13. The "definite article" pattern asserts category-of-one
Most reproducible private-aviation copy device: *"The Charter Provider"* (Amalfi), *"The Jet Card"* (Sentient), *"The Promise of Always"* (NetJets), *"Your Personal Jet Company"* (Jet Linx). Two words. The article *the* claims the entire category. Apply to Latitude 43: *"The Travel Office"* > *"A Travel Office"*. ([brand-register-private-aviation.md](research/brand-register-private-aviation.md))

### 14. Founder visibility is paradoxical in premium aviation
Visible off the home page (TikTok, LEADERS Magazine, board seats). Conspicuously invisible on it. Latitude 43's about.html currently has a `[FOUNDER NAME]` placeholder — that placeholder is the single biggest piece of damage on the current site and must be addressed before launch.

### 15. The brand patterns that work (extracted from VistaJet, NetJets, Amalfi)
- Single full-bleed photograph, never a slider
- Restraint over enthusiasm — no adverbs, no exclamation marks
- Numbers as proof, not adjectives as proof
- Place-name specificity (TEB, LCY, HND, not "New York" / "London")
- Members not customers; the desk not the team; apply not sign up
- Cross-discipline reference (Aman, Hermès, Phaidon), never other travel agencies

## V. Lifestyle memberships — the precedents Atlas should mirror

### 16. Knightsbridge is the model; Quintessentially is the cautionary tale
Knightsbridge: tight scope, 4–5 members per manager, 95%+ renewal at premium price. Quintessentially: founded 2000, broader scope, Trustpilot littered with lifestyle managers literally booking OpenTable for clients. The lesson: **scope discipline beats scope breadth**. Atlas does business travel. Period.

### 17. The application + curation pattern works
Soho House's house rules + application + waiting list signal that membership is earned, not bought. The Atlas analog: a published "Atlas Conduct" document and two-reference application at all tiers. Quarterly intake committee. Member cap published.

### 18. App-first concierge is the wrong register for Atlas
Velocity Black tried app-first at $3,100/yr and got absorbed by Capital One. The category proof: the desk wins. Atlas is desk-first, app-aware. Build a member portal but never let the app become the brand.

## VI. Where to take the most ground

### 19. Six things every direct competitor either hides or fails
Cross-cutting from all teardowns:

| | They | We |
|---|---|---|
| Publish pricing | No | Yes — on the home page |
| Publish an SLA | No | Yes — 60 minutes, in writing |
| Name the principal | No (pod model) | Yes — one human, on the contract |
| Run modern brand | No (~2014 register) | Yes — operator-luxe, 2026-grade |
| Speak to the traveler | No (built for travel managers) | Yes — dashboards for the principal, not procurement |
| Cover after-hours with no friction | No (codes, queues) | Yes — direct line to the desk |

### 20. The positioning sentence to use against any of them
*"They scaled until they were owned by spreadsheets. We're not going to. That's the whole thesis."*

This is the line. It survives any comparison conversation — ALTOUR, Cadence, Brownell, Navan, Amex GBT. It collapses every competitive frame into one sentence about what we deliberately refuse.
