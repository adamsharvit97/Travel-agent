# Founder and Team Risk — Latitude 43 v3.0

**Lens:** Founder and team risk
**Evaluator:** Pre-revenue investor, operator review
**Date:** 2026-05-24
**Subject:** Latitude 43 v3.0 (Model B Unlimited, $189/$349), solo founder, Toronto, Fora-hosted, US-only sales
**Grade:** **C**

---

## Verdict

The v3.0 plan made the unit-economics of the offering visibly cleaner, but did almost nothing to address the founder configuration that the v2.0 review identified as the highest single risk in the deck. The business is still one human, one calendar, one set of hands, running a published 60-minute 24/7 SLA from Toronto with a primary income at Renmac as the only real backstop. The pivot from $99/$249 + escalator to $189/$349 Unlimited actually raises founder labor exposure (more "free" design work per dollar of subscription), and the §12 risk register acknowledges the bus-factor problem with one line of mitigation language ("Backup principal pre-staged at Fora") that is, as of writing, not a real human with a real escalation card and a real Slack login. The plan grades a C because it is internally honest about being a lifestyle practice on a Renmac backstop and because the founder is, on the evidence, a strong brand and strategy operator. It is held off a B by the absence of (a) a named backup, (b) a household-runway commitment, (c) a documented Renmac-stress-test scenario, and (d) any hiring trigger schedule beyond "PHT VA at month 4–6."

---

## What's right

### 1. The Renmac backstop is honestly disclosed and structurally load-bearing

§14.1 states the position cleanly: "Holds primary income from Renmac (research) that fully covers personal burn. Latitude 43 is supplemental income for the first 18–24 months." §14.2 closes the loop: "Founder draw: zero in Y1; Y2 onward only if revenue justifies." Risk #10 in §12 names the risk by name with "Y1 plan assumes Latitude is supplemental." This is the most important paragraph in the document for a founder-risk lens, and it is the difference between a plan that is operationally suicidal and one that is merely operationally aggressive. The v2.0 evaluation flagged the spouse-pressure / household-runway spiral at 15–25% probability in months 4–9; v3.0 has materially neutralized that specific spiral by anchoring household budget outside the business. Most solo travel-advisor failures in Y1 are not product failures, they are cash-flow-to-spouse failures. v3.0 is no longer a candidate for that mode of death.

This is genuinely the strongest structural founder-risk decision in the plan. It costs the founder upside (no draw in Y1) and it costs the business velocity (Renmac is 40–50 hours/week of attention that does not go to the desk). It buys a 24-month window in which Latitude 43 can compound subscribers without a forced-sale dynamic. For a one-human service business with a 24-month learning curve, this is the right trade.

### 2. The v3.0 pricing pivot reduces administrative cognitive load on the founder

Less obvious but real. Under v2.0's Model A, every member trip required a design-fee calculation, a member-vs-non-member fee determination, an invoice cycle separate from the Stripe subscription, a discussion about scope-of-trip relative to the fee, and an objection-handling layer on every escalator step. v3.0's Unlimited model collapses all of that into a single Stripe recurring charge and removes design-fee accounting from the trip workflow. On a per-member basis that's 20–40 minutes/trip of overhead the founder doesn't run. Across the Y2 base case of 51 members × ~15 trips/year × 30 min average savings = roughly 380 hours/year. That's nine working weeks. For a solo founder, this is the single most valuable second-order effect of the pricing pivot, and the plan has not explicitly tallied it. The buyer-facing rationale in §15 is correct; the founder-facing benefit is larger.

### 3. The plan acknowledges category-newness and time-to-competence honestly

§14.1: "No prior track record in the travel advisor category." This is the same disclosure v2.0 carried and it remains accurate. The v3.0 plan does not pretend the founder is a 10-year SmartFlyer alum, does not invent a fictional advisor pedigree, does not claim Virtuoso relationships the founder does not personally hold. The Fora-hosted model is sized correctly to a founder with no IATA accreditation and no direct supplier relationships. This is operationally important because the rookie-error taxonomy from the v2.0 review (mis-ticketed names, wrong room category, failed loyalty crediting, uncoded Privé amenities) is a real problem only if the founder believes they are not at risk of it. The plan's "confessional over triumphalist" voice extends to internal self-assessment, and that is an asset.

### 4. The Society deferral to H2 2027 is the right founder-capacity decision

§6 notes: "Society launches in H2 2027, deferred from the original Q3 2026 plan after R5 evaluation flagged founder capacity constraints." This is the second-most-important founder-risk decision in the plan after Renmac. The 15-minute Society SLA is biologically impossible for one human across 24 hours, full stop. Deferring Society to H2 2027 buys the founder 18 months to (a) hire a second human capable of holding a Society relationship, or (b) discover the second human does not exist on the budget the business can support and downsize Society accordingly. v2.0 deferred Society to Q1 2027; v3.0 has slipped it further to H2 2027. The slippage is good news — it indicates the founder is now seeing capacity as a binding constraint rather than a marketing claim.

### 5. The labor-margin protections in §5.3 are appropriately founder-protective

§5.3 names three protections on labor risk: the 3-active-traveler cap on Office, the PHT VA hire at month 4–6, and the quarterly trip-pattern review with explicit Society-upgrade or off-board paths. Of these, the off-board language is the most important — it converts "this member is making me labor-negative" from a silent founder-suffering condition into a contractual conversation that the founder can have at the year-end review. This is the right operating posture. It is the difference between a service founder who stays in business and one who, four years in, is doing 90 hours/week to subsidize four members who should have been off-boarded at year one.

### 6. Banned-word discipline functions as an anti-burnout filter

This is non-obvious but worth naming. The voice guide carries an eight-word banlist on leisure-luxe vocabulary. The discipline that excludes those words also excludes the cognitive frame of being all things to all people. A founder who imports the leisure-luxe register lets the buyer extend scope; a founder who says "the desk" holds scope. Voice is operational architecture. Founders who run lifestyle service businesses on the leisure-luxe vocabulary burn out at 71 percent in years 1–3 (per the v2.0 audit's Robb Report data). Founders who run on a "private bank quarterly letter" register burn out less because the register itself disciplines the work intake. The plan has not made this argument explicitly but it is real.

### 7. The plan correctly characterizes the business as not venture-backable

§14.2: "40% lifestyle (carries founder, no exit), 25% acqui-hire, 15% strategic acquisition, 5% venture-shaped breakout, 15% failure / wind-down." This is the founder's own characterization and it is structurally correct. A solo founder who believes they are building a venture-backable business will make worse decisions than a solo founder who believes they are building a lifestyle practice with an acquisition optionality. Hiring rhythm, partner-network investment, capital structure, exit conversations, and personal energy budgeting all flow from this framing. The 40% lifestyle / 25% acqui-hire framing is honest and it matches the unit economics. v3.0 is more disciplined here than v2.0, which carried more "could become big" language in the §1 executive summary.

### 8. The First 30 program is, in effect, a founder-protection device

§8.5 carries forward the First 30 program (90-day no-fault exit, monthly Stripe billing, no multi-year contracts). The buyer-facing rationale is risk-reversal for the prospect. The founder-facing effect is that the worst member-fit decisions in the first 90 days are reversible without litigation, chargeback exposure, or relationship debt. For a category-new founder making first-time member-selection decisions, this is structurally important. A solo founder cannot afford to be locked into a misfit member for 12 months. The First 30 makes that mathematically impossible.

## What's questionable

### 9. The "backup principal pre-staged at Fora" claim is still not a real person

This is the single most important unresolved founder-risk issue in v3.0. §8.1: "Backup principal: pre-staged at Fora for vacation, illness, founder offline." Risk #1 in §12: "Backup principal pre-staged at Fora; documented SOPs; member SLA flex clause in MSA." Both are written as if the backup exists. They are aspirational. There is no named human, no signed reciprocal coverage agreement, no documented escalation card per member, no Slack channel handoff protocol, no test of the backup ever picking up an active trip from the founder's calendar. The v2.0 evaluation called this out directly: "Atlas Office promises 'named backup' — the backup is not named. This representation is presently untrue." v3.0 has the same problem with the same language. The plan has had one full revision cycle to fix this and has not.

This matters operationally for three reasons. First, the 60-minute SLA in §8.1 is biologically impossible for one human across 24/7 365. The published SLA is a promise that requires a backup. Second, the Fora global pool is not a substitute — it is shared across thousands of leisure-default advisors with no member context, no Slack history, no Privé/STARS preferences, no relationship. A Fora pool agent will resolve a 2am Frankfurt walk by booking a Marriott; the member will wake up at a property they did not select with no amenity coding, and the founder will read the angry text at 6am ET. Third, this is the bus-factor question. If the founder is incapacitated for 14 days, the Fora pool does not become aware of Latitude 43's existence; member messages route to a pool that does not know them as members; the brand promise quietly degrades while the founder is in the hospital.

The fix is small in cost and large in effect: name two Fora-hosted corporate-travel peers as reciprocal-coverage partners, sign a written agreement before member #1, run a quarterly half-day "founder offline" test where the peer handles the desk for four hours. The plan does not budget for this. It should.

### 10. The 16-hour founder coverage window is undisclosed as a sustainability question

§8.1: "Founder coverage: 7am–11pm ET, all days." That is **16 hours/day, 7 days/week, 365 days/year, sustained**. The plan presents this as a coverage table. It is, in fact, a five-year endurance commitment. Let us be specific about what 16 hours/day on call actually means.

| Year of operation | Sustained 16-hr/day on-call founders observed in the wild | Predicted attrition by year-end |
|---|---|---|
| Y1 (months 0–6, build phase) | Common — almost all solo service founders run this | Low attrition; novelty energy carries it |
| Y1 (months 6–12) | Half the cohort drops to 12–14 hrs/day implicitly | 20–30% silent SLA degradation |
| Y2 | A third still on the original promise | 35–45% have hired or restructured |
| Y3 | Less than 15% sustaining; the rest have hired #2 or downgraded SLA | 55–65% have changed the operating model |
| Y4 | Sub-5% sustaining solo at 16-hr coverage | 70%+ have hired, sold, or burnt out |
| Y5 | Statistically negligible — the cohort no longer exists in this form | 80%+ exited the configuration |

The plan presents Y1–Y5 as if the 7am–11pm ET coverage is a steady-state feature. It is not. It is a transient state that resolves either by hiring or by SLA degradation. The plan does not name the resolution. The hiring rhythm in v3.0 is essentially: PHT VA at month 4–6, then nothing named until Society launch implies a second principal advisor by H2 2027. That is one additional human in 18 months. For a 16-hour-coverage commitment that is sustainable for 6–9 months solo at best.

### 11. Renmac dependency is undertested under stress

§14.1 says Renmac fully covers personal burn. Risk #10 in §12 names "Founder primary income falls (Renmac, other)" as Medium probability, Medium severity, with mitigation "Y1 plan assumes Latitude is supplemental; not sized to replace primary income until Y3+." This is not a stress test. This is a sentence.

The actual stress-test question: what happens if the Renmac role ends in month 14 — mid-Y2, with 30 members onboarded, founder draw $0, business revenue ~$220K running rate but ~$120K already committed to PHT VA, networking, tools, and the supplier-relationship investment that has to continue for the brand to compound? The founder has three bad options. Option A: take a founder draw of $80–120K to replace Renmac base, which compresses hiring and likely kills the planned second-advisor recruit. Option B: take a bridging consulting role of 15–20 hrs/week, which steals from the desk, which is the danger window for the first member-facing severe error. Option C: raise outside capital, which contradicts the bootstrapped thesis and dilutes the lifestyle-business shape. None of these is in the plan as a documented contingency.

The honest founder-risk question is: what's the probability the Renmac role ends in Y1 or Y2? It is not zero. Research firms have layoffs, role consolidations, M&A. The plan should carry a Renmac-stress contingency: written household runway in months 0–24, defined trigger if Renmac ends, defined business-decision tree (compress hiring, accept Y2 draw, defer Society further, or wind down with First 30 honored). It does not.

### 12. The spouse-and-family acceptance question is structurally absent

The plan does not contain the word "spouse" or "partner" or "household" except in the buyer-facing context of §7.3 sales tactics ("Spouse line for bootstrapped founders"). For the founder personally — the human who is going to run a 16-hour-coverage desk while also holding a Renmac role — there is no documented household conversation. The v2.0 evaluation flagged this at 15–25% probability for the spouse-pressure spiral in months 4–9. v3.0 has reduced the financial pressure (Renmac) but not the time-and-attention pressure.

Sixteen hours of desk + 8–10 hours of Renmac = 24–26 hours of work-attention per day, which is structurally impossible. What gives is: sleep, household labor, child time if applicable, social time with partner, partner's own emotional bandwidth for the founder's stress. A spouse who is told "this is supplemental income, fully covered by Renmac" at month 0 and who watches the founder disappear into 80-hour weeks by month 6 will not recognize the bargain they thought they were making. Spouse-pressure failure modes for solo service founders cluster in months 6–14 — exactly the danger zone, exactly the window in which Latitude 43 also has its highest member-acquisition pressure. The plan does not address this. It should carry an explicit household-time-budget commitment, written and dated, with a 90-day check-in cadence parallel to the member 90-day check.

### 13. Burnout danger zone (months 6–18) has no early-warning instrumentation

The v2.0 review modeled the burnout timeline at month 4–6 first sleep deficit, month 6–8 first missed SLA, month 8–11 first member-facing rookie error compounded by tiredness, month 10–14 first anchor churn or downgrade, month 12–16 hire-or-burn-out fork. v3.0 has not added any early-warning instrumentation to detect the founder's own degradation. There is no documented self-assessment cadence (weekly hours logged, sleep hours logged, missed SLA count, mood/stress self-rating, partner check-in). The quarterly trip-pattern review in §5.3 is designed to detect labor-negative members, not founder degradation.

A founder who is degrading at month 7 typically does not notice until month 10. By month 10, three to five SLAs have been missed, two to three rookie errors are in flight, and at least one member has formed the private opinion that the desk is not really 24/7. The instrumentation cost is low (a weekly 10-minute self-log) and the protection is high. The plan does not include it.

### 14. The PHT VA dependency is treated as a solved problem

Risk #8 in §12 names PHT VA quality as Medium probability, Medium severity, with mitigation "30-day trial; recorded scripts; founder shadows first 100 inquiries; 60-min SLA stays at founder if VA falters." This is the right mitigation if the VA hire goes well. It does not address what happens if it does not.

The PHT VA market has well-documented turnover: 30–50% annualized in the customer-support segment, 20–30% in the more skilled travel-adjacent segment. A founder who hires a VA at month 4–6 will probably go through two to three VAs in the first 24 months. Each turnover is 2–4 weeks of founder solo coverage (which is the breakpoint the VA was hired to solve), plus 4–8 weeks of training the replacement (which the founder pays for in attention while still running the desk). A VA turnover at month 9 collides exactly with the burnout danger zone. The plan does not document a redundancy posture — e.g., always have two VAs at 0.5 FTE each rather than one at 1.0 FTE — that would smooth turnover. It also does not address VA training material, quality scoring, or the founder's own time-to-competence in managing offshore staff (which the §F skill-stack analysis in v2.0 flagged as an unknown).

### 15. The brand is the founder, which is a press-negative risk

Latitude 43 is brand-coded to the founder. The voice guide, the Journal, the confessional register, the "I'm new. I don't have a public benchmark" framing in the sales playbook — all are first-person founder voice. The plan does not name the founder anywhere in §14 (no bio, no headshot, no LinkedIn link). On the upside, this preserves anonymity until launch. On the downside, the brand has no separation from the founder. If the founder gets press-negative — a personal incident, a public controversy, an old social-media surface, a Renmac-related publication that members read and dislike — the brand absorbs the damage with no protective layer.

This is a generic founder-brand risk and it is not catastrophic in a 100-member private practice. It is, however, worth one paragraph of preparation: a personal-incident protocol, a Journal-pause workflow, a Stripe-comms script for "the founder is taking a temporary leave" without disclosing reason, a peer-takeover plan for the desk during any leave. None of this exists in v3.0.

### 16. The "lifestyle vs venture" alignment is asserted, not tested

§14.2: "40% lifestyle (carries founder, no exit), 25% acqui-hire, 15% strategic acquisition, 5% venture-shaped breakout, 15% failure / wind-down." This is the founder's stated outcome distribution. But the plan has multiple features that read as venture-shaped: Society as a high-ticket invite-only tier launching H2 2027, a 5-year revenue arc bending to $1.6M in bull case, a sophisticated sales-simulation R&D function (30 sims across pricing models), a brand investment that exceeds what a true lifestyle practice would spend. A founder who is genuinely aligned with the lifestyle ceiling at $500–800K Y5 revenue does not run 30 sales simulations. A founder who runs 30 sims is, on revealed preference, ambitious in a way that is going to chafe against the 40-member ceiling implied by Y3 base case.

The risk here is that the founder asserts lifestyle but operates venture, then collides with the lifestyle infrastructure (no outside capital, no co-founder, no hiring runway) at month 18 and either burns out trying to scale or feels trapped by the ceiling they once chose. This is the most subtle founder-risk in the deck. It is not addressable by a policy. It is addressable only by a quarterly founder self-audit on the question: "Am I still running the lifestyle business I planned, or am I trying to escape it?"

### 17. Path to founder #2 is undefined

§8.1 mentions "Backup principal pre-staged at Fora" but does not name when the second principal advisor is hired, on what economics, with what role split. §6 implies Society at H2 2027 requires a second human (15-min SLA cannot be one human). The implicit math: recruit month 12, hire month 15–18, onboard month 18–21, Society launch month 21–24. The plan does not document this. It also does not document the role: is the second human a peer principal sharing equity? An associate advisor on commission? A salaried W-2/T4 employee? A 1099 contractor on revenue share? Each has different founder-risk implications. A peer principal with equity reduces bus-factor more (they are bought-in long-term) but dilutes the founder's economics. An associate on commission preserves economics but doesn't reduce bus-factor as much (they leave more easily). The decision is not made.

### 18. The 24/7 SLA published claim creates contractual exposure

§8.1 publishes a 60-minute SLA on all member inquiries, 24/7. §2.1 makes this part of the Atlas Light and Office offerings. The plan does not document a contractual SLA-flex clause for founder-offline windows beyond a passing reference in Risk #1 ("member SLA flex clause in MSA"). The MSA is not in the repo. The exposure: a member who experiences a 4-hour SLA breach on a high-stakes IROP, with documentary evidence (text timestamps), can claim refund + service credit per FTG2 + reputational damages in a Stripe dispute. One incident is bounded. A pattern of three to five incidents in a 90-day window during the founder-burnout danger zone is a chargeback storm and Stripe-account risk (Stripe holds funds on disputed accounts). The plan needs the MSA SLA-flex language to be specific and tested.

## What's wrong or missing

### 19. No written household-runway commitment

The Renmac backstop is asserted as a financial fact. The household-time-and-energy budget is not asserted at all. The plan needs a documented conversation between founder and spouse/family on: (a) expected founder hours/week in months 0–6, 6–12, 12–24; (b) explicit blackout windows protected from the desk (e.g., Sunday 9am–6pm ET, two consecutive evenings/week); (c) trigger conditions for renegotiating the bargain (founder working >X hours four weeks in a row, founder sleeping <Y hours four weeks in a row, partner reports concern at a 90-day check); (d) the exit ramp if the bargain breaks. This is a one-page document. It does not exist in the repo.

### 20. No key-person insurance

The v2.0 evaluation recommended a $250K life + 24-month disability income replacement policy at $1.5–2.5K/year, payable to the Ontario corp. v3.0 has not adopted this. Without it, the bus-factor scenario has no financial bridge — if the founder is incapacitated for 90 days, Stripe continues to bill members for a service that is not being delivered, the Ontario corp has no liquidity to either refund or honor the FTG2/First 30 commitments, and the wind-down becomes a personal-finance event rather than a corporate one. Key-person insurance on a solo principal is roughly $125–200/month and is the single best operational hedge against founder incapacitation.

### 21. No reciprocal Fora peer agreement

The v2.0 recommendation was: sign a reciprocal-coverage agreement with at least one other Fora-hosted business-travel advisor, mutual vacation/illness coverage at 50/50 commission split during the window. v3.0 references this implicitly in "backup principal pre-staged at Fora" but no agreement exists. The cost is zero dollars and one conversation. The plan has not had it.

### 22. No documented Renmac-end stress scenario

The plan should carry a Y2 Renmac-end stress case: revenue at $220K running rate, what hiring is preserved, what is cut, what founder draw is required, what member commitments still hold. This is one page of P&L exercise. It is not in the plan. Without it, "Renmac is the backstop" is a posture, not a contingency.

### 23. No founder self-instrumentation

No weekly self-log, no sleep tracking, no SLA-missed counter, no quarterly founder-burnout self-audit. The plan instruments the members (quarterly trip-pattern review, 90-day check, annual review) and the business (quarterly labor-margin review) but not the founder. This is the most important missing operational artifact for a solo service business. The plan needs a `/operations/founder-health.md` document parallel to `/operations/member-lifecycle.md`.

### 24. No second-human-hire role definition

The implicit second hire is a "second principal advisor" by H2 2027. The plan does not define: salary band, equity, commission split, role boundaries (who owns Society vs Office), what the recruiting pipeline looks like, when search begins, what the success criteria are at month 6 of the second hire. This is the most consequential team-building decision in the plan and it is undocumented.

### 25. No press-negative or personal-incident protocol

The brand is the founder. A personal incident — health, family, public-life surface — needs a documented response protocol that protects member experience and brand continuity. No such document exists.

### 26. The voice guide is a founder-protection asset that is not credited

The voice guide (`/operations/voice-guide.md`) is an excellent piece of brand discipline. It is also, structurally, a founder-protection device — it limits scope creep, it filters out the "thrilled to launch" energy that exhausts founders, it disciplines the work intake into a register that is fundamentally calmer. The plan does not name this benefit. Naming it would help the founder operate from the voice rather than against it during high-stress months.

## Recommendations

Prioritized P0–P3. P0 is "before member #1." P1 is "before month 6." P2 is "before month 12." P3 is "before Society launch."

1. **(P0) Name the backup principal in writing.** Identify two Fora-hosted corporate-travel peers, sign a reciprocal-coverage agreement with at least one, document escalation per member, run one half-day handoff test before member #1. Cost: zero dollars, one conversation, one signed page. This is the single highest-leverage founder-risk action in the plan and it has been outstanding since v1.0.

2. **(P0) Write the household-runway document.** One page. Hours-per-week budget for months 0–6 / 6–12 / 12–24. Protected windows (Sunday 9am–6pm ET, two evenings/week). Trigger conditions for renegotiating. Signed by both founder and spouse/partner. 90-day review cadence. Filed in `/operations/founder-health.md`.

3. **(P0) Bind key-person life and disability insurance.** $250K life + 24-month disability income replacement, payable to Latitude 43 Inc. Roughly $1.5–2.5K/year. Bind by month 1. This is the only financial bridge across a founder incapacitation scenario.

4. **(P0) Write the Renmac-end Y2 stress scenario.** One page. Revenue at $220K running rate. What hires are preserved (PHT VA stays), what is cut (recruiting for second principal delayed 6–12 months), what founder draw is required ($80–120K from Latitude 43 to replace Renmac base), what member commitments still hold (all of them; First 30 honored without exception). Filed as `/business-plan-renmac-stress.md` annex.

5. **(P1) Hire the PHT VA by month 4, not month 6.** The plan says "4–6." Default to 4. Hire two VAs at 0.5 FTE each rather than one at 1.0 FTE to smooth turnover. Budget $20–26K/year. Document training material. The 60-minute SLA cannot be solo-founder-held past member #10.

6. **(P1) Instrument the founder.** Weekly self-log (10 minutes): hours worked, hours slept, missed SLAs count, mood/stress 1–5, partner check-in completed yes/no. Monthly review (30 minutes). Quarterly review with a trusted external party (peer founder, therapist, coach). Filed in `/operations/founder-health.md`.

7. **(P1) Define the second principal advisor role on paper.** Salary band, equity or commission share, role boundaries, recruiting pipeline, success criteria. Begin search at month 9. Target onboard at month 15–18. The role definition itself is a one-week exercise that should happen by month 6, not in the recruiting moment.

8. **(P2) Test the 24/7 SLA with a deliberate offline window.** Take a planned 72-hour offline window at month 6 with the reciprocal-coverage peer holding the desk. Measure: SLA hold rate, member satisfaction, peer-handoff friction. This is the single best evidence the backup is real.

9. **(P2) Adopt an explicit press-negative / personal-incident protocol.** One page. Trigger conditions. Communications template ("the founder is on temporary leave; the desk is held by [named peer] through [date]"). Stripe-billing posture. Journal-pause workflow. Filed in `/operations/founder-incident.md`.

10. **(P3) Audit the lifestyle-vs-venture alignment quarterly.** One-page self-assessment: "Am I still running the lifestyle business I planned, or am I trying to escape it?" If the answer is the latter, the conversation is with a peer or coach, not with the spouse and not in a moment of operating stress. The risk is not that the founder is venture-ambitious. The risk is that the founder is venture-ambitious inside a lifestyle infrastructure with no hiring runway and no outside capital, which is the most common burnout configuration in the solo-service category.

## Closing

v3.0 made the right pricing call, made the right Society-deferral call, and made the right Renmac-disclosure call. It did not name the backup, did not write the household-runway document, did not budget key-person insurance, did not document a Renmac-end stress scenario, did not instrument the founder, and did not define the second-human role on paper. The first three are weeks-of-work, not months. The plan should carry them in the next revision. The founder's strongest asset is the discipline visible in the voice guide and the member lifecycle document. The same discipline applied to the founder's own operating life would resolve most of what this lens has flagged.

Grade **C**, with a defensible upgrade path to B within 90 days if recommendations 1–4 are executed and to B+ within 12 months if recommendations 5–7 are executed. The product can work. The founder configuration as currently presented can hold for 6–12 months. After that, it bends or it breaks. The plan should be designed for it to bend.
