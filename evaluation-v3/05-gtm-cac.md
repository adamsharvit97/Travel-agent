# 05 — GTM and CAC under Model B Unlimited

**Lens:** Go-to-market realism, channel mix, customer acquisition cost, and Y1 funnel feasibility.
**Plan version under review:** v3.0 Model B Unlimited ($189 Light / $349 Office, all design included).
**Anchored on:** v2.0 lens at `/home/user/Travel-agent/evaluation/05-gtm-cac.md`, short-form v3 eval Lens 3 at `/home/user/Travel-agent/business-plan-v3-evaluation.md`, business-plan.md §10, and `/home/user/Travel-agent/sales-kit/cold-outreach.md`.

---

## Verdict

**Grade: C+.** Model B Unlimited improves the per-member economics enough to make cold acquisition arithmetically possible for the first time — but the plan's stated Y1 funnel (100/day cold, 22 Light + 8 Office, single warm anchor) is built on the optimistic end of every conversion range, double-counts founder hours that already belong to the desk and the newsletter, and treats the newsletter itself as a working lead magnet when it is not yet a working channel. The honest Y1 number, after stress-testing the funnel, capping discovery calls at the founder's real available hours, and accepting that the brother-in-law network is undefined, is **12–18 closes (8–12 Light, 4–6 Office) on roughly $55–85K of first-year revenue**, not 30 closes on $115K. The plan does not break — the GTM still grinds out a real business by Y3 — but the Y1 target is set against the bull funnel, and there is no instrumented loop in the plan to know which channel is paying for itself.

## What's right

### 1. Model B's higher per-member economics finally make cold email arithmetically defensible — for Office, not Light

The v2.0 lens (`/home/user/Travel-agent/evaluation/05-gtm-cac.md` §G.1) was emphatic: cold acquisition of Light at $99/mo was upside-down by an order of magnitude. Light Y1 econ was $2,400; CAC against a solo founder's 100-sends-per-week throughput was $30K+. The math literally did not close.

Under Model B, Light Y1 economics move from $2,400 to **$3,370–$3,820** and Office Y1 moves from $6,300–$7,000 to **$7,844–$8,894** (business-plan.md §5.1). The lift is 27–40% on Light and 24–27% on Office, all of it in retained subscription. That changes the CAC payback story in three ways:

- Light cold-acquisition payback at $4K of CAC moves from 20+ months to roughly 13–14 months on full Y1 econ. Still long, but no longer broken.
- Office cold-acquisition payback at $4K of CAC drops to about 5–6 months on full Y1 econ. That is now in the SaaS-respectable band.
- The "stop cold-prospecting Light" recommendation from the v2.0 lens (§I.3) softens. Cold for Light at Model B is bad but no longer absurd; cold for Office at Model B is genuinely viable.

This is a real and consequential change. The plan does not foreground it, but it should: the single largest commercial benefit of Model B Unlimited is not the simpler pitch, it is that cold email becomes a channel that pays back inside 12 months on the Office mix.

### 2. The "everything included" anchor solves the most expensive friction in the cold funnel

The v2.0 cold-outreach scripts at `/home/user/Travel-agent/sales-kit/cold-outreach.md` show the problem the plan has correctly diagnosed. The Office tier is quoted at "$12,000 a year" in email A2 with no design-fee mention, then the buyer hits the plan, sees the design-fee escalator, and the conditional-yes call breaks on "what's the all-in?" That breakage was the dominant failure mode under Model A. R6+R7 sim data (per business-plan.md §15) confirmed it: no rep could answer "what's the all-in?" with one number under Model A; every rep could under Model B.

This matters specifically for cold conversion. The 30–50% reply-to-call rate the plan claims is achievable only if the call agenda is "see if there's a fit." With Model A, the call always became "explain the pricing." With Model B, the call can stay on fit. That single change probably moves cold reply-to-call by 8–15 percentage points and call-to-conditional-yes by 5–10 percentage points. Compounded across the funnel, Model B's pricing structure alone is worth a 1.5–2× lift in end-to-end cold conversion vs. Model A — not because the price is lower (it isn't; it's higher in nominal terms), but because the question the buyer has at the end of the call has changed.

### 3. The pitch language has tightened in a way that helps both cold and warm

Business-plan.md §7.1 has the new one-paragraph pitch. It contains: two prices, two coverage descriptions, one SLA, the included-design promise, the EA seat, the FTG hook, and the "apply, don't sign up" close. It is 95 words. It can be repeated verbatim by an EA to a principal. That is operationally important — the EA-to-principal handoff was the single most leaky moment in the Model A sims, because the EA could not summarize the offer without missing the escalator. Under Model B, she can. The plan's claim in §7.4 that "no EA in 30 sims successfully sold the Model A escalator internally; multiple EAs in Model B sims explained the membership to the principal in one sentence" is the most operationally useful single finding from R6+R7.

For GTM, this means the **second-touch close rate** (EA reads cold email, forwards to principal, principal replies) is structurally higher under Model B. The v2.0 lens (§F.3) put aggregate cold close at 12–20% over 90 days. Under Model B, with the cleaner pitch and the EA-summarizable structure, the realistic range is **15–24% over 90 days** on cold-sourced leads. Still a haircut against the simulated numbers, but a genuine improvement.

### 4. The FTG-by-stage architecture is the right unlock for cold

Business-plan.md §2.3 matches FTG mechanic to sales-cycle stage rather than to tier. FTG3 (30-day money-back) is paired specifically to "new-buyer onboarding and price-negotiator closes" — i.e. the modal cold-inbound prospect. That is the right pairing. The 30-day money-back deflects the single largest cold-funnel objection: "I don't know you, why would I commit?" The answer becomes "you don't have to commit; you decide if it was worth it."

The reason this matters for CAC is that FTG3 lifts the conditional-yes-to-close conversion materially. The v2.0 lens put that conversion at 40–60%; with FTG3 explicitly on the table at the close, the realistic Model B range is **50–65%**. A 5-point lift at the closing step is worth roughly 12% in total funnel yield, which means the cold-sends-per-close figure drops from ~5,000 to ~4,200 at the midpoint.

### 5. The disqualify-up move to Society defends the high-end funnel without burning the lead

Lesson 16 in §7.3 — "Disqualify up to Society for HNW: '$349 is the wrong product for you' flips dynamic" — is the right play and it is rare in the segment. Most advisors anchored at $349 will push HNW prospects into Office and leave $15–25K of upside on the table. The plan's disqualify-up move converts a few of those HNW prospects to Society at $14K average, which is a 40× revenue lift over the Office close. Even at a 10–15% conversion rate, this is the single highest-EV move in the cold-and-warm funnel.

The reason this matters for CAC math: a single Society close out of every 20 cold prospects pays for **all** the cold outreach against the rest of the cohort. The funnel does not have to close at the median to pay for itself; it has to produce one Society close per quarter. That is a much lower bar than 6 Office closes per quarter.

### 6. The newsletter strategy correctly identifies its own role

Business-plan.md §9.3 calls out Desk Diary 2x/week + 43rd Parallel monthly, and §10.4 says "Newsletter-first. Social as distribution." The strategic role is right — the newsletter is the long-cycle credibility builder that makes the cold and warm channels work. The plan does not pretend the newsletter is a direct-response channel; it positions it as the substrate that lifts every other channel's response rate.

What is right about this: the 2025–26 evidence on B2B service businesses with $5K+ ACVs is that a credible founder-led newsletter at 1,200–2,500 subscribers lifts cold email reply rate by 0.5–1.5 percentage points (because recipients have seen the founder's name before) and lifts warm-intro conversion by 5–10 points (because the referrer can point at the newsletter as proof of competence). The newsletter is not the lead source; it is the **conversion multiplier** on every other lead source. The plan understands this implicitly even if it doesn't say it out loud.

## What's questionable

### 1. The 100/day cold volume cannot survive the rest of the founder's day

Business-plan.md §10.2 commits to **100 targeted outreaches per day from day one**. The plan's own short-form eval (Lens 3, p. 87) flags this: "100 targeted, well-researched outreaches per day is roughly 3 hours of solo work. Across 250 working days, that's 750 hours/year." That is correct, and it is the structural problem.

Founder's annual hour budget at 50 working hours/week × 50 weeks = **2,500 hours**. Subtract:
- Primary income at Renmac: assume 40% of time, ~1,000 hours
- 24/7 desk coverage for Y1 members (12 active by mid-year, 30 by end-year): at the plan's own ~25 hrs/member/yr for Light + ~50 hrs/member/yr for Office, this is 22 × 25 + 8 × 50 = **950 hours minimum**
- Newsletter writing at the plan's 2x/week Desk Diary (500w each) + monthly 43rd Parallel (2,500w): ~75,000 words/yr at 8–12 hrs per 1,000 words of publication-quality output = **600–900 hours**
- Discovery and close calls at 30 closes through a ~10% close rate = 300 calls × 1.5 hours including prep + follow-up = **450 hours**
- Operational admin, Fora compliance, accounting, supplier work, member onboarding calls: **200 hours minimum**

Subtotal before any cold prospecting: **3,200–3,500 hours**. The founder is already 700–1,000 hours over budget before sending a single cold email. Adding 750 hours of cold prospecting on top puts the total at **3,950–4,250 hours against a 2,500-hour denominator**. That is 158–170% of available time.

The plan does not address this. The short-form eval's recommendation (cut cold to 30–50/day, hire list VA at month 3) is correct but is not in the canonical plan. **At the volume committed in §10.2, something gives. The most likely casualties, in order, are: newsletter cadence, then desk SLA response time, then cold volume itself, then primary income.**

### 2. The cold-email math the plan publishes is the bull case, not the realistic case

Business-plan.md §10.2 publishes this funnel:
- 100 outreaches/day × 250 days = 25,000 outreaches/year
- 1–3% reply rate = 250–750 replies
- 30–50% reply→call = 75–375 calls
- 15–25% call→conditional yes = 11–94 conditional yeses
- 40–60% conditional yes→close = 4–56 closes

The plan then states: "Math: 10 cold closes requires ~5,000 outreaches." That figure is derived from the **optimistic** end of the funnel: 3% × 50% × 25% × 60% = 0.225% end-to-end. The **midpoint** is 2% × 40% × 20% × 50% = **0.08% end-to-end**, which means 10 closes require ~12,500 sends, not 5,000. The **pessimistic** end (1% × 30% × 15% × 40% = 0.018%) requires ~55,000 sends per 10 closes.

Solving for Y1 closes at the founder's stated 25,000 sends:
- **Optimistic:** 25,000 × 0.225% = **56 closes** — wildly above plan, implies founder is overloaded with onboarding before December
- **Realistic midpoint:** 25,000 × 0.08% = **20 closes** — credibly hits the cold portion of the 30-member target
- **Pessimistic:** 25,000 × 0.018% = **4.5 closes** — cold barely contributes; the year depends entirely on warm and inbound

The midpoint case (20 cold closes) is the one the plan is implicitly assuming. The problem is twofold. First, the midpoint assumes 100 sends/day actually happens — which requires the 750 founder hours that don't exist (see point 1). Second, the midpoint funnel rates are sourced from B2B SaaS benchmarks for cold email in 2023–24. **In 2026, cold email reply rates for finance/founder personas have compressed to 0.5–1.5%** because every Apollo-and-Smartlead stack is now sending to the same Series A/B founders with the same "saw your raise, congrats" opener. The 1–3% range the plan publishes is the optimistic 2023 range, not the 2026 reality.

Applying the 2026 compressed funnel (1% reply, 35% reply-to-call, 18% call-to-conditional, 45% conditional-to-close = **0.028% end-to-end**) against the actual sustainable solo volume of 40–50/day × 220 productive days = **10,000 sends**, expected closes = **2.8**. That is the realistic cold contribution: 2–4 closes in Y1, not 20, not 56.

### 3. The warm channel cap is much tighter than the plan implies

Business-plan.md §10.1 lists, in full:
- 1 warm anchor (old boss, ~$100K T&E)
- 1 brother-in-law network
- Soho House Toronto
- EO Toronto
- 6 brother-in-law-hosted dinners
- $15–20K total networking budget

The implied warm pipeline:

| Source | Realistic intros Y1 | Close rate | Closes Y1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Old boss as anchor | 1 (himself) + 2–4 referred | 70–85% on him, 25–35% on refs | 1–2 |
| Brother-in-law network | 8–15 useful intros (depends on his network) | 25–40% | 2–5 |
| Soho House (if admitted by month 6) | 5–12 substantive conversations | 5–15% | 0–2 |
| EO Toronto (if admitted by month 4) | 10–20 forum + event conversations | 8–18% | 1–3 |
| 6 brother-in-law dinners (36 covers, ~10–18 ICP-fit) | 3–7 follow-up calls | 30–50% | 1–3 |
| Founder LinkedIn 1st-degree DMs | ~200 messages | 2–4% | 4–8 |
| **Warm total** | | | **9–23** |

Midpoint of the warm column: **~16 warm closes** — but every cell in that range is the optimistic end. Realistic adjustments:

- **Soho House admission timing.** v2.0 lens §C.1 documented 6–18 month application backlog. If admission lands month 9+, this is a Y2 channel. **Take to 0–1 closes Y1.**
- **EO Toronto admission.** Application + interview is 3 months; founder pre-revenue may not qualify on the personal-revenue gate. Realistic: 50/50 the founder is admitted in Y1. **Take to 0–2 closes Y1.**
- **Brother-in-law network depth.** Unspecified in the plan, and the v2.0 lens flagged this as ±5 Y1 closes of variance. If BIL's network is local Toronto SMB (most likely default), the dinners produce affection and limited US-resident pipeline (compounded by TICO Path A: Toronto-resident leads are unaddressable). **Take BIL+dinners to 2–4 closes combined.**
- **Old boss.** Most reliable single bet. 1–2 closes Y1 is honest.
- **LinkedIn 1st-degree.** The 200-DM figure assumes the founder has 200 relevant 1st-degree connections. Plausible if the founder has a 10+ year career in finance/research; less so if his network is mostly Renmac colleagues. **3–6 closes Y1 conditional on actual network composition.**

Realistic warm total: **7–14 closes**, mid-point **~10**. Add the 2–4 cold closes from §2 above and the 1–3 inbound from newsletter/social (see point 4): **Y1 expected closes = 10–21, midpoint ~15**, against a plan target of 30.

### 4. "The newsletter signup is itself the lead magnet" is a structural overclaim

Business-plan.md §9.3 ends with: "Newsletter signup is itself the lead magnet." This is asserted, not engineered. A newsletter signup converts to buyer through one of two paths:

- **Path A — long compounding:** Reader subscribes, reads weekly for 6–12 months, builds trust, eventually inbounds when a travel pain hits. Realistic conversion: 0.5–1.5% of subscribers become buyers per year by month 18. At 600 subs by month 12 (the plausible Y1 ceiling for a founder writing well from a cold start), that's 3–9 buyers/year — by **month 18 onward**, not in Y1.
- **Path B — direct CTA:** Newsletter contains explicit call-to-apply each issue. Realistic conversion: 0.2–0.8% per-issue reply rate to a CTA, where "reply" includes any inbound interest. At 600 subs × 0.4% × 100 issues (Desk Diary 2x/week × 50 weeks) = ~240 inbound interactions, of which ~10% are qualified ICP, of which ~25% close = **6 closes/year — but only by end of Y1 after the subscriber base is built**.

Both paths require the **subscriber base to exist first**. In Y1 the founder is building from zero. Realistic Y1 newsletter close contribution: **1–3 closes**, almost all in months 10–12.

The structural problem with the "signup is the lead magnet" framing is that the cold email's only call-to-action becomes "subscribe to my newsletter." Examining the cold-outreach scripts at `/home/user/Travel-agent/sales-kit/cold-outreach.md`, the actual CTA in every template is "worth a 15-minute call?" — not "subscribe." The signup-as-lead-magnet claim is descriptive of how the plan thinks about content, not prescriptive of what the cold emails actually ask for. The two channels are not integrated.

### 5. The 2x/week Desk Diary cadence is the most likely point of quiet failure

Business-plan.md §9.3 commits to Desk Diary 2x/week (Tue/Thu, ~500 words) + 43rd Parallel monthly (~2,500 words). Annualized: **104 Desk Diary posts × 500w + 12 long-form × 2,500w = 82,000 words of original content**.

Base rates from B2B founder content programs (v2.0 lens §D.1):
- ~70% post weekly for 6 weeks
- ~40% make it to week 12
- ~20% make it to week 26
- ~8–12% make it to week 52

Those base rates are for **weekly**, not 2x/week. The 2x/week cadence has roughly half those completion rates. Realistic probability the founder hits 104 Desk Diary posts in Y1: **4–8%**. Realistic probability he hits 52 (i.e. cadence reduced to weekly mid-year): **20–30%**. Most-likely outcome: the founder publishes 30–45 Desk Diary posts and 6–9 long-form pieces, with a 4–8 week silent gap in months 5–7 when the desk load spikes.

This is not catastrophic, but it materially changes the inbound contribution: at 30–45 posts and probably 350–500 subs by month 12 (instead of 800–1,200), Y1 newsletter close contribution drops from 1–3 to **0–2**.

### 6. The Y1 networking budget of $15–20K is mis-allocated against ROI

Business-plan.md §10.1 budget:
- Soho House Toronto: $3K
- EO Toronto: $3K
- 6 BIL dinners: $3–5K
- "Conference/event ROI: Soho House, EO, ACG, SBIA — sized at $15–20K/yr"

Adding ACG ($1.5–2K membership + $2–3K events) and SBIA ($2–3K membership + $2–4K events) brings the total to $14–20K. Cross-checked against the v2.0 lens table (§A), realistic close-per-channel for these:

| Channel | Cash cost | Founder hours | Realistic Y1 closes | All-in CAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Soho House Toronto | $3K | 80 | 0–1 (admission timing) | $11K+ if 1 close |
| EO Toronto | $3K | 120 | 1–2 | $5–7K per close |
| BIL dinners | $4K | 60 | 1–3 | $3–4K per close |
| ACG (US chapter) | $3.5K | 80 | 1–2 | $6–8K per close |
| SBIA (US) | $4.5K | 80 | 0–2 | $8K+ per close |

**Total: 3–10 closes for $18K cash + 420 hours.** Best case is reasonable; worst case is $18K and 420 founder hours for 3 closes at **$6,000 CAC**, which against Office Y1 econ of $7,844 is a sub-12-month payback. Against Light Y1 econ of $3,370, it's a 21-month payback that requires the member to renew through Y2.

The structural issue: ACG and SBIA are US conferences. Each requires the founder to fly from Toronto, expense hotels, take 3–4 days per event. The plan does not include travel cost in the $15–20K budget. Realistic all-in for 4 conference attendances/year = $15–20K cash **plus $6–10K of founder travel cost** = $21–30K. **Adjust the Y1 networking budget upward to $22–28K** or cut to 2 conferences.

### 7. The CAC payback math against $189 Light is still strained

Pulling the numbers together for Light specifically:

| Channel | All-in CAC | Light Y1 econ | Sub-only payback | Full-econ payback |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Warm referral | $1,500–2,500 | $3,370 | 8–13 months | <12 months |
| Newsletter (Y2+) | $2,000–3,000 (amortized) | $3,370 | 11–16 months | <12 months |
| Cold email (Model B) | $3,500–5,500 | $3,370 | 19–29 months | 13–20 months |
| EO/conference | $5,000–8,000 | $3,370 | 26–42 months | 18–28 months |

Cold-for-Light at Model B is still upside down on sub-only payback, and marginal on full-econ. The v2.0 lens's recommendation — reserve cold for Office and Society candidates — **still applies under Model B**. The plan does not adopt this segmentation. It runs cold against the entire ICP list (founders, VC partners, PE OPs, RIA principals, solo professionals). For solo professionals at sub-$100K T&E, Light is the only fit, the CAC payback is 20+ months, and Y1 churn risk is highest. **The cold list should be filtered to $250K+ T&E firms only.** That is roughly Office-tier-or-society territory.

### 8. The brother-in-law channel is the largest single unknown in the GTM

Business-plan.md §10.1 names "Brother-in-law network: business owner referral engine" with zero further specification. The v2.0 lens (§C.3) flagged this as ±5 Y1 closes of variance. Nothing in v3.0 has tightened it. As written, it is a load-bearing assumption with no documented backing. An investor or co-founder grilling the plan would push on this in the first ten minutes:

- Names? T&E bands? US vs. Canadian residency?
- Has BIL agreed to host 6 dinners or is that aspirational?
- Has BIL personally committed to introduce N people?
- Is BIL incentivized (revenue share, referral credit) or just helpful?

Until those four are answered, the BIL channel is plan-fiction, not GTM.

## What's wrong or missing

### Missing 1: No instrumented attribution loop

The plan describes channels but does not describe how the founder will measure which channel is paying. There is no CRM-grade lead-source tagging, no monthly channel review, no kill criterion. The v2.0 lens flagged this in §I.8 as the single biggest gap. **It is still the single biggest gap in v3.0.** Without measurement, the founder runs 8 channels at 60% effort for 12 months and ends Y1 not knowing which one earned the closes. That kills Y2 optimization.

Specific fix: build a flat spreadsheet from week 1 with columns for source (warm/cold/Journal/EO/Soho/dinner/press/referral), date in, date qualified, date closed, ARR, hours invested, cash spent. Update weekly. At month 6, kill the bottom quartile. At month 12, double down on the top quartile.

### Missing 2: No discovery-call calendar discipline

The plan describes lead volume but does not budget the conversion-step bottleneck: discovery calls. At realistic Y1 funnel rates, 15–25 closes require 100–200 qualified discovery calls. At the founder's available hours after primary income + desk + newsletter, the realistic discovery-call capacity is **3–5 per week × 45 weeks = 135–225 calls**. That is roughly aligned to the funnel, but only if discovery-call slots are protected on the calendar like board meetings. The plan does not specify this.

The v2.0 lens (§I.10) recommended capping discovery calls at 4/week. That is the right number for v3.0 too. **Add it to the plan.**

### Missing 3: No referrer compensation mechanism

Business-plan.md §10.3 says "Warm conversion runs 50–70% vs cold's 1–3%. Each warm intro is worth roughly 50 cold emails of pipeline value." But there is no referrer mechanism: no credit per intro, no commission for closed referrals, no published referrer-of-the-year ritual. Without a mechanism, warm intros happen only from natural enthusiasm, which is volatile. With a mechanism, warm intros become a system.

Specific fix: by month 6, publish a referrer policy. Recommended: 15% of first-year subscription revenue as referrer credit, payable as a Latitude membership-extension credit or a $500 voucher to a Virtuoso preferred property. Both keep the relationship inside the channel.

### Missing 4: No fallback if Renmac changes

Business-plan.md §14.1 calls Renmac "primary income that fully covers personal burn" and Risk #10 acknowledges it but does not stress-test. If Renmac ends or reduces in months 6–18 — a non-trivial possibility for any single-employer income — the founder has to either accelerate Latitude revenue or take outside capital. The plan does not specify the Latitude revenue threshold at which the founder could survive on Latitude alone, nor what the acceleration playbook is. **This is a GTM-adjacent gap because the answer to "what if Renmac changes?" is usually "spend more on GTM," which requires capital the plan says is zero.**

### Missing 5: No Y1 monthly milestone calendar

Business-plan.md §6 gives annual revenue. §10 gives annual GTM motion. There is no month-by-month operating timeline. When does the warm anchor close? When does the first cold close land? When does the newsletter hit 500 subs? When does EO Toronto admission happen? When does PHT VA start? Without these monthly milestones, the founder cannot tell in June whether he is on plan or off it.

## Recommendations (prioritized, executable)

### 1. Revise the Y1 target to 12–18 members (8–12 Light + 4–6 Office), ~$55–85K revenue

The plan's 30-member, $115K target is the optimistic case. The realistic case is 12–18 members. Push the 30-member milestone to end of Y2. This is the single most important change. It cascades into hiring, capital, and stress-testing. **The honest planning case is Y1 = $55–85K, Y2 = $130–170K, Y5 = $795K (per the short-form Lens 2 eval).**

### 2. Cut cold volume to 40–50/day and hire a list-build VA at month 3

The 100/day target is undeliverable solo without sacrificing desk SLA or newsletter cadence. 40–50/day is sustainable. A part-time VA in Manila or Cape Town at $800–1,200/month doing list-building and Apollo enrichment frees the founder's hours for the personalization layer (which doesn't transfer to a VA). Annualized cost: $10–14K. Reallocate from the LinkedIn ad budget the plan already correctly excluded plus a $4K trim from the conference budget.

### 3. Filter the cold list to $250K+ T&E firms only

Cold acquisition of Light at Model B has a 19–29 month sub-only payback. That is not viable. Reserve cold entirely for prospects whose T&E qualifies them for Office or Society. Volume drops, hit rate rises, ACV doubles. **Net cold expected closes Y1: 4–7 Office, 0 Light. Light flows from warm, referral, and inbound.**

### 4. Cut Desk Diary cadence to 1x/week and 43rd Parallel quarterly for Y1

The 2x/week + monthly cadence has a 4–8% probability of completion. The 1x/week + quarterly cadence has a 25–35% probability of completion. The latter still produces 52 posts/year and meaningful subscriber growth. **The shorter cadence buys back roughly 300–400 founder hours/year**, which is most of the deficit identified in "what's questionable" point 1.

### 5. Specify the brother-in-law network on paper, by name, before Y1 launches

This is the single largest unscoped lever. Make BIL list 15 names with T&E band and US-vs-Canadian residency. If the list cannot support 8–12 useful US-resident introductions, the BIL channel is worth 1–2 closes, not 4. The plan needs that calibrated number to set the rest of the funnel.

### 6. Defer Soho House to Y2; protect EO Toronto for Y1

Soho House's admission timing (3–9 months) and direct-conversion yield (0–2 closes) make it a Y1 vanity spend. Apply but don't budget. Reallocate the $3K to one extra US conference (Future Proof or Schwab IMPACT in the RIA segment) where direct-conversion yield is 2–4 closes per event.

### 7. Build a flat-spreadsheet attribution log from week 1

Columns: source, lead name, date in, date qualified, date closed, ARR, founder hours, cash spent. Update Friday afternoons. At month 6 and month 12, run a channel review. Kill bottom quartile, double top quartile. This is the difference between GTM-as-strategy and GTM-as-operation. Without it, Y2 is a guess.

### 8. Publish a referrer mechanism by month 6

15% of first-year subscription as referrer credit (paid as membership extension or as a $500 voucher to a member's preferred property). Make it specific. Make it claimable in one form. Publish it on the website. **This is the difference between warm-as-channel and warm-as-luck.**

### 9. Cap discovery calls at 4/week on the founder's calendar

Block them as a single 3-hour window two days a week. Outside that window, decline. This forces qualification at the top of the funnel and protects desk + newsletter + primary income hours. Yields ~180 discovery calls/year, which at a realistic 12–18% close rate produces 22–32 closes — enough for the revised target with margin.

### 10. Add a one-page month-by-month Y1 operating timeline to the plan

Months 1–12, one row each, columns: GTM milestone (e.g. cold campaign go-live), product milestone (e.g. member-portal v1), people milestone (e.g. list VA start), revenue target (cumulative ARR), key risk. This is a 4-hour exercise that turns the strategy into an operation. Without it, the founder will not know in May whether he is on plan.

## Bottom-line GTM verdict

Model B Unlimited fixed the most expensive friction in the funnel (the unanswerable all-in question) and lifted per-member economics enough that cold email for Office is now a defensible channel. The cold-for-Light arithmetic is still strained but no longer absurd.
