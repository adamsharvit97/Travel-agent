# Latitude 43 v3.0 — Market Sizing and TAM Honesty

**Lens 2 of 10. Author: Pre-revenue investor, sharpened pencil.**
**Plan version evaluated: v3.0 (Model B Unlimited, $189 Light / $349 Office / $12K–$30K Society)**
**Ground truth: `business-plan.md` v3.0, `/tmp/v2-facts.md`, `/home/user/Travel-agent/evaluation/02-market-sizing.md`, research/sub-500k-smb-buyer.md, research/businesspeople-buyer-research.md, research/under-researched-personas.md**

---

## Verdict — Grade B-

The v3.0 pivot from $99/$249 to $189/$349 cuts the addressable population materially. The v2.0 lens that earned a B on the same TAM question was working against a $99 entry point that lived inside the personal-decision zone ($50–$200/mo per `businesspeople-buyer-research.md` §8c). The v3.0 entry sits at $189 — still inside the zone but at its upper edge, and the Office tier at $349 now sits squarely in the justification zone where the founder will defend the line to a spouse or partner before signing. The honest US addressable market for the v3.0 product is roughly **$50–80M of capturable revenue** against a **5,500–7,000 buyable-unit SAM** — meaningfully smaller than the v2.0 9,200-unit estimate. The Y5 base case of 120 members (60 Light / 40 Office / 20 Society) requires **1.8–2.2% penetration of that tighter SAM**, which is at the upper edge of plausible for a solo Toronto-based operator with no track record. Society at 12–15 globally is correctly capacity-bound but understated as a revenue lever: the v3.0 plan models 20 Society seats by Y5, not 12–15. The plan never reconciles "capped at 12–15" in §2.1 with "20" in §6. That contradiction is the single biggest sizing error in the document.

## 1. What's right (about 1,300 words)

### 1.1 The pricing pivot honestly accepts the v2.0 market-sizing pressure test

The v2.0 lens (which I wrote) concluded that the plan was systematically inflating its TAM by citing $1.57T global and "$625B unmanaged SMB" framing, when the honest ceiling was ~$77B of US-only unmanaged SMB and a buyable revenue SAM closer to **$50M**. The v3.0 plan removes the headline TAM-inflation language entirely. There is no mention of $1.57T, no "$625B unmanaged pool," no "less than one-tenth of one percent" framing. The competitive section (§13) is now grounded in named competitors and their structural failures rather than abstract market totals. This is a real improvement and reflects the v2.0 critique landing.

### 1.2 The buyer's value math (§4) is internally honest at the $189/$349 price points

The plan computes the realized value of an Office membership at **$16,000–$46,000/year** against a $4,188 annual fee — a 4× to 11× value-to-price ratio. The components are mostly grounded: Virtuoso/FSPP/STARS benefits on 20 hotel nights at preferred properties run $150–$400/night of trade-able value, which checks against published Virtuoso preferred-partner economics. The trip design value at $300–$500/trip × 22 trips/year is conservative against the §2.4 non-member rate card ($250–$1,875 base + variable). The math is doing what it should: showing that the offering is "stupid to say no to" *before* the relationship layer is priced in. The v2.0 plan tried to do this but the headline numbers were polluted by the unlimited-design framing not being committed. Now the price is the price, and the math survives a first-pass audit.

### 1.3 The five-segment ICP is correctly bounded

The §3 segments — Series A–C founders, VC/PE partners at sub-$1.5B funds, lower-mid PE operating partners, RIA principals at sub-$1B AUM, and solo professionals — are the right five for this product. Each segment is defined by T&E band ($60K–$500K), trip cadence (20–60 nights/year), and a specific failure-mode the buyer can name. The v2.0 lens found these five plausibly count to ~43,000 buyable units across the US after pressure-testing. The v3.0 plan does not re-state segment counts in the document, which was probably a deliberate omission — the v2.0 plan got beaten up for over-stating, and v3.0 simply doesn't restate the count. That is defensible, but it dodges the question rather than answers it. I will compute the v3.0-adjusted counts in §2 below.

### 1.4 Society capacity-bound at 12–15 globally is the right ceiling — if 12–15 is the ceiling

§2.1 caps Society at 12–15 members globally. This is consistent with founder capacity at the 15-minute SLA, dedicated principal coverage, and quarterly in-person Year-in-Review. Knightsbridge Circle, the closest London analog at $50K+/yr, has fewer than 100 members globally after 12+ years and is staffed by a team of relationship managers; a 12–15 cap for a solo Toronto operator + one VA + occasional backup principal is realistic. The market-depth pressure-test from v2.0 still holds: US family offices and UHNW principals willing to pay $14K–$30K/yr for a travel-only relationship number roughly **300–500**. Capturing 12–15 of that pool is **3–5% segment share** — achievable but slow-building, and the plan correctly defers Society launch to H2 2027.

The Society persona is also where the under-researched personas research is most relevant: the document at `/home/user/Travel-agent/research/under-researched-personas.md` identifies CRE brokers at boutique firms, boutique M&A bankers, MDRT-tier insurance brokers, and specialty physicians in private practice as five highest-LTV under-researched personas with $40K+/yr T&E and no category-defining advisory. None of these are in the ICP. They are not in Society either. If Society is the Y5 revenue driver — and at $440K of $870K total it is — the founder should be hunting these five Tier-1 under-researched segments for Society leads, not the canonical founder/VC cohort which is over-targeted.

### 1.5 Geographic concentration is correctly inferred even without being stated

The v3.0 plan does not state geographic concentration explicitly. But the GTM section (§10) implicitly accepts the v2.0 finding that six metros — NYC, SF, Boston, Chicago, LA, Miami — hold roughly two-thirds of the SAM. The cold-email program at 100/day to PitchBook-sourced lists will index heavily on these metros by default. The Toronto network plan correctly avoids the "skip Altea, Equinox, Toronto Club / Granite / York in Y1" trap that would have wasted founder time on Canadian-residents-as-prospects (a TICO Path A violation). The implicit geography is correct; explicit geography in the plan would strengthen the GTM math and force the founder to acknowledge the Pacific Time zone penalty on ~25% of the SAM.

### 1.6 Competitor member-count benchmarking is realistic

The v3.0 plan now names competitors and explicitly notes member-count and operating constraints (Indagare M-F 9-6 ET, SmartFlyer $500 design fee floor, etc.). Comparing v3.0's Y5 target of 100 Light+Office + 20 Society = **120 members** against the named competitors:

- **Indagare:** undisclosed but back-of-envelope from $10.7M raised, 128 employees, and the $2,850 Custom Planner tier suggests **~2,000–4,000 paid Custom Planner members + 5,000–15,000 $395 Self Planner subscribers**. They've had ~17 years and a $10M+ capital base.
- **SmartFlyer:** 250+ advisors, $1B GBV across the network. The *corporate* division within SmartFlyer's book is roughly 10% — **~150–250 corporate accounts across 250 advisors**, less than one corporate account per advisor.
- **Bell & Bly Travel:** 7 employees, undisclosed member count, per-trip fee model. Reasonable estimate: **150–300 active clients, 30–50 repeat high-value at the $40K+/yr book**.
- **Brownell Corporate:** Brownell as a host has ~200 advisors; the corporate-leaning subset is small, possibly under 30 advisors and a few hundred corporate accounts in aggregate.

Latitude 43's Y5 target of 120 members is **~30× smaller than Indagare's Custom Planner book**, **~5× smaller than SmartFlyer's corporate slice**, and **~3× the size of Bell & Bly's repeat high-value book**. As an absolute floor, this is a defensible target for a solo founder with no track record by Y5. The v3.0 plan's competitive framing — "every named competitor either has business hours, a design fee, both, or is a large TMC with no relationship layer" — is structurally correct, and the Bell & Bly comparison is the only direct overlap that matters.

### 1.7 The Society dollar-weighting is correctly modeled

§6 shows that 20 Society seats at average $14K subscription + $13K–$33K of commission and event design contribute **~$420K of Y5 subscription + ~$540K of commission/design = ~$960K against the $870K total**. The arithmetic clearly hides a base-case rounding (the total is closer to $1M with Society contributing half, not $870K with Society at half), but the directional finding is correct: **Society is the disproportionate revenue driver at maturity**, and the bear case (5 Society seats) cuts Y5 revenue by roughly $250K-$300K. The market-sizing question for Society is whether 12–15 (or 20) is achievable; the v2.0 finding holds that 300–500 plausible US Society buyers exist, so even 20 is 4–7% segment share — achievable but contingent on referrals from members and on at least one anchor relationship by Y3.

### 1.8 The non-member design rate card creates a real qualifying funnel

§2.4 prices non-member design work from $500 floor to $10K cap, with a stated conversion-to-Light expectation of 30–40% within 12 months. This is a legitimate top-of-funnel mechanism that did not exist as cleanly in v2.0. A buyer who pays $500–$2,000 for a single trip is self-qualifying out of the noise band — they are pre-validated as willing-to-pay-for-help, which is the cultural-fit coefficient that does the most damage to my SAM math. If the conversion rate holds at 30–40%, this is worth roughly 8–12 incremental Light closes/year against the cold-funnel volume.

## 2. What's questionable (about 1,300 words)

### 2.1 The $189 price floor effect: who gets filtered out

Moving the floor from $99 to $189 changes the addressable population non-trivially. The mental-model anchor research in `sub-500k-smb-buyer.md` §5.2 is explicit: $99 sits inside the impulse-buy band; $249 sits at the top of the "obvious SaaS" band; the danger band starts at $300 and the very-dangerous band at $500. **The v3.0 Light price of $189 is in the obvious-SaaS band, not the impulse-buy band.** The shift removes the "I sign up tonight because it's a Slack seat" reflex documented across the bootstrapper, solo-professional, and sub-$500K founder archetypes.

Quantitative impact on segment populations:

| Segment | v2.0 buyable units | v3.0 filter (Light at $189 vs $99) | v3.0 buyable units |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Founders, seed–Series B | 11,700 | ~20–25% bootstrapped sub-$80K T&E filter out | ~9,000 |
| VC partners, sub-$2B | 5,500 | <5% filter; price-insensitive | ~5,200 |
| PE deal teams (firms) | 1,800 | <5% filter | ~1,750 |
| RIA principals $100M–$1B | 6,500 | ~15% filter at lower-AUM end | ~5,500 |
| Solo professionals | 17,500 | ~30–35% filter (sub-$80K T&E and price-sensitive) | ~11,500 |
| **TOTAL** | **43,000** | | **~33,000** |

The corrected v3.0 addressable population is **~33,000 buyable units in the US** — roughly **23% smaller** than the v2.0 figure. The cuts concentrate in the two segments that fed the cold funnel hardest (bootstrapped founders and solo professionals) and exit the two that fed it least (VC and PE, where $189 vs $99 is rounding error).

Applying the same cultural-fit and principal-buyer gates from v2.0 §B.1, the **honest v3.0 SAM is approximately 7,000 buyable units**, down from 9,200 — a 24% reduction. That is still a real number, and the Y5 base case of 120 members is **1.7% penetration** instead of 1.3%. Penetration math has gotten harder, not easier.

### 2.2 The Office tier at $349 sits in the justification zone, not the obvious-SaaS zone

The thresholds in `businesspeople-buyer-research.md` §8c are explicit:

| Threshold | Above it... |
|---|---|
| <$50/mo | Personal expense, no justification |
| $50–$200/mo | Personal-decision zone |
| **$200–$500/mo** | **Justification zone — defended to spouse or business partner, needs explicit ROI line** |
| $500–$1,500/mo | CFO/partner zone |
| >$1,500/mo | Board-line zone |

**Office at $349/mo sits in the justification zone.** The plan acknowledges this implicitly in §7.4 lesson 28 ("when a buyer balks at $349, walk them through their last anniversary trip and price it at agency rates"), but the structural implication is not modeled: Office sales require a fundamentally different sales motion than Light sales. Light at $189 is a self-serve sign-up after a 30-minute call. Office at $349 needs a documented ROI conversation, a spouse-aligned rationale, and (per the plan's own observation) often an EA-as-buyer mechanic. The cycle time for Office is 2–4× longer than for Light. The plan's funnel math treats both as a single "cold close" unit.

The implication for Y5 mix: if the natural conversion is 70% Light / 30% Office under price friction, not 60% / 40% as the plan models, Y5 mix becomes **84 Light / 36 Office / 20 Society = ~$820K**, $50K under base case. If price-friction pushes mix to 75 / 25, the bear case becomes more likely than the base case.

### 2.3 The penetration math at 20 / 60 / 200 / 1000 members

Re-running the penetration arithmetic against the v3.0-adjusted SAM of **~7,000 buyable units** plus **300–500 Society-plausible UHNW principals**:

| Member count | Light + Office penetration | Society penetration | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 20 (Y1 base) | 0.28% of 7,000 | 0% | Trivial penetration; cold-funnel math is the constraint, not market depth |
| 60 (Y2–Y3) | 0.86% | ~1% | Plausible; matches the early-Indagare cohort pace |
| 100 (Y4 Light+Office) | 1.43% | 2.4% | Stretches; requires Journal compounding by month 24 |
| 200 (bull-case Y5+) | 2.86% | 6–10% | Very high segment share for solo operator; capacity-constrained |
| 1,000 (acquisition-thesis Y10) | 14.3% | Capped at 12–15 | Implausible without 4–8 advisor team; this is not the v3.0 business |

The Y5 base case (120 total) is **~1.7% penetration of buyable Light+Office population + ~4–7% of Society-plausible UHNW**. The bull case (170 total: 80 Light / 60 Office / 30 Society) is **~2.4% / ~6–10%**. The bear case (95 total) is **~1.4% / ~1%**.

Two observations the plan does not surface:

1. **The penetration percentages are not the binding constraint at any of these target counts.** What binds is the cold-funnel arithmetic (10 closes per 5,000 outreaches at v3.0 prices, possibly worse), the founder-hour CAC, and the relationship-density of the warm network. The plan's own §10 implicitly accepts this when it allocates Y1 effort to warm > cold by volume of effort.

2. **The Society penetration math is the most aggressive assumption in the plan.** 20 Society seats against a market depth of 300–500 plausible buyers is **4–7% share**. Knightsbridge Circle achieves <2% of its plausible market after 12 years. The plan needs either a referral mechanic that delivers 5+ Society leads/year from existing members (no such referral mechanic is documented) or an admission that 20 is the bull case and 8–12 is the base.

### 2.4 The 12–15 Society cap vs. the 20 Y5 target contradiction

**§2.1 caps Latitude Society at 12–15 globally. §6 models 20 Society seats by Y5.** This is an unresolved internal contradiction. Either:

- The plan intends 12–15 as a permanent ceiling and Y5 of 20 is impossible without lifting it
- The plan intends 12–15 as a Y1–Y2 cap with a Y4+ raise to 20 (which is the most likely intent but is not stated)
- The plan is using 12–15 as a positioning anchor for scarcity but modeling 20 as the real target

If the cap is genuinely 12–15, the Y5 base case revenue drops by roughly **$140K–$200K** (the 5–8 Society seats above the cap, each contributing $20K–$35K net). The bear case (5 Society seats) is structurally inside the cap; the base and bull cases require lifting the cap. The plan needs to pick a number and commit. **Recommended language: "Cap 15 in Y1–Y3, lift to 25 by Y5 conditional on backup-principal staffing."**

### 2.5 The buyer-acceptance erosion from a Canadian operator at the $349 price point

The v2.0 lens concluded that ~85% of the SAM is geographically tolerant of a Toronto-based operator, with friction concentrated in PE/family-office segments where it runs to ~15–25%. The v3.0 pricing changes this calculation in one direction: at $349/mo for Office, the buyer is *more* sensitive to operator credibility than at $249/mo, because the justification zone forces the buyer to defend the spend. The plan's mitigations (Fora US-registered, W-8BEN-E filed, no US permanent establishment) are correct legally, but commercially the answer the buyer gives to their CFO is "we're using a Canadian travel agent because..." — and that sentence has to end with something better than "they're cheaper than the alternative" because they aren't.

The realistic adjustment: drop the geographic-tolerance coefficient on Office and Society to **~80% from ~85%**, which trims the practical SAM by another ~3–5% to **~6,700 buyable units**. Y5 penetration becomes **1.8%** at base case, still plausible but tighter.

### 2.6 The Y5 member count of 60 / 40 / 20 against the addressable

Against a v3.0-adjusted **~6,700 buyable units + ~400 Society-plausible**, the Y5 base case (60 Light / 40 Office / 20 Society):

- 100 Light+Office / 6,700 = **1.5% penetration**
- 20 Society / 400 = **5.0% penetration**

The Light+Office penetration is **above the v2.0 finding of 1.3%** despite a smaller base, because the v3.0 Y5 mix is 100 Light+Office instead of 100. This is not a number-go-up problem; it is honest math against a tighter denominator.

**Comparable benchmarks for 1.5% segment penetration after 5 years for a solo founder with no prior track record:** there is no public dataset of boutique-advisor SAM penetration at this stage. Sollis Health hit 1–2% of NYC concierge medicine within 5 years but with $40M+ of capital. Indagare took 17 years to its current penetration with $10.7M raised. Bell & Bly is a closer analog at 7-person scale and has not disclosed penetration but is plausibly at 0.5–1% of corporate-leaning leisure travel after 8+ years.

A Y5 of 1.5% segment penetration as a solo Toronto operator with zero outside capital is at the **upper edge of plausible**, not in the middle. The bear case (60 Light / 30 Office / 5 Society) at **0.9% / 1.3%** is closer to the historical comparable.

### 2.7 The "Society as separate market" framing is correct but the count is wrong

The plan treats Society as effectively a separate market (different pricing, different SLA, different cap), and this is structurally correct. UHNW family-layer travel buyers are not the same buyers as VC partners or RIA principals. The US market depth — single-family offices in the $50M–$250M AUM band number ~3,000–4,000 per Campden Wealth, of which 300–500 plausibly pay for a travel-only relationship at $14K–$30K/yr — is the right denominator.

But **the cap of 12–15 globally is wrong if Society is genuinely a separate market**. A separate market with 300–500 plausible buyers can support 30–50 members at the natural founder-capacity limit of 2 senior relationship-managers. A 12–15 cap reflects single-founder capacity, not market depth. The plan should either:

1. Commit to single-founder Society and cap at 12–15 permanently, accepting Y5 revenue of ~$700K base case, or
2. Plan for a second Society principal hire in Y4 and raise the cap to 25–30, accepting incremental $80K/yr salary cost for $300–$400K/yr revenue lift.

The plan currently does neither. It implies (2) by modeling 20 Y5 Society but commits to (1) in §2.1.

## 3. What's wrong or missing (about 700 words)

### 3.1 No US-only addressable market revenue figure is stated in the plan

The v2.0 lens spent significant effort establishing that the honest US addressable market for the v2.0 product was **~$50M of annual revenue if 100% of the SAM were captured**. The v3.0 plan removes the bad TAM claims but does not replace them with the honest dollar SAM. The plan that should be pitched is: *"US capturable annual revenue at full SAM penetration is ~$60–80M. We target 1.5% of that by Y5 = $1M."* That number is missing.

Recomputing for v3.0 pricing and a 6,700-unit SAM:

- 60% Light × $189 × 12 = $1,361 subscription ARPU
- 30% Office × $349 × 12 = $1,256 (× 0.30 mix-weighted: $1,256 share)
- 10% Society × $14K = $1,400 mix-weighted share
- Blended subscription ARPU: ~$3,200/year/account

Add commission and design net (~$2,000–4,000/account/year mix-weighted): **blended total revenue per account ≈ $5,500–6,000**.

**Honest v3.0 dollar SAM = 6,700 × $5,750 ≈ $39M annual revenue at 100% SAM penetration + ~$5M from Society capture = ~$44M total.**

This is the number that should anchor any future pitch. It is lower than the v2.0 $50M because the price floor pushes ~25% of the addressable population out, even though per-account revenue is higher. The Y5 base case of $870K is **2.0% of $44M** — a credible share for a solo founder with no track record, but no longer "<1%."

### 3.2 No segment prioritization

The v2.0 lens found the strongest white-space wedge in **VC partners sub-$2B + RIA principals + Series A/B founders with $200K+ T&E**. The v3.0 plan does not prioritize segments. §3 lists five segments equally, §7 treats them with a uniform sales motion. This is probably wrong for the v3.0 product specifically, because:

- VC partners are **less price-sensitive than founders** at the $189/$349 boundary — the Light-vs-Office shift hurts founders, not VCs.
- RIA principals have **the longest sales cycle and the highest renewal stickiness** — they should be Y2+ priorities, not Y1.
- Solo professionals at sub-$80K T&E are **filtered out by the $189 floor** — they were borderline at $99 and should be deprioritized at v3.0 pricing.

The honest segment priority for v3.0 is: **(1) VC/PE partners sub-$1.5B (cleanest white space, least price-sensitive); (2) Series A–C founders with $200K+ T&E (highest peer-referral density); (3) RIA principals (Y2+ focus, conference-driven); (4) PE operating partners (Y3+ focus, sales-cycle-intensive); (5) solo professionals (deprioritize, refer in via non-member design)**.

### 3.3 The under-researched personas are missing entirely

The under-researched-personas research (1,031 lines) identifies five Tier-1 high-LTV segments — CRE brokers at boutique firms, boutique M&A bankers, MDRT-tier insurance brokers, specialty physicians in private practice, boutique CPA partners with M&A exposure — with $40K–$120K T&E each, no category-defining advisory, and discoverable conference circuits (ICSC Las Vegas, NAIOP CRE.Converge, AM&AA conferences). **None of these appear in the v3.0 plan.** Each is roughly the same size as one of the canonical five segments (CRE brokers alone are ~15,000 active producers in the top-quartile income band). Even adding two of them (CRE brokers, boutique M&A bankers) at half the buyable-unit count of the canonical five would lift the v3.0 SAM by ~3,000–4,000 units and the dollar SAM by ~$15–20M. The plan ignores this expansion lane.

### 3.4 The Y5 staffing-vs-account-count contradiction is unresolved

§8 commits to a 60-minute SLA, 24/7, with founder coverage 7am–11pm ET, PHT VA on overnight, backup principal pre-staged at Fora. §6 models 100 Light+Office + 20 Society = 120 accounts by Y5. The v2.0 plan's positioning-master cap of "15 accounts per advisor" implies a 4-advisor floor by Y5; the v3.0 plan does not staff to that level. Either the cap-per-advisor is wrong (and 30–40 accounts per advisor is sustainable with the included-design model), or the staffing is too thin (and the SLA will break by Y3). The plan needs a clear statement: at the v3.0 unlimited-design model, what is the realistic per-advisor account cap and what is the Y5 headcount? Until that is resolved, the Y5 revenue number is upper-bounded by labor-margin reality, not by market depth.

### 3.5 The $50–80M v2.0 US addressable claim — what it should become in v3.0

The v2.0 figure of $50–80M was a wide band reflecting uncertainty in cultural-fit coefficients. The v3.0 honest equivalent is **$40–60M**, narrower because the $189 price floor sharpens the segment filters and the unlimited-design model raises per-account ARPU. The Y5 target of $870K is **1.5–2.2% of this band**, which is plausible but no longer "obviously conservative" as it was at v2.0 pricing.

## 4. Recommendations (prioritized, executable)

1. **Resolve the 12–15 vs. 20 Society contradiction in §2.1 and §6 within 30 days.** Pick one number and commit. Recommended: cap at 15 in Y1–Y3, plan a second Society principal hire by Q4 2028, raise cap to 25 by Y5 conditional on the hire landing. Re-model the Y5 base case with 15 Society = ~$700K base.

2. **Add the honest US dollar SAM to §13 of the plan.** Recommended language: "US capturable annual revenue at full SAM penetration is ~$40–60M. Y5 base case targets 1.7% segment penetration ($870K). Y5 bear case at 0.9% penetration ($540K) is the planning case." This replaces the absence of any TAM figure with a defensible one.

3. **Prioritize segments explicitly in §3.** Reorder: VC partners (#1), Series A–C founders with $200K+ T&E (#2), RIA principals (#3), PE operating partners (#4), solo professionals (#5 / deprioritize / channel through non-member design funnel only). Commit specific Y1 outreach allocation: 50% VC, 30% founders, 15% RIA, 5% other.

4. **Add two under-researched segments as Y2–Y3 expansion lanes:** CRE brokers at boutique firms (target ICSC Las Vegas May 2026) and boutique M&A bankers (target AM&AA conferences). Each lifts SAM by ~1,500–2,000 buyable units and provides a different conference circuit than the canonical five segments.

5. **Model the Office-vs-Light cycle-time differential in the funnel math.** Office at $349 sits in the justification zone and requires ~2–4× the cycle time of Light at $189. The Y5 mix of 60/40 implies a separate Office acquisition motion that the plan does not document. Either build one (EA-as-buyer track, CFO ROI deck) or rebase Y5 mix to 70 / 30 / 20.

6. **Stress-test Y3 with Journal-fails-to-compound scenario.** If the Journal has <1,500 LinkedIn followers at month 24, Y3 net adds drop to 15–20 instead of 30, and Y5 base case follows by 30% downward. The plan does not currently include this stress.

7. **Resolve the Y5 staffing-vs-account-count contradiction in §8.** State the per-advisor cap under the unlimited-design model (recommendation: 35 active members per senior advisor + 1 VA). At 120 Y5 accounts that implies 3 senior advisors + 1 VA, not the founder + 1 VA + 1 associate the plan implies.

8. **Add the geographic-tolerance coefficient to the planning model.** At v3.0 pricing, ~80% of SAM is geographically tolerant of a Toronto-based operator (down from ~85% at v2.0 pricing). Apply this to all funnel math; the plan currently treats geographic friction as zero.

9. **Re-model the $189 price-floor filter on the bootstrapped founder and solo professional segments.** Show the segment-level filter loss in §3 (recommendation: ~25% loss in bootstrapped founders, ~30% in solo professionals). This makes the v3.0 SAM honest and gives the founder a precise estimate of who the price pivot filtered out.

10. **Cap the absolute Y5 ambition at 150 members under the current solo+VA+associate operating model.** 120 is plausible; 200+ requires a fundamentally different team structure and that is outside the lifestyle-business framing of the plan. State this constraint explicitly in §14.2.
