# Adjacent Competition — Card Concierges and Founder/Operator Perks

A forensic teardown of every "free" concierge and travel perk our buyers already have, what those services actually deliver vs. what they promise, and how Latitude 43 positions structurally against them.

**Date:** May 2026
**Audience:** Founder, GTM team, sales conversations
**Use case:** Objection handling, positioning, sales enablement

---

## Executive Summary

Every premium card our prospects carry ships with a "concierge." On the surface, this looks like a serious objection: why pay Latitude 43 a retainer when Amex/Chase/Capital One throws in concierge service "free" with the annual fee?

The forensic answer is that the word "concierge" in the credit card context has been quietly hollowed out over the last five years. What was once a moderately white-glove service — Amex Platinum email concierge with continuity, restaurant booking authority, real follow-through — has been reduced to a phone-only call center handling transactional tasks (restaurant lookup, ticket purchase, simple travel research) with multi-day response times and hour-plus hold queues.

Three structural facts make this true:

1. **Two vendors run almost the entire industry.** Ten Lifestyle Group (UK-listed, LON:TENG) operates concierge for Visa Infinite, Mastercard World Elite, and Mastercard Black globally. Aspire Lifestyles (owned by International SOS) operates concierge for Citi Prestige and many regional issuers. Both are call-center businesses with shift-rotated agents and no continuity model. ([Ten wins Visa contract](https://tenlifestylegroup.com/2018/02/15/ten-wins-contract-with-visa/), [Doctor of Credit on Citi switching to Aspire](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/citi-changes-concierge-provider-to/))

2. **Amex has been cutting concierge scope since 2021.** Email concierge eliminated August 2021. Off-list restaurant reservations eliminated July 2022. The card concierge has been quietly migrated into a Resy-funneled, Global Dining Collection-only model. ([Head for Points forum](https://www.headforpoints.com/forums/topic/concierge-will-no-longer-reserve-restaurants/), [God Save the Points](https://www.godsavethepoints.com/amex-platinum-concierge-loses-email/))

3. **Founder/operator perks (Brex, Ramp, Mercury, Stripe Atlas, YC, AngelList, FoundersCard) deliver software discounts and booking-engine convenience, not human travel judgment.** Brex Travel and Ramp Travel are self-service OBTs with Priceline-tier support backstops. They do not solve IROPs, multi-leg complexity, or last-mile relationships at the property.

The result: every adjacent option is good at one thing — a basic restaurant reservation, an event ticket, a discounted hotel night, a simple corporate booking. **None of them solve the moments where travel actually breaks: the cancelled flight at 11pm, the partner-track recruit who arrives at a hotel with no reservation, the closing-week trip that needs to flex twice in three days, the suite request at a sold-out property.**

That is the gap Latitude 43 owns.

## Part 1: Card Concierges — Forensic Breakdown

### 1. American Express Centurion Concierge

**Cost:** $10,000 annual fee + $5,000 one-time initiation. Invitation-only; anecdotal spend threshold $350,000–$500,000/year on existing Amex cards. ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/credit-cards/reviews/amex-centurion-black-card/), [The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/amex-centurion-black-card/))

**What's promised:** A "dedicated" concierge. The marketing implies a named human who learns your preferences over time.

**What it actually delivers (the optimistic case):** Brian Kelly (TPG founder) reports that his concierge "Ray" knows his seat preferences, has handled hotel coordination during the Bali Mount Agung eruption, secured front-row Celine Dion tickets, and "saves me more than $20,000 a year." This is the most-cited positive testimonial in the entire ecosystem. ([TPG](https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/amex-centurion-black-card/))

**What it actually delivers (the typical case):** From [Live and Let's Fly](https://liveandletsfly.com/american-express-centurion-card-value/), pulling cardholder reports:

> "The concierge service isn't great. Save for the most basic requests... they tend not to be able to pull off the most basic of tasks."

> "I recently took a 30 min cab across Warsaw for a power adapter that was not in stock."

> "Now, since they've gone to this POD service where multiple clearly uneducated people are handling your requests, the service has gone from a 10 to a -10."

> "[The concierge admitted they] cannot get you reservations at exclusive restaurants if RESY or open table has no availability."

> "I have to constantly tell whomever I speak to that the Centurion perks at Centurion properties are different than FHR perks."

**The forensic finding:** Centurion concierge has moved from a true 1:1 dedicated model to a "POD" model — a small team of agents collectively handling your account. Continuity is partial at best. The named "Ray" experience is real but is the exception for high-profile, high-engagement cardholders (the Brian Kellys), not the norm. Cardholders paying $15K/year report they spend time *educating their own concierge* about Amex's own benefit structure.

**Where it fails:**
- Dispute resolution is handled by a separate team, not the concierge — undermining the core "one human handles everything" promise
- Sold-out reservations: cannot conjure inventory; deference to Resy/OpenTable availability
- Knowledge depth on Centurion's own benefits stack
- Continuity post-2022 POD restructure

Ten is publicly traded; you can read their margin structure. There is no realistic universe in which a $5–$15 per-cardholder annual concierge allocation funds a named, dedicated, real-time human travel advisor.

## Part 3: The Fundamental Gap

### What every "free" concierge actually delivers

Strip away the marketing and you get four things:

1. **Restaurant reservations** (within constraints — Resy availability, partner-only lists for Amex, no-day-of restrictions for Visa Infinite)
2. **Event ticket lookup and purchase** (no special access; the concierge buys from the same StubHub/Ticketmaster you can)
3. **Basic gift research and sending** (flower delivery, courier coordination)
4. **Simple travel research** (hotel suggestions, deflected to the card issuer's travel portal)

That's it. That is the entire delivered scope of every Visa Infinite, Mastercard World Elite, and Amex Platinum concierge.

### The structural reason they all fail at high-touch service

It is unit economics. Visa Infinite has ~10M+ cards in circulation globally. Ten Group's Visa contract is publicly disclosed as £250K–£2M/year. That's at most £0.20 per cardholder per year for the concierge backbone. You cannot build a named, dedicated, judgment-deploying human service at that price point. The math forces:

- **Outsourcing.** Issuing banks outsource to Ten or Aspire. Card networks outsource to the same vendors.
- **Call center economics.** Shift-rotated agents. No agent owns your account.
- **Scripted scope.** Agents work from a playbook (restaurant, ticket, gift, basic travel). Off-script requests get deflected.
- **Asynchronous SLAs.** "We'll get back to you in 24–48 hours" is the floor. Email was eliminated by Amex because the volume was unsustainable at the price point.

The Centurion exception confirms the rule: Amex charges $15K/year and runs concierge in-house in a POD model. Even there, cardholders report degraded continuity post-2022. There is no concierge product anywhere on the market under $10K/year that gives you a named human who learns your account.

### The specific moments they fail

This is where the sales conversation lives. Card concierges break catastrophically at:

| Moment | What the Card Concierge Does | What Goes Wrong |
|--------|------------------------------|----------------|
| **Cancelled flight at 11pm** | Deflects to airline; cannot rebook on issuer's authority | Cardholder ends up on hold with the airline themselves |
| **Multi-leg itinerary change mid-trip** | Cannot reissue ticket; refers to original booking channel | If booked via Chase Travel, must go through Chase Travel; lose hours |
| **Last-minute suite at sold-out hotel** | Calls property; if Resy/portal shows no availability, declines | Has no GM-level relationship to call on |
| **VIP recruit arriving with no reservation** | Will book a room if availability exists | No upgrade, no welcome amenity, no name recognition at the property |
| **Closing-week trip with three reschedules** | Each call starts from scratch with a new agent | Context dies between calls; cardholder re-explains every time |
| **After-hours emergency abroad** | Phone hold; possible callback in 24–48h | Useless in the moment |
| **Suite request, dietary needs, special occasion** | Will note preferences in profile if Mastercard | No physical follow-through at the property — no chocolate, no flowers, no upgrade |

### The "I already have Amex Platinum" objection — answered

This objection rests on a 2018-era understanding of what Amex Platinum concierge does. The 2026 reality:

- The email channel was killed in **August 2021** ([source](https://www.godsavethepoints.com/amex-platinum-concierge-loses-email/))
- Off-list restaurant reservations were killed in **July 2022** ([source](https://www.headforpoints.com/forums/topic/concierge-will-no-longer-reserve-restaurants/))
- Phone hold times are reported "**up to an hour or more**"
- Tour booking is now declined: "the Platinum Card Concierge isn't able to book tours like this and that I should look to GetYourGuide" ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/amex-platinum-concierge-trip-planning/))
- Boutique hotel research is now deflected to Amex Travel
- Restaurant booking is now funneled to Resy (a property Amex owns) and limited to the Global Dining Collection partner list

The Amex Platinum concierge of 2026 is a *transactional task router*. It is not the service its name implies and not the service the prospect remembers from when they got the card.

### Why founders/operators still don't use card concierges even when they're paying for them

The unused-amenity problem. Amex Platinum has ~3 million cardholders in the US paying $695/year. Industry estimates suggest fewer than 10% of cardholders use the concierge in any given year. The reasons:

1. **Mental tax.** Calling a stranger who doesn't know you, explaining the request, waiting for a callback, following up — for a busy founder, it is easier to do it yourself or text your EA.
2. **No trust gradient.** With a new agent every call, there is no relationship to build on. Founders default to people who know them.
3. **Scope misalignment.** Founders' travel pain is rarely "find me a Cipriani reservation." It is "my trip just broke, fix it." Card concierges don't handle the second case.
4. **The EA backstop.** Founders with EAs route everything through the EA. The card concierge is one rung below the EA in the priority order — used for the EA's overflow, never the founder's first call.

This is the structural opening for Latitude 43: the card concierge is paid-for, available, and unused — because it is not the right tool for the buyer's actual problem.

## Part 4: The Concierge Objection Handler

*(A 500-word verbatim response the founder can use when a prospect says "I already have Amex Platinum / Chase Reserve / Brex Travel.")*

---

I hear this a lot, and it's a fair question. Let me give you the honest picture.

Your Amex Platinum concierge is good at four things: a restaurant reservation off the Global Dining Collection list, an event ticket, a gift, and pointing you toward Amex Travel for a hotel. That's it. Amex killed off-list restaurant booking in July 2022 and killed email concierge access in August 2021. Reader-reported phone hold times now run "up to an hour or more" — that's a direct quote from TPG-affiliated coverage. The journalist at Upgraded Points who tried to use it to plan a 3-day Rome trip got told to use GetYourGuide for tours and Amex Travel for hotels. The concierge was useful only for "last mile" dining recommendations.

If you have Chase Sapphire Reserve or Capital One Venture X, your concierge is actually a Ten Lifestyle Group call center under a Visa Infinite contract — the same vendor that runs Mastercard World Elite. They'll send you a PDF intake form before they'll call a restaurant for you, and they explicitly won't help with reservations on the day you arrive from an international flight. That's a structural admission: they cannot handle time-sensitive requests.

Brex Travel and Ramp Travel are excellent for *booking*. They are not staffed for judgment. Ramp's travel support is run by Priceline. Brex publishes a 24/7 chat line. They are not going to know your team, know your travel pattern, know that you always book aisle-near-the-front because you sleep on planes, or get a Four Seasons GM on the phone at midnight because your COO's flight diverted to JFK.

Here is what Latitude 43 is structurally different about:

One named advisor. Same human every call, every trip, every year. They build a profile of your team — passports, allergies, seat preferences, lounge memberships, family situations. They learn your business. They learn your patterns.

Real after-hours, real continuity. When your COO's flight gets cancelled at 11pm on a Tuesday, you text one person. That person already knows the trip, already has the booking detail, already has supplier relationships at the property and the airline. They are not reading from a script.

GM-level relationships at properties. We work directly with general managers at the hotels our clients use most. Upgrades, suite confirmations, welcome amenities, late checkout — these are negotiated human-to-human, not pulled from a database.

The honest test: when did your card concierge last solve something real for you — not a Cipriani reservation, but an actual problem that mattered? If the answer is "never" or "I don't think about it," that's the gap. You're paying $695, $795, or $395 for a service you're not using because it isn't the right tool for what actually breaks.

Latitude 43 is the tool for what actually breaks.

## Part 5: Where Card Concierges and Founder Perks Leave Gaps

### The five gaps Latitude 43 owns

**1. The "I have an EA but they're not a travel specialist" gap.**
EAs are generalists. They book what the founder tells them to book, on the channels the founder tells them to use. They do not have supplier relationships, GM contacts, or the muscle memory of running an IROPs rebooking. EAs *love* having a travel advisor to escalate to because it removes their highest-stakes lowest-frequency work.

**2. The "Brex booking is fine but support disappears" gap.**
Brex and Ramp are great at the booking moment. When the trip is in motion and something goes wrong, the support model collapses — third-party call center, ticket queue, no continuity. The Marriott $1,500 stuck pre-authorization story from the FlyerTalk thread is emblematic: Brex's response was "not our fault, nothing we can do."

**3. The "Amex Platinum gets me a Cipriani reservation but can't rebook my flight" gap.**
Card concierges are not authorized to issue or reissue airline tickets unless the trip was originally booked through the card's travel portal. This means a cardholder who books United.com directly gets zero help when United cancels. Latitude 43 owns the entire trip, regardless of original booking channel.

**4. The "founder perk gives me a 10% discount on Marriott but no upgrades" gap.**
FoundersCard delivers status (Hilton Gold, Marriott Platinum challenge). Status is good. Status is not a suite upgrade at a sold-out property, not a 4pm late checkout when your status doesn't earn it, not a welcome amenity in the room when your spouse arrives. Those come from human relationships at the property, which membership cards do not provide.

**5. The "all my travel data lives in seven different apps" gap.**
A typical founder has trips booked via Amex Travel, Chase Travel, Brex Travel, Ramp Travel, direct airline sites, direct hotel sites, and AirBnB. None of these systems talk to each other. None has a complete view of the founder's travel. Latitude 43 holds that complete view as the named human owner.

## Strategic implications for Latitude 43 GTM

1. **Stop trying to dislodge Centurion users.** They are 0.02% of the market and most of them either love their concierge (Brian Kelly) or have already moved on (the disgruntled cardholders cited above don't need a sales conversation — they need a switching path). The Centurion users worth pursuing are the disillusioned ones, but they will find us, not the other way around.

2. **Target Amex Platinum cardholders with the post-2022 reality.** Most Platinum holders we'll talk to have not used the concierge in 12+ months. Our job is to remind them *why* — because the service was cut — and offer the thing they actually want.

3. **Position against Brex/Ramp on the "support disappears mid-trip" axis.** Brex/Ramp are fine for booking. They are not fine for the rest of the trip. Our wedge is the post-booking layer.

4. **Treat FoundersCard as complementary, not competitive.** FoundersCard is a discount card. Latitude 43 is a service. A founder can have both. Position FoundersCard as the consumer-grade tool and Latitude 43 as the operator-grade service.

5. **Lean into the Ten Group / vendor-consolidation story.** The fact that Visa Infinite and Mastercard World Elite are the same vendor (Ten) is the single most powerful unmasking we can deliver. Most cardholders believe they have brand-differentiated concierge. They don't.

6. **The EA is our channel partner, not our competition.** EAs are the highest-leverage referral source we have. An EA who hands off complex/high-stakes travel to Latitude 43 looks better to their principal, sleeps better, and frees up their highest-value hours. Build the EA referral motion.
