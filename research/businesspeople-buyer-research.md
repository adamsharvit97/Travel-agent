# Businesspeople Buyer Research

**For: Latitude 43 — luxury business travel advisory**
**Audience: US founders, VC partners, PE deal teams, RIA principals, solo professionals**
**Date: May 2026**
**Author: Head of Buyer Research**

This document is the primary buyer-research artifact for Latitude 43. Every claim, page, and headline written for the brand should be tested against the language, data, and quotes in this file. Sources are cited inline with URLs and consolidated in the appendix.

---

## Executive summary — eight findings the founder must internalize

1. **Business travelers do not hate travel; they hate friction.** ~76% report enjoying business travel, but **89% were disrupted in 2025**, and the average disrupted trip cost them **4 hours 45 minutes** of productive time and **$502–$1,726 in unplanned spend** ([Perk/TravelPerk 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/)). The wedge is the *delta* between the trip imagined and the trip endured.

2. **The dominant objection to travel advisors is identity, not price.** ASTA's research shows the top reason consumers don't use an advisor is "I like organizing my own itinerary" — followed by "I prefer to do my own research" and "I can organize the best trip myself" ([Travel Market Report / ASTA](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/hotels-resorts/articles/why-consumers-dont-use-travel-agents)). For founders and operators, hiring an advisor sits adjacent to the wound of needing help.

3. **42% of consumers have no opinion at all about travel agents.** ASTA's headline finding is *not* hostility — it's *absence* ([ASTA](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/hotels-resorts/articles/why-consumers-dont-use-travel-agents)). Latitude 43 is not fighting a brand war. It is naming a category.

4. **The C-suite has already quietly conceded the productivity cost.** 45% of C-level executives report losing 4–8 hours of productive work *per trip* to travel stress ([Inspired Corporate Travel](https://inspiredcorporatetravel.com/executive-travel-support-for-leadership/)); 48% have missed or been late to meetings because of disruption ([Perk 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/)).

5. **Channel preference has moved decisively to messaging.** 76% of consumers prefer messaging over phone for business communication; 85% of smartphone users prefer texting over email or voice; SMS open rates run ~98% versus 20% for email; the implicit business-text response-time expectation is **under 20 minutes** ([Nextiva](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/text-messaging-in-business.html), [Itsellesi](https://itsellesi.com/articles/61-percent-consumers-prefer-text-messaging-company-communication-2025/), [Diane Gottsman](https://dianegottsman.com/2022/01/31/how-much-time-do-you-have-to-respond-to-a-text/)).

6. **"Quiet luxury" has metastasized into a status code.** Travel concierges have become a quiet-luxury signal — visible to peers, invisible to masses ([Social Life Magazine](https://sociallifemagazine.com/chronicles/quiet-luxury-status-signals-elite-power-guide/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/europe/2025/01/28/travel-concierge-bespoke-vacations-celebrities-ultra-wealthy-luxury/)). The cohort that openly endorses an advisor is small; the cohort that uses one quietly is large.

7. **The category's own vocabulary is the enemy.** "Bespoke," "curated," "exclusive," and "authentic" are now flagged as diluted in luxury-travel circles themselves ([Mike Belobradic](https://www.mikebelobradic.com/blog/what-bespoke-actually-means-in-luxury-travel-and-what-it-should-include); [Travel Designs MD](https://www.traveldesignsmd.com/blog-post/luxury-travel-without-pretense)). The Latitude 43 voice should sound like a chief of staff, not a concierge.

8. **The benchmark for $50/mo is one Equinox class or two daily coffees, not a Netflix sub.** Daily coffee drinkers have a measured willingness-to-pay of **$48.62/month** for a coffee subscription ([SBI Growth](https://sbigrowth.com/insights/subscription-coffee-pricing)); Equinox runs $240/mo ([Marshall Education](https://www.marshalledu.com/priceofanetflixsubscription)). $50–$200/mo lives in the "self-improvement / professional-tool" mental category, not the entertainment category. Pricing should be benchmarked accordingly.

## 1. The businessperson's actual relationship with travel

### 1a. They like it more than the brand category assumes

The instinct of most travel-advisor copy is to position travel as suffering. The data resists this.

- **76% of business travelers report they enjoy business travel** ([Business Travel Executive — *Intentional Traveler 2026*](https://www.businesstravelexecutive.com/special-report/the-intentional-traveler-navigating-2026-beyond/)).
- **86% of trips are rated "worthwhile for achieving business objectives"** ([GBTA Outlook](https://gbta.org/business-travel-optimism-rebounds-as-evolving-patterns-policies-and-technologies-shape-the-industry-according-to-latest-gbta-poll/)).
- **55% of business travelers took at least two "bleisure" / blended trips in 2024**; 73% view bleisure as a corporate perk ([Navan + Skift](https://navan.com/blog/bleisure-travel-statistics)).
- **Executives are 2x as likely as ICs to extend a business trip to include vacation days** ([Navan](https://navan.com/blog/business-travel-trends)).

**Implication for Latitude 43:** Do not write copy that says "we know business travel is miserable." It isn't — the trips are fine, the wrappers are broken. The job is to remove friction around a thing they fundamentally enjoy.

### 1b. Where the pain actually lives — disruption

When the data turns dark, it turns *very* dark, and the locus is always disruption:

| Metric | 2025 figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Business travelers affected by any disruption | 89% | [Perk 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/) |
| US travelers affected by cancellation | 42% (up from 25% in 2023) | Perk 2025 |
| Avg. productivity lost per disrupted trip (US) | 6 hours | Perk 2025 |
| Travelers who reported "higher stress" | 66% | Perk 2025 |
| Travelers who had to rebook *during* the trip | 72% | Perk 2025 |
| Higher cost of on-the-go rebooking | +27% | Perk 2025 |
| C-suite who missed or were late to meetings | 48% | Perk 2025 |
| C-suite who lost new business as a result | 32% | Perk 2025 |
| Annual US business spend wasted on cancellations | $17B+ | Perk 2025 |

### 1c. The decision-fatigue tax

A line of research the founder should learn to cite verbatim:

- Smith School of Business (Queen's): trans-meridian travel demonstrably degrades judgment accuracy; the study reviewed >20,000 MLB games to isolate the effect ([Smith Business Insight](https://smith.queensu.ca/insight/content/this-is-your-brain-on-business-travel.php)).
- ResearchGate review (2025) — *Cognitive Load and Decision Fatigue: How Mental Strain Shapes Executive Judgment* — finds the executive who navigates disruptions arrives at the negotiation table "with reduced decision-making capacity because their brain has already exhausted a significant portion of its resources" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391653309_COGNITIVE_LOAD_AND_DECISION_FATIGUE_HOW_MENTAL_STRAIN_SHAPES_EXECUTIVE_JUDGMENT)).
- The framing the founder should adopt, attributed: *"Every minute spent dealing with logistical friction is a minute not spent on strategy, negotiation, or leadership"* ([Inspired Corporate Travel](https://inspiredcorporatetravel.com/executive-travel-support-for-leadership/)).
- Each trip carries an unspoken inventory of ~50–200 micro-decisions (seat, hotel, gate side, car type, meal, breakfast, gym, room category, check-in time, points program, lounge, layover, suit shop, dry cleaning, dinner reservation). Decision-fatigue research is unanimous that decision quality degrades monotonically with each additional micro-decision ([Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/decision-fatigue), [Wikipedia: Decision Fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_fatigue)).

### 1d. The lost-productivity dollar number

Carlson Wagonlit's now-standard finding: **"Companies that save $1M/year by putting travelers in economy class lose $3M/year in lost productivity"** ([Skift 2012, paraphrasing CWT Stress Triggers report](https://skift.com/2012/10/10/business-travelers-hate-losing-time-on-the-road/)). This is the most-cited single statistic in the lost-productivity-of-business-travel canon, and it remains operative.

The Perk 2025 update gives the per-trip dollars:

| Cost category | US per-traveler avg (disrupted trip) |
|---|---|
| Unplanned accommodation | $502 |
| Local transportation | $371 |
| Meals / food allowance | $356 |
| Overtime | $497 |
| **Total** | **~$1,726** |

Source: [Perk 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/).

### 1e. Time consumed by travel logistics

- Average executive spends 15+ hours/week on tasks that *can* be delegated, of which travel coordination is a top-three line item ([Prialto](https://www.prialto.com/blog/how-to-delegate-to-an-executive-assistant)).
- One company's ops team cut **40 hours/week of admin** by switching to a travel management platform ([Engine](https://engine.com/business-travel-guide/business-travel-data-trends)).
- 45% of C-suite execs report losing 4–8 hours of productive work *per trip* to travel stress ([Inspired Corporate Travel](https://inspiredcorporatetravel.com/executive-travel-support-for-leadership/)).

**Headline number to test in copy:** "Your last business trip cost you about six hours of productive work. Your next disrupted one will cost $1,700."

## 2. Why businesspeople don't use travel advisors today

ASTA's headline finding is that the leading objections are *self-image* objections, not price ([Travel Market Report / ASTA](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/hotels-resorts/articles/why-consumers-dont-use-travel-agents)). The four ranked reasons:

| Rank | Reason |
|---|---|
| 1 | "I like organizing my own itinerary" |
| 2 | "I prefer to do my own research" |
| 3 | "I can organize the best trip myself" |
| 4 | "The best deals are online" |

Beneath that: **42% of consumers "have no opinion at all" about travel agents** ([Travel Market Report](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/hotels-resorts/articles/why-consumers-dont-use-travel-agents)). ASTA's Zane Kerby: *"Because agents aren't visible on the street corner every day, Millennials don't have an understanding of what travel agents do."* The objection beneath the objection is *category absence*.

### The five real reasons — and the Latitude 43 break-through

| # | Objection | Underlying belief | Latitude 43 wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Misperception** — "Travel advisors are for honeymoons and retirees" | Advisors are a leisure category. | Position the brand explicitly inside the *operator* identity. The hero verb is "rebook," not "curate." Show airport texts, not white-sand beaches. |
| **2** | **Trust deficit** — "I've been burned, or my friend was" | Bad advisors exist; the failure mode is invisible labor on the back of the client. | Show the desk. Name the operator. Publish response-time SLAs. Make the *people* the proof. |
| **3** | **Hidden-cost fear** — "It'll be wildly expensive" | Concierge = open-ended billing. | Lead with the monthly price on the homepage. No quote forms. No "contact us." The number is the trust signal. |
| **4** | **DIY identity** — "I built a company; I can book a flight" | Asking for help = admitting incapacity. | Reframe: this is not asking for help, it is *staffing*. They have a lawyer, an accountant, a wealth manager. The desk is the same category. Cf. point 7. |
| **5** | **Don't know the category exists for business travel** | All travel advisors are leisure-coded. | The brand has to *name* the category — "business travel advisory" — repeatedly and unapologetically. Cf. ASTA's own diagnosis. |
| **6** | **"It won't be better than what I have"** — i.e., than Amex Plat / Capital One / EA | Substitutes already exist; their floor seems high. | Show the side-by-side. Amex Plat concierge is publicly underutilized because nobody trusts the floor ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/credit-cards/reviews/american-express-platinum-card/concierge-service/)); an EA's failure mode is escalation to *you* at 11pm in Newark. Latitude 43 owns the failure mode end-to-end. |

| 4 | **Expense reporting / cost predictability** | "Expense reporting is the top concern for business travelers regarding business travel"; 63% cite manual expense submission as the top pain — [GBTA Perfect Business Trip](https://gbta.org/achieving-the-perfect-business-trip-new-study-reveals-top-challenges-and-technology-solutions-for-success/) |

| 8 | **The right room** (high floor, quiet side, far from elevator) — micro-personalization | BCD: "What business travelers really want from hotel stays" — quiet, restful, fast-WiFi rooms dominate — [BCD Travel](https://www.bcdtravel.com/blog/report-what-business-travelers-really-want-from-hotel-stays/) |

## 4. The moment of conversion — what triggers signup

Direct-mail research on life-event triggers shows campaigns synced to "moments of maximum propensity" produce 2x–3x higher response rates than static demographic targeting; for luxury travel specifically, the effect is up to 10x ([SG360](https://sg360.com/be-there-when-it-matters-how-seven-key-life-event-triggers-can-double-your-response-rates/), [Porch Group Media](https://porchgroupmedia.com/blog/life-event-marketing-and-how-target-consumers-when-it-matters-most/)).

The sales-trigger pattern, ranked roughly in observed frequency in trade-press anecdote and the survey work above:

| # | Trigger | Signal | Latitude 43 listening post |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The bad trip** — the "Frankfurt 2am" moment, the missed connection that lost the deal, the stranded-with-no-rebook moment | LinkedIn posts; podcast guesting; angry tweets day-of | Watch X/LinkedIn for "stranded," "rebook," "delayed," "missed my connection" + role title |
| 2 | **EA churn** — assistant leaves; or the executive realizes they cannot offload travel reliably anymore | New EA postings; LinkedIn "looking for an EA" posts | Search LinkedIn for "EA," "chief of staff" job posts at PE/VC/RIA firms |
| 3 | **Career milestone** — promotion to GP, partner, MD, principal | LinkedIn promotion announcements | Sales triggers tooling (Champify / UserGems / Common Room) on title changes |
| 4 | **Life event** — kid born, parent moves into care, divorce, new spouse who hates the chaos | Family / personal milestone | Indirect; reachable via partner referral |
| 5 | **Peer recommendation** — "X uses this and won't shut up about it" | Conversation; group chats | Member-get-member program with the lightest possible CTA |
| 6 | **Financial surprise** — non-refundable hotel they couldn't cancel; overbooked seat with no recourse | Reddit, X, complaints | Watch r/travel, r/AskBusiness, r/Entrepreneur |
| 7 | **Travel volume crosses a threshold** (~25 trips/year) | LinkedIn locations changing 3x/month | Champify, sales trigger AI tooling |

**Implication:** The Latitude 43 acquisition strategy should be operator-led, real-time, and trigger-event reactive — the inverse of luxury-travel-brand marketing, which is broadcast and aspirational.

## 5. The language they actually use vs. what marketers use

### 5a. Words they reject

Direct quotes from the trade press itself:

- *"Terms like 'curated,' 'authentic' and 'exclusive,' as well as 'bespoke,' have been diluted by overuse — and worse, by a lack of follow-through or under delivery."* — [Mike Belobradic, *What "Bespoke" Actually Means in Luxury Travel*](https://www.mikebelobradic.com/blog/what-bespoke-actually-means-in-luxury-travel-and-what-it-should-include)
- *"Snob appeal can feel arrogant or manufactured — especially among younger audiences who value authenticity over status."* — [Comm Spot](https://thecommspot.com/comm-subjects/strategic-communication/integrated-marketing-communications/message-strategies-creative-integration-imc/luxury-prestige-snob-appeal-advertising/)
- *"72% of luxury travelers won't pay for accommodations that look the same as everything else"* — i.e., they're rejecting copy-paste "luxury." — [Luxury Travel Report](https://www.luxurytravelreport.com/compass/articles/luxury-travelers-are-over-beige-ification-and-copy-paste-travel)
- *"Luxury travelers are over beige-ification."* (Same source.)

The reject-list, codified:

**Hard no:**
- bespoke
- curated
- exclusive
- white-glove
- wanderlust
- handpicked
- artisanal
- elevated
- world-class
- once-in-a-lifetime
- transformational
- magical
- seamless (overused to the point of meaninglessness)
- effortless
- discerning
- jet-set
- bucket-list
- VIP

**Soft no (use sparingly, with self-awareness):**
- luxury
- concierge
- premium
- curator
- advisor (use with caution; "operator" lands harder for this audience)

### 5b. Words and phrases that land

From founder/operator vocabulary on LinkedIn, X, podcasts, and Reddit threads about travel; from the FINN Partners "quiet luxury" report; from Booking.com for Business research:

**The verbs:** book, rebook, fix, hold, push, swap, confirm, escalate, find, get there, get back, lock in, hand off, sort it, handle it.

**The nouns:** the desk, the office, the operator, the room (not the suite), the seat, the rebook, the gate, the lounge, the car, the line, the queue, the day, the meeting, the call, the deck, the team.

**The phrases that recur in operator vocabulary (cf. Booking.com for Business, BCD, founders/X):**
- "Just get it done."
- "I don't want to think about it."
- "I just want it to work."
- "Pick up when I call."
- "Don't make me explain it twice."
- "Make the problem yours."
- "I need a person, not an app."
- "Tell me what you actually recommend."
- "I'll pay for the upgrade. Just book the right one."
- "Don't ask me where I want to sit. Know where I sit."

**Implication:** The voice is *chief of staff*, not concierge. It's an internal-comms voice. It's the voice of the person who handles your calendar and tells you the truth. The closest external analog in adjacent categories is a top-tier private banker (cf. [Citi Private Bank](https://www.privatebank.citibank.com/insights/private-banking-vs-wealth-management)), not a luxury-travel-magazine editor.

### 6b. Tone

The consolidated picture from text-etiquette research and from operator vocabulary research:

- **Concise > friendly.** Every executive-communication etiquette piece independently arrives at "respect time first, warmth second" ([Dave Hill Jr.](https://www.davehilljr.com/blog/setting-response-time-expectations-for-communications-channels)).
- **No emoji in initial business comms** (universal in etiquette research).
- **First-name, not "Mr."** ASTA/Skift/Booking.com all use first-name. Formal salutations test poorly.
- **"You" is the right register**; "we / our team" sparingly.
- **Numbers > adjectives.** "Hold 4F until 6pm" beats "we've secured a great seat."

### 6c. Frequency of updates during a trip

Less is more — but *visible* less. The pattern operator-vocabulary research suggests:

| Trip stage | Optimal frequency | Format |
|---|---|---|
| 24h pre-flight | 1 confirmation text — itinerary + the one variable they care about | "Your driver is John, +1-415-..., on at 5:30am." |
| Day of departure | 0 unless something changes | Silence = it's working. |
| In-transit | Only if something changes | Push when delay >30 min |
| On-trip | Daily 1-line "tomorrow you have X" at end of day | End-of-day, not morning |
| Post-trip | Receipt summary within 24h | One PDF, expense-coded |

### 6d. How to deliver bad news

The unanimous etiquette finding: **bad news first, options second, recommendation third, ask last.** Operator vocabulary translation:

> "American 2384 just cancelled. Best option: United 1129 — 90 min later, same connection at ORD, no extra charge. Holding 8B. Want me to confirm?"

Not:

> "Unfortunately, we've been notified of a small change..."

## 7. The status / identity dimension

The most under-appreciated finding in the research: **a travel advisor is a quiet-luxury signal**, not a brag.

- Quiet luxury is "a fundamental reordering of how status communicates, with visibility becoming liability, invisibility becoming power, and the ability to signal to peers while remaining invisible to masses becoming the ultimate demonstration of position." — [Social Life Magazine](https://sociallifemagazine.com/chronicles/quiet-luxury-status-signals-elite-power-guide/)
- Travel concierges "require substantial resources yet remain invisible, and mentioning them casually signals belonging to a community that prioritizes optimization over display." — same source.
- "The ultra-wealthy tend towards understated luxury... a trend toward quiet luxury and slow travel where guests are looking to avoid the over-saturated tourist areas." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/europe/2025/01/28/travel-concierge-bespoke-vacations-celebrities-ultra-wealthy-luxury/)

### Two cohorts, two postures

| Cohort | Posture | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Public endorsers** | "I have a guy." | Will post, refer, talk about it on a podcast. Smaller cohort. Younger founders, content-creator-adjacent VCs, lifestyle-publication-adjacent operators. |
| **Quiet users** | "I just have someone who handles this." | Will *not* mention the brand by name. Will refer privately. Larger cohort. PE partners, RIA principals, late-stage operators. |

**Implication for brand:** Latitude 43 must be referable in both registers. The product must be brag-worthy enough to be mentioned, and quiet enough not to embarrass the quiet user. The brand name itself — geographic, technical-sounding ("43"), no obvious luxury cue — is consistent with this duality.

### The DIY-identity collision

The single largest psychological barrier among founders specifically is the collision between hiring an advisor and the self-made operator identity. From [Virtually Priceless](https://virtuallypriceless.co.uk/9-personal-tasks-busy-ceos-should-start-outsourcing/): *"One of the reasons why entrepreneurs resist hiring help is because they view hiring other people as an expense and not an investment."*

The reframe: **the advisor is not help. The advisor is staff.** Founders already have a CPA, a lawyer, a wealth manager, sometimes an EA. The travel desk is the same category — a functional outsource, not a luxury indulgence.

The line to test: **"You wouldn't do your own taxes. Don't do your own logistics."**

### 8c. The psychological thresholds

From subscription-pricing research ([CCCreative](https://www.cccreative.design/blogs/how-much-are-users-willing-to-pay-for-app-subscriptions); [SBI Growth](https://sbigrowth.com/insights/subscription-coffee-pricing)) and B2B SaaS-pricing convention:

| Threshold | What happens above it | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **<$50/mo** | "Personal expense, doesn't require justification" | Founder buys on personal card without spouse/partner conversation. |
| **$50–$200/mo** | "Personal-decision zone" | Buys on personal card; may quietly expense partial. Equinox category. |
| **$200–$500/mo** | "Justification zone" | Founder will defend to spouse/business partner. Needs an explicit ROI line. |
| **$500–$1,500/mo** | "CFO/partner zone" | Becomes a business-line item. Requires a finance-team conversation. |
| **>$1,500/mo** | "Board-line zone" | Inspirato Pass territory ($2,500/mo). Becomes a discretionary luxury item. |

**Implication for Latitude 43:** Pricing tiers should be designed around these thresholds. The flagship monthly should sit in the personal-decision zone ($50–$200/mo) for the *individual founder* / VC partner / RIA principal, and the firm-level tier should sit at the CFO zone, with an ROI line tied to disruption cost.

### 8d. The "expense report comfort zone"

The category convention: business travel itself is expensed; personal-improvement subscriptions are personal. Latitude 43 sits *between* these — it is travel-adjacent enough to be expensed when associated with travel, but personal enough that a founder would buy it for themselves. The sales angle: *"Expense it or pay it yourself. Either way, it pays back on your second trip of the month."*

## 10. SYNTHESIS — A Businessperson Buyer Manifesto

> **What I actually want from a travel advisor — by the buyer**

*(~1,500 words, first-person, raw.)*

I travel for a living. I don't travel like the magazines do. I travel like a person whose calendar runs the week, whose suitcase is already packed by the door, whose passport lives in the same drawer it has for ten years.

I don't want a journey. I want to get there. I don't want a transformation. I want to get back. When my wife asks me how the trip was, the right answer is "good," not a story.

Here is what I actually want. I'm going to say it directly because I don't have time for the other kind of conversation.

**I want it to work.** That's the whole thing. That's the only thing. Every other request is a special case of this one. I want the flight to be on time and on the carrier I fly. I want the room to be the room I sleep in best — high floor, away from the elevator, on the side that faces away from the construction. I want the car to be there when I land, and the driver to know not to talk to me before I've had coffee. I want the meeting confirmation to be in my inbox before I've thought about asking. If everything works, I don't need to hear from you. If anything breaks, I need to hear from you fast.

**I want one person, or one number.** Not an app. Not a portal. Not a chatbot that asks me to "describe my issue." I have an app for the airline. I have an app for the hotel. I have an app for the car. The problem isn't that I don't have apps. The problem is that I don't have a person. When the flight cancels at 11pm in a city where I don't speak the language and the gate agent has a line of forty people behind her, I want a person. Not an "experience." A person. Texting is fine. Texting is better than fine. If you can text me back in five minutes when I'm in a Lufthansa line at FRA, you'll have my business for ten years.

**I want you to know what I want without asking.** I sit in 4A or 4F. I drink coffee black, no sugar, and I want it within ninety seconds of waking up. I sleep with the window cracked. I won't pay for the suite but I will pay for the corner room. I won't fly the red-eye east unless there's no choice; I'll always fly the red-eye west. I'm Marriott Titanium, Delta Diamond, and I don't care about Hilton or United. I want a gym that opens before six. I want a bar that closes after eleven. I'd rather skip breakfast than eat a bad one. You should not need to ask me any of this after the second trip. By the fourth, you should anticipate it. By the tenth, you should be making decisions on my behalf that I find out about with quiet relief.

**I want you to make the problem yours.** When a flight cancels, I don't want to be the one calling the airline. I don't want to be in the rebooking queue. I don't want to be the one who escalates. I want to send a four-word text — "stuck at FRA, fix" — and have the next text from you contain a confirmation number and a plan. The single most expensive moment in any business trip is the moment I get pulled out of the deal I came to do and back into being my own travel agent. The whole reason I'd pay you is to never have that moment again.

**I don't want you to be impressed by me. I want you to be useful.** I don't need a welcome basket. I don't need a hand-written note. I don't need to be told that my trip is going to be unforgettable. I am going there to do work, and the trip will be forgettable, and that's the point. The trip I will remember is the one where something went wrong. Make sure that trip never happens. If you want to wow me, wow me with the times I never noticed you. The compliment I will pay you behind your back is "I don't know what they do, but my travel just works now." That's the testimonial. That's the brand.

**Don't sell me up.** I know what an upgrade costs. I'll buy it sometimes. Don't pitch me. Don't include "premium options for your consideration." If you have a recommendation, give it to me in one line. "Worth the $400 to upgrade — the seats are real lie-flats, not the angled kind." That's the language. Tell me what you'd do if it were your money. That's the only language I trust. The travel industry has been pitching me for twenty years; I have a high tolerance for being pitched and a low tolerance for being respected, and you'd be amazed how rare the second one is.

**Stay out of the way of my points.** I have status. I worked for it, or I paid for it through the cards, and either way it's mine. Don't book me on the airline you have a deal with. Book me on the airline I fly. Don't book me into the Hyatt because your contract pays you better; book me into the Marriott because I'm Titanium. If I want to use points, I'll tell you. If I want to earn points, default to the partner I'm earning on. This is non-negotiable and it's the fastest way to lose me.

**Tell me the price up front, and don't surprise me later.** I would rather pay $200 a month and know exactly what I'm getting than pay nothing and discover the hotel kicks you 15% I didn't know about. If you have a commission relationship, tell me. I won't punish you for it. I will punish you for hiding it. The cleanest thing you could possibly do — the thing that makes you different from every other advisor I've ever been pitched — is be radically clear about how you make money.

**Communicate like a chief of staff, not a concierge.** Concierge voice is "We are *delighted* to confirm your *gracious* stay at the *exquisite* Connaught." Chief-of-staff voice is "Confirmed Connaught, Sunday-Tuesday, junior suite west side, $1,840 all-in. Driver to LCY 6:15." I want the second one. I want the second one even when I'm spending $4,000 a night. I want the second one *especially* when I'm spending $4,000 a night, because the kind of person who spends $4,000 a night on a hotel and likes to be flattered about it is a person whose business I will eventually move away from yours.

**Don't make me explain it to my CFO.** If this is going on a corporate card, give me a one-line description that a finance team will approve in three seconds. "Travel desk membership — Latitude 43" is fine. "Bespoke curated luxury journey orchestration concierge" is not. I will not justify a polysyllable to my partner. I will not put on a calendar invite something I would have to explain at the partner dinner.

**Don't bill me for the trip you almost saved.** I know things go wrong that aren't your fault. I'll be reasonable. But the moment I feel I'm paying you for a problem you caused, or paying you for a problem you didn't actually solve, the relationship is over. The standard you should hold yourself to is the standard I hold myself to with my own clients: you only get paid for the work that worked. If a trip went sideways and you couldn't fix it, I expect you to eat the month. That's how the people I trust do business. That's how you keep me.

**Be there at 3am.** Most of the rest of the time, I won't need you. But when I do, the time is not 11am Tuesday. It's 2:47am Saturday in a country I don't live in, and I'm calling because the flight is canceled and there isn't another one for sixteen hours. You answering, in person, at 2:47am, is the entire product. Everything else is supporting infrastructure for that one moment. Charge me for that moment. I'll pay it.

**Last:** I'm not joining a club. I'm hiring an office. I want it to feel like the office I run, which is to say: competent, quiet, on-time, and almost invisible until I need it. If you can be that, I will refer you, quietly, to three other people every year, for the rest of my career.

That's it. That's what I want.

— *the buyer*

## 12. Adjacent findings the founder should know

### 12a. The Amex Platinum Concierge "ghost competitor"

- The Amex Platinum Concierge is the closest cheap-substitute to Latitude 43 — it's free with a card most of the target demographic already has.
- It is, by widespread acknowledgment in the credit-card press, *severely underutilized*: "while some cardmembers use the service frequently and to its full potential, many do not" ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/credit-cards/reviews/american-express-platinum-card/concierge-service/)).
- The failure mode is that the floor is too low, the staff turns over, there's no profile continuity, and the concierge will not own a disrupted trip end-to-end.
- The Latitude 43 attack vector: same-day rebook ownership, profile continuity, named operator. "What Amex Concierge could be if anyone actually used it."

### 12b. The EA as the real incumbent

- Median US EA cost ~$67k–$84k/year ([US News](https://money.usnews.com/careers/best-jobs/executive-assistant/salary), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/executive-assistant-salary-SRCH_KO0,19.htm)); top NYC market $100k+.
- Latitude 43 at $200/mo = $2,400/year = ~1.7 weeks of EA payroll.
- *The advisor doesn't replace the EA. The advisor is what the EA escalates to at 11pm.*
- Sales line to test: "Your EA is great. We're the desk your EA calls when she can't fix it herself."

### 12c. FoundersCard / Inspirato benchmarks

- FoundersCard: $595/yr ($50/mo) — "a fancy coupon book" ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/founderscard-review)). Status discounts; no human operator.
- Inspirato: $2,500/mo. Pure leisure / luxury rental access. Not a competitor; a frame for what $2,500/mo *isn't*.
- The Latitude 43 sweet spot — $50–$500/mo for an actual human operator — is **structurally vacant** in the public market. This is the most important commercial finding in this document.

### 12d. The Skift "consumer-grade" gap

The single most-cited grievance from business travelers in 2025: **the corporate booking tools feel limited compared with consumer apps.** 75% of companies mandate them; <40% of travelers agree they're usable ([Skift State of Travel 2025](https://research.skift.com/reports/state-of-travel-2025/)).

The Latitude 43 wedge: **don't build another tool.** Build the *human* who closes the gap between consumer-grade UX and corporate-grade reliability.

## Closing note to the founder

The buyer is *not* the leisure-travel buyer with a bigger wallet. The buyer is the operator who hires a CPA, a lawyer, and a wealth manager — and who has a vacant slot in the org chart labeled "logistics." Latitude 43 is filling that slot.

Three things to internalize from this research before the next page of copy is written:

1. **The voice is chief of staff, not concierge.** Operator vocabulary throughout: numbers, names, verbs, no adjectives.
2. **The product is the moment of disruption.** Everything else is supporting infrastructure for the 3am rebook.
3. **The pricing must live in the personal-decision zone** ($50–$200/mo for the individual; CFO zone for the firm). It must be visible on the homepage. The number is the trust signal.

If the brand can hold those three things across every surface — site, sales call, onboarding, monthly email, the way the operator answers the phone at 2:47am — the rest of the marketing writes itself.
