# The Adjacent Ecosystem of Travel Help

*A corporate-development map of every meaningful company in the "help-people-travel" space outside of traditional travel agents and TMCs — built to inform Latitude 43's partnership pipeline, competitive map, and target-customer list.*

**Date:** May 2026
**Author:** Latitude 43 corporate development desk
**Audience:** Founder, GTM lead, BD lead, board
**Status:** Working document. Pricing pulled live; will drift quarterly.

---

## How to read this document

This is not a vendor list. It is a *positioning map*. For every company in the adjacent ecosystem, three questions matter to Latitude 43:

1. **Do they refer to us, or do we refer to them?** (Partnership viability.)
2. **Are they pulling our buyer toward a different solution?** (Competitive threat.)
3. **Do their customers leak unsolved problems that we can pick up?** (Adjacent ICP.)

Every entry below is scored against those three. The synthesis sections (K, L, M, N) consolidate the answers.

A note on what is and is not in scope. We deliberately exclude:

- Traditional retail travel agents (already covered in `competitor-brownell.md`, `competitor-altour.md`, `competitor-cadence.md`).
- Pure OTAs (Expedia, Booking, Hopper) — they compete on price; we don't.
- Pure corporate TMCs running self-service (Navan, TravelPerk, Spotnana) — the closest-analog file covers them.

We include adjacent firms that *touch* a traveler's life around the trip — aviation, concierge, family-office services, talent operations, member networks, crisis support, immigration. The proposition is that Latitude 43's wedge is precisely the *bridge* between these silos, and you cannot draw the bridge until you have mapped the islands.

## A. Private aviation operators and brokers

The private aviation ecosystem splits cleanly into four sub-models: fractional ownership (NetJets, Flexjet), subscription operators (VistaJet, Wheels Up), jet-card programs (Sentient, Magellan, Jet Linx), and brokers (Air Charter Service, Stratos, PrivateFly, Amalfi). Each sub-model has a distinct customer profile and therefore a distinct partnership shape for Latitude 43.

The fundamental fact that founds the partnership thesis: **none of these companies do hotels, ground, visas, or itinerary glue well, and most of them admit it openly.** The aircraft is the product; everything before, after, and around it is logistics that the operator either ignores, white-labels to a concierge, or hands back to the client's assistant.

### 1. NetJets (Berkshire Hathaway)

**Business model:** Fractional ownership plus jet cards plus leases. Owns the fleet. Card275 jet card at ~$215,000 for 25 hours on a Phenom 300 (~$8,600/hr including FET). 1/16th share for a light jet runs $500K–$850K upfront, $12K–$15K monthly management, ~$8,500/hr ([flycraft.com](https://flycraft.com/ownership-cost/netjets-fractional-ownership/)).

**Customer profile:** Berkshire-style multi-generation wealth, F500 CEOs, top-tier RIA clients, principals at PE/HF firms. Older skew. Buys *certainty* — guaranteed availability, FAA Part 121-equivalent safety standards, brand inheritance.

**Geographic footprint:** Global. US, Europe, MEA.

**Partnership viability with Latitude 43:** **High but slow.** NetJets has a small in-house "Owner Services" group that handles hotel and ground for owners, but its scope is narrow (FBO transfers, branded hotel partners, Centurion-property loops). Anything bespoke — a non-chain villa in Lombardy, a partner-track recruit ferried from Teterboro to a sold-out W-class property, multi-leg European routing with kids on different legs — falls through the gap. Latitude 43's pitch to NetJets is *not* "let us sell to your members" — it is "let us be the named hotel-and-ground operator your concierge calls when a Berkshire-rated client asks for something Owner Services cannot do." That's a per-trip referral relationship measured in dozens, not thousands.

**Brand register:** Restrained to the point of severity. "The Promise of Always." No "Book," "Buy," or "Sign Up." Pricing is hidden. (See `brand-register-private-aviation.md` §1.)

**Specific gap Latitude 43 fills:** The off-network hotel relationship and the multi-day, multi-leg itinerary work that happens *between* the jet legs. NetJets does not have property GMs on first-name basis; it has a hotel-partner program.

### 2. VistaJet

**Business model:** Subscription-only ("Program"). Three-year commitment, 50+ hours/year minimum. VJ25 jet card at 25 hours/year, three-year term. Hourly rates ~$15,000 Challenger 350, $18,000–25,000 Global. First-year cost ~$375K–$400K for 25 hours on a Challenger ([privatejetcardcomparisons.com](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/vistajet/)).

**Customer profile:** International. UHNW principals who cross continents. Crypto founders, sovereign-adjacent capital, Gulf and Indian-subcontinent UHNW, European industrial families. *Younger* skew than NetJets. "Private World" partner program signals lifestyle ambition beyond the jet.

**Geographic footprint:** Global, with a real European and Middle Eastern presence NetJets cannot match domestically.

**Partnership viability:** **Highest in category.** Vista's Private World is *explicitly* a partner-curation program — Norland-trained crew, British Butler Institute training, a stated thesis from CMO Matteo Atti that "Private World marks the start of a radical transformation of the top end of the travel industry — a shift in the business model not governed anymore by volumes of booking but led by quality, trust and shared values" ([Luxuria Lifestyle interview](https://www.luxurialifestyle.com/luxuria-lifestyle-international-interviews-matteo-atti-cmo-of-vistajet/)). Latitude 43 fits Private World's curation criteria more naturally than 90% of its current partners do. The outreach play is: pitch as a US-and-EMEA "ground and stay" partner specifically for principals who fly Program but do not have a US-based fixer.

**Brand register:** Restrained warmth. Small caps for moments of weight. "Make an Inquiry."

**Specific gap:** US-side property and ground relationships. Vista is European at its core; its US partner stack is thin compared to its European one. Latitude 43 sits inside that gap.

### 3. Wheels Up (UP, NYSE)

**Business model:** Hybrid jet card + on-demand charter + Delta partnership. Recent restructuring: $165M Delta-led financing May 2026, fleet consolidated to Phenom 300 and Challenger 300/350 only, first positive Adjusted EBITDAR in early 2026. SkyMiles integration: members can self-book Delta commercial flights inside the Wheels Up portal as of January 2026 ([prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wheels-up-announces-first-quarter-results-and-new-delta-led-financing-302768604.html)).

**Customer profile:** "Democratized" private aviation — the tier below NetJets. Affluent but not UHNW; entrepreneurs, mid-tier finance, professional athletes, family principals who fly 25–75 hours/year. *35% growth in corporate sales* most recent quarter signals a deliberate pivot from leisure to a mixed book.

**Geographic footprint:** US-dominant, with Delta-routed international.

**Partnership viability:** **High and immediate.** Wheels Up is the rare aviation company that is *actively* trying to become a travel platform, not just an aircraft operator. The Delta tie means a SkyMiles elite is now one click away from a Wheels Up portal. The gap: that portal has no off-airport solve. A Wheels Up member booking a Delta flight to Heathrow needs a UK driver, a Mayfair property, and a Monday breakfast at the right Coutts-adjacent club — none of which exists in the portal. Latitude 43's pitch is: "We are the human layer your portal cannot replace."

**Brand register:** Aspirational, slightly louder than NetJets/Vista. "Be Up" language, lifestyle-driven imagery.

**Specific gap:** Same as NetJets but more acute — the membership base is younger, less likely to have a family-office concierge, and more likely to assemble travel with credit-card concierge + Wheels Up + Booking.com.

### 4. Magellan Jets

**Business model:** Jet card with no peak days, no fuel surcharge, no blackouts. All-in hourly rates: light $6,200, mid $8,100, super-mid $9,800, heavy $13,200. 25/50/100-hour increments. Rate-lock 12 months on 25-hour card, 18 on 50-hour. Explorer membership at $11,500–$16,500 initiation + $4,500 annual dues ([magellanjets.com](https://magellanjets.com/jet-card-ownership/), [privatejetcardcomparisons.com](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/magellan-jets/)).

**Customer profile:** Northeast US (Boston HQ), entrepreneurial wealth, second-home owners, families who fly 25–100 hours/year. The "we wanted NetJets but we wanted clarity" buyer.

**Geographic footprint:** US-only with international one-off charter.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium-high.** Smaller team, easier door to open, and Magellan markets itself on transparency — they would benefit from a named hotel/ground partner they can list on their site without conflict. Outreach: introduce as a Latitude 43 "fly with Magellan, stay with us" co-marketing bundle, white-label optional.

**Specific gap:** Concierge depth. Magellan ships the aircraft cleanly; everything else is the cardholder's problem.

### 5. Sentient Jet

**Business model:** Jet card, sold by cabin class in 25-hour increments. Owned by Kenn Ricci's Directional Aviation (not Berkshire — common error). Recently rolled progressive-pricing Large Cabin Edition: $12,990/hr domestic dropping to $10,315/hr for flights over 6 hours, ex-FET ([privatejetcardcomparisons.com](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/sentient-jet/), [sherpareport.com](https://www.sherpareport.com/aircraft/large-cabin-edition-sentient.html)).

**Customer profile:** Original jet-card buyer. US-East. Mature wealth, lower hour count than NetJets fractional buyers.

**Geographic footprint:** Continental US dominant, transcon and Caribbean common.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium.** Directional Aviation also owns Flexjet, PrivateFly, Skyjet — a partnership at Directional level would touch multiple brands. Outreach should be against the *parent*, not the operating brand.

**Specific gap:** Same as Magellan.

### 6. Jet Linx

**Business model:** Home-base operator. $25,000 initiation + $10,000 annual + prepaid hourly deposit drawn down by flight time. 22 US base locations. Top-10 North American operator by fleet ([jetlinx.com](https://jetlinx.com/jet-card/)).

**Customer profile:** Regional power buyers. The buyer who flies out of one airport 70% of the time — the founder in Omaha, the developer in Nashville, the law-firm chair in Charlotte. Local relationships matter more than fleet size.

**Geographic footprint:** US, 22 bases.

**Partnership viability:** **High and underrated.** Jet Linx's edge is that members get an in-person base team. That base team is a perfect distribution channel for a Latitude 43 referral — every Jet Linx base has 100–300 active local member families who travel internationally several times a year and have no fixer. Pitch: per-base partnership, *not* corporate. Start with one base (Nashville is a candidate given founder-density and lack of competing concierge).

**Specific gap:** Anywhere a member flies that isn't their home base, Jet Linx is back to a generic operator. Latitude 43 owns the "destination side" of that round trip.

### 7. Air Charter Service (ACS)

**Business model:** World's largest charter broker. FY2025 revenue $1.34B (+10.3% YoY), Q1 2026 $380M (+37% YoY), 33% EBITDA growth ([corporatejetinvestor.com](https://www.corporatejetinvestor.com/news/air-charter-service-sees-10-jump-in-revenues-to-1-34b-in-2025/), [aircargonews.net](https://www.aircargonews.net/charter-company/2026/02/air-charter-service-records-10-increase-in-revenues/)). Owns no aircraft. Five divisions: private jets, time-critical, ACMI & leasing, helicopter charters, cargo and group charters.

**Customer profile:** Governments, multinationals, royalty, aid agencies, sports teams, music tours, oil and gas crew rotations, evacuation contracts. Not consumer.

**Geographic footprint:** Truly global. Offices on every continent.

**Partnership viability:** **Low for consumer, high for institutional.** Where ACS overlaps Latitude 43 is on the *one-off mission* — a 200-person corporate offsite to Madeira, a sports-team charter that needs hotel-block coordination on landing. Latitude 43 is too small to be a default ACS partner globally, but could be the "destination services" arm ACS subcontracts for specific corporate or sports verticals in the US.

**Brand register:** Industrial. The opposite of VistaJet — earnest, B2B, charter-as-logistics.

**Specific gap:** Hotel blocks, ground transport, visa coordination for charter passengers. ACS arranges the aircraft; the customer arranges everything else.

### 8. PrivateFly (Directional Aviation / OneSky)

**Business model:** Digital-first charter broker. Acquired by Directional Aviation 2018, folded into OneSky alongside Skyjet ([businesswire.com](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20180905005267/en/Directional-Aviation-Acquires-PrivateFly-Creating-World%E2%80%99s-Largest)). White-labels to Lastminute.com, Manchester Airport Group, Addison Lee. App-based booking, online quotes.

**Customer profile:** Younger, mobile-native private flyers. UK-and-EU dominant. Often the gateway-tier flyer before NetJets/Vista.

**Geographic footprint:** UK/EU/US.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium.** PrivateFly's white-label proposition is the interesting hook — they have shown willingness to bundle into others' brands. A "PrivateFly-powered" leg inside a Latitude 43 trip is technically simple. The other direction (PrivateFly bundling Latitude 43 destination services) is the more interesting one.

**Specific gap:** Anywhere off the aircraft.

### 9. Stratos Jet Charters

**Business model:** ARGUS-certified broker. Soar and Soar Higher fixed-rate membership programs (6 or 12 month) covering five aircraft classes, no fuel/de-icing/repositioning/pet surcharges. **In 2024 pivoted to a franchise model** — Stratos no longer sells charters directly to most clients, instead licensing its Flight Management System (FMS) to franchisee brokers ([globenewswire.com](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/03/13/2845558/0/en/Stratos-Jets-Launches-World-s-First-Private-Air-Charter-Franchising-Program.html), [globenewswire.com](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/09/03/3143798/0/en/Stratos-Jets-Welcomes-Its-12th-Franchise-as-Demand-for-Franchise-Ownership-Continues-to-Surge.html)). 12 franchisees by Sept 2025.

**Customer profile:** Business-executive frequent flyers, post-pivot mostly funneled through franchisee brokers.

**Geographic footprint:** US.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium-low directly; high via franchisees.** Each Stratos franchisee is effectively a one- or two-person brokerage with no destination capability. They are *exactly* the kind of small partner who would happily refer a Latitude 43 retainer in exchange for a referral fee. Build a Stratos-franchisee referral program — 12 partners today, scaling.

**Specific gap:** Anything not aircraft.

### 10. Solairus Aviation

**Business model:** Aircraft management + charter for the managed fleet. 355+ aircraft under management, 14 locations, $75M annual revenue per 2025 disclosure (some sources cite higher figures including managed assets) ([solairus.aero](https://www.solairus.aero/), [zoominfo.com](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/solairus-aviation-co/352822416)). Added ~50 aircraft in 2025, 36 of which were ULR/heavy.

**Customer profile:** Aircraft owners (the principals own the jet, Solairus runs it), plus charter clients flying the managed fleet.

**Geographic footprint:** US, with international charter capability.

**Partnership viability:** **High but oblique.** Solairus's *real* customer is the principal who owns the airplane — and that principal also owns houses, has a family office or not, travels internationally, and has all the same gaps as a Vista subscriber. The owner-services team at Solairus is small; a Latitude 43 partnership at the management-company level reaches 355 ultra-affluent principals directly. The outreach: "We are your owners' destination team."

**Specific gap:** The aircraft is managed; the rest of the principal's life is not.

**Business model:** Multi-line: aircraft management (~300 globally, ~200 US, recently added 16 jets to US charter fleet in 2024), FBO network, completions, MRO (hubs Basel/Singapore/Dubai), staffing ([jetaviation.com](https://www.jetaviation.com/perspectives/jet-aviation-grows-us-aircraft-management-and-charter-offerings/), [bjtonline.com](https://www.bjtonline.com/company/jet-aviation)). 4,000 employees, 50 locations. Subsidiary of General Dynamics.

**Customer profile:** Institutional. Principals, charter operators, OEMs, governments.

**Geographic footprint:** Global.

**Partnership viability:** **Low.** Too institutional, too large, too B2B. Useful as an FBO touchpoint (Latitude 43 staff knowing which JA FBO to call in a given city), not as a commercial partner.

### 12. Amalfi Jets (the founder's reference)

**Business model:** Broker (no owned aircraft) with a jet-card and membership wrap. Network of 3,500 aircraft across 170+ countries. ~$120M scale reported. Built on TikTok-first marketing — founder Kolin Jones has 4M+ followers across platforms ([leadersmag.com](https://www.leadersmag.com/issues/2026.2_Apr/HOS/LEADERS_Kolin_Jones_Amalfi_Jets.html), [ibtimes.com](https://www.ibtimes.com/24-year-old-built-120m-luxury-jet-business-no-planes-uses-tiktok-fame-win-clients-3801810)).

**Customer profile:** Younger UHNW, social-first, athletes, creators, entertainment principals. The buyer who finds private aviation through Instagram, not their CFO.

**Partnership viability:** **High and culturally compatible.** Amalfi's audience is the exact buyer who has aviation solved but not destination, ground, or visa — and is most likely to ask their concierge in a DM, not on a phone call. Latitude 43 fits as the destination team Amalfi recommends in caption or DM. The cultural register (modern, founder-led, social-fluent) maps to Latitude 43's own brand more naturally than any of the legacy operators.

**Specific gap:** Everything off-aircraft, particularly in international destinations where Amalfi's network is broad but their service depth is shallow.

## B. Luxury lifestyle and concierge services

The concierge category has *consolidated* in a way that creates Latitude 43's most defensible adjacent positioning. Two facts dominate:

1. Two vendors (Ten Lifestyle Group, Aspire Lifestyles) run the back-end concierge for almost every premium credit card and most bank private-client programs globally. They are call-center businesses with no continuity model.
2. The high-end consumer concierges (Quintessentially, Knightsbridge Circle, John Paul, One Concierge, Pure Entertainment Group) are *general* lifestyle businesses where travel is one vertical among many, and where the dedicated travel work is usually outsourced to a back-office TMC or hotel-rate consolidator.

Latitude 43 is structurally narrower (travel only) and structurally deeper (named property GMs, multi-leg orchestration, IROPs handling) than any of them.

### 1. Quintessentially

**Business model:** Tiered annual membership. Dedicated tier ~£7,000+VAT/yr. Quintessence/Elite up to £27,500+VAT. USD equivalents $5,500–$45,000/yr ([quintessentially.com](https://quintessentially.com/membership), [alltopstartups.com](https://alltopstartups.com/2020/01/02/quintessentially-a-concierge-service-company-with-202m-turnover/)). ~$202M turnover (last published).

**Customer profile:** UK-led UHNW, plus Middle Eastern, Asian, and Russian-adjacent wealth. The buyer who wants the *category symbol* of belonging to a concierge as much as the service itself.

**Pricing:** $5.5K–$45K/yr disclosed; rumored six-figure private tiers.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium-high.** Quintessentially historically outsources hard travel work to TMC partners. Latitude 43's pitch is: "When your Quintessentially manager needs a US property handled with operator-grade depth, we are the team."

**Brand register:** British, generalist-luxury, slightly old-world. Marketing-heavy.

**Specific gap:** Travel depth. A Quintessentially manager will book your Cipriani table; they won't be the one who knows the on-duty manager at the Carlyle on a Tuesday in February.

### 2. Knightsbridge Circle

**Business model:** Ultra-selective. Standard tier £25,000/yr. Elite tier £100,000/yr (launched 2023) — billed as "world's most expensive concierge service" ([luxuriousmagazine.com](https://www.luxuriousmagazine.com/knightsbridge-circle-elite-tier-service/), [tempusmagazine.co.uk](https://tempusmagazine.co.uk/news/knightsbridge-circle-launches-worlds-most-expensive-concierge-service/)). 1:4 staff-to-member ratio (industry's highest).

**Customer profile:** Global 0.01%. Heads of state, dynastic family heads, royalty. Knightsbridge famously secured private COVID-19 vaccinations for members in 2020–21.

**Partnership viability:** **Low directly, high as a benchmark.** KC is the brand Latitude 43 should *not* try to displace; it occupies a tier so high that referrals would be one-off. But KC's pricing structure (£100K/yr for true 1:4 service) is the strongest market evidence that Latitude 43's retainer model is *underpriced relative to comparable depth*.

**Brand register:** Restrained, British, never sells.

**Specific gap:** Geographic. KC is London-led; US-grounded principals get a London-time-zone service.

### 3. John Paul (Accor)

**Business model:** White-label B2B concierge. Acquired by Accor 2016. Three lines: premium concierge, experiences/events, loyalty program design ([press.accor.com](https://press.accor.com/accorhotels-finalizes-acquisition-of-john-paulthe-world-leader-in-the-concierge-market/?lang=eng), [johnpaul.com](https://www.johnpaul.com/en/delight/john-paul-accor-group-business-accelerator)). 1,000+ employees, 50,000+ partner network, 50+ countries. Tech-enabled concierge with proprietary CRM. Merged with LesConcierges (US) in 2015.

**Customer profile:** B2B — financial services, automotive, health/pharma, luxury brands, large employers running employee perks.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium-low.** John Paul's positioning *is* "we are the white-label that powers the bank's concierge" — they would view Latitude 43 as either a competitor (for a card program) or a sub-vendor (if Latitude is providing the travel layer below their concierge). The interesting play is *exactly* the second: pitch Latitude 43 as the high-touch travel escalation behind a John Paul desk, brandless to the cardholder.

### 4. Velocity Black (Capital One)

**Business model:** AI-powered concierge app — chat-driven, with human experts behind. Acquired by Capital One June 2023 to compete with Amex Centurion's "Insider" / Chase Sapphire Reserve concierge ([capitalone.com](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/velocity-black-acquisition/), [bankingdive.com](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/capital-one-acquires-digital-concierge-velocity-black/652264/)). Now bundled into Capital One Venture X and forthcoming premium card tiers.

**Customer profile:** Capital One premium cardholders — younger affluent, mobile-native.

**Partnership viability:** **Low directly, high via the cardholder.** Capital One owns the experience; Latitude 43 won't be the named partner inside the app. But Velocity Black customers are *exactly* the buyer who will hit the wall when they need true multi-leg orchestration, and is one search away from looking outside the app.

### 5. Aspire Lifestyles (International SOS)

**Business model:** Outsourced concierge. ~2,000 staff in 21 countries, 30 languages. Powers Citi Prestige and many regional bank-card concierges ([aspirelifestyles.com](https://www.aspirelifestyles.com/about/), [aspirelifestyles.com/history](https://www.aspirelifestyles.com/history/)). Owned by International SOS, which gives them a real medical-and-security adjacency.

**Customer profile:** B2B — banks, insurance, luxury retail, automotive, luxury property. The end-user is the issuer's cardholder.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium.** Same logic as John Paul — Latitude 43 sits below them in the stack, taking the hard escalations the call-center cannot solve. The International SOS connection is the more interesting hook (see §H).

### 6. Ten Lifestyle Group (LON:TENG)

**Business model:** Publicly listed (LSE) outsourced concierge. FY2026 revenue ~$66.6M (per RocketReach / public filings). 50+ corporate clients including HSBC, Coutts, Royal Bank of Canada, Westpac ([tenlifestylegroup.com](https://tenlifestylegroup.com/what-ten-does/), [tenlifestylegroup.com/investors](https://tenlifestylegroup.com/investors/)). Operates concierge for Visa Infinite, Mastercard World Elite, Mastercard Black globally. Reports "3x ROI" for corporate clients.

**Customer profile:** B2B at issuer level; millions of end-cardholders.

**Partnership viability:** **Highest in subcategory.** Ten is publicly listed and explicitly seeking margin on member engagement. Latitude 43 is the kind of "premium escalation" Ten could refer to *for fee* when a Visa Infinite member's request exceeds the call-center scope. Outreach should be at the BD level, not the call-center floor.

### 7. Pure Entertainment Group (Montreal)

**Customer profile:** International HNW/UHNW with sports-and-fashion-event taste.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium.** Direct competitor on aspiration, partner on logistics. Pure books a Wimbledon hospitality package; Latitude 43 handles the hotel, transfers, and side trip the client appends to it.

### 8. One Concierge

**Business model:** Tiered membership $3,000–$25,000/yr; Custom Lifestyle Management Program from $20,000/yr (1-year minimum); virtual assistants $1,200/mo (PT) – $2,400/mo (FT) ([oneconcierge.com](https://www.oneconcierge.com/services/lifestyle/lifestyle-management/), [virtualassistantassistant.com](https://www.virtualassistantassistant.com/one-concierge/)). 24/7 multi-channel (text, WhatsApp, phone).

**Customer profile:** US-based affluent professionals, family-office adjacent.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium-high.** One Concierge sits in the same price band as Latitude 43 and is similarly travel-heavy but broader (errands, personal admin, gifting). They are more likely to be a referral partner than a competitor — they hand off the heavy travel, Latitude 43 hands off the personal admin.

## C. Family office travel services

Family offices are not travel companies, but they are the *gatekeeper* to most of Latitude 43's target buyers — the principals who fly Vista, who hold Centurion or Coutts cards, who own the second home in St. Barts. Three sub-types matter:

- **Multi-family offices (MFOs)** — Bessemer Trust, Stonehage Fleming, Rockefeller Capital, Hawthorn (PNC).
- **Single-family offices (SFOs)** — typically run by a 4–20 person team; travel is one piece of an Operations Director's portfolio.
- **The "outsourced family office" wave** — RIAs and wealth firms scaling family-office-style services downward (see §E).

For Latitude 43, the structural fact is: **MFOs serve too many families to do travel deeply themselves**, and SFOs *do* the travel themselves with one harried operations person. Both are addressable.

### 1. Bessemer Trust

**Business model:** Independent MFO, founded 1907. ~$140B AUM for 2,500+ families/foundations. 19 regional offices. 3:1 client-to-employee ratio, 27-year average specialist tenure, 98% 10-yr asset retention ([bessemertrust.com](https://www.bessemertrust.com/), [bessemertrust.com/key-facts](https://www.bessemertrust.com/what-makes-us-different/key-facts)).

**Customer profile:** Ultra-stable multi-generation wealth.

**Travel coordination:** A small "concierge / lifestyle" services pocket exists inside the family-office services line, but it's not a deep capability. Travel happens *around* Bessemer relationships, not inside them.

**Partnership viability:** **High but slow-burn.** Bessemer's relationship managers are exactly the right referral source for Latitude 43, but the firm moves at family-office speed. Strategy: target individual offices and named RMs, not corporate BD.

---

### 2. Stonehage Fleming

**Business model:** EMEA's largest MFO. ~$45B AUM. Offices in 8 countries. Manages family properties, art, aircraft, boats, philanthropy ([stonehagefleming.com](https://www.stonehagefleming.com/services/family-office-1), [andsimple.co](https://andsimple.co/family-offices/stonehage-fleming/)). Acts as "outsourced COO" for UHNW families.

**Customer profile:** EMEA-headquartered multi-generation families with global asset bases.

**Partnership viability:** **Highest in subcategory for a US/EU bridge play.** Stonehage's families fly between London, Geneva, Riyadh, and New York routinely. Their internal team handles aviation and properties; the *trip glue* (US ground, US property pre-arrival, US restaurant reservations) is hard for a Zurich-based team to do well. Latitude 43 is the US-side ground partner.

---

### 3. Rockefeller Capital Management

**Business model:** Independent wealth/family-office firm, founded 2018 (re-platformed from the original Rockefeller family office). Lifestyle Advisory program is explicit, with curated partners across health, security, passions, legacy ([rockco.com](https://www.rockco.com/rockefeller-global-family-office/)). 12-month curriculum for family members originally designed for the 7th-generation Rockefellers.

**Customer profile:** UHNW US families with multi-generation horizons.

**Partnership viability:** **Highest in category.** "Lifestyle Advisory partners" *is* a partnership program with a procurement function. Latitude 43 should pursue this directly — it is the cleanest, most institutionalized path to becoming a referred provider for several hundred UHNW US families.

### 4. Hawthorn (PNC Private Bank)

**Business model:** Multi-family office inside PNC Bank, founded 1997. Serves families >$20M investable. Headquartered Philadelphia. Institute for Family Success runs governance and education programming ([pnc.com](https://www.pnc.com/en/personal-banking/private-bank/hawthorn.html), [pnc.com/about](https://www.pnc.com/en/personal-banking/private-bank/hawthorn/about.html)).

**Customer profile:** $20M+ Northeast/Mid-Atlantic US families.

**Partnership viability:** **Medium.** Bank-owned MFOs are slower to onboard external partners than independents, but the Institute for Family Success has external-partner programming that maps to Latitude 43's positioning.

### Single-family office travel coordinators

This is the unsexy gold mine. The structural reality of a 4–20 person SFO is that *one person* (typically titled Family Office Manager, Director of Operations, or Personal Assistant to the Principal) handles travel. Latitude 43's pitch to that person:

- Frees up 8–15 hours a week of trip-prep.
- Adds named property relationships the SFO does not have.
- Costs less than hiring an additional FT operations hire.

This is a *direct outbound* play, not an MFO-style partnership. The named role to target is the **Family Office Operations Director** — found via FOX (Family Office Exchange) member lists, FOI conferences, and LinkedIn searches for "Director of Operations, family office."

## G. Expat / nomad / remote-work travel

This category has cratered in the last two years. The two giants — Selina and Remote Year — are both bankrupt or shuttered. The remaining viable model is *not* full-stack co-living: it's lighter, members-network co-working/co-stay layers (Outsite) and pure remote-friendly hotel/serviced-apartment booking (Sondder, FlexLiving, Blueground).

### 1. Selina (insolvent)

**History:** Hostels-plus-coworking-plus-events brand. Once $1.2B valuation, near 30,000 beds across ~150 properties at peak ([skift.com](https://skift.com/2024/07/22/selina-collapses-in-liquidity-crisis-seeks-buyers/), [travelweekly.com](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Hotel-News/Selina-Hospitality-headed-for-insolvency)). Filed for insolvency July 2024, acquired by Singapore-based Collective Hospitality 5 weeks later ([skift.com](https://skift.com/2024/08/27/selina-sold-out-of-insolvency-5-weeks-after-collapse-exclusive/)).

**Lesson for Latitude 43:** Full-stack hospitality for nomads is operationally brutal — real estate leases, F&B, programming, all on volatile demand. The asset-light service layer (curation, booking, support) is the defensible position.

### 2. Remote Year (shut down December 2024)

**History:** Curated cohort travel ($2K–$3K/mo) — housing, coworking, and community programming as professionals traveled month-to-month between cities. Acquired by Collective Hospitality with Selina; shut down in Dec 2024 after Collective declined to fund ([skift.com](https://skift.com/2024/12/21/remote-year-shuts-down-despite-new-owners-earlier-promises-of-a-bright-future/), [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/19/remote-year-which-helps-you-work-while-traveling-the-world-lays-off-50-of-staff/)). 1,000+ active participants left scrambling.

**Lesson:** The cohort-travel model is non-resilient. A more durable proposition: independent professionals who occasionally need long-stay coordination, *without* a curated cohort.

### 3. Outsite

**Pricing:** $149/yr annual, $399 lifetime. Monthly stays $1,400–$3,500 depending on location ([outsite.co](https://www.outsite.co/), [outsite.co/how-it-works](https://www.outsite.co/how-it-works)). Discounts scale with length-of-stay.

**Customer profile:** Self-directed remote professionals, mid-career, mostly individual.

**Partnership viability:** **Low.** Outsite's buyer pays $149/yr and chooses their own destination — they are price-conscious by definition. Not a Latitude 43 buyer.

### G summary

The nomad-and-coliving category is **not a Latitude 43 partnership opportunity** in any volume. It is, however, a useful *competitive negative* — Latitude 43's clients are *not* nomads; they are professionals with stable home bases who travel for stretches of weeks, not years. Saying "we do not serve digital nomads" sharpens the positioning.

**Partnership viability:** **High.** ISOS has the medical-and-security layer; Latitude 43 has the trip-glue and accommodation-handoff layer. An ISOS contract that *bundles* Latitude 43 as a "premium operational support" line for executive travelers is a real product.

**Pricing:** From $119 (7-day) or $329 (annual) for travel membership. High-Altitude Evacuation Package $495 individual / $995 family. **Customer profile:** Outdoor / expedition travelers, plus business and family travelers buying low-cost peace of mind.

**Partnership viability:** **High.** Global Rescue's product is *narrow* (rescue and evacuation). Cost is also asymmetric — Global Rescue is $329/yr, Latitude 43 retainers are several orders of magnitude higher — so the cross-sell flows naturally toward affluent Global Rescue members.

### 3. Crisis24 (GardaWorld)

**Business model:** Integrated risk management, crisis response, consulting, global protective solutions. Replaced International SOS at Stanford for travel risk ([crisis24.com](https://www.crisis24.com/solutions/medical-evacuation-assistance), [internationaltravel.stanford.edu](https://internationaltravel.stanford.edu/)). 1.2M vetted medical providers, 24/7 multilingual hotline.

**Business model:** Travel insurance + assistance, 30+ years operating, 15M travelers/year. Standard duty-of-care insurance and assistance providers. Similar to WTP. Lower priority partnerships.

### H summary

Crisis travel is **Latitude 43's most credible upstream partner category** for a defensible bundled offering. Two named partnerships (Global Rescue + International SOS or Crisis24) constitute a complete *duty-of-care + operational-care* package that no current single vendor offers.

**Business model:** Online visa expediting. Embassy fee + service fee (e.g., UK Business ETA $23 embassy + $59 service). White-label product for TMCs ([visahq.com](https://www.visahq.com/), [visahq.com/tmc](https://www.visahq.com/tmc/)).

### J summary

**Latitude 43 counter:** Travel-only positioning. The buyer who wants *more* travel depth, not less, will choose Latitude 43 over a generalist.

### 3. Pure Entertainment Group

**Why dangerous:** Bespoke luxury concierge with strong event hooks (Wimbledon, Roland Garros, Monaco). The aspirational HNW/UHNW buyer who already has aviation might pick Pure for the whole package.

**Latitude 43 counter:** Operational depth versus event-glamour positioning. Pure trades on access; Latitude 43 trades on execution.

### 4. The "founder concierge" of any new fintech card

None will solve hard cases, but all will *occupy the airspace* in a buyer's mind.

**Latitude 43 counter:** Always present as the *escalation layer*, not the *alternative*. The buyer should think "my card gets me the easy stuff; Latitude 43 gets me the hard stuff."

### 5. The high-end "outsourced family-office travel coordinator"

A growing micro-category — boutique firms positioning explicitly as "we handle travel for SFOs." They will compete with Latitude 43 directly on RFPs.

**Latitude 43 counter:** Scale and named relationships. The boutique firm has one operations director and 200 hotel contacts; Latitude 43 should have a real bench.

## N. The cross-ecosystem flywheel — a strategic note

Latitude 43's most defensible long-term position is not "the best concierge" or "the best TMC." It is **the connective tissue between four ecosystems that all touch the same person and none of which talk to each other.** Aviation runs the airplane. The card concierge runs the restaurant reservation. The family office runs the financial life. The visa expediter runs the document. The crisis vendor runs the evacuation. *Nobody runs the trip.*

The buyer feels this absence as a constant low-grade tax. They have a $215,000 NetJets card, a $695 Amex Platinum (or $15K Centurion) concierge, a $4,000-a-month Athena EA, a $329 Global Rescue membership, a CIBT corporate visa account, and a Bessemer Trust relationship — and every individual trip still demands six phone calls, three tabs of personal coordination, and one inevitable failure of handoff at a hotel front desk in a city where nobody knows them. Each vendor is technically doing its job. The trip is still bad.

The flywheel proposition for Latitude 43 is this. Latitude 43 is *named* by each of those upstream vendors as their operational layer. The NetJets owner-services team escalates to Latitude 43 when an owner needs a non-network hotel held open at 11pm. The Athena EA escalates when a delegated trip exceeds their template. The Bessemer RM hands off the trip because the RM is a wealth manager, not a logistics operator. The Global Rescue customer-success team cross-sells Latitude 43 at upgrade time. Ten Lifestyle's call-center floor flags premium escalations to Latitude 43 with a fee. CIBT's TMC API powers the visa layer underneath. None of these relationships are exclusive. None require Latitude 43 to be the brand the buyer sees first. All of them route the same kind of buyer — the principal whose life is partially solved and structurally fragmented — to Latitude 43 at the moment of greatest pain.

Three reasons this flywheel is durable.

First, **none of the upstream vendors want to build it themselves.** NetJets does not want to be a hotel operator. Athena does not want to be a TMC. Bessemer does not want to take on operational risk. Global Rescue does not want to manage front-of-house logistics. Each of them has a strong reason to *refer out* the function and a strong reason *not* to verticalize it. Latitude 43 is the negative-space partner.

Second, **the buyer overlap is real and quantifiable.** A NetJets Card275 holder has an 80%+ probability of also holding Amex Platinum or Centurion, a 50%+ probability of being a multi-family-office client, a 30%+ probability of holding Global Rescue or an equivalent, and a near-100% probability of having an EA — whether in-house, Athena, or Belay. The same person crosses every adjacent ecosystem in this document. Each referral channel is non-exclusive; they all point at the same buyer.

Third, **the proposition gets more defensible as the network grows.** With one upstream partner, Latitude 43 is a vendor. With three, Latitude 43 is a recommended provider. With ten, Latitude 43 is the *default* operational layer of the premium-travel stack — the company that every premium-card concierge, every aviation operator's owner services team, every family-office operations director, and every founder's EA already knows by name. At that point, the customer-acquisition cost goes near zero, and the moat is the relationship graph, not the technology.

The pre-condition for the flywheel is operator credibility. Latitude 43 will not be referred by NetJets or Rockefeller until Latitude 43 can show specific case studies — IROPs handled, sold-out properties opened, multi-leg complexity resolved — at a rate of execution that survives a partner's reputational exposure. The first 12 months of partnership work is therefore not BD; it is operational proof. Five real escalations handled cleanly per partner, documented, citable. Then the flywheel.

The competitive risk to the flywheel is consolidation upstream. If a single buyer — likely a large card issuer or a private-aviation operator with ambitions — were to acquire one of Athena, Global Rescue, and a concierge brand, the bundle moves from "Latitude 43 as connective tissue" to "Latitude 43 as a feature inside someone else's stack." The defense is to be *contracted into* multiple parallel partners early, so that no single consolidator can sweep the field. The aviation, family-office, EA, and crisis ecosystems are all structurally fragmented today. They will not be in five years. The window to be the connective tissue is now.

That window is also why Latitude 43 should *not* try to build any of the adjacent capabilities in-house. Building a visa-expediting team would be margin-dilutive and would close off CIBT as a partner. Building a medical-evacuation network would be uninsurable and would close off Global Rescue. Building a concierge call-center would make Ten Lifestyle a competitor instead of a referrer. The discipline is to stay narrow on the travel-ops function, partner-thick across the rest, and earn the connective-tissue position by being the only operator the upstream partners trust to *not* compete with them.

The position is not glamorous. The connective tissue is rarely the brand on the front page; the brand on the front page is usually the airplane, the bank, the card, or the family-office logo. The connective tissue is the company that gets paid every time the airplane lands and the bank doesn't have a hotel and the card concierge can't pull off the suite and the family-office RM has another fire to fight. That is the position Latitude 43 should hold for the next decade.
