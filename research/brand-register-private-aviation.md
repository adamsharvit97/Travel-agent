## 1. NetJets — netjets.com

The category leader. Owned by Berkshire Hathaway. The grown-up in the room.

**Headline copy (verbatim, above the fold):** *"Experience The Bombardier Global 7500/8000."* (The home page leads with an aircraft, not a promise.) Sub-line: *"Explore the Possibilities — We have the right solution to fit your unique travel needs."*

**CTAs (verbatim):** *"Request Information"* and *"Call Us."* Notice the absence of "Buy," "Book," "Sign Up," or "Get Started." The verbs are reception-desk verbs.

**Navigation labels:** *Unmatched Experience · Safety · Service · Access · Exclusives · Our Programs & Aircraft.* Each is one or two words. None is clever.

**Tagline:** *"The Promise of Always."* It appears in the Access section, italic, set down. It is not asked to do too much.

**Membership / tier structure:** *NetJets Share*, *NetJets Card*, *NetJets Lease* (with the legacy Marquis Jet Card folded into the Card product post-acquisition). Each tier is a noun, not a benefit. The page that compares them is called *"Compare Shares & Cards"* — not "Pricing," not "Plans."

**Tone:** Restrained to the point of severity. No exclamation marks. No "we're excited." Sentences end with periods. Adjectives are used sparingly — "*Dedicated*," "*Beyond*," "*Unmatched*" — and never stacked.

**Pricing visibility:** Hidden. The link is called *"Understand Our Pricing,"* which is a deeply NetJets sentence: pricing is something you are educated about, not quoted on.

**Proof elements:** Absent from the hero. No testimonial slider, no Trustpilot widget, no press logos above the fold. The proof is the brand itself — a Buffett property does not need a five-star review. Lower down, mentions of the 60-year history and FAA Part 121-equivalent safety standard appear.

**Photography:** Single cinematic exterior shots of a Global 7500 on a clean ramp. No happy families. No cabin selfies. Often the aircraft is shown alone, at golden hour, on a wet apron.

**What's conspicuously absent:** Founder visibility (Buffett is referenced in interviews, never on the home page). No live chat. No "starting from" prices. No urgency language. No discounts. No countdowns. No "new" badges.

**Sources:** [netjets.com](https://www.netjets.com); [Compare Shares & Cards](https://www.netjets.com/en-us/programs-and-aircraft).

## 2. VistaJet — vistajet.com

Thomas Flohr's subscription-only fleet. The brand that taught the industry restraint can also be charismatic.

**Headline copy (verbatim):** *"Flying private made simple."* Sub-line, in small caps: *"MEMBERSHIP WITHOUT JOINING FEES. SIMPLY PAY FOR THE HOURS YOU NEED."*

That sub-line is the most important sentence on a private-aviation home page right now. It does three things at once: it positions VistaJet against NetJets (no joining fees), it commits to a transaction model (hours), and it does it in small caps so that the *manner* of the promise is itself a piece of luxury vocabulary.

**CTAs (verbatim):** *"Plan a flight," "View membership," "Explore the fleet," "EXPLORE THE GLOBAL 8000," "MAKE AN INQUIRY."* Two registers — sentence case for utilities, small caps for moments of weight.

**Navigation:** *Memberships · Fleet · Experience · Private World · About Us · News · Contact Us · Careers · Log In.* "Private World" is the brand-defining nav entry — it is the name of their content universe and partner program, not a product.

**Membership / tier structure:** *Program* (subscription, three-year commitment, 50+ hours/year), *VJ25* (25-hour jet card), *Corporate Program*. The word *Program* is doing the work that "membership" does at NetJets. It is institutional, not consumer.

**Tone:** Restrained but warm. The signature line of body copy on the home page: *"Renowned for its consistency, the distinctive silver and red fleet is equipped for all needs — whether you need a fully enabled business suite or a restful home away from home space."* (vistajet.com) Notice "renowned" (not "world-class"), "distinctive" (not "iconic"), "restful" (not "luxurious").

**Pricing visibility:** Disclosed but downplayed. *"Private jet charter flight prices are subject to the market rate and start from $11,000 per hour."* (vistajet.com home page footer). Hourly rates on the Program are not on the home page; trade press fills the gap — *"Challenger 350 from $15,000 per hour; Global 5000 from $18,000 per hour"* ([Private Jet Card Comparisons](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/vistajet/)).

**Proof elements:** Operational, not testimonial. *"Cabin crew trained by British Butler Institute and Norland College."* *"24/7 concierge service."* Norland is a 130-year-old British nanny college — a sub-cultural marker so specific that knowing it is itself a proof of audience.

**Founder visibility:** High but editorial. Thomas Flohr is profiled at *Spear's*, *Business Jet Traveler*, *McKinsey*, *CEOWORLD* and *Financial Times* — not on the home page. His CMO Matteo Atti gave a useful framing in *Luxuria Lifestyle*: *"Private World marks the start of a radical transformation of the top end of the travel industry — a shift in the business model not governed anymore by volumes of booking but led by quality, trust and shared values."* That sentence is the brand thesis.

**Photography:** The signature silver-and-red livery, almost always shown on a wet ramp or against a slate sky. Cabin interiors are empty — no passengers — which is the most Vista photographic decision of all. The aircraft is a stage that you, the member, will populate.

**What's conspicuously absent:** Comparison charts. Discount language. Stock photography. Anything sold "from $X/month." Loyalty perks. Sponsored badges.

**CTA pattern:** *"Make an Inquiry"* is the master CTA. Never "Book," never "Join."

**Sources:** [vistajet.com/en-us](https://www.vistajet.com/en-us/); [Private World expansion](https://www.vistajet.com/en-us/news/vistajet-expands-its-private-world-portfolio/); [Matteo Atti interview, Luxuria Lifestyle](https://www.luxurialifestyle.com/luxuria-lifestyle-international-interviews-matteo-atti-cmo-of-vistajet/); [McKinsey, *Travel Disruptors*](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/travel/our-insights/travel-disruptors-vistas-thomas-flohr-on-business-aviation-as-a-subscription-service); [Flohr profile, Spear's](https://spearswms.com/luxury/private-aviation/thomas-flohr-vistajet-liquid-lunch/).

## 3. XO Global — flyxo.com

The "modern" register of private aviation. Vista-owned. Mobile-first. The brand that broke the convention that private aviation cannot look like a fintech app.

**Headline copy (verbatim):** *"Experience the Freedom to Fly."* Sub: *"The Easy Way to Fly Private."*

Both lines tell you XO is positioning against the seriousness of NetJets and the restraint of VistaJet. "Easy" is a word the older operators do not use.

**CTAs (verbatim):** *"Search," "Get the XO mobile app," "Explore the fleet," "Contact Slate."* "Slate" is the name of their shuttle/shared-flight product — a confident piece of sub-brand naming.

**Navigation:** *Private Jet · The Fleet · Charter Membership · Member Benefits · Shared Flights · How XO Works · Ways to Fly · Jet Deals · The XO Experience.* Two nav entries — *"Jet Deals"* and *"How XO Works"* — are anathema to the NetJets register. They tell you XO is, deliberately, the gateway tier.

**Tagline:** *"XO is the global private aviation network, providing access to more than 2,000 hand-selected aircraft across all cabin classes."* It is a statement of network density, not of feeling. The number does the work.

**Membership / tier structure:** *Charter Membership*, *Corporate Membership*, *Shared Flights*, *On-Demand Charter*. Tiers are named by *use case*, not by status.

**Tone:** Aspirational but operational. The voice picks up some XO-only words — *"unrivaled," "premium aircraft," "in seconds," "24/7 aviation experts"* — that the upper tier (Vista, NetJets) would never use.

**Pricing visibility:** Hidden on the home page, but their Jet Deals page surfaces last-minute one-way fares — this is the most transactional thing in the genre.

**Proof elements:** Numbers, not voices. *"2,200+ Aircraft Worldwide. 60,000 routes worldwide. 32% annual traffic growth."* Certifications: *Wyvern, NBAA, IS-BAO, Argus, EBAA*. Five acronyms shown as a row of marks.

**What's conspicuously absent:** A founder. (Flohr owns it; he is not on the XO site.) Editorial content. A magazine. A "Private World."

**Sources:** [flyxo.com](https://www.flyxo.com); [Vista Global news on XO Asia expansion](https://vistaglobal.com/news/vista-brings-xo-to-asia-strengthening-flying-solutions-amid-continued-demand-for-vistajet/).

## 4. Wheels Up — wheelsup.com

The IPO'd, de-listed, Delta-partnered, formerly-Kenny-Dichter brand. The lesson here is what *not* to do: when a private-aviation brand positions itself as "democratizing," it forfeits the register.

The live page returned 403 to direct fetching, so this is drawn from investor announcements, trade press, and the [Private Jet Card Comparisons profile](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/wheels-up-aviation/).

**Membership / tier structure:** *Connect* (entry, $2,995/year), *Core* ($17,500–$25,000 initiation + $15,000 annual + $100,000 deposit), *UP for Business* ($29,500 first-year initiation, $14,500 annual thereafter). All disclosed publicly. ([Wheels Up Connect launch](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wheels-up-launches-newest-membership-offering-with-wheels-up-connect-a-new-tier-democratizing-private-aviation-300798857.html))

**Tone (from investor materials):** *"Wheels Up Launches Newest Membership Offering with Wheels Up Connect, a New Tier Democratizing Private Aviation."* The word *democratizing* is exactly the register Latitude 43 must avoid. *"Wheels Up Introduces a Seamless, Concierge-Level Customer Engagement Model Under a Single Brand"* (investors.wheelsup.com, 2026) — the sentence is corporate, not categorical.

**Proof:** Delta Air Lines partnership, Delta SkyMiles integration, FoundersCard, hotel discounts. Loyalty-program proof — *retail* proof — not category proof.

**What it shows that the others hide:** Member benefit grids. Discount rates. Hourly rates by aircraft class.

**What this teaches:** The vocabulary of "democratize," "accessible," "for everyone" is the inverse of the vocabulary of the register Latitude 43 wants. Wheels Up sells access. NetJets and VistaJet sell admission.

**Sources:** [wheelsup.com](https://www.wheelsup.com); [Investor Relations](https://investors.wheelsup.com/overview/default.aspx); [Wheels Up 2026 profile, PJCC](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/wheels-up-aviation/).

## 5. Jet Linx Aviation — jetlinx.com

The geographic counter-positioning. Local terminals, not a national operations center.

**Tagline (verbatim, master line of the brand):** *"Your Personal Jet Company."* It runs in their nav bar, their title tag, and their voice email greetings. It is repeated as: *"This is private aviation, done differently. It's Your Personal Jet Company."* The phrase *Your Personal* is a deliberate move against NetJets-scale anonymity.

**Membership / tier structure:** *Jet Card Membership* and *Aircraft Management*. The defining structural concept is the *Base* — *"more than 20 local Base locations nationwide, giving localized, personalized service from wherever you call home to wherever you want to go."* Each Base has *"local customer service, local planes, local pilots, and a local private terminal."* The word *local* is repeated four times in one sentence on the FAQ page — deliberate, not accidental.

**Founding mythology:** From Omaha, Nebraska — itself a Buffett-shaped piece of geographic counter-positioning. Grew by partnering with *"Base Partners,"* successful local individuals in each new market.

**Tone:** Warm, plain, mid-American. *"Personal,"* not *"private."* *"Company,"* not *"network."*

**Pricing visibility:** Hidden. Jet Card Membership pricing only on inquiry.

**Proof:** *Top 1% safety ratings.* Argus Platinum. NBAA. A 90-Day Access Jet Card option for new members.

**What this teaches Latitude 43:** The "from-a-place" positioning is a recognised category move in private aviation. Toronto/43°N is Latitude 43's Omaha — but only if we make the geographic register *the* register, not a decoration. Jet Linx puts *"local"* in every fourth sentence. Latitude 43 currently mentions Toronto twice on the home page.

**Sources:** [jetlinx.com](https://jetlinx.com); [Jet Linx Difference](https://jetlinx.com/why-jet-linx/); [Locations](https://jetlinx.com/our-locations/).

## 6. Magellan Jets — magellanjets.com

Boston-based, founder-led, jet card boutique. The clearest example in the category of "boutique register at premium price."

**Headline (verbatim, current rotating hero):** *"Introducing the World's Game Jet Card."* Sub: *"An Exclusive Package Built for the Premier International Football Tournament."* (A summer 2026 Magellan one-off for the World Cup.) The evergreen hero is built around: *"Your Journey, Your Way."*

**CTAs (verbatim):** *"Talk to an advisor," "Build your jet card," "Book an on-demand charter," "Own your private travel solution."* Note: *"advisor,"* not *"agent,"* and *"build,"* not *"buy."*

**Membership / tier structure:** *Jet Card Programs* (25-hour increments), *Fractional Ownership* (Challenger 850), *On-Demand Charter*, *Sales & Management*. Tiers are named by ownership posture, not status word.

**Tone:** Confident but accessible. They will use *"luxurious"* and *"sophisticated"* in the same paragraph — slightly more colourful than VistaJet, considerably more restrained than Wheels Up.

**Pricing visibility:** Mostly hidden, but they break the convention for the limited-edition card: *"$500,000"* shown on the home hero for the World's Game Card. This is the only premium operator currently showing a six-figure number above the fold — and it works, because the number itself becomes a piece of brand vocabulary.

**Proof:** *"4.9/5 satisfaction rating. 90 average NPS score. 100+ countries flown. Debt-free, privately owned."* The financial-stability claim — *debt-free, privately owned* — is a private-aviation-specific proof element after the 2023–24 wave of operator failures. Latitude 43 should note: in this category, financial conservatism is a brand asset.

**Sub-cultural marker:** Their press section references hiring a sommelier from *The French Laundry*. The Norland-College move at VistaJet, exactly.

**What's conspicuously absent:** Boston-the-city. They do not lean on origin. This is a missed opportunity in their brand register and a useful warning for Latitude 43 — origin is brand equity *only if used*.

**Sources:** [magellanjets.com](https://www.magellanjets.com); [Magellan Jets press, Robb Report mentions](https://robbreport.com/topics/magellan-jets/).

## 7. Sentient Jet — sentient.com

Vista-owned. The 25-hour card category-definer. The most editorially polished website of the bunch.

**Headline (verbatim):** *"Discover the Jet Card."*

Three words. Definite article. Period. A masterclass in restraint. It assumes you know what a jet card is; if you don't, you're not the customer.

**Sub-headline:** *"Your gateway to seamless journeys across the U.S., Europe, and to the world's most coveted destinations."*

**Tagline:** *"The destination. The event. The story you'll create."* — three noun phrases separated by periods. This is the cadence of the register: short clauses, full stops, no conjunctions.

**Membership / tier structure:** *SJ25* (light jets, 25 hours) and *SJ25+* (mid, super-mid, large cabin). Two products. Named by code, not by tier label. The code *SJ25* is itself a piece of insider vocabulary — once you know it, you signal you know it.

**Body copy (verbatim):** *"At Sentient, we know your time is your most valuable asset — that's why we've created a simple and seamless booking experience."* The most-quoted line of private-aviation copywriting, used as an archetype.

**Proof:** *Robb Report 2024 Best of the Best*. Rob Gronkowski as brand ambassador. Founded 1999, "25-year industry history."

**Sub-cultural marker:** They have a *"Sentient Press"* section in their footer nav — they publish their own press archive as if it were a brand asset.

**Photography:** Empty cabin interiors, ramp shots at dusk, full-bleed editorial spreads. No people in the cabin photographs.

**Sources:** [sentient.com](https://www.sentient.com); [Sentient launches 25-hour Large Cabin card, Sherpa Report](https://www.sherpareport.com/aircraft/jet-cards/sentient-large-cabin-jet-card.html).

## 8. PrivateFly — privatefly.com

A charter broker, now folded into FXAIR/Flexjet. Useful as the *transactional* end of the register.

**Headline (verbatim, current home):** *"Discover Our New Direction."* Sub: *"PrivateFly is growing globally."*

**CTAs:** *"VISIT FXAIR.COM," "explore our aircraft," "explore destinations," "explore jet card," "SPEAK TO OUR FLIGHT TEAM 24/7," "+44 (0)20 7100 6960."*

That phone number is doing brand work. A live, listed UK number says *"we are not a marketplace; we are an office with a phone that rings."*

**Tagline:** *"Fly your way."*

**Pricing visibility:** Hidden on the home page, but they direct to FXAIR.com to *"Search for a flight and receive estimate prices."* Distinct from the rest of the cohort: they will let you self-serve a price. This is the transactional move — and it pushes the brand down a tier in the mental category.

**Proof:** *"Argus Certified Broker," "safety & service accredited aircraft fleet,"* sister-company reference to Flexjet.

**What this teaches:** A listed phone number with country code and 24/7 promise is a register element on its own. A search-for-a-price box is the opposite register element. Latitude 43 currently has neither.

**Sources:** [privatefly.com](https://www.privatefly.com).

## 9. Amalfi Jets — amalfijets.com (the founder's reference)

Kolin Jones and Calvin Yoon. Founded 2020 in Beverly Hills. *The* TikTok-native private-aviation brand. The reason the Latitude 43 founder said *"adjacent to Amalfi"* — they have managed to make a brand-new brokerage feel like it belongs in the room with NetJets.

**Headline (verbatim):** *"Built Around You."* Sub: *"Exploring the World With You."* The eyebrow above the hero: *"The Charter Provider."* (Definite article: "The," not "A.")

**CTAs (verbatim):** *"Book a flight," "Request a Quote," "Download App," "Learn more," "Explore aircraft."* The "Request a Quote" is in five places. *"Request a Quote"* is the dominant CTA verb in the category — it is reception-desk language without being precious.

**Navigation:** *Programs · Aircraft · Why Amalfi · Leadership Team · Amalfi Technology · On-Demand Charter · Amalfi One Jet Card · Amalfi Reserve.* The nav itself is a proof element: a *Leadership Team* page (rare in young brokerages); *Amalfi Technology* as a sub-brand (legitimises them as more than a phone room); *Amalfi One* and *Amalfi Reserve* as two-tier products that mirror the NetJets Share/Card cadence.

**Tone:** Restrained, founder-confident, slightly more colourful than VistaJet. They use *"elevated"* and *"exclusive"* alongside *"transparent"* and *"consistent."* The transparency-and-consistency pair is the load-bearing rhetorical move — it earns them the right to also say "elevated."

**Pricing visibility:** Completely absent. No rates, no membership costs, no charter pricing on the public site. (Their TikTok shows the prices; their website does not. This is a deliberate two-channel strategy.)

**Proof elements:** *"3,500 aircraft across 170+ countries."* References to *"world-class aircraft"* and *"experienced aviation professionals."* Emphasis on exceeding *"highest global standards."* The number is the proof.

**Founder visibility (the most important section in this study):** *Minimal on the home page. Maximum off the home page.* Kolin Jones has [4M+ followers across platforms](https://flarearticle.com/kolin-jones-net-worth/), 3M+ on TikTok, [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kolinj/), profiles in *Voyage LA Magazine*, *LEADERS Magazine*, and [Aerotime](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/amalfi-jets-ceo-founder-kolin-jones-private-jet-charter). The *Leaders Mag* April 2026 interview is the canonical founder-document. He is also on the Embry-Riddle Industry Advisory Board.

But on amalfijets.com, you find him through a single *"Leadership Team"* nav link. The home page does not have a "Letter from the Founder." This is the Vista move — founder visibility is achieved through earned media, not owned media.

**Visual register:** Black, silver, tan, white. Sans-serif throughout (no serif). Cinematic aircraft close-ups, cockpit interiors, branded hospitality details (a coupe glass, a folded throw, a hand on a yoke). No customer faces. No team photos on the home page.

**What's conspicuously absent:** Pricing. Specific aircraft tail numbers. Customer testimonials. Safety statistics. Competitor comparisons. Founder bios on the home page. Any office address. Any phone number above the fold.

**The Amalfi-adjacent thesis:** The brand achieves the register through (a) one definite-article phrase repeated as an identity — *"The Charter Provider"*; (b) tightly numbered proof — *"3,500 aircraft, 170+ countries"*; (c) a Leadership Team page existing as a credential, not as a sales asset; (d) one sub-brand (*Amalfi Technology*) that turns a phone room into a platform; (e) a website that hides the founder while the founder personally drives a billion impressions a quarter on TikTok.

**Sources:** [amalfijets.com](https://amalfijets.com); [LEADERS Magazine, April 2026](https://www.leadersmag.com/issues/2026.2_Apr/HOS/LEADERS_Kolin_Jones_Amalfi_Jets.html); [Aerotime profile](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/amalfi-jets-ceo-founder-kolin-jones-private-jet-charter); [Voyage LA](https://voyagela.com/interview/community-highlights-meet-kolin-jones-of-amalfi-jets-inc/).

## 10. Solairus Aviation — solairus.aero

The aircraft-management end of the category. Different shape — they don't sell hours, they run your jet. Useful as the "service-not-asset" end of the spectrum.

**Headline (paraphrased from search snippets; the live site returned 503):** *"Safe, Reliable, and Efficient Management for Aircraft Owners."*

**Service-promise copy (verbatim):** *"When you select Solairus Aviation to support the operation of your aircraft, you gain a full range of services focused on Safety, Service and Savings."* And: *"Our focus on the management of your aviation asset allows you to simply enjoy the benefits of your investment — flexibility, convenience and reliability."*

**Tone:** Spartan, technical, owner-facing. Different vocabulary set from the consumer brands above: *"asset," "owner," "operation," "management."* No "experience," no "journey," no "destination." This is what private aviation sounds like when it is talking to its actual customer (the aircraft owner) and not to the aspirational customer (the renter).

**What this teaches:** Service-business language *to operators* is plainer than service-business language *to clients*. Latitude 43's "Atlas" tiers should sound more like a Solairus engagement letter than a Sentient hero.

**Sources:** [solairus.aero](https://www.solairus.aero); [Aircraft Management](https://www.solairus.aero/aircraft-management/).

## 11. The twelve sensory codes of "private-aviation register"

Distilled from everything above. These are the named, specific, reproducible codes that make a brand feel premium in this category.

1. **Single full-bleed photograph, never a slider.** NetJets, VistaJet, Sentient, and Amalfi all open with one static cinematic frame. Sliders are the mark of an operator pretending to be a brand.
2. **Definite-article statements.** *"The Charter Provider." "Discover the Jet Card." "The Promise of Always."* The article *the* is the most luxurious word in the category — it asserts a category of one.
3. **Restraint over enthusiasm.** No exclamation marks. No "we're excited." No "join the family." A sentence ends with a period, then the next sentence begins.
4. **Place names, not destination names.** *"London. Geneva. Aspen."* — not "Discover Italy this summer." VistaJet's Private World writes about *Sirenuse, Cheval Blanc, La Réserve* by name, never by category.
5. **Empty cabins.** Cabin photographs do not contain passengers. The cabin is a stage; you populate it.
6. **Small-caps sub-headlines.** A premium pattern across the category: SENTENCE-CASE HEADLINE, then SMALL-CAPS PROMISE. (VistaJet uses this every section.)
7. **Phone number listed and dialled, with a country code.** *+44 (0)20 7100 6960.* Visible on PrivateFly, VistaJet, NetJets. A live human at the end of a number is itself a proof.
8. **Numbered proof, not voiced proof.** *"3,500 aircraft." "2,200+ aircraft worldwide." "170+ countries." "60 years."* Testimonials are pushed below the fold or removed entirely.
9. **Sub-cultural markers as credentials.** Norland College, The French Laundry, Robb Report 2024 Best of the Best, Embry-Riddle Advisory Board. Specific names a specific audience recognises; opaque to the general reader.
10. **CTA as reception-desk verb.** *"Request a Quote," "Make an Inquiry," "Open the Desk," "Speak to Our Flight Team."* Never *"Get Started," "Sign Up," "Join Now."*
11. **Tier names as nouns, not status words.** *Share, Card, Program, VJ25, SJ25, Reserve.* Never *Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum.* Never *Basic, Pro, Premium.*
12. **Founder visibility achieved through earned media, not owned media.** Flohr is everywhere except vistajet.com's home page. Jones is on a billion-impression TikTok and on Embry-Riddle's board. Latitude 43's founder belongs in *Robb Report* or *Skift* before they belong on the home page.

Words and phrases premium private-aviation brands use — and the ones they conspicuously do not.

| Words they use | Words they do not use |
|---|---|
| Renowned | World-class |
| Distinctive | Iconic |
| Restful | Luxurious (overused) |
| Considered | Curated (overused) |
| Quietly | Effortlessly |
| Membership | Subscription |
| Program | Plan |
| Card | Tier |
| Reserve | Premium |
| Inquire | Sign up |
| Request a quote | Get started |
| Desk | Team |
| The Atlantic, The Pacific, the 43rd parallel | Worldwide |
| Geneva, Aspen, Sirenuse | Europe, the Mediterranean |
| Built around you | For everyone |
| Make an inquiry | Apply now |
| Always | Anytime |
| Hours | Trips |
| Cabin | Cabin (same — this is one of the few shared words) |
| The Charter Provider | A leading provider of |

| Accountable | Trusted |

## 13. The typography rules

The pattern across NetJets, VistaJet, Sentient, Magellan, Amalfi:

- **One serif, one sans, never two of either.** The serif carries headlines and tag lines; the sans carries navigation, captions, body, and CTAs. Anything more is a luxury-hotel website, not a private-aviation website.
- **Preferred serif families:** Garamond derivatives (Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond, Adobe Garamond), GT Sectra, Tiempos, Canela. Latitude 43 already uses Cormorant Garamond — correct call.
- **Preferred sans families:** Inter, Söhne, Neue Haas Grotesk, Founders Grotesk. Geometric sans (Futura, Avenir) read as luxury hotel, not as private aviation — avoid.
- **Weights:** Serif at 400 regular and 500 medium only. Sans at 400, 500, and very rarely 600 for nav. **No serif bolds.** Bold serif headlines read as Sunday-supplement editorial, not as institutional.
- **Italics for emphasis. Never bold inside a paragraph.** Italics are the editorial register's underline.
- **Small caps for sub-headlines and labels.** VistaJet uses small caps for every sub-line on the home page. NetJets uses them for nav. Sentient uses them for section eyebrows. The small cap is the register's quietest signal of category.
- **Hero headline sizes are large — but the tracking is *negative*.** Letter-spacing tight on display, normal on body. The luxury-hotel error is wide tracking on a thin serif; the private-aviation move is tight tracking on a medium-weight serif.
- **Sentence case headlines, not title case.** *"Flying private made simple."* not *"Flying Private Made Simple."* (NetJets is the outlier and reads older as a result.)

## 14. The photographic rules

What private-aviation brands show:

- **One cinematic exterior on a wet ramp at golden hour, blue hour, or under low cloud.** Aircraft from three-quarter front. Lit from one side. No motion blur.
- **Empty cabin interiors.** Side window light. One coupe glass, one folded throw, one open book. No passengers.
- **The hand on the yoke.** Cockpit shots from behind, single hand, daylight on the panel.
- **The car at the curb.** Black sedan beside the airstairs, doors closed, no faces.
- **Place-name landscapes.** Aspen, the Hamptons, the Amalfi coast — but always identifiable, never stock.

What they never show:

- Customers smiling at the camera.
- Champagne flutes being clinked.
- Hands raised in the air.
- "Diverse group of business travellers in a meeting."
- Flight crew in active service to a customer.
- Sliders or carousels.
- Stock photography.
- Maps with red lines connecting cities.
- Aircraft against a digital background gradient.

The single most common error in operator-luxe brands trying to climb into the register: a hero slider of four lifestyle photographs of smiling customers. The register is single, static, empty, and lit.

---

## 15. The pricing / membership presentation rules

How to show tiers without it looking like a SaaS pricing page:

- **Two or three tiers, never four or five.** NetJets Share / Card. Amalfi One / Reserve. Sentient SJ25 / SJ25+. Magellan Jet Card / Fractional / On-Demand. The category does not believe in five-tier menus.
- **Tier names are nouns, not status words.** *Share. Card. Program. Reserve. One. VJ25.* You earn the right to a status word (*Platinum*) by being a credit card company.
- **Disclose the price-of-admission, conceal the rate card.** VistaJet states the *starting* hour rate ($11,000/hr) on the home page footer; the *real* hour rate by aircraft type is in trade press and inquiry-only PDFs. Magellan publishes the *$500,000* of the World's Game Card and nothing else.
- **No side-by-side comparison table on the home page.** The comparison page is one click away, called *"Understand Our Pricing"* (NetJets) or *"Compare programs"* (VistaJet) — never *"Pricing."*
- **No "Most Popular" badge. Ever.** This is the single most SaaS-y move and the single fastest way to leave the register.
- **No "Save 20%" annual toggle.** Annual billing exists because it is how the category is sold — you don't congratulate the customer for choosing it.
- **The tier description is one paragraph, not a bullet list.** Bullet lists belong in technical specs. The tier description should read like a private-banker prospectus.

## 16. How to feel adjacent without faking it: a minimum credibility set

For a brand-new service business that wants to sit in the private-aviation mental category from day one. The minimum credibility set in 2026:

1. **One named human, accountable, with a real bio and a real face.** Not the founder photograph on the home page — but the founder on a Leadership Team page that *exists*, with a real LinkedIn, a real career, and a real story. Amalfi did this from day one.
2. **One sub-cultural credential a specific audience will recognise.** Virtuoso. STARS. Four Seasons Preferred. Belmond Bellini. Latitude 43 already has these on the page. The move is to *italicise* them, not to list them.
3. **One published number that hangs together.** Amalfi says *"3,500 aircraft, 170+ countries"* — and they don't operate those aircraft, they broker them. The number is true; it does the work. Latitude 43's equivalent might be *"60-minute first response, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year"* — already on the page, but should be the hero, not a pillar.
4. **One sub-brand or named instrument that is not a tier.** *Amalfi Technology, Private World, The 43rd Parallel.* Naming an internal capability turns a service into an institution. Latitude 43's *Atlas* is exactly this — but it is currently named *as a tier*, not as an instrument.
5. **One real phone number, listed, with country code, and a 24/7 promise.** Latitude 43 currently has *"Open the Desk"* as the master CTA. Open is the right verb. There is no number behind it. Add the number.
6. **One published failure mode, owned in writing.** *"Flight cancelled at 02:00 in Frankfurt. Visa rejected at the gate. Hotel walks you on arrival."* Latitude 43 already does this on the home page. It is the single most credible thing on the existing site. Promote it.
7. **One Journal entry every six weeks.** Not a blog. A journal. *"Notes from the 43rd parallel."* Written by the founder. 600 words. One photograph. This is how the register is built over time — VistaJet has been writing Private World pieces for six years; Sentient has *Sentient Press* in its footer.
8. **No founder photograph on the home page.** Earned-media presence elsewhere — Robb Report, Skift, Business Jet Traveler — is the move. The home page belongs to the institution.
9. **One refusal stated publicly.** Atlas's *"What we do not do"* section is exactly this. Keep it. Make it longer.

The thing none of this requires: a private jet. The register is *the way a service business comports itself*, not the asset under it.

## 17. A specific brief for Latitude 43's next website iteration

Six concrete changes. Each reads against the existing pages (`index.html`, `approach.html`, `atlas.html`, `about.html`, `contact.html`, `journal.html`, `fees.html`, the four `for-*.html` files).

### Change 1 — Replace the home hero with a single small-caps promise under a definite-article statement

Current `index.html` hero: *"A travel office for people whose time costs more than their tickets."* Long. Editorial. Good — but two clauses where the category does one.

Move to:

> **The travel office.**
> A MEMBERS' DESK FOR FOUNDERS, PARTNERS, AND THE FIRMS THEY RUN.

Three words on the headline. Small caps sub-line. Definite article. The existing sentence becomes the eyebrow on the next section, *"Where business travel becomes a service."*

### Change 2 — Replace the Unsplash hero photograph with a single empty interior, shot to the rules

The current image is *"Editorial aerial view of a metropolitan financial district at dusk."* It is the right register but the wrong subject. The category does not show cities; it shows the things that surround a traveller — a black sedan at a curb, an empty cabin, a hand on a yoke, a hotel lobby in the half-hour before opening. Commission (or properly license) one image: a half-empty hotel lobby at first light, Toronto, with brass and stone, no people. Shoot it once. Use it for two years.

### Change 3 — Rename the Atlas tier presentation to read like a private-banker prospectus, not a SaaS pricing page

Current `atlas.html` hero: *"An annual membership. Disclosed pricing. Three tiers."* The word *tiers* is correct vocabulary. The presentation is currently a three-column comparison — which is the SaaS error.

Rebuild as three sequential sections, one per tier, each a paragraph and a single number. Tier names move from *"one traveler, one office, one firm"* to nouns:

- **Atlas Card** (one traveller)
- **Atlas Desk** (one office)
- **Atlas Practice** (one firm)

Drop the comparison grid. If a comparison is needed, it lives at `atlas.html#compare` and is called *"Understand Atlas pricing,"* not *"Compare plans."*

### Change 4 — On `about.html`, remove the `[FOUNDER NAME]` placeholder and replace with a Leadership entry written like a Magellan / Amalfi bio

The current `about.html` shows *"[FOUNDER NAME]"* as an H2. Until it has a name, it is the single biggest piece of brand damage on the site. Even if the founder does not want their face on the home page (correctly — the category hides faces), the Leadership Team page *must* exist with a real name, a real CV, and a single black-and-white portrait. The Amalfi pattern.

Add a second section: *"The desk."* One paragraph per role — Travel Director, Service Lead, Atlas Concierge. Names if you have them, titles if you don't. The point is that the service has a shape.

### Change 5 — Add a listed phone number and a 24/7 promise to the global footer and to `contact.html`

The PrivateFly move: *"SPEAK TO OUR FLIGHT TEAM 24/7 — +44 (0)20 7100 6960."* Latitude 43 does not yet have this. *"Open the Desk"* is the right CTA verb but it has no number behind it. Add a Toronto number with the +1 country code, and write *"First response within 60 minutes, twenty-four hours a day."* — which already exists on `approach.html` and should be elevated into the global footer.

Place this in the footer of every page. The phone number, sitting under a Cormorant Garamond *"Latitude 43"* wordmark in the footer, is the single biggest register move available.

### Change 6 — Convert `journal.html` into "The 43rd Parallel" — a six-times-a-year, founder-authored, named instrument

*"Journal"* as a nav label is a hotel-website convention. *Sentient Press* and *VistaJet Private World* and *Magellan Insights* are all *named*. Rename `journal.html` to *"The 43rd Parallel"* (already the eyebrow on the home page — make it a destination). Commit publicly to a publishing cadence: *"Six dispatches a year. The first Monday of February, April, June, August, October, December."* The cadence is the credential.

The first three pieces, drafted in the existing voice from the home page:

1. *Notes from the desk: the trip that taught us we needed a 60-minute promise.*
2. *Why we publish our fees.* (Reference: `fees.html` already exists; the journal entry justifies it.)
3. *The 43rd parallel: a short essay on why Toronto.*

---

### Closing note on register

What separates *Amalfi-adjacent* from *operator-luxe* is not the budget on the photography. It is the count of words on the page, the count of CTAs in the nav, the count of tiers in the menu, and the count of exclamation marks in the body. The register is mostly subtraction.

Latitude 43's existing site is already further into the register than it realises — Cormorant Garamond, the navy-and-bone palette, the brass accent at #B8924C, the *"43°N — Toronto"* eyebrow, the *"What we do not do"* section, the disclosed fees. The six changes above are the difference between *sitting in the category* and *belonging in it*.

The vocabulary is already right. The next iteration is about saying less of it.
