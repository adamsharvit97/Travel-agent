# Pricing Stress-Test: The $50/mo Founder Proposal

*A brutal analysis of the proposed Latitude 43 pricing model against competitive benchmarks, unit economics, brand register, and the actual willingness-to-pay data for the target buyer. Written for the founder's decision, not for the deck.*

---

## TL;DR for the founder

The $50/mo proposal is a category error. It is not a refinement of the original Atlas pricing. It is a different business entirely — one that is unviable at the founder-labor levels implied by the service description, structurally hostile to the Fora 80/20 economics, and brand-incompatible with the Aman/Patek visual register the website currently runs. The escalator math does not survive contact with a 24-trip-per-year business traveler. The "members get half off" mechanic actively destroys the membership's value proposition.

There is a refined version of this instinct that works: a **$99/mo Concierge tier paired with a $12,000/yr invite-only Society tier** (Model E in this doc, with one modification). That hybrid preserves the founder's correct intuition — that $3,600 is too high a wall for the first conversation — while preserving the path to a sustainable solo practice.

If the founder is unwilling to charge above $100/mo to a business owner, they are in the wrong category. The numbers below show why, with sources.

### 2.3 The labor problem

This is where the model breaks.

**Trip volume implied:**
- 50 members × 24 trips = **1,200 trips/year**
- 100 members × 24 trips = **2,400 trips/year**
- 200 members × 24 trips = **4,800 trips/year**

**Hours per trip (conservative for "easy email/text 'book my trip'"):**
- Simple domestic hotel + air: 45 min (intake, search, book, calendar, confirm)
- International with multi-leg or premium cabin: 2–3 hours
- Average across mix: ~1.0 hour

**Founder hours required:**

| Scenario | Trips/yr | Hours/yr | Hours/week (50-wk yr) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 members | 1,200 | 1,200 | **24** |
| 100 members | 2,400 | 2,400 | **48** |
| 200 members | 4,800 | 4,800 | **96** |

**A 100-member book breaks the solo practice.** 48 hours/week of pure trip execution leaves zero hours for sales, content, supplier relationships, FAM travel, conferences, brand-building, or 24/7 emergency escalation. The 200-member scenario is mathematically impossible without 2–3 FTE — and FTE travel coordinators cost $55K–$85K loaded ([Salary.com on travel coordinator pay](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/posting/executive-personal-assistant-salary) is a reasonable proxy).

**The economics with one full-time hire at 100 members:**
- Gross: $284,700
- Opex including $75K loaded hire: $123,000
- **Net to founder: $161,700** — less than the original Path A pricing delivers at 18 members with vastly fewer trips, vastly less risk, and vastly less SLA pressure.

### 2.4 The 24-trip assumption is doing a lot of work

The proposal implicitly assumes the average member takes 24 trips/year — i.e., two trips/month — to make the unit economics work. Let's check that against real benchmarks:

- Inspired Corporate Travel data: "smaller companies might spend $50,000–$250,000 per year" on T&E ([source](https://inspiredcorporatetravel.com/how-much-do-companies-spend-on-business-travel-in-2025/))
- Per-trip cost at small companies: $1,783 average ([Concur benchmark](https://www.concur.com/en-us-resource-center/reports/benchmark-travel-expense-spend))
- That math: $50K–$250K ÷ $1,783 = **28–140 trips/year for the whole company** — meaning a single principal probably takes 8–15 trips/year, not 24

The 24-trip assumption is double the realistic frequency for the stated ICP. **If members actually take 12 trips/year, halve every revenue line above except membership and design fees.**

Recomputed at 12 trips/year, 100 members:

| Line | Annual $ |
|---|---:|
| Membership | $60,000 |
| Net hotel commission | $46,100 |
| Air service fees | $60,000 |
| Design fees | $12,500 |
| Gross revenue | $178,600 |
| Opex | $(48,000) |
| **Net to founder** | **$130,600** |

At 100 members and 12 trips/year, **the founder nets $130K** before tax. Compare against Path A in PRICING-DECISION.md, which projects $171K net at ~18 members. The $50/mo model needs **5.5x the members for less net income** and **higher SLA pressure**. This is not a refinement. It is a worse business.

## Section 3 — Five pricing models scored

### Model A — Founder's proposal as stated

**Structure:** $50/mo membership. Non-members pay $0 design fee under $10K trip value, then $100 per $1K above. Members get 50% off design fees.

**Target ICP:** SMB owner with $50K–$250K personal T&E, doesn't want to "join a club," does want to text someone "book my trip."

**Projected Y5:** Best case 150 members, 12 trips/yr each.

**Projected Y5 net (single-person practice, 12 trips/yr):** ~$165K. Realistic case with 1 hire to handle volume: ~$110K.

**Risks:**
- Brand floor far below the website's current aesthetic register
- Founder labor breaks at ~80 members without hiring
- 50% commission share at Fora until $300K in sales; new advisor likely takes 18 months to clear that threshold
- "No design fee under $10K" means a $9,000 European premium-economy trip = $0 to advisor beyond commissions
- Members get half off — destroys the most-monetizable users (heavy travelers)
- Churn risk: 12.7% on consumer recurring subscriptions ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/)) — at $50/mo, churn means $7,200/yr cohort revenue evaporating per 10 lost members

**Scores (0–5):** Accessibility 5, Brand integrity 1, Labor sustainability 1, Unit economics 2. **Total: 9/20.**

### Model B — Three-tier ladder $50/$200/$500

**Structure:**
- Concierge $50/mo: text/email service, design fees at 50% off founder's escalator
- Office $200/mo: includes 4 trips/year design at no extra, 1-hour SLA business hours
- Firm $500/mo: 6-hour SLA 24/7, named advisor only, unlimited trips, no design fees ever

**Target ICP:** Spans founder (Concierge), growing small-team founder (Office), Series A+ founder or RIA principal (Firm).

**Projected Y5:** ~40 Concierge / 20 Office / 8 Firm = 68 members.

**Projected Y5 net:** Membership $69,600 + commissions ~$110K + air fees ~$80K + design fees ~$15K = ~$275K gross, ~$210K net.

**Risks:**
- Three SLAs at once. Founder operates three service models simultaneously.
- The middle tier is the hardest sell — $200/mo is too much for the casual, too little to signal real luxury.
- Cannibalization: heavy users at $200 should be at $500; the design-fee math doesn't perfectly enforce upgrades.

**Scores:** Accessibility 4, Brand integrity 3, Labor sustainability 2, Unit economics 4. **Total: 13/20.**

### Model C — Subscription only, $99/mo all-in

**Structure:** $99/mo flat. No design fees ever. All trips covered. Members keep 100% of hotel/cruise commissions on the back end being advisor-retained.

**Target ICP:** The "I want one number, never want to talk about money again" SMB founder. Indagare-Custom-adjacent but at 42% of the price.

**Projected Y5:** ~180 members.

**Projected Y5 net (12 trips/yr/member, 1 hire required):**
- Membership: $213,840
- Commissions (net 80/20): $166,000
- Air fees: $108,000
- Gross: $487,840
- Opex inc. 1 FTE: $(125,000)
- **Net: $362,000**

**Risks:**
- Adverse selection: the heaviest travelers cost the most to serve at the same $1,188/yr
- Removing the design fee removes the price signal for big trips — the $30K trip gets the same effort math as the $3K trip
- Renewal sensitivity: members who took only 3 trips last year will churn
- Still below the credible "named relationship" floor in Section 1.4 ($200–$400/mo band)

### Model D — Trip-based with annual cap

**Structure:** No monthly. $500 design fee floor on trips ≥ $5K. $100/$1K above $10K. Annual cap of $1,500 per client per calendar year (akin to Bell & Bly's per-trip pricing plus a "frequent flyer" cap).

## Section 4 — The design-fee escalator analysis

The founder's stated escalator: **$0 at <$10K trip value, then $100 per $1K above. Members get 50% off.**

### 4.1 Trip-size math against labor

Assume a complex international trip with multi-leg air, two hotels, one private transfer, one ground experience, takes **6 hours** of advisor labor. Founder's target effective hourly rate should sit at $200–$400/hr to justify a solo practice (this is below Cravath but above a fractional COS at $150/hr — [TakeItEasy Group on COS rates](https://www.takeiteasygroup.com/post/part-1-chief-of-staff-salary-guide-what-elite-professionals-actually-pay)).

**At $200/hr target, a 6-hour trip needs $1,200 of advisor revenue.**

| Trip value | Design fee (non-member) | Commission (10% × 30% commissionable × 80% to advisor) | Total advisor revenue | Effective hourly (6 hr) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $5,000 | $0 | $120 | $120 | $20 |
| $10,000 | $0 | $240 | $240 | $40 |
| $15,000 | $500 | $360 | $860 | $143 |
| $20,000 | $1,000 | $480 | $1,480 | $247 |
| $30,000 | $2,000 | $720 | $2,720 | $453 |
| $50,000 | $4,000 | $1,200 | $5,200 | $867 |
| $75,000 | $6,500 | $1,800 | $8,300 | $1,383 |
| $100,000 | $9,000 | $2,400 | $11,400 | $1,900 |

**For members** (50% off design fees):

| Trip value | Design fee (member) | Commission | Total | Hourly (6 hr) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $15,000 | $250 | $360 | $610 | $102 |
| $20,000 | $500 | $480 | $980 | $163 |
| $30,000 | $1,000 | $720 | $1,720 | $287 |
| $50,000 | $2,000 | $1,200 | $3,200 | $533 |
| $100,000 | $4,500 | $2,400 | $6,900 | $1,150 |

### 4.2 Findings

**Under-priced zone: $5K–$15K trips.** The advisor takes a loss on labor for any trip below $15K. At $10K, effective hourly is $40 — less than a junior associate at a coffee shop. The "no design fee under $10K" rule cannot stand. **Floor should be $5K, with a $250 minimum design fee** that escalates from there.

**Slope check.** The $100/$1K linear slope is *correct* up to about $40K. Above $40K, two things change: (1) the labor doesn't scale linearly — a $75K trip is maybe 8 hours of work, not 15; (2) the buyer is now in the band where Knightsbridge ($25K floor) is the alternative. **The slope should taper.**

**Recommended escalator (non-member, refined):**
- $0–$5K: Not accepted, or flat $250 "intake fee"
- $5K–$10K: $250 flat
- $10K–$30K: $250 + $100 per $1K above $10K (so $30K = $2,250)
- $30K–$60K: $2,250 + $75 per $1K above $30K (so $60K = $4,500)
- $60K+: $4,500 + $50 per $1K above $60K (so $100K = $6,500)

**Cap.** The founder hasn't proposed a cap. Without one, a $300K honeymoon-redux ten-year-anniversary trip generates $33K in design fees — which sounds great until the client asks "why am I paying you $33K when Indagare's annual fee is $2,850 and includes everything?" **Cap at $10,000.** Above $10K design fee, you are by definition in Society-tier territory and should be selling membership upgrades, not booking the trip à la carte.

### 4.3 Stacking with commissions

The founder's model treats design fees and commissions as additive (correct industry practice — see [SmartFlyer](https://smartflyer.com/), [Bell & Bly](https://bellandblytravel.com/services/)). Confirmed: commissions and fees stack. Disclose this clearly on the site so the client doesn't feel double-charged when they see a Virtuoso amenity package; the offset language is "you pay us for our time; the hotel pays us to bring them business; these are separate transactions and we disclose both."

**Cruise commissions are 10–16% and should be treated specially** ([PTN Travel guide](https://ptntravel.com/travel-advisor-fees-and-commission-models-your-complete-pricing-guide/)). Recommend: no design fee on cruises, full commission retained. This signals expertise differential.

## Section 5 — The "members get half off" mechanic

### 5.1 What the discount actually does

The founder's logic: "Members pay for membership; members should get a discount on services."

The actual effect: **the customers most valuable to acquire and retain (high-trip-frequency, high-trip-value members) get the steepest discount on the revenue line that scales with their value.**

Numerical example: A non-member taking three $20K trips in a year pays:
- Design fees: 3 × $1,000 = $3,000

A member taking the same three trips pays:
- Membership: $600
- Design fees: 3 × $500 = $1,500
- **Total: $2,100 (saves $900 vs. non-member, but advisor revenue drops $900)**

**At what trip count does membership pay off for the buyer?**

| Annual trip value (sum) | Non-member design fee | Member total (600 + 50% fee) | Member breakeven? |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| $15K (one trip) | $500 | $850 | No — non-member wins by $350 |
| $30K (two $15K trips) | $1,000 | $1,100 | Almost breakeven |
| $45K (three $15K trips) | $1,500 | $1,350 | Member wins by $150 |
| $60K (four $15K trips) | $2,000 | $1,600 | Member wins by $400 |

**The break-even for the member is at $45K in annual trip volume.** Below that, the membership is a money-losing decision for the buyer. Above that, it's a money-saving decision — but it's also where the advisor's revenue takes its biggest haircut.

This is a **structurally inverted incentive**: the busiest customers (heaviest workload) generate the lowest per-trip revenue. The least-busy customers subsidize them via the membership fee.

### 5.2 Discount alternatives

| Discount level | Renewal incentive | Signal | Advisor margin impact |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0% (members pay full design fee) | Weak — what does membership buy? | Membership = priority + access | Best margin |
| 25% | Modest renewal pull | "Modest privilege" | Good margin |
| 50% (proposed) | Strong renewal pull | "Half-off everything" | Worst margin |
| 75% | Very strong | "Almost free" | Worse than 50% |
| 100% (Model C) | Maximal | "Membership = all-in" | Depends on price |

**Recommendation:** Drop to **25% member discount, applied only above $30K trip value, and only on the design fee not the commission**. This:
- Preserves the membership-as-status story without bleeding the heaviest users
- Makes the discount feel real only on big trips (where it matters psychologically)
- Lets membership do its primary job: 24/7 access, named relationship, priority SLA — not a coupon

Or, more cleanly: **remove the discount entirely**. Membership buys access, priority, and the SLA. Trips are billed at the same rate for everyone. This is the Indagare structure and it works at $2,850/yr.

## Section 6 — Brand positioning implications

The website currently runs an Aman/Patek/Loro Piana visual register. Cinematic photography, Cormorant Garamond and an editorial layout, dark mode by default. That register reads at four price points and four buyer types. The proposed $50/mo lands in none of them.

### 6.1 Register-to-price map

| Price band | Visual register | Brand examples | Buyer signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| $25–$50/mo | Tech-startup-clean. Sans-serif. App-screen-heavy. | One Medical, Ramp, Mercury | "We are software with a human attached" |
| $50–$200/mo | Premium-approachable. Photo-driven. Some serif but not editorial. | The Standard, Equinox, Soho Friends | "We are a lifestyle brand for people on the way up" |
| $200–$500/mo | Considered editorial. Mixed serif/sans. Mature photography. | Soho House, Velocity Black, Soho House CWH | "We are a club for people who have arrived" |
| $500–$2,000/mo | Old-money editorial. Single typeface. Restrained image use. | Indagare Custom, MDVIP, Sollis | "We are a relationship, not a product" |
| $2,000+/mo | Quiet-luxe. Often *less* visual. Word-of-mouth only. | Knightsbridge, Sienna Charles, Optimize by Equinox | "If you have to ask the price, this isn't for you" |

The current Aman/Patek register fits the **$500–$2,000/mo band** — i.e., the original Atlas $12K Office tier and adjacent product.

### 6.2 What pricing does to the brand

**At $50/mo with the current register:** Buyers experience a register mismatch. The site looks like it should cost $400/mo and the price is $50/mo. The instinct response, per the luxury pricing literature ([Luxury Daily on pricing psychology](https://www.luxurydaily.com/luxury-unfiltered-price-psychology-is-the-hidden-reason-luxury-brands-lose-desirability/)), is **distrust**. "Why is this so cheap? What's the catch?" That instinct is correct here — the catch is the founder's labor.

**At $50/mo with a register flex toward Standard/Equinox/Ramp:** The brand register matches the price. But now the site cannot also do the heavy lifting needed for the $12K Society tier in Model E. You'd need a sub-brand or a separate site.

**At $99/mo with the current register:** Tighter fit. The register tilts slightly toward editorial-premium, the price clears the credible-floor band, and the same site can credibly carry a $12K Society tier mentioned discreetly.

**At $200/mo with the current register:** Perfect fit. This is the Velocity Black / Soho House CWH / MDVIP register and price. Best brand-price coherence.

### 6.3 Recommendation on register

If the founder commits to $50/mo Concierge, the brand register must flex toward Ramp/Mercury/Standard. That means:
- New typography pairing (sans-dominant)
- App-screen-heavy imagery, less hotel/landscape
- "We are software with a human" framing, not "we are a travel office"
- Drop the "Latitude Journal" content product or flex it to a "Concierge newsletter" register

If the founder commits to $99–$200/mo, the current register holds and improves.

If the founder commits to a hybrid ($99 + $12K), the current register works for both — but the $99 page must use slightly more app/tool imagery and the $12K page must use slightly more editorial/portrait imagery to maintain the two-product story.

## Section 7 — What business people actually pay for service

### 7.1 SMB subscription willingness

From [Monetizely on SMB SaaS pricing](https://www.getmonetizely.com/blogs/enterprise-vs-smb-software-pricing-whats-the-real-difference): "A small business isn't going to negotiate a custom contract for a $50/month tool – it's not worth their time or yours." This is the *positive* read of the $50/mo price: it is below the SMB negotiation threshold, which means faster sales cycles.

The *negative* read: it is also below the SMB "this is a serious tool" threshold. SMB founders pay $30/mo for Notion, $50/mo for Linear, $80/mo for HubSpot Starter. **They pay $200–$500/mo for tools that handle revenue or relationships.** Travel — money out the door, relationships with clients on the line — sits closer to the latter.

### 7.2 T&E spend among target ICPs

From [Inspired Corporate Travel](https://inspiredcorporatetravel.com/how-much-do-companies-spend-on-business-travel-in-2025/): smaller companies spend $50K–$250K/yr on T&E. From [Brex](https://www.brex.com/spend-trends/corporate-travel-management/corporate-travel-budget-for-business-travel): decision-makers spend ~12% more per trip than employees. From [Concur](https://www.concur.com/en-us-resource-center/reports/benchmark-travel-expense-spend): per-trip cost for small-company employees is $1,783.

**Implication:** A founder spending $150K/yr on T&E is making a service-buying decision against a $150K cost center. Paying 0.4% of that cost ($600/yr) for a service is a rounding error to them — and that's why $600/yr is *too cheap* to feel serious. Paying 1.5–2.5% of T&E ($2,250–$3,750/yr) is the band where the buyer pauses, evaluates, and treats it as a real procurement decision. That is where the original Atlas $3,600 lived.

### 7.3 The "human in the loop" premium

From [Reach Capital on the AI/SMB economy](https://www.reachcapital.com/resources/thought-leadership/ai-smb-economy/): SMB owners are increasingly buying AI-augmented expertise, and "AI enables small businesses to try expertise, see its value, and discover their true willingness to pay." This is double-edged. Founders are willing to pay for expertise — but they're also being trained to expect AI-led pricing, which is fundamentally cheaper than human-led pricing. **A new pure-human service launching in 2026 either has to price *clearly above* the AI band ($200+/mo) to signal "I am the human alternative" or below it ($30/mo) to compete on app price. The $50–$100 band is the dead zone.**

### 7.4 The underpriced-service distrust instinct

From the luxury pricing literature: "Pricing is a direct signal of trustworthiness and expertise" and "regular fluctuations in pricing can erode trust, suggesting instability or opportunism" ([Luxury Daily](https://www.luxurydaily.com/luxury-unfiltered-price-psychology-is-the-hidden-reason-luxury-brands-lose-desirability/)).

This applies doubly to the buyer of luxury services. The Series A founder, the RIA principal, the family office buyer — these people have been burned by under-priced services before. They know the failure mode: the under-priced service over-promises in year one, struggles with capacity in year two, raises prices abruptly in year three, and either becomes the over-priced service they were trying to avoid or shutters. **The "no free lunch" instinct in this buyer is correctly calibrated to the actual failure mode of new advisory firms.** Pricing too low triggers that instinct.

### 7.5 Subscription churn realities

[Recurly's 2025 churn data](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/) shows consumer subscription churn at 6.5–8% monthly in some categories, 12.7% for meal kits, 3.5% for B2B SaaS. A travel advisory subscription sits somewhere between B2B SaaS and consumer lifestyle — let's call it 6% annual voluntary churn plus 2% involuntary (payment failures).

**At 8% annual churn:**
- 100 members start of year → 92 end of year
- At $50/mo, that's a $4,800 revenue cliff to backfill
- At $99/mo, $9,504
- At $12K/yr Society tier, $96,000 for one churn — the entire opex of the firm

The Society tier churn risk is real, which is why invite-only matters. The Concierge tier churn is large in absolute member terms but small in dollar terms. **The pricing model needs to be robust to ~10% annual member turnover. Model E is. Model A only is if growth keeps pace, which is itself a labor problem.**

## Section 8 — Strategic recommendation

### Is the founder's proposal viable?

**No, not as stated.** The proposal fails on four independent grounds:

1. **Unit economics.** At a 70/30 Fora split (where any new advisor starts), 12 trips/year (realistic, not the assumed 24), and the proposed escalator, the founder nets ~$130K at 100 members — and 100 members is the labor ceiling for a solo practice. The original Atlas Path A delivers more net at one-fifth the member count.

2. **Labor.** 100 members × 24 trips/year is 48 hours/week of trip execution before sales, content, supplier relationships, or escalation. The model assumes founder superhuman-ness, then hides it under the words "easy email/text 'book my trip.'" Easy for the client; not easy for the advisor.

3. **Brand register.** The current Aman/Patek visual identity reads at $500–$2,000/mo. A $50/mo price triggers the "no free lunch" instinct in the exact buyer the brand is trying to attract. Either the brand flexes radically toward Ramp/Standard, or the price moves up to where the brand already credibly sits.

4. **Mechanic flaws.** The 50% member discount on design fees is a structurally inverted incentive — it gives the steepest discount to the heaviest users. The $10K design-fee threshold under-prices the $5K–$15K band where most business trips actually sit. The escalator has no cap, which creates a "why am I paying $33K?" objection at the top end.

### The closest viable alternative

**Model E with one modification: $99/mo Concierge + $12,000/yr invite-only Society.**

Why this works:
- $99 clears the credible-floor band (Section 1.4) without losing the founder's correct instinct that $3,600 is too high a first ask
- $99 sits where the brand register already comfortably reads
- $12,000 Society preserves the upmarket optionality from PRICING-DECISION.md without committing to the full Path B ladder in year one
- Two products, two SLAs, two acquisition motions — but at scales that a solo practice can sustain (120 Concierge max + 12 Society max)
- Projected Y5 net of ~$390K is meaningfully better than the original Path A ($171K) and competitive with the most aggressive Path B scenarios — without requiring Knightsbridge-tenure brand authority in year one

### What the founder does not see

Three things.

**First: the founder is pricing against the wrong reference.** The framing "I can't see myself charging more than $100/mo to business owners" implicitly compares the service to Netflix, Notion, or a gym. But the actual reference set is *services that handle money or relationships on behalf of the business owner*: bookkeepers ($300–$1,500/mo), fractional CFOs ($3,000–$10,000/mo), executive assistants ($2,000–$4,000/mo, [ProAssisting](https://proassisting.com/resources/articles/how-much-does-a-virtual-assistant-cost/)), chiefs of staff ($5,000–$20,000/mo retainer, [TakeItEasy Group](https://www.takeiteasygroup.com/post/part-1-chief-of-staff-salary-guide-what-elite-professionals-actually-pay)). Travel advisory belongs to the latter category in the founder's promised service register. The buyer is comparing the founder to their bookkeeper and their EA, not to their gym membership.

**Second: the founder's labor is being given away for free in the proposal.** The "members get half off design fees" mechanic is the founder's own time being given to members at a discount. Every member who flies twice a month is being subsidized by the member who flies twice a year. This is fine *if and only if* the heavy-user members are also the most valuable referral source — but the proposal hasn't articulated why that would be true. Without that justification, the discount is a leak.

**Third: the founder is conflating "approachable" with "cheap."** These are not the same thing. Indagare is approachable at $395/yr because it is content-driven and self-serve. Velocity Black is approachable at $258/mo because the brand is built around it. The founder can build an approachable Latitude 43 at $99/mo with the right register and the right onboarding — but approachability is a brand and UX problem, not a pricing problem. **Solving it with price destroys the brand. Solving it with brand preserves the price.**

### The bigger strategic question this pricing reveals

The $50/mo proposal is not really about pricing. It is about whether the founder believes they are building a software-flavored service with a human attached (volume, scale, eventual hires, possibly venture-fundable) or a relationship-flavored boutique with software underneath (low volume, high ACV, no employees ever, lifestyle business).

The current website, brand register, business plan, deck, and segment workflows all argue for the second. The proposed pricing argues for the first. **You cannot run both simultaneously without confusing the buyer.**

The founder's discomfort with charging $3,600 is real and worth taking seriously. But the answer to "I'm uncomfortable charging $3,600" is not "let me charge $600." It is one of two questions: "Have I built enough proof to make $3,600 feel earned to the buyer?" — in which case the answer is launch case studies and content, not lower pricing — or "Am I actually building a different business than the one the brand currently describes?" — in which case the brand needs to change before the price does.

**Decide which business is being built. Then price accordingly. Do not price first and let the business shape itself around the number.**
