**Prepared for:** Founder, Latitude 43
**Date:** 24 May 2026
**Purpose:** A forensic study of Amalfi Jets — the brand, the founder, the customer, the marketing engine, the economics — and a concrete 90-day plan for Latitude 43 to be perceived as the natural hotel-and-ground partner to anyone who books a jet with Amalfi.
**Synthesises:** amalfijets.com (every public page), the Leadership Team roster, the LEADERS Magazine April 2026 interview with Kolin Jones, Aerotime, Voyage LA, Galavante, IBTimes UK/US, Yahoo, Inkl, Prime Gazette, Flare Article, Private Jet Card Comparisons, the Jet Linx / Forbes Travel Guide partnership precedent, the Tradewind / Le Toiny precedent, and the existing Latitude 43 brand-register and positioning files in `/research`.

## 0. Why this document exists

The founder of Latitude 43 said *adjacent to Amalfi*. The phrase was deliberate. It is not *partner with Amalfi*, it is not *compete with Amalfi*, and it is not *resemble Amalfi*. It is *adjacent* — the kind of brand that, when an Amalfi client books a jet and asks the account manager "where am I staying?", the account manager already has the answer, and the answer is Latitude 43.

The reason that ambition is realistic is that Amalfi has, in the last twelve months, quietly built the very partner roster that we want to be on. Amalfi Reserve already routes its members to villa partners, helicopter partners, ground partners, and restaurant partners. There is a hotel-shaped hole in that roster. The Jet Linx / Forbes Travel Guide precedent (2019) and the Tradewind / Le Toiny precedent (recurring) both prove that the category metabolises hotel partnerships — but the partnerships that work are the ones where the hotel partner has the same brand register as the jet partner. That is the opening.

This document is six thousand words. It assumes the reader has read the existing brand-register file in `/research/brand-register-private-aviation.md` and the positioning-master file. It does not repeat what is there; it goes deeper on Amalfi specifically.

### 1.3 Business model — what kind of company is this, exactly

Amalfi is a **charter brokerage**. Not a fractional operator. Not an aircraft owner. Not a Part 135 operator. They are a Part 380 indirect air carrier that sources flights from third-party Part 135 operators. The model is identical in structure to **Stratos Jet Charters, PrivateFly, Air Charter Service, and Magellan Jets** — what differs is the brand register and the customer interface.

This matters for the Latitude 43 strategy because it means Amalfi's *unit of inventory* is identical to its competitors. The thing that separates Amalfi from Stratos or PrivateFly is not the airplane. It is the brand, the technology stack (Atlas), the founder-led marketing engine, and the membership wrapper (Amalfi Reserve). Amalfi sells the same Phenom 300 that Stratos sells; the wrapper around it is different.

## 2. The product and pricing

### 2.1 The four products

Amalfi sells four things, in this order of marketing emphasis:

1. **On-Demand Charter** — pay-as-you-fly. No commitment. The default for the majority of clients.
2. **Amalfi One Jet Card** — fixed hourly rates, guaranteed availability, no membership fee model. Direct analogue of NetJets Card and Magellan Jet Card.
3. **Amalfi Reserve Membership** — lifestyle membership; concierge, partner access, empty legs, villa access. The product that makes Latitude 43 relevant.
4. **Amalfi Exclusive Golf Experiences** — vertical-specific concierge service for golf trips.

Plus a fifth product that is institutional, not consumer:

5. **Atlas** — proprietary sales-and-operations software. Used internally; announced as planned for external SaaS subscription Q4 2024 (status of external rollout unconfirmed).

### 2.2 Pricing — what is public, what is not

| Product | Entry price | Hourly rate | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amalfi Reserve Membership | **$500/month** | Light jet from $7,500/hr (incl. FET) | [Private Jet Card Comparisons](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/providers/amalfi-jets/) |
| Amalfi One Jet Card | Not publicly disclosed | "Fixed hourly pricing with guaranteed availability" | Owned site, PJCC |
| On-Demand | No subscription | Dynamic, "up to 24% savings" claim | amalfijets.com/on-demand-charter |
| Empty leg | No subscription | "Dynamic pricing with up to 24% savings" | Owned site |
| Atlas (SaaS) | Not disclosed | Q4 2024 planned external | [Business Air News](https://www.businessairnews.com/mag_story.html?ident=31262) |

The pricing is **deliberately opaque** on the owned site. This matches the VistaJet pattern (no hourly rates above the fold) and the NetJets pattern (no rates at all on the home page). The category convention is to disclose rates *to inquiries* and to use the website as a brand experience, not a quote engine. Amalfi follows that convention precisely.

The Private Jet Card Comparisons disclosure that Amalfi Reserve members "saved 16.8% from capped jet card rates" (April 2026) is the cleanest pricing-vs-competitor number in the public record. It is the one Amalfi proves with: not the rate, but the savings.

### 2.4 The "Charter Provider" positioning — what it operationally means

The headline phrase on amalfijets.com is **"The Charter Provider Built Around You."** Three deliberate moves are inside that sentence.

First, the definite article. *The* Charter Provider, not *a* charter provider. The brand register file in `/research` has a longer treatment of this — *the* is the most luxurious word in the category, because it asserts a category of one. NetJets does the same with *"The Promise of Always."* VistaJet does it with *"the distinctive silver and red fleet."*

Second, **"Charter Provider"** is a deliberately spare role-noun. Not "private aviation company," not "luxury jet brand," not "membership." It is the operational job description — *we provide charters* — used as a brand line. This is a register move borrowed from law firms (*the firm*), from auction houses (*the house*), and from the older private banks (*your bank*).

Third, **"Built Around You"** does the personalisation work. It is the tagline. It appears on the home hero. It is also the brand's promise to executive assistants, family offices, and corporate travel managers — the three buyer types that the category actually sells through. *Built Around You* in practice means: one named Flight Operations Associate; one named Account Executive; 24/7 contact; the app and Atlas behind both.

Operationally, the model is identical to a high-end legal practice: a Senior Partner (the AE) on the relationship, an Associate (the FOA) on the trip details, a back office (Atlas + operations) on the logistics. The brand register *sounds* like luxury hospitality; the operational structure *is* a professional-services firm.

## 3. The customer profile

### 3.1 Who actually flies Amalfi

From the LEADERS interview, the Galavante interview, and the Aerotime profile, triangulated:

- **95% of bookings** are corporate or UHNW pricing inquiries — *"Hi I need an airplane price"*
- **5% of bookings** are the eccentric leisure requests that show up on TikTok (one client booking separate flights for two romantic partners on the same day; pet flights; last-minute Masters tournament bookings)
- **Decision-makers:** Executive assistants and travel agents are explicitly named in the Aerotime profile as the conversion target — *"Corporate customers of executive assistants and travel agents...love just watching you guys' content and seeing how you handle things and love to work with you"*
- **Geographic core:** Los Angeles County (founder's home market), with strong secondary markets in New York, Miami, Austin (Matt Walton, AE), and the broader West Coast
- **Industry concentration:** Entertainment (Dylan Flyer, AE, has film-industry connections; founder operates in LA; Maverick Helicopters Las Vegas partnership), sports (Harrison Hall, AE, is a former collegiate golfer; the Golf Experiences vertical), finance and tech (Will McNear, VP Sales, ex-enterprise software; Brian Francis, ex-JetSuite, brought the corporate-aviation Rolodex)
- **Income / net worth band:** UHNW ($30M+ net worth) for the core. The $500/month Reserve membership is structured to bring in the *light-UHNW* and *HNW with corporate* segments at the bottom of the funnel.
- **Trip frequency:** The Jet Card is built for 25-hour-plus annual users (the category convention). The On-Demand product captures the once-or-twice-a-year UHNW who refuses to commit. Empty legs convert the "I would fly private if it were cheaper" tier.

### 3.2 The three buyer types

The brand register file already names these. Restating for Amalfi specifically:

1. **The principal** — the UHNW themselves, often a founder, athlete, entertainer, or family-office head. Books some flights themselves; delegates most. Lives on Instagram and (increasingly) TikTok.
2. **The executive assistant** — the actual conversion target for 60–70% of corporate bookings. The Aerotime profile is explicit that EAs are the audience of the TikTok content as much as the principal.
3. **The family office / travel agent** — the institutional buyer for the largest single accounts. Often unbranded; Amalfi competes for this seat against NetJets and VistaJet, and wins on price + responsiveness, loses on fleet certainty.

### 3.3 What this means for Latitude 43

The Amalfi customer is a Latitude 43 customer with a private jet attached. The decision-maker is — in 60–70% of cases — *the same person*: a senior executive assistant who manages travel, expenses, calendars, and lifestyle for a principal. The same EA who books the Amalfi G450 to Aspen books the FBO transfer, the hotel block at the St. Regis, the SUV from Aspen Limo, the dinner at Element 47, and the ski instructor. The EA is the ICP. The principal is the budget.

Latitude 43 has, in the existing positioning, named the EA and the firm-tier admin as part of the *Office* and *Firm* tier ICPs. The Amalfi-adjacent move is to recognise that the Amalfi EA and the Latitude 43 EA are *literally the same EA* in the highest tier of the market.

### 4.2 The typography

Sans-serif throughout. Variable weights for hierarchy — display weight on the headline, regular on the body, medium on the CTA button copy. No serif. No script. No display-serif accent. The closest comparator in private aviation is XO Global (also pure sans-serif), but XO leans heavier and more compressed; Amalfi leans more open and more spaced.

The typeface family is not publicly named, but the visual character is consistent with a contemporary geometric humanist — in the family of *Söhne*, *Inter*, *Aktiv Grotesk*, or a customised member of that group. The body sets at a relatively long measure (60–70 characters per line) which is unusual in private aviation, where the convention is tighter columns. The longer measure makes the site read more like editorial than like a brochure.

### 4.3 The colour palette

| Role | Colour | Visual character |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant | Black / near-black (#0A0A0A or similar) | Cabin-at-night, ramp-at-dusk |
| Secondary | Off-white / warm white | Type, negative space |
| Tertiary | Champagne / sand / camel | Single-suite hotel register; not gold |
| Accent | Polished metal greys (engine cowl, runway) | Photographic, not graphic |

Conspicuously absent: navy, forest green, burgundy, gold, royal blue. Those are heritage colours; Amalfi reads modern. Also absent: high-saturation accents. The site has no orange, no red, no electric anything.

### 4.4 The photography

The pattern is consistent and rigorous. Three photographic registers, in this proportion:

1. **Aircraft on ramp** (≈40% of imagery) — single-frame cinematic exterior shots. Golden hour, blue hour, or low-key dusk. Wet ramp preferred. Aircraft alone — no ground crew, no waving family, no luggage cart.
2. **Cabin details** (≈40%) — coupe glass on a folded napkin, hand on a yoke, a folded throw on a cream leather seat, a single business card on a polished walnut sidewall. Macro and tactile. Never with passengers visible.
3. **Branded hospitality moments** (≈20%) — a pour, a plated course, a polished engine, the Amalfi logo on something small. These are the moments that look like a Le Labo Instagram post — small, deliberate, tactile.

What is *not* in the photography: passengers, smiling people, hand-shaking handshakes, ground crews, celebrity-spotting, before-and-after travel happiness, stock landscapes, "lifestyle" couples on tropical beaches. The discipline is total. The customer is invited into the frame; they are not depicted inside it.

### 4.5 The voice and tone

Three layers, stratified by surface:

- **Owned site:** Restrained, founder-confident, slightly more colourful than VistaJet. Uses *elevated* and *exclusive* alongside *transparent* and *consistent.* The transparency-and-consistency pair is the load-bearing rhetorical move — it earns them the right to also use *elevated.*
- **LEADERS Magazine, Aerotime, Galavante:** Founder-personal, conversational, slightly self-deprecating, opinionated about hotels and destinations. *"Heaven better look like Le Toiny."*
- **TikTok / Instagram:** Founder voice is loud, fast, performative. *"You have to be over the top, ridiculous to stand out."* The TikTok voice is **deliberately discordant** with the owned-site voice. This two-channel strategy is the most copied move in the category right now and it is working.

### 4.6 "Built Around You" — analysis

A four-word tagline that does six jobs. (1) It is a *promise* — we conform to you, not the reverse. (2) It is a *positioning* — *built*, not *bought*, implying engineering and intent. (3) It is *category-adjacent*, borrowed from advertising's "Built for [audience]" lineage (Built Ford Tough, Built to Last). (4) It is *operationally true* — the named-AE model is in fact the principal-around-the-client structure. (5) It is *legally defensible* — there is no claim about safety, fleet size, or price; everything in the line is about the relationship. (6) It is *short* — four words, all common, no Latin, no exclamation, no pun.

It is also defensible against drift. *"Built Around You"* is hard to dilute because the verb is in the past participle — the building is done; it is not a process the customer is paying to be part of.

### 4.7 The logo and visual identity

The mark is a minimalist wordmark — *amalfi* set in a custom sans, with the *jets* (or *Jets*) reading as a subordinate label. The mark sometimes appears as the wordmark alone, sometimes paired with a winged-circle device (visible on the leadership-team page and in select Instagram posts). The mark is consistently white on black or black on warm cream. There is no gold. There is no rendering of an aircraft in the logo. The mark is, deliberately, hospitality-adjacent rather than aerospace-adjacent.

### 5.2 The TikTok engine — what it actually is

Three things are happening on TikTok simultaneously:

1. **Operational transparency** — Kolin posts the actual logistics of a charter. "Here is how we sourced this aircraft." "Here is why this trip cost what it did." "Here is the air traffic control transmission you just heard." This is *category-disruptive* — the convention since Marquis Jet was to never explain the business to outsiders.
2. **Founder voice** — Kolin himself appears in 70–80% of videos. He is the brand. The face. The salesperson. This is the *anti-NetJets* move; NetJets has spent sixty years hiding its leadership.
3. **Aspirational shorthand** — the cabin, the ramp, the destinations. Visible enough to be desired; abstract enough to remain inaccessible.

The conversion is measurable. Aerotime quotes Kolin: *"We're tracking website engagement and flight requests that come from the website" with documented "record growth."* The principal conversion path is *TikTok → website → flight request → AE pickup → quote → first booking → Reserve upsell.* That is a six-step funnel and it has produced 4–5x revenue growth two years in a row.

Each plays a different role: LEADERS is the institutional credibility, Aerotime is the industry trade, Galavante is the lifestyle credibility, Voyage LA is the local-roots story, Robb Report is the wealth-segment proof, PJCC is the operational benchmarking.

The press is **earned, not paid** as far as is disclosed. The founder is on the Embry-Riddle Industry Advisory Board, which is the kind of unpurchased credibility that hangs together with the press placements.

## 7. The Amalfi-adjacent brand opportunity

This is the strategic core of the document.

### 7.1 The structural opening

Amalfi Reserve already has these partner categories: yacht (Ocean Independence), helicopter (Maverick Helicopters), exotic car (Monza Exotics), ground (Go Rentals, 250+ locations), villa (the unnamed "Amalfi's exclusive partners"), restaurants (Fabel Miami, The Braywood), and experiential (Dream Racing). There is a conspicuous gap: **branded hotel partnership**. Amalfi has *villas* through partners and *restaurants* by name. They do not have an *anchor hotel partner* on the public record.

Jet Linx took this seat with Forbes Travel Guide in 2019. Tradewind took it with Le Toiny on a single-property basis. NetJets has unbranded loyalty into Four Seasons and Aman. **Amalfi has not yet anchored a hotel partner** as of the publicly-available record.

That is the seat to occupy.

### 7.2 The four kinds of adjacency

Amalfi-adjacency for Latitude 43 has to be earned across four register dimensions simultaneously. Half-measures on any one of them break the perception.

| Dimension | Amalfi's mark | Latitude 43's required match |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual register** | Black, off-white, champagne. Sans-serif. Empty cabins. Single full-bleed photography. No script. No gold. | Black, off-white, sand-stone. Editorial sans (or restrained serif). Empty interiors. Single full-bleed. No retail gold. |
| **Voice register** | Restrained owned-site; founder-led off-site. "Built Around You." "Request a Quote." | Restrained owned-site; founder-led press. *"Sixty-minute first response, 24/7/365."* Same reception-desk verb pattern. |
| **Operational register** | Named AE + named FOA + Atlas + app + 24/7. | Named advisor + documented secondary + Atlas membership + 60-min SLA. Already structurally true. |
| **Customer-journey register** | The EA experience from quote → trip → upsell is one continuous conversation. | The EA experience from inquiry → trip → renewal is one continuous conversation. *Already structurally true.* |

### 7.3 The customer-journey integration — five seams

A trip that an Amalfi client takes has these five operational seams. Each seam is a place where Latitude 43 *could* be the natural partner.

1. **The "where am I staying?" seam** — the moment after the AE confirms the aircraft and the EA needs the hotel. Today, the EA defaults to whatever advisor the principal already uses, or to their own contacts. *If Amalfi's Reserve member benefits include a Latitude 43 introduction, this seam is captured.*
2. **The "ground transfer" seam** — Amalfi already has Go Rentals (250+ locations) and Monza Exotics. This is *not* where Latitude 43 enters. The ground partner already exists.
3. **The "destination concierge" seam** — Amalfi Reserve already does this through its 24/7 concierge. *Latitude 43 enters here as a wholesale partner to the Reserve concierge desk, not as a parallel concierge to the principal.*
4. **The "trip planning" seam** — the multi-day, multi-stop itinerary. Amalfi handles the air; the rest is open. *This is the most concrete Latitude 43 opportunity — the "we'll plan everything except the jet" wrapper.*
5. **The "return" seam** — post-trip retention. Amalfi's AE owns the upsell. *Latitude 43's renewal cycle should mirror this — the Atlas anniversary call.*

### 7.4 The three partnership archetypes

Three structurally distinct ways Latitude 43 can relate to Amalfi, from least to most committed.

**A. The unbranded preferred partner.** Amalfi Reserve concierge has Latitude 43 in their inbound queue. No public announcement. No co-branded marketing. A Reserve member who asks for hotel/ground gets a warm introduction. The economics are a referral fee paid by Latitude 43 to Amalfi per converted introduction. Industry benchmark for this kind of referral, per the finder's-fee research, is 5–15% of first-year membership revenue (so $180–$540 on an Atlas Office tier at $3,600/year). This is the easiest to start; lowest upside; lowest signal.

**B. The named partner.** Amalfi Reserve discloses Latitude 43 as a named hotel-and-ground partner on the Reserve benefits page, alongside Ocean Independence and Maverick Helicopters. Co-branded copy on amalfijets.com; reciprocal page on latitude43.com. The economics are still a referral fee (8–12%, customary in the lifestyle-membership category) but the volume is 5–10x because of the visible placement. Closer to the Jet Linx / Forbes Travel Guide model.

**C. The integrated wrapper.** A co-branded product: *Amalfi + Latitude 43 — the trip wrapper.* A member books the jet through Amalfi; the hotel, ground, and itinerary through Latitude 43. Single invoice or two coordinated invoices; one named human at each side; a shared chat thread. The economics are a profit-share on the trip (not a referral fee on the membership) — Latitude 43 retains its membership economics; Amalfi captures a coordination fee per integrated trip. Closest analogue is the Tradewind / Le Toiny model — same trip, same client, two brands, one experience.

**The recommended path:** start at A (within 30 days), graduate to B by day 60, propose C in the next-90-day window contingent on traction. The graduation logic is that each step is enabled by the prior step's signal data.

## 8. The cross-referral economics

### 8.1 How private jet and hotel/ground attach

The industry data on attach rates between private jet and hotel/ground is not cleanly disclosed by any operator. The triangulation:

- The luxury concierge industry (the proxy market) has 25–35% gross margins on bundled travel, per the 360iresearch / Coherent Market Insights summaries. Of that, 8–15% is typically the agency commission on hotel rooms booked through preferred-supplier programs.
- The Four Seasons Private Jet Experience pricing bundles *all* nights at FS properties into the trip price — implying the hotel margin is fully internalised at $150K+ per traveller.
- The Jet Linx / Forbes Travel Guide model exclusively refers Jet Card members; the commission structure is undisclosed but is presumed to be the Forbes Travel Guide standard 8–10% hotel commission split.
- The Tradewind / Le Toiny model uses **a 10% discount on a future Tradewind trip** as the cross-incentive, plus complimentary ground. The economics are reciprocal-discount, not referral-fee.

### 8.2 The realistic Latitude 43 / Amalfi cross-referral math

A back-of-envelope, conservative model.

**Inputs (assumed, defensible):**
- Amalfi Reserve members: ≈1,500 (estimated from the $500/month price, $9M ARR contribution at scale, and the Joshua Kaiser growth role — public data point not disclosed)
- Average Reserve member hotel-nights / year: 30–40 (multiple trips, multi-night)
- Average Reserve member hotel ADR: $1,200 (typical UHNW property mix)
- Attach rate if Latitude 43 is the named partner: 8–15% (conservative)

**Output (Latitude 43 → Amalfi side):**
- 1,500 members × 12% attach × 35 nights × $1,200 = ≈$7.6M in hotel spend annually flowing through a Latitude 43 partnership
- At 10% commission to Latitude 43: ≈$760K gross
- Less 10% referral fee to Amalfi: ≈$76K back to Amalfi, ≈$684K net Latitude 43

**Output (Amalfi → Latitude 43 side):**
- Of the 1,500 attached, 5–10% convert to Atlas membership
- 75–150 net new memberships × $3,600 weighted average = $270K–$540K Atlas ARR
- Less 10% introduction fee to Amalfi: ≈$27K–$54K back to Amalfi

**Total Amalfi take from a structured partnership: $100K–$130K / year in years 1–2, growing as Reserve scales.** That is a number Amalfi will take a meeting on. It is also small enough that the partnership is not the load-bearing element of Amalfi's economics — which is the right shape for a partnership-of-equals positioning.

### 8.3 What Amalfi will not do

Amalfi will not do exclusivity. The Reserve model is built on *many partners, one membership.* They will not lock to a single hotel partner. The Jet Linx / Forbes Travel Guide model was an exception because Forbes Travel Guide is a *standards body*, not a single hotel. Latitude 43 should not propose exclusivity. Propose **preferred-partner status with a named, co-branded entry on the Reserve benefits page.** That is the realistic ceiling.

## 10. The brand-adjacent moves to make

### 10.1 Visual register

1. **Photography:** commission a single-frame, no-people, full-bleed hero — *not* a slider. Subject: an interior at dusk; a desk lamp on a polished wood table; a folded throw; a coupe glass. Same register as Amalfi's cabin macros. *No travel landscapes. No people.*

## 11. Verdict — the 90-day Amalfi-adjacent strategy

The opportunity is real and the window is narrow. Amalfi has spent four years building the brand register Latitude 43 needs to be next to, and they have spent the last twelve months building a Reserve membership that has a hotel-shaped hole at its centre. There is no entrenched hotel partner on the Reserve benefits page. Forbes Travel Guide owns the Jet Linx seat; the Amalfi Reserve seat is still open. The brokerage is on a 4–5x growth trajectory and is hiring in operations and growth, which means the partnership conversation has air. If Latitude 43 does not occupy this seat in the next twelve months, someone else — most plausibly Indagare, Embark Beyond, or a more aggressive newcomer — will.

The work in the next ninety days is not strategy. It is four moves, in this order.

**Days 1–30: the visual register match.** Latitude 43's site has to read like an Amalfi-adjacent property when an Amalfi AE opens it on their phone. That means: a single full-bleed hero (no slider); a sans-only or sans-plus-one-serif typography lock; a black / off-white / sand-stone palette with no gold; a *"the"*-statement headline; a small-caps sub-head with the 60-minute promise; a "Leadership" page that exists, with the founder's full name, real bio, single black-and-white portrait, real Embry-Riddle-equivalent credential, and a real LinkedIn. The brand-register file already names this work; it is unfinished as of May 2026 and is the blocker on every downstream move.

**Days 15–45: the founder note and the AE note.** Email Kolin Jones, Brian Francis, and Joshua Kaiser. Three notes, in parallel, each two paragraphs. The pitch is unbranded preferred partner — Tier A in §7.4 — with a roadmap to named partner by Q4. The economics in §8.2 are the meat of the pitch. The St. Barts trip in §10.5 is the proposed proof. Founders read three-sentence emails from peers; long decks go to the inbox.

**Days 30–60: the co-branded asset.** Whichever of the three responses lands, the next move is one coordinated trip — an actual Reserve member, an actual stay at a Latitude 43-arranged property, an actual ground transfer, an actual itinerary. Document the trip with photography that matches the Amalfi cabin-detail register (single frames; tactile; no people). The asset is *one* trip, *one* article in *one* outlet (LEADERS or Galavante), and *one* shared post-trip note from Kolin Jones and the Latitude 43 founder.

**Days 60–90: the partnership formalisation.** Convert the unbranded relationship into a named Reserve benefits-page entry. The structure is a 12-month preferred-partner agreement, non-exclusive on Latitude 43's side (we keep our own membership), non-exclusive on Amalfi's side (they keep their existing villa and ground partners), with a documented introduction-fee structure (8–12% on first-year Atlas memberships, 5–10% on Latitude 43-arranged hotel commissions). The agreement is a one-pager; the brand register file's discipline applies — no Latin, no exclamation marks, no "join the family."

What is *not* realistic in the next ninety days, and should not be promised in any pitch: an Amalfi takeover, an acquisition, a co-branded product, a Vista-style Private World counterpart, a Jet Linx-style Forbes Travel Guide partnership. Those are 12–24-month outcomes if the 90-day moves earn them. The thing being earned in the 90 days is *legibility* — Amalfi's leadership reading Latitude 43's site and the LEADERS piece and recognising the brand register as adjacent rather than competitive. That is the trip-wire. Cross it and the rest is downstream.

The single biggest risk is overreach — putting an Amalfi name on Latitude 43's home page before the partnership is real, leaning into the *"if you fly Amalfi, you stay with Latitude"* line publicly before it has been earned, or proposing a co-branded product in the first call. The brand register file's most quoted line applies: *"the register is mostly subtraction."* The same discipline applies to the partnership. Earn the visual register first. Earn the founder relationship second. Earn the public-page placement third. Earn the co-branded product fourth. Skip a step and the perception of adjacency collapses into the perception of fan-imitation, which is the single worst outcome in this category.

The 600-word verdict, then, in one line: **Match the register, earn the introduction, document one real trip, take the Reserve seat, do not name a co-branded product publicly for at least nine months.** Everything else in this document is either inputs to that line or guardrails on it.
