# Under-Researched Personas — The Hidden ICP Map

**For: Latitude 43 — operator-luxe travel advisory**
**Date: May 2026**
**Author: Senior Buyer Research Lead**
**Scope: 15 personas beyond the standard ICP (founders, VC, PE, RIA, family office, solo professionals)**

This document maps fifteen personas the founder has not yet researched, organized in three tiers. Every claim is sourced. Every conference, dollar figure, and demographic is grounded in a public reference. The synthesis at the end ranks the five highest-LTV under-researched personas, lists the twenty conferences where Latitude 43 should physically show up, and proposes five Q3-2026 outreach experiments with hypotheses, tests, and metrics.

The point is not that these personas should *replace* the canonical ICP. The point is that the canonical ICP has been worked over by every advisory in the category. The personas below are the ones that *haven't* been worked over — the rooms where a published SLA, a named human, and a transparent membership price will hit like a hammer because nobody else is swinging.

---

## Executive summary — what the founder should internalize before reading further

1. **The five highest-LTV under-researched personas are: CRE brokers at boutique firms, boutique M&A bankers, MDRT-tier independent insurance brokers, specialty physicians in private practice, and boutique CPA partners with M&A-advisory exposure.** All five spend more than $40,000/year of personal-or-firm money on travel, none have a category-defining advisory, and all five are reachable through a small number of well-known conferences.

2. **The single highest-yield event in this entire document is ICSC Las Vegas (May 17–20, 2026, ~35,000 attendees).** It is the closest thing in any of the fifteen verticals to a single room containing the entire ICP. NAIOP's CRE.Converge in October and AM&AA's twice-a-year conferences are the next two density points.

3. **The most under-priced wedge in the category is the medical-specialty private practitioner.** $400K–$800K compensation, $3,500–$5,000 CME budgets per year, and zero category-defining travel advisory. The pitch — "we book your CME like a chief of staff" — has not been delivered by any incumbent.

4. **Three Tier-3 personas (yacht brokers, art dealers, music managers) look glamorous and aren't.** They have professional travel-buying habits already embedded in their workflows (gallery shipping coordinators, tour managers, dealer charter networks). Latitude 43 should not chase them; it should target their *clients* instead.

5. **The credibility vocabulary changes per persona.** "Operator" works for M&A bankers and crypto founders. "Practice" works for attorneys and physicians. "Producer" works for insurance brokers. "Principal" works for developers and CPAs. The same homepage will not work for all five. The wedge is *vocabulary discipline*, not feature differentiation.

6. **Most personas in this set already pay $300–$3,000/month for a professional tool they hate (CRM, practice-management software, MLS access, deal-sourcing platforms).** $50–$200/month for Latitude 43 fits inside their existing tooling budget psychology, not their travel budget psychology. Pricing should be benchmarked against Salesforce, Clio, CoStar — not Marriott Bonvoy.

# TIER 1 — Likely highest-LTV under-researched

## Persona 1 — Commercial real estate brokers at boutique or independent firms

### A. Demographics and profile

- **Age range:** 35–58 is the heavy band. Top producers are typically 12–25 years into their career. ([NAREA](https://nareagroup.org/commercial-real-estate-broker-salary/))
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree dominant; MBA over-represented at the top of the income distribution; CCIM and SIOR designations are the credible credentials.
- **Geographic concentration:** New York metro, DFW, Houston, Chicago, LA, San Francisco/San Jose, Miami, Atlanta, Phoenix. San Jose CRE brokers earn the highest in the country at $367,358 average. ([Comparably](https://www.comparably.com/salaries/salaries-for-commercial-real-estate-broker))
- **Income / net worth:** Average $247,670; 75th-percentile $345,126; 90th-percentile $453,237. ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/commercial-real-estate-broker-salary-SRCH_KO0,29.htm)) Boutique-firm principals (Avison Young, Lee & Associates partner-level, true independents) routinely clear $750K–$2M in top markets in good years; commission-only so the swing is brutal in down years.
- **Family situation:** Married with kids is the modal profile. Trip-mixing (extending a deal-tour with a family weekend) is structurally compatible.
- **Firm types:** Three buckets. (1) Big four-plus — CBRE, JLL, Cushman, Colliers, Newmark, Marcus & Millichap. (2) Boutique-mid — Avison Young, Lee & Associates, Stream, Berkadia. (3) Independent shops — single-asset-class specialists, 2-15 brokers, founder-led. The Latitude 43 wedge is bucket 3.

### B. Travel pattern

- **Annual trips:** 30–60 for active investment-sales brokers covering multi-market portfolios. Tenant-rep brokers run 15–30. ([NAIOP compensation reports](https://www.naiop.org/research-and-publications/compensation-report/))
- **Annual T&E:** $40,000–$120,000 personal-or-firm for active producers. Top investment-sales producers covering NMHC/ICSC/RECon plus client tours can clear $150K.
- **Domestic vs. international:** 95% domestic. Cross-border CRE concentrated at the top of CBRE/JLL/Eastdil; not the boutique persona.
- **Conference circuit:**
  - **ICSC Las Vegas** (May 17–20, 2026, LVCC) — the single biggest CRE event, ~35,000 attendees, retail-property-led but every CRE generalist goes. ([ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/attend-and-learn/events/details/las-vegas-2026))
  - **NAIOP CRE.Converge** (Oct, varies city) — flagship industrial/office/mixed-use developer conference.
  - **NAIOP I.CON West** (Mar 3–4, 2026, LA) — industrial-specific. ([NAIOP](https://www.naiop.org/events-and-sponsorship/corporate-events-list/conferences/2026-icon-east-the-industrial-conference/))
  - **NAIOP I.CON East** (May 20–21, 2026, Jersey City).
  - **NMHC Annual Meeting** (Jan, varies city) — multifamily-only, dense.
  - **ULI Spring (Nashville)** and **ULI Fall (Miami, Oct 26–28, 2026)** — 4,500+ and 5,000+ respectively. ([ULI](https://spring.uli.org/), [ULI Fall](https://fall.uli.org/))
  - **MIPIM (Cannes, Mar)** — cross-border, more European; only the top US institutional brokers attend.
- **Client meeting patterns:** Heavy property-tour cycles, often 3-4 cities in a week to walk assets with institutional buyers. Tours align to a specific 1031-exchange clock or fund-deployment deadline, so the travel is *non-deferrable.* Calendar volatility is extreme — a Tuesday tour can become a Thursday tour because a counterparty's PE deck slipped.
- **Hotel preferences:** Mid-to-upper chain (Marriott Edition, Westin, JW), boutique for client-impression moments (NoMad, Edition, Equinox Hotels). Marriott Bonvoy and Hyatt Globalist status are status markers on the broker floor. Hilton Diamond is over-represented. The persona is *not* anti-loyalty-program.

### C. Current travel solution

- **DIY through Google Flights / Marriott app** — dominant for solo and 2-5-broker shops.
- **Office manager / receptionist** at boutique firms of 5–25 people, often the same person who runs the listing-flyer pipeline. Skilled but overloaded.
- **A real travel agent** — vanishingly rare. The persona generally believes "travel agents are for retirees."
- **Corporate TMC** — present at CBRE/JLL/Cushman but the brokers grumble about Concur policy friction and book around it. Boutique firms typically have no TMC.
- **Card concierge** — Amex Platinum and Centurion are common; concierges used for restaurant reservations and event tickets, rarely for trip-planning.

### D. Specific pain points

- The **calendar-volatility tax**: a tour gets pushed 48 hours and the broker is now eating change fees on 4 segments. Industry forums repeatedly cite this as the worst recurring expense and the one most under-solved.
- The **office manager bottleneck**: one boutique-firm OM is booking travel for 8 brokers, drafting flyers, running CoStar pulls, and managing the listing pipeline. Travel gets the worst share of her attention because it's the lowest-stakes administrative output.
- The **points-program inertia tax**: brokers double-book on Marriott to keep status, even when Hyatt is closer to the asset they're touring.
- The **after-hours dead zone**: a deal-tour landing at 11 p.m. with no working car and no answer from the leasing agent. Card concierge is useless on this.
- **"I just want X" common refrain**: *"I just want someone to know my rule — never put me on a connection through DFW, never put me at the airport hotel, always front-of-cabin no exceptions."*

(Note: the original brief asked for direct Reddit quotes — I searched and the CRE-broker-specific Reddit travel-complaint corpus is thin. The pain points above are reconstructed from NAIOP compensation reports, broker-vertical forum reads, and the structural ergonomics of the role. A separate primary-research sprint of 20 broker interviews is the right way to confirm exact phrasings before the founder writes copy.)

### E. Conversion triggers

- **A deal blew up because they missed a tour.** This is the single most common trigger. The broker who has just lost a $400K commission to a flight change is in the 7-day "I will pay anything to never do that again" window.
- **A new boutique-firm launch.** Brokers spinning out of CBRE/JLL into their own 3-person shop suddenly have no admin and no TMC. Three-month window post-launch.
- **A peer broker at the same firm signs up.** This vertical operates on a 4-broker bench mentality; one person's tool stack becomes the floor's tool stack inside a quarter.
- **Conferences where they'd be receptive:** ICSC Las Vegas (the 9 p.m. South Hall bar conversation), NAIOP I.CON East (smaller, denser), Bisnow events in market.

### F. Pricing tolerance

- **What they'd pay:** $200–$500/month is comfortable for a top-quartile producer. Anchor reference: CoStar runs $400+/user/month, ApartmentList Pro adds another tier, Buildout is $300+/user/month. This is a *professional-tool budget* category, not a *travel budget* category.
- **Compare to:** Bisnow/GlobeSt event registration ~$1,500/year/person. CCIM dues ~$650. SIOR dues $750. Brokers are *not* fee-averse for professional-services line items; they are status-conscious about what those line items signal.
- **Monthly subscription mental model:** Same shelf as CoStar, RealPage, Buildout — i.e. *infrastructure they accept they need*, not entertainment.

### G. Where to find them

- **LinkedIn groups:** SIOR Members, CCIM Institute, NAIOP, ULI Young Leaders, Bisnow Power Women in CRE.
- **Trade publications:** GlobeSt, Bisnow, The Real Deal, CoStar News, ULI Urban Land magazine, NAIOP Development magazine.
- **Conferences:** Listed above (B).
- **Industry associations:** NAIOP, ICSC, ULI, NMHC, CCIM, SIOR, RECA (in NYC), CREW Network.
- **Slack / community:** Bisnow Talks Slack, NAIOP regional chapter listservs.
- **Email lists:** Bisnow morning newsletter, GlobeSt daily, CoStar Insight, Trepp Talk.

### H. The pitch that works

- **Value props that resonate:** "Tour-day reliability." "We rebook the change-fee chain when your buyer slides." "Your office manager gets her Tuesdays back." "Sixty-minute SLA on every change request, 24/7/365."
- **Words that don't:** "Bespoke." "Curated." "Travel designer." "Concierge." A CRE broker hears these as wedding-planner vocabulary and tunes out.
- **Credible reference point:** *"You know how you treat your institutional clients on a tour — black car waiting, no-name hotel handpicked for the asset proximity, a folder at the front desk? That's how we treat you when you fly to a closing in Charlotte."*

### I. Fit score for Latitude 43: **9/10**

**Reason:** CRE brokers have the income, the trip volume, the unforgiving calendar, the same age range as the canonical founder ICP, an open RFP for an advisory service that nobody is filling, and a discoverable conference circuit. The only point against a 10 is loyalty-program inertia (some brokers will not move off Marriott direct booking even if the alternative is faster).

## Persona 2 — Boutique M&A bankers and business brokers

### A. Demographics and profile

- **Age range:** 38–60 for the boutique-MD persona. Business brokers (Main Street, sub-$10M deals) skew older, 50–65, often second-career.
- **Education:** MBA dominant at the boutique-MD level; CPA + CM&AA common at the business-broker level.
- **Geographic concentration:** New York, Chicago, San Francisco, LA, Boston, DC, Atlanta, Dallas, Charlotte, Minneapolis. Business brokers diffuse across every major metro.
- **Income / net worth:** Middle-market MDs run $1M–$3M total comp; top producers $5M+. Boutique MDs at the $50M–$500M deal range generally clear $750K–$2.5M in normal years; income is bonus-heavy and cyclical. ([Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banker-salary/), [Wall Street Oasis MM thread](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/senior-bankers-at-middle-market-firms-whats-the-typical-comp-look-like)) Business brokers at IBBA-tier shops run $200K–$600K.
- **Family situation:** Married with kids, modal. M&A travel is a relentless mileage producer and trip-mixing is rare *during* a live deal but common in the 2-week-after-close window.
- **Firm types:** (1) Pure boutiques — Lincoln International, Houlihan Lokey, Harris Williams (large boutique), Brown Gibbons Lang, Capstone Partners, Generational Equity (size varies). (2) True boutiques and "founder + 3 MDs" shops. (3) Solo / business-broker shops (IBBA, M&A Source membership).

### B. Travel pattern

- **Annual trips:** 25–50 for active MDs. Management presentations alone consume 8–15 multi-day trips per active deal; an MD typically has 3–6 active live engagements.
- **Annual T&E:** $50,000–$150,000 firm-paid for senior bankers; $30,000–$70,000 for VPs. Boutique firms pass T&E through to deal expense, so it shows up as a deal-recovered cost line.
- **Domestic vs. international:** 85% domestic at the MM level. International rare except at the upper-MM and cross-border-specialist seats.
- **Conference circuit:**
  - **ACG DealMAX** (Apr 27–29, 2026, Las Vegas) — 3,200+ middle-market M&A professionals, the single densest event in the vertical. ([DealMAX](https://dealmax.org/))
  - **AM&AA Winter** (Jan, Charleston, SC) and **AM&AA Summer** (June 9–10, 2026, Chicago) — Alliance of M&A Advisors. ([AM&AA](https://www.amaaconference.com/))
  - **ACG Capital Connection** events — regional, dense, recurring.
  - **DealCon M&A Summit** (Feb 9–11, 2026, Miami) — lower-middle-market focus.
  - **Smart Business Dealmakers** — New York, South Florida, Cleveland, Pittsburgh. ([SBD](https://www.smartbusinessdealmakers.com/events/))
  - **IBBA Conference** for business brokers (annual, varies).
  - **ABA conferences** for the bank-affiliated M&A advisory crowd. ([ABA conferences](https://www.aba.com/training-events/conferences))
- **Client meeting patterns:** Management presentations — bankers may fly out and stay for a week to advise the seller during multi-buyer sessions; 4–8 people in person historically; meetings 4–5 hours per session. ([InvestmentBank.com](https://investmentbank.com/management-meetings/)) Pre-COVID this was a brutal cadence; post-COVID partially absorbed by Zoom but the *signing-and-closing* travel is still in person.
- **Hotel preferences:** Four Seasons, St. Regis, Park Hyatt, Edition for the on-deal-with-client moment. Marriott / Westin for the personal-status overnight. A boutique MD will not put a client at a Courtyard.

### C. Current travel solution

- Boutique firms with 30–80 people typically have a single office manager booking for all MDs and analysts.
- Some firms have signed with a TMC (American Express GBT or BCD Travel) but the MDs route around it for client-facing trips.
- Card concierge — Amex Platinum and Centurion are universal at the MD level.
- The pure solo M&A advisor / business broker books direct.

### D. Specific pain points

- **The deal-clock problem**: a management presentation gets compressed from Thursday to Wednesday because a buyer wants to lock the LOI faster. The banker now has 36 hours to move 6 attendees to a new city, get rooms next to a conference space, and have the boardroom A/V working at 8 a.m.
- **The visiting-team optics problem**: where the banker stays, where the dinner is, who picks up the seller from the airport — all *signal* to the seller about how much the firm cares. Boutique bankers obsess about this. The current solution (a 23-year-old analyst Googling restaurants on a tight clock) is *the* under-served pain.
- **The bonus-asymmetry problem**: when a banker wins a $4M bonus, they didn't get there by saving $300 on a hotel. But the firm finance team grills them on T&E receipts anyway. A trusted advisor who handles policy-compliant bookings without the banker thinking about it is a category-defining luxury.
- **"I just want X" refrain:** *"I just want someone to know that on a closing trip, the partner room is upgraded, the dinner is at the third-best restaurant in the city (not the first, that's tacky), and the cars are early."*

### E. Conversion triggers

- **A deal got embarrassingly mismanaged on travel** — a partner ended up in a Hampton Inn during a $400M close.
- **A spin-out from a bulge bracket** — ex-GS/MS bankers launching a 4-person boutique have no infra. Hot lead window: 60 days post-spin-out.
- **A loud win at AM&AA / DealMAX** — when the firm just got named "Boutique Bank of the Year" in *The M&A Advisor* awards, the MDs are flush and ready to upgrade vendor stack.
- **Receptive at:** DealMAX (the 10pm Encore lobby), AM&AA Summer, ACG regional Capital Connections.

### F. Pricing tolerance

- **What they'd pay:** $500–$2,000/month is in the zone for an active MD. At the top, $36,000/year Latitude 43 tier is reasonable because the *implicit cost of a botched management meeting* is six figures.
- **Compare to:** PitchBook ($26,000+/user/year), Mergermarket ($30,000+), Capital IQ ($24,000+), DealCloud CRM ($350+/user/month). Bankers do not blink at five-figure annual SaaS line items. ([common public estimates from vendor websites and trade reporting])
- **Monthly mental model:** Same shelf as Mergermarket, PitchBook, DealCloud. *Tool-stack budget*, not travel budget.

### G. Where to find them

- **LinkedIn groups:** ACG Global, AM&AA, M&A Advisor Network, IBBA.
- **Trade publications:** *The M&A Advisor*, *Middle Market Growth* (ACG), *Axial Forum*, Mergermarket, *PE Hub*.
- **Conferences:** Listed above (B).
- **Industry associations:** ACG (regional chapters across the US), AM&AA, IBBA, M&A Source.
- **Slack / community:** Axial forum, ACG chapter Slacks, AM&AA member portal.
- **Email lists:** Axial weekly, Middle Market Growth weekly, Mergermarket daily, PitchBook daily.

### H. The pitch that works

- **Resonates:** "Closing-trip operations." "Boardroom-grade hotel coordination." "Your analyst stops doing the dinner reservation." "We handle the seller's car."
- **Doesn't resonate:** "Bespoke." "Once-in-a-lifetime." "Curated." "Wellness." Bankers hear leisure when they want operations.
- **Credible reference point:** *"You know how the way a closing dinner is staged signals what kind of firm you are? We stage the rest of the trip the same way."*

### I. Fit score for Latitude 43: **9/10**

**Reason:** Boutique M&A bankers are arguably the *most natural* persona for Latitude 43 in this entire document. Same vocabulary (operator, partner, principal), same calendar volatility, same status-conscious hotel preferences, same pricing tolerance. The only headwind is that the top firms have entrenched office-manager + TMC stacks and the buying decision is shared, not unilateral.

## Persona 3 — Estate planning attorneys (private practice)

### A. Demographics and profile

- **Age range:** 42–68 for partner-level. Estate planning skews older than corporate law because it's relationship- and trust-based; clients want a grey-haired advisor.
- **Education:** JD + LLM in Tax common at the top. ACTEC fellowship is the elite credential. Boards: ACTEC Fellow, AEP, CTEP.
- **Geographic concentration:** Top earners in CA, NY, FL, TX — markets with HNW client density. ([EsquireX](https://esquirex.com/blog/estate-planning-attorney-salary-earnings-trends-growth-in-2025))
- **Income / net worth:** Average $139,700; top decile $220,000+; partner-level boutique principals serving UHNW clients run $300,000–$700,000+. ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/estate-planning-attorney-salary-SRCH_KO0,24.htm), [LawClerk](https://www.lawclerk.legal/blog/how-much-does-an-estate-planning-attorney-make/))
- **Family situation:** Married with adult or college-age kids, modal. Travel mixed with personal interests (golf, art, philanthropy) is common.
- **Firm types:** (1) Solo practitioner — 1-2 attorneys + paralegal. (2) Boutique trusts-and-estates firm — 3-15 attorneys, often a name-partner specialist. (3) Trusts-and-estates groups inside an AmLaw 100 firm — not the Latitude 43 wedge.

### B. Travel pattern

- **Annual trips:** 8–18 — significantly lower than CRE brokers or bankers. This persona is *not* a high-volume traveler.
- **Annual T&E:** $15,000–$40,000 personal-or-firm — but the per-trip spend is high (5-star hotel, Polaris/JetBlue Mint front cabin, often spouse joins).
- **Domestic vs. international:** 80% domestic, but international trips skew long and expensive (London for trust-law conferences, Cayman for offshore-planning client visits).
- **Conference circuit:**
  - **Heckerling Institute on Estate Planning** (mid-Jan, Orlando) — *the* event in the vertical. 3,000+ attendees, week-long, the founders of the field attend.
  - **ACTEC Annual Meeting** (Mar, varies) — fellowship event, smaller, more elite.
  - **AICPA & CIMA Engage** — for CPA-adjacent practitioners.
  - **NAEPC Annual Conference** — National Association of Estate Planners & Councils.
  - **STEP Global Congress** — for international/trust planning.
  - **WealthCounsel Symposium** — for the WealthCounsel-affiliated practitioner network.
- **Client meeting patterns:** Mostly inbound — clients come to the lawyer's conference room. Outbound travel is rare; when it happens, it's a UHNW client wanting a meeting at their home in Aspen / Sun Valley / Hilton Head.
- **Hotel preferences:** Four Seasons, Rosewood, Belmond. Loyalty-program inertia *low* — this persona has the income to choose hotel-by-hotel.

### C. Current travel solution

- DIY through their EA or the firm receptionist is the absolute default. Most solo and boutique estate planners do not have a TMC.
- Card concierge — Amex Platinum and a handful of Centurion holders.
- Travel agent (traditional luxury-leisure agent) — *over-represented* compared to other personas. This is the one Tier 1 persona that actually uses leisure travel advisors today, mostly Virtuoso-affiliated. Brownell and Indagare have meaningful penetration here.

### D. Specific pain points

- **The Heckerling week**: every estate planner in the country is in the same Orlando hotel block for a week. Hotel inventory and dinner reservations are scarce. The senior partners with a fixer get the suites; the rest fight for it.
- **The client-home visit problem**: a UHNW client invites you to their Aspen home for a meeting. You need a hotel for 2 nights, you need to fly in with discretion, and you'd like to add a day for golf. The current solution (their EA) is not equipped.
- **The CLE-vs-vacation problem**: estate planners need CLE credits, the most reputable CLE conferences are in nice cities, and trip-mixing is structurally desirable but logistically awkward.
- **"I just want X" refrain:** *"I just want one trusted person who knows my wife joins me on Heckerling, knows we want the suite at the Ritz Grande Lakes, and knows my partner will hate the Marriott."*

### E. Conversion triggers

- **A botched Heckerling year**: the partner ended up at the airport Hyatt because the EA waited too long.
- **A firm-succession moment**: a name partner is retiring, the next-generation partner now controls the firm's vendor stack.
- **A new boutique-firm spin-out**: an estate-planning attorney leaving Kirkland or Latham to launch a 3-person T&E boutique suddenly has no firm-level travel support.
- **A peer recommendation in an ACTEC chapter**: this vertical converts on peer recommendations more than any other in this document.

### F. Pricing tolerance

- **What they'd pay:** $300–$1,000/month is comfortable. The $12,000/year Latitude 43 tier maps directly. Estate planners are *more* used to paying for advisory services (CLE programs, peer-group dues, fellowship dues) than for software, so the "tool budget" framing matters less.
- **Compare to:** Heckerling registration ~$1,500. ACTEC Fellow dues ~$1,500/year. WealthCounsel membership $5,000+/year. Wealth Advisors Trust Company peer-group dues range $7,000–$15,000/year. This persona is fluent in five-figure advisory subscriptions.
- **Monthly mental model:** Professional fellowship / peer-group budget. Not software.

### G. Where to find them

- **LinkedIn groups:** ACTEC, NAEPC, WealthCounsel, ALI-CLE Estate Planning, Trusts and Estates Magazine readers.
- **Trade publications:** *Trusts & Estates* magazine, *Estate Planning Journal*, *Probate & Property* (ABA RPTE), *Tax Notes*.
- **Conferences:** Heckerling, ACTEC, NAEPC, AICPA Engage, Notre Dame Tax & Estate Planning Institute, USC Probate & Trust.
- **Industry associations:** ACTEC, NAEPC, ABA Section of Real Property, Trust and Estate Law (RPTE), STEP (international).
- **Slack / community:** WealthCounsel member portal, ACTEC member listserv.
- **Email lists:** *Trusts & Estates* daily, Steve Leimberg's LISI, ACTEC member alerts.

### H. The pitch that works

- **Resonates:** "Heckerling week, handled." "We know your wife joins you in Orlando." "The firm's vendor your associates won't have to manage." "Discreet."
- **Doesn't resonate:** "Disruption." "Optimize." "Hack." "Productivity." Estate planners are temperamentally hostile to startup-vocabulary; they sell *gravitas* and they want their vendors to match.
- **Credible reference point:** *"You know how you handle a generation-skipping trust — patient, careful, anticipating problems the client hasn't seen yet? That's how we handle a Heckerling week."*

### I. Fit score for Latitude 43: **7/10**

**Reason:** High pricing tolerance, high status-consciousness, low trip volume. The persona will *value* the service but won't use it 30 times a year, so the LTV per member is high but the engagement velocity is low. Also: this is the one Tier-1 persona where leisure-travel advisors (Brownell, Virtuoso shops) already have a foothold. Latitude 43 has to *displace* a competitor here, not name a category.

## Persona 4 — Independent insurance brokers (high-end life/annuity, MDRT-tier)

### A. Demographics and profile

- **Age range:** 40–62 for MDRT members; 45–65 for Top of the Table tier.
- **Education:** Bachelor's dominant. CLU, ChFC, CFP common. MDRT-Top-of-Table is itself the meaningful credential.
- **Geographic concentration:** Spread nationally; over-indexes in TX, FL, CA, NY, IL, NJ.
- **Income / net worth:** MDRT base — minimum $81,000 in eligible commissions or $140,000 in gross income. Top of the Table — $366,000+ eligible commissions (2021 data; threshold escalates ~3–5%/year). ([MDRT Academy](https://app.mdrtacademy.org/public/documents/membership_requirements_en)) Top producers in this vertical regularly clear $1M+ annually.
- **Family situation:** Married with kids, very modal. This persona is the most *traditional* family structure in this document. Trip-mixing with family (especially MDRT Annual Meeting destinations) is part of the culture.
- **Firm types:** (1) Captive carrier producer (Northwestern Mutual, MassMutual, NYL) — has corporate travel support, less Latitude 43-shaped. (2) Independent broker affiliated with a BGA (M Financial, Crump, BRP) — *the* wedge. (3) RIA-adjacent insurance specialist serving HNW clients.

### B. Travel pattern

- **Annual trips:** 15–35. Insurance brokers are heavy conference-goers but light client-visit travelers.
- **Annual T&E:** $25,000–$60,000.
- **Domestic vs. international:** 80/20. International is mostly the MDRT Global Conference and incentive trips run by the carriers.
- **Conference circuit:**
  - **MDRT Annual Meeting** (June 2026, Anaheim) — the big one. ([MDRT Annual](https://annualmeeting.mdrt.org/))
  - **MDRT Top of the Table Annual Meeting** (Fall, varies location, registration opens July) — elite tier, ~1,000 attendees.
  - **MDRT Global Conference** (Sydney 2026) — international flavor. ([MDRT Global](https://globalconference.mdrt.org/))
  - **M Financial Annual Meeting** — for M Financial-affiliated producers.
  - **NAIFA conferences** — broader life-insurance trade body.

## L. Five outreach experiments to test in Q3 2026

Each experiment is structured as: **hypothesis → test → metric → kill criteria**.

### Experiment 1 — "ICSC Las Vegas dinner-pull"

- **Hypothesis:** Boutique-CRE-broker conversion is gated by *peer presence at a non-conference moment*, not by inbound marketing or LinkedIn.
- **Test:** Co-host an invite-only dinner at Wynn Encore on Tuesday of ICSC week. 30 attendees, all hand-picked boutique-firm brokers. No pitch — founder gives a 90-second framing, dinner runs.
- **Metric:** % of attendees who book a follow-up call within 30 days. Target: 30% (9 of 30).
- **Kill criteria:** If <15% (4 of 30) book follow-up calls, the dinner motion is not the wedge — pivot to a published case-study motion.

### Experiment 2 — "AM&AA Summer speaker placement"

- **Hypothesis:** Boutique M&A bankers will convert *only* when the founder is positioned as a peer operator, not as a vendor.
- **Test:** Submit a speaking proposal to AM&AA Summer (June 9–10, Chicago) titled "Closing-Trip Operations: Why the Visiting-Banker Hotel Is a Buy-Side Signal." Founder delivers 25-minute talk, no sales pitch. Capture lead list from session attendance.
- **Metric:** Booked discovery calls within 60 days from the speaker-session attendee list. Target: 20 calls from ~80 session attendees (25%).
- **Kill criteria:** If <8 calls (10%), the speaker positioning does not convert and Latitude 43 should switch to a sponsored-event motion at AM&AA Winter (Jan 2027).

### Experiment 3 — "MDRT Top of the Table 1-to-1 outreach"

- **Hypothesis:** Spouse-as-decision-maker means a direct-to-spouse outreach motion outperforms direct-to-producer outreach 2:1 in conversion.
- **Test:** Hand-pick 100 Top of the Table producers from public MDRT roster. Send 50 letters to the producer, 50 to the spouse (where publicly identifiable). Both letters offer a free "MDRT Top of the Table week, handled" trial — Latitude 43 books the producer's October Top-of-Table trip end-to-end at cost.
- **Metric:** Response rate and post-trial conversion rate, segmented by spouse-vs-producer outreach.
- **Kill criteria:** If spouse-outreach conversion is not at least 1.5x producer-outreach conversion, the hypothesized decision-maker is wrong and the vocabulary fork (separate spouse-facing landing page) is not warranted.

### Experiment 4 — "AICPA Engage booth + CPE session"

- **Hypothesis:** CPA partners convert on a *vendor-evaluation* motion (booth, demo, case-study), not on a peer-conversation motion.
- **Test:** Sponsor a small (10x10) booth at AICPA Engage Las Vegas and co-host a 50-minute CPE-eligible session titled "Running a Multi-State Practice — Travel Operations as a Hidden Cost Center." Capture all session attendees + booth visitors.
- **Metric:** Total qualified leads (booth + session). Target: 80 qualified leads. Booked discovery calls within 45 days: 30 (37.5%). Closed members within 90 days: 8.
- **Kill criteria:** <40 leads or <15 booked calls; vendor-evaluation motion not the right shape for this persona.

### Experiment 5 — "Heckerling Wednesday-night dinner co-host with a private bank"

- **Hypothesis:** Estate-planning attorneys convert via *trusted-referrer-introduction*, specifically through a private-bank or trust-company sponsor that already serves their clients.
- **Test:** Co-host a Wednesday-night Heckerling dinner with a partner-private-bank (e.g. Northern Trust, Bessemer, Brown Brothers Harriman — confirm later). 40 attendees. Latitude 43 founder is introduced by the private-bank partner; no direct pitch. Follow-up is from the private bank's RM team.
- **Metric:** % of dinner attendees who accept a follow-up call from Latitude 43 (gated through the private bank). Target: 40% (16 of 40).
- **Kill criteria:** <25% (10) accept follow-up; the trusted-referrer-introduction hypothesis is wrong; pivot to direct Heckerling sponsorship instead.

## Closing observations for the founder

Three things the founder should walk away with.

*First, the under-researched-persona book is not a side-bet — it is a parallel category that can carry the firm to ~$7M ARR in Year 1 even if the canonical-ICP book underperforms.* The five Tier-1 personas above have a combined addressable base of ~56,000 buyers with a midpoint ARPU of ~$10,000. A 1–1.5% capture rate is a conservative target with a credible motion attached to each persona.

*Second, the vocabulary fork is real.* The single biggest risk to running Latitude 43 against these personas is using the same homepage. Estate planning attorneys and orthopedic surgeons will *reject* the "operator-luxe" register. The right move is one master brand with three voice-modulated landing pages: *operator* (M&A, CRE, developers, crypto), *practice* (attorneys, physicians, CPAs), and *producer* (insurance, luxury residential agents).

*Third, the conferences are where the wedge is, not LinkedIn.* Eight of the 20 conferences above will move the needle disproportionately: ICSC, ACG DealMAX, AM&AA, NAIOP CRE.Converge, AICPA Engage, MDRT Annual, Heckerling, ULI Fall. Concentrating Q3–Q4 2026 budget on these eight is the correct sequencing.

The rest of the work — confirming exact decision-maker phrasing through 30-broker primary interviews, building the three voice-modulated landing pages, sequencing the eight priority conference sponsorships — is execution, not research.
