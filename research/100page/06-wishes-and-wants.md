# 06 — Wishes & Wants: What Business Travelers Say They Wish Was Easier

*Research compiled May 2026. Focus: the positive frame — what entrepreneurs and executives say they want, imagine, fantasize about. Not the pain. The pain we know. This is the daydream.*

---

## Methodology

We mined seven veins of source material: (1) frequent-flyer and road-warrior forums (FlyerTalk, Quora, Ask MetaFilter, Bogleheads), (2) travel-agency testimonial pages (CIRE, Gravitate, The Travel Agent Inc., Artisan Journeys, Renshaw), (3) GBTA / Deloitte / SAP Concur / Amex GBT / Skift industry surveys 2025–2026, (4) executive-assistant industry coverage (Office Dynamics, Boldly, Belay, ExecutiveSupportMagazine, OfficeNinjas), (5) CEO-praise threads in Inc., Entrepreneur, Chief Executive, IndustryWeek, (6) hotel and airline preference research (BCD, Engine, JTB, Tourism Review), and (7) jet-lag / wellness coverage (TravelPerk, Timeshifter, Fast Company, CNN Travel).

Where a specific named human is quoted, we credit them. Where the language comes from an article paraphrase rather than a direct interview, we mark it as **[paraphrase]** and treat it as a softer signal. Where it comes from anonymous forum users we credit by handle / role / forum.

Every quote in Section A is real and traceable to one of the URLs in the source list. Where the quote is paraphrase-attributed in the original article (e.g., GBTA's "perfect business trip" findings) we say so.

## Section B — The 15 underlying wishes (synthesis)

Reading across all fifty quotes plus the broader corpus, the wishes cluster into fifteen deep desires. Each is a positive-frame restatement of the dominant pain in that cluster.

**1. "I want one person who knows me."** The single most frequent praise pattern: a named human (Eric, Nikki, Robyn, Scott) who has accumulated context on the speaker over many trips. The wish underneath: relational continuity. Anti-wish: being routed through a pod or call-center pool. (Quotes 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 43, 48.)

**2. "I want the rebook to happen before I notice."** Predictive disruption management — the system flips the itinerary before the speaker has opened the email. (Quotes 1, 3, 7, 33, 38, 39.)

**3. "I want my family time protected from work travel."** Clustering trips, avoiding weekends, "home for Friday dinner" — travelers want the office of travel to be a guardian of personal time, not just a logistics layer. (Quotes 12, 27, 37; supporting articles: lawaltravel.com, Inc., Bright Horizons.)

**4. "I want to arrive ready, not exhausted."** Choosing direct flights, business-class for sleep, jet-lag protocols, recovery buffers built in. The win-condition is *capacity at the destination*, not the journey. (Quotes 16, 17, 31.)

**5. "I want to never sit on hold again."** Customer-service hold times of one to twelve hours during disruption are the universally hated experience. The wish: a number that picks up. (Implicit in quotes 3, 6, 7, 46; Washington Post on hold times.)

**6. "I want my preferences remembered forever."** Aisle seat, decaf, low floor, no early-morning departures, hates connecting in ORD — captured once and applied every booking, by everyone. (Quotes 8, 13, 20, 23, 43.)

**7. "I want trust that someone has my back at 2am."** The 2am-flight-cancellation test. The wish isn't a chatbot; it's the certainty that a competent human will answer. (Quotes 3, 6, 7, 11.)

**8. "I want my points to actually do something."** Loyalty programs have devalued; "free" flights come with hundreds in fees. The wish: that accumulated loyalty translates into a real, redeemable outcome. (Quote 30; Travel and Tour World loyalty-trap article. https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/travel-loyalty-scam-2026-airlines-and-hotels-accused-of-turning-rewards-into-a-points-trap/)

**9. "I want a hotel room that feels like the same room."** Standardisation of the consequential details (mattress, Wi-Fi, breakfast, soundproofing, working desk) across every property, every city. (Quotes 6, 19, 21.)

**10. "I want the boring parts of travel to disappear."** Expense reports, receipt-capture, visa renewals, seat selection, hotel re-confirmation. Zero-touch reconciliation. (Quotes 23, 26, 32.)

**11. "I want the privacy of not being tracked by my company."** A subtle but persistent wish in the founder/CEO segment — agency that doesn't surface to the board, expense lines that don't broadcast personal preferences. (Implied in quote 12; reinforced by IndustryWeek's "under the radar" finding.)

**12. "I want my EA to focus on higher-leverage things."** When EAs spend twenty hours a week booking flights, that's twenty hours not spent on calendar strategy, board prep, or relationship management. The wish: a travel office that absorbs the EA's lowest-leverage work. (Implicit in quote 49, 50; reinforced by ProAssisting and Belay.)

**13. "I want the airline to know who I am."** Recognition at check-in, lounge access without showing the same card twice, gate-side rebook because the system flags the elite status. (Quotes 20, 44, 45.)

**14. "I want to not pack three times."** The "I keep a duplicate toiletry kit packed" hack is universal among road warriors. The wish: a packing layer that just exists. (Implicit in the Where I've Been and CBoarding Group road-warrior listicles. https://whereivebeentravel.com/20-business-travel-tips-from-experienced-road-warriors/)

**15. "I want to control the chaos."** Above everything, control. The single common thread is that travelers want the travel system to behave less like a casino and more like an appliance — predictable, recoverable, sealed-off from their actual workday. (Quotes 14, 17, 22, 31, 35.)

## Closing observation

The single most striking pattern across 150+ quotes reviewed is how rarely anyone wishes for *more* — more options, more upgrades, more rewards, more apps. The dominant wish is for *less*: less surface area for things to go wrong, less time on the phone, less context-switching, less to remember, less surprise, less performance. The road warrior's daydream is not a luxury experience. It is a *frictionless* one.

That asymmetry — luxury markets selling more, the customer wishing for less — is the gap Latitude 43 occupies. "The travel office" works because the word *office* implies subtraction: someone else holds the work, you get the result. Every wish in Section A and Section B is a vote for that shape of service. Every quote in Section D is a description of the operational character — anticipation, discretion, attention — required to actually deliver it.

The positioning is already in the customer's mouth. We just have to be the firm that means it.
