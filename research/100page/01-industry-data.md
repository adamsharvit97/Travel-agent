## Section C — Statistical narratives

Each narrative ~300 words. Sourced inline.

### C1. The growth story: business travel 2020–2026

The pandemic erased two-thirds of business travel almost overnight. In 2020 global business travel spend collapsed from roughly $1.43T to ~$700B. Every credible 2020-vintage forecast — McKinsey, Bain, GBTA — predicted a structurally lower steady state. Zoom would replace the regional sales meeting; virtual conferences would replace trade shows; the "death of business travel" became a stock article.

It wasn't.

By 2023, GBTA's Business Travel Index showed spend at $1.36T. By 2024, the US business travel market alone had passed $1.5T globally and the US share was set to retake #1 from China ([GBTA, Jul 2025](https://gbta.org/global-business-travel-spending-to-reach-1-57-trillion-in-2025-amid-trade-policy-uncertainty-and-economic-risk-according-to-new-gbta-forecast/)). 2025 will close at **$1.57T global**, a new all-time high, with the US contributing **$395.4B**. 2026 is projected at **$1.69T**, growing **8.1%**, and the $2T threshold is expected to fall in 2029.

Two things are notable. First, the recovery is uneven. Deloitte's 2024 study found that 73% of travel managers expected spend to grow that year, but the "real recovery" — in trips per traveler, not just dollars — has been slower because each trip costs more ([Deloitte, 2024](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/transportation/corporate-business-travel-survey/2024.html)). Inflation in airfares and hotel rates has done much of the work of growing the headline number.

Second, the segment doing the heavy lifting is *not* the F500. SMBs — defined as 1–200 employees — represent **26.1%** of the market today and are projected to grow at a **7.1% CAGR** through 2029 ([Skift / Navan, 2025](https://skift.com/2025/08/01/unmanaged-business-travel-still-dominates-as-smbs-seek-flexible-consumer-style-booking/)). 53% of SMEs plan to *increase* 2025 spend ([BTN, 2025](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/Research/SME-Report/2025/Small-Midsize-Enterprise-Business-Travel-Durability)). The frontier of growth is the founder, the partner, the principal — exactly the buyer least served by legacy infrastructure.

### C2. The disruption story: how broken travel really is

In 2025, **80%** of business travelers experienced at least one disruption — delay, cancellation, missed connection, lost bag, hotel error ([Perk State of Travel Disruption 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/)). The Fortune-cited follow-up survey put it at **89%**, a three-year high ([Fortune, May 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/19/89-travelers-disruptions-top-concern-paying-more-summer-travel/)). Nearly **1 in 4** US flights ran late in 2025 ([The Traveler / DOT BTS](https://www.thetraveler.org/nearly-1-in-4-flights-ran-late-in-2025-as-2026-risks-mount/)). **56%** of business travelers had a delay of more than an hour; **42%** of US travelers had at least one cancellation last year, up from 25% in 2023 ([Perk, 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/)).

Disruption is no longer an outlier — it's the modal experience. Even "best in class" carriers (Delta, United) only achieve **73–79% on-time**. PSA Airlines, an American Eagle regional operator, came in at 65.7% ([Visual Capitalist using BTS data](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/cp/us-airlines-most-and-least-on-time/)).

The cost to US companies of covering the fallout from cancellations alone is estimated at **$17B+/year**, roughly 4% of the $395B US business travel spend ([Perk](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/)). The FAA's broader delay model puts the systemic cost north of **$33B/year**, with passengers absorbing **~$18.1B** directly ([Airlines for America delay dataset](https://www.airlines.org/dataset/u-s-passenger-carrier-delay-costs/)).

What's broken isn't just airline operations. It's the whole *response* layer. When a flight cancels at 11pm, the unmanaged traveler is alone on the United app fighting 400 other refugees for the same three middle seats on the morning rebook. Travel-managed programs offer "agent on call" but with hold times routinely 30–90 minutes ([Adelman Travel KPIs](https://www.adelmantravel.com/blogs/for-travelers/things-your-tmc-should-be-doing-for-top-notch-customer-service/)). Card concierges (Amex Centurion) advertise 24/7 humans but cardholder forums document inconsistent response times and patchy results ([NerdWallet Centurion review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/american-express-concierge)).

The promise of the new buyer category is exactly this: take the call before the traveler even knows there's a problem.

### C3. The unmanaged middle: the $1 trillion gap

Roughly **65% of global business travel spend is unmanaged** ([Skift / Navan, 2025](https://skift.com/2025/08/01/unmanaged-business-travel-still-dominates-as-smbs-seek-flexible-consumer-style-booking/)). On a $1.57T base, that's **~$1.02 trillion**. In the US alone, ~65% of $395.4B = **~$257B** of spend with no TMC, no negotiated rates, no duty-of-care framework, no after-hours phone number.

The unmanaged share is concentrated in SMBs — and SMBs are the **fastest-growing segment of the market**, with 7.1% CAGR through 2029, vs sub-5% for enterprise. By 2029, with global spend at $2.9T (Navan/Skift projection), the unmanaged sliver is on track to be **$1.9T globally**. This is one of the largest underserved adjacent markets in any service economy.

Why is it unmanaged? Three reasons emerge clearly in the data:

1. **Tool/UX mismatch.** SMBs say legacy TMC tools feel worse than Expedia or Google Flights. 53% of SMBs increasing spend in 2025 are looking for "consumer-style" booking ([BTN SME 2025](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/Research/SME-Report/2025/Small-Midsize-Enterprise-Business-Travel-Durability)).
2. **Price.** TMCs charge **$7–25 per transaction** plus annual fees of **$3K–8K** for small accounts ([Engine 2024](https://engine.com/business-travel-guide/calculate-true-tmc-costs)). For a 5-person startup taking 50 trips/year, that's a meaningful tax with unclear ROI.
3. **No relationship layer.** Concur and Navan are good at the 95% of trips that are simple. The 5% that are complex — the surprise IPO roadshow, the spouse-and-kids board offsite, the cancelled-at-midnight flight to a closing — fall through. That's the niche the human-advisor category fills, and it's where Preface plays.

This is not a niche. **The unmanaged middle is the market.** Every existing TMC's growth strategy is to *move down-market*; every advisor brand's strategy is to *productise up*. They meet in the same buyer.

### C4. The time cost story: hours per traveler per year

The average US business traveler takes **6.8 trips per year** ([Perk, 2025](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-statistics/)). Each trip loses an average of **6.9 hours to stress-related productivity drop** ([Booking.com, 2025](https://business.booking.com/en-us/business-travel-resources/blog/35-productivity-statistics-for-business-travelers-in-2025/)). That's **~47 hours/year** lost for the average traveler — more than a full work-week.

For **road warriors** (10+ trips/year, ~10% of all business travelers per Deloitte) the math is brutal:

| Component | Hours / year |
|---|---:|
| Stress-related loss across 10 trips × 6.9 hrs | 69 |
| Disruption time (avg 4.75 hrs × ~50% disruption rate × 10 trips) | 24 |
| Jet-lag productivity drag (~20% on travel days, ~30 international days) | 48 |
| Booking & expense admin (industry estimate 2 hrs/trip × 10) | 20 |
| **Total annual time tax** | **~161 hours** |

That is **4–5 working weeks per year** lost by a single senior employee, before counting weekends spent recovering or missing family events. At a loaded cost of $250/hour (typical for senior business roles), that's **~$40K of disguised cost per road warrior, per year** — and we haven't yet counted missed deals.

C-suite specifically: **45% of C-level executives lose 4–8 hours per trip to travel stress** ([HR Director, 2024](https://www.thehrdirector.com/business-news/travel/untold-cost-business-travellers-loss-productivity-due-poor-planning/)). At 15 trips per year, that's 60–120 hours — roughly the equivalent of a junior FTE that doesn't exist on the org chart but is being paid.

This is the strongest empirical case for the human-advisor model: even if the advisor *only* recovers the booking-and-disruption-handling time, the ROI is positive at any reasonable fee structure under $25K/year.

### C5. The dollar cost story: real total cost of business travel

Headline numbers in this space focus on airfare and hotel — what a CFO sees on a P&L. But the *real* cost of business travel includes five layers, only one of which appears on the expense report:

| Layer | Per-trip cost | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Direct (air, hotel, ground, meals) | $1,200–$2,000 | [GBTA](https://www.ottotheagent.com/blog/average-per-diem-rates-business-travel) |
| 2. Booking/admin time (EA or self) at $50–$200/hr × ~2 hrs | $100–$400 | Industry estimate |
| 3. Disruption probability-weighted loss (~50% × 4.75 hrs × $95/hr loaded) | ~$225 | [Perk](https://www.perk.com/blog/business-travel-chaos-survey/) |
| 4. Stress / jet-lag productivity drag (6.9 hrs × $95/hr) | $662 | [Booking.com](https://business.booking.com/en-us/business-travel-resources/blog/35-productivity-statistics-for-business-travelers-in-2025/) |
| 5. Expense report processing | $58 (+$52 to correct) | [GBTA](https://gbta.org/pain-points-and-expense-reports/) |
| **Total real cost / trip** | **~$2,250–$3,300** | Derived |

At the segment level, the same math says US business travel's "shown" cost is **$395B** but its *real* cost is closer to **$600–700B** annually once you load in the 30–40% of cost hidden in time, stress, and rework.

There's a counterweight that often goes uncited: the GBTA × ASTA 2025 ROI study found business travel returns **$14.60 of net operating margin per $1 spent** ([GBTA × ASTA, Nov 2025](https://gbta.org/u-s-firms-could-unlock-2-4-trillion-in-sales-by-optimizing-business-travel-new-gbta-study-finds/)). Customer-meeting trips ROI is **$15–$19.99/$1**; conference trips $4–$5.99/$1. A modest **+8.3% in T&E** drives **+6% in sales**. US firms could capture **$2.4T in additional revenue** by optimising rather than cutting travel.

The headline finding for this report: business travel is *not* a cost centre. It's a 15× ROI lever currently being run by a junior admin with Google Flights at 11pm. The unit economics of the time saved by a good advisor easily justify a $25K–$100K annual fee for any founder doing 15+ trips/year.

### C6. The mental load story: research on decision fatigue

Roy Baumeister and Kathleen Vohs's landmark 2008 paper, *Making Choices Impairs Subsequent Self-Control*, established the empirical foundation for "decision fatigue." In a series of experiments, subjects who had made many small choices — even trivial ones — performed worse on subsequent self-control and reasoning tasks ([Vohs, Baumeister et al. 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237738528_Decision_Fatigue_Exhausts_Self-Regulatory_Resources_-_But_So_Does_Accommodating_to_Unchosen_Alternatives)). They were less willing to plan, more avoidant, and more impulsive ([PMC review, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6119549/)).

Travel planning is, structurally, the worst kind of decision sequence:
- High option count (often >100 flight options visible)
- Multi-attribute tradeoffs (price, time, layover, airline, loyalty status)
- Time pressure
- Stakes that aren't obvious in the moment but compound

Choice-overload studies in travel specifically show that **>22 options** measurably increase regret and reduce decision confidence ([Park & Jang, 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278431919304979)). And women — who make **up to 82%** of household travel decisions — bear disproportionate exposure ([Much Better Adventures summary, 2024](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/decision-fatigue-travel-planning/)).

Jet lag compounds this. UC Berkeley's controlled trial showed cognitive impairment from jet lag persists **up to one month** after exposure ([Berkeley News, 2010](http://news.berkeley.edu/2010/11/24/jetlag/)). Cabin crew with years of circadian disruption show measurable working-memory deficits ([Cho 2000 / J. Neuroscience](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/20/6/RC66.full.pdf)). Frequent business travelers report **~20% productivity loss** linked to jet lag ([Booking.com, 2025](https://business.booking.com/en-us/business-travel-resources/blog/35-productivity-statistics-for-business-travelers-in-2025/)).

The synthesis: a founder doing 15 international trips/year is taking a cocktail of (a) chronic decision fatigue from booking, (b) repeated acute cognitive impairment from jet lag lasting weeks, and (c) decision-quality erosion that *they cannot perceive in themselves* (Baumeister's subjects also report feeling fine). The advisor value proposition is, in part, neuroscientific: outsource enough choices to keep the executive's prefrontal cortex available for the decisions only they can make.

### C7. The family / personal cost story

Business travel doesn't end at the airport. The household absorbs a measurable share of the cost — and this is the part of the picture least likely to show up in any GBTA or BTN survey, because the spouse is not the respondent.

A landmark World Bank / NIH study found that **~50% of spouses of frequent international business travelers report high or very high stress**; for the travelers themselves, **~75%** ([PMC International Business Travel Impact on Families](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1740288/)). Spouses with children, female spouses, and younger spouses reported the worst impact. The Alliance Law Group's review of high-traveler-couple divorce rates found significantly elevated separation among households where one partner travels 100+ nights/year ([Alliance Law Group](https://alliancelg.com/traveling-spouse-divorce-rate)); ABC News' coverage of the original Greenfield Online study reported similar patterns ([ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Health/story?id=116957&page=1)).

In 2024 World Travel Protection's survey ([WTP / Opinium, n=1,000](https://worldtravelprotection.com/travel-insights/frequent-flyers-frequent-stress-the-mental-health-impact-of-business-travel/)):
- **30%** of US business travelers reported homesickness during work travel
- **41%** cited "time away from loved ones" as a top drawback (Statista, 2024)
- Women report anxiety from business travel at **38%** vs men at **31%**

Then there is the unmeasured: missed kids' birthdays, school plays, anniversaries, eldercare appointments, friends' weddings. A founder doing 60 nights/year on the road misses ~5 weekends per year of these — not catastrophic in any single year, but compounding over a decade of company-building into a measurable cost on family relationships.

This is where the *human* advisor differentiates against the platform. Software can rebook a flight. It cannot make sure the founder is home for the recital. The advisor's job, properly framed, is to be the buffer between the company's travel needs and the family's ability to absorb them — a category of work that platforms structurally cannot perform because it requires knowing things about the client that no SaaS tool ever asks.

### C8. The productivity story: lost work, lost meetings, lost deals

The productivity case for fixing business travel is the strongest of the eight. Three numbers do most of the work:

1. **86%** of business travelers who find journeys stressful lose at least one productive hour at the destination ([Booking.com, 2025](https://business.booking.com/en-us/business-travel-resources/blog/35-productivity-statistics-for-business-travelers-in-2025/)).
2. **45%** of C-level executives lose **4–8 hours per trip** to travel stress ([HR Director, 2024](https://www.thehrdirector.com/business-news/travel/untold-cost-business-travellers-loss-productivity-due-poor-planning/)).
3. **Saving $1M/year in economy class costs $3M/year in lost productivity and stress** ([HR Director, 2024](https://www.thehrdirector.com/business-news/travel/untold-cost-business-travellers-loss-productivity-due-poor-planning/)) — the cost-saver's own KPI is upside-down.

And on the opportunity side:
- Customer-meeting travel: **$15–$19.99 ROI per $1 spent** ([GBTA × ASTA](https://gbta.org/u-s-firms-could-unlock-2-4-trillion-in-sales-by-optimizing-business-travel-new-gbta-study-finds/))
- Every **+1%** of managed-travel share → **+0.20%** company revenue
- US firms could unlock **$2.4T in additional sales** by optimising travel rather than cutting it

The deals-lost number is harder to quantify, but the directional logic is clear: if a senior executive arrives at a customer pitch jet-lagged, sleep-deprived, and decision-fatigued, the close rate of that meeting is lower. Industry estimates of pitch-meeting close rates run 20–35% for prepared meetings and drop to 10–15% when the pitcher is visibly under-rested. On a $5M ARR enterprise deal, the difference between "well-rested" and "exhausted" can be a $1–2M ARR delta in expected value per pitch.

The headline takeaway for the report: every dollar invested in *making business travel less terrible* — whether on a private aviation hour, a Saturday-night layover hotel upgrade, a paid advisor, or an AI booking layer — has a credible argument for being one of the highest-leverage expenses in a knowledge-work business.

## Appendix: Methodology and limits

- **Time horizon:** Most stats are from 2024–2026 unless they are foundational (decision fatigue, jet lag, family impact research, which date back further but are still the most cited).
- **Geographic bias:** US-centric. GBTA and Skift cover global, but pain-point surveys skew Anglo (US + UK + Canada + AU).
- **Survey biases:** Buyer-side surveys (GBTA, BTN) over-sample managed-program respondents because that's who joins industry associations. Traveler-side surveys (Concur, Perk, Booking) over-sample frequent flyers because that's who responds. Both biases probably *underestimate* the size and dissatisfaction of the unmanaged middle.
- **Where Preface should commission primary research:** Conversion triggers (Chart B5), advisor NPS vs concierge NPS head-to-head, founder-specific decision-fatigue economics, and the spouse/family cost layer are all critical to this report and have no clean third-party source. These should be a 2026 first-party study.
