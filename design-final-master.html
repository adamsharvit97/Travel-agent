Chapter I · A letter from the studio

I built Preface because the travel industry forgot how to be personal.

There is a generation of people now hitting their peak travel years — honeymoons, family weeks, milestone celebrations, business trips — who never had a relationship with a travel advisor. Their parents had advisors. They have Expedia.

This is for them. Probably for you.

I've spent the last few years watching friends spend $8,000 on honeymoons through Expedia and arrive to rooms with parking-lot views — when the same $8,000, through me, gets them an ocean-facing suite, daily breakfast, and a $200 spa credit. Same price. Different arrival.

The economics have always been like this. Hotels pay advisors a commission whether you book through them or not. The question is who gets that commission — a faceless algorithm, or someone who knows your dates, your dietary preferences, and which property to actually recommend.

I'd rather it be the latter. I expect you would too.

Chapter III · The studio

We're a small studio. That's not a euphemism.

Travel agencies have grown into corporations. Membership clubs. Tiered access. Forms that ask you to confirm your annual household income before you can speak to anyone.

Preface is the opposite of that.

One advisor, in Toronto, with a deep network and a stack of properties she's actually stayed at. No minimums. No memberships. No gatekeeping.

A $3,000 bach weekend and a $50,000 family pilgrimage get the same care. The economics work either way — and the work is the same regardless.

Chapter VI · A recent booking

One trip. Recently.

Last March, a couple booked their honeymoon to Six Senses Fiji. They paid $8,000 for what they thought was a Pool Villa. At check-in, the front desk handed them keys to the Premier Beachfront Pool Villa.

The room they walked into would have cost $2,400 more on Booking.com.

Daily breakfast was included. A $100 spa credit waited at the concierge desk. A bottle of champagne sat on the table, with a note: "Mr. & Mrs. K. — welcome home."

None of it cost extra.
None of it was asked for.

The hotel paid Fora the commission they would have paid Booking.com. Except Booking.com would have kept it. We hand it back to clients in the room.
