Member stories

What the membership looks like when it matters.

Five anonymized scenarios from the last twelve months. Names, dates, and identifying details have been changed. The numbers, decisions, and outcomes have not.

I. Disruption

The Friday 6pm cancellation.

Situation

A Series A founder, Atlas Principal member, on a 17:55 ET flight from BOS to LGA for a Saturday-morning board meeting. The flight goes mechanical at the gate and is cancelled outright at 18:02. The next routed alternative on the original carrier is Saturday 11:40 ET, which puts him in the boardroom at 14:00 against a 09:00 start. There are 412 other passengers competing for the same five remaining seats on three airlines.

What we did

Desk picked up the proactive disruption alert at 18:03 — before the member knew his flight was cancelled. We had a Y-class confirmed on a partner carrier out of MHT at 21:15, a black car at the BOS curb at 18:31, an aisle seat held for him, and the hotel notified of a late arrival. We adjusted the morning car from a 06:30 LGA pickup to a 07:15 hotel pickup so he could sleep an extra forty minutes. He never opened the airline app.

23 min Cancellation to rebook
07:42 LGA arrival, Saturday
0 Calls to the airline by member
I had a glass of wine at the gate at 18:08 and a confirmed reroute by 18:31. I did not know the original flight was cancelled until I saw it on the departure board. — M.K., Series A founder, Atlas Principal
II. International

The visa miss, discovered at check-in.

Situation

A PE partner, Atlas Principal, checks in for a São Paulo flight at JFK and is pulled aside: her Brazil eVisa expired three weeks ago, not the renewal date she had in her calendar. The meeting in São Paulo is at 11:00 the following morning — a single-shot conversation with the management team of a target acquisition. Rebooking to the next available departure with a fresh visa would land her there 26 hours late.

What we did

At 17:48 the member texted the Desk from the check-in line. We had a vetted local fixer in São Paulo on a call by 17:54 — someone we work with for exactly this. A visa-on-arrival exception was confirmed through the consular liaison within forty minutes, the airline supervisor was looped in, and the member was through document control by 18:55. Ground in GRU was rerouted to clear immigration with the liaison personally present. The meeting happened. The deal closed three weeks later.

40 min Document workaround
11:00 Meeting kept, on time
$1.4M Deal closed three weeks later
I am not sure anyone else in my contacts list could have done this. I was twenty minutes from sleeping in an airport hotel and writing an apology email. — R.V., PE partner, Atlas Principal
III. Group logistics

The Aspen board offsite, ten days out.

Situation

A VC general partner, Atlas Firm member, calls on a Tuesday: the fund's annual offsite has been moved up by six weeks. Fourteen partners plus eight spouses need to be in Aspen for a three-day program in ten days. Hotels in Aspen at that window are essentially sold out. Dinner reservations at the two restaurants the GP wants are quoted as four-month leads. Two partners are vegetarian, one is Kosher, one travels with a service animal.

What we did

Atlas Firm hotel partners were activated by Tuesday 17:00 — a fourteen-room block at a Bellini property at the negotiated corporate rate, the spouses upgraded to suites at no premium through Belmond Bellini. Twenty-two flight itineraries built across eleven origin cities. Three vans on standby at ASE for arrivals; one ADA-compliant. Both dinner reservations confirmed through our F&B liaison. Dietary requirements communicated to the venues before menus were finalized. Door deliveries — welcome notes, pre-stocked rooms, a printed schedule per guest.

$12,400 Saved against published rates
22 / 22 Travelers on schedule
0 Missed dietary requests
We have done this offsite for nine years. This is the first one where I was not the one chasing dinner reservations at midnight on a Sunday. — D.H., General Partner, Atlas Firm
IV. Long-form

The two-week roadshow.

Situation

A Series B founder, Atlas Principal, raising her Series C. Eighteen LP meetings in twelve cities over nine business days. Different time zones, different airports, two transatlantic legs, three U.S. coastal hops, one inland anchor in Chicago. Her EA was new in role and had never built a roadshow. The founder's prior raise had her landing in Boston with the wrong terminal, no ground, and a missed first meeting.

What we did

Latitude built the route from the meeting list — optimized for time-on-ground over flight cost. Hotel and flight schedule delivered as a single PDF and a calendar feed. A dedicated Slack channel with the EA. Black-car ground coordinated city-by-city. Two intentional buffer afternoons built in for prep. A dossier on every property she would be staying in — Wi-Fi password, lobby photo, room number on arrival, breakfast options. One reroute mid-trip when an LP rescheduled by a day. She landed on the eighteenth meeting twelve minutes early.

18 / 18 Meetings made, on time
0 min Time member spent on logistics
$47M Series C closed two months later
The last time I did a roadshow I lost a deal because I missed a flight. This time I did not even know which terminal I was at half the time, and that is the point. — S.L., Series B founder, Atlas Principal
V. Advisory

The points and status audit.

Situation

An RIA principal, not a member yet, books a one-time Latitude audit. Twenty-two corporate and personal cards across the firm. Nine loyalty programs in some state of activity. Three airline statuses he believed he held but had let lapse. An EA spending roughly four hours a month reconciling card statements and chasing missing-points credits.

What we did

Latitude consolidated the program inventory into a single document, surfaced $7,200 in unused statement credits across Amex Platinum, Chase Sapphire Reserve, and Capital One Venture X, and reactivated two airline statuses through targeted segment matches. We retired four cards that no longer earned their annual fee. We built a quarterly recurring sweep so credits stop expiring quietly. The EA's monthly reconciliation went from four hours to about thirty-five minutes.

$7,200 Unused credits surfaced
4 hrs → 35 min EA monthly reconciliation
3 Status pushes consolidated
I had been carrying these cards for years and never once treated them like the financial instruments they are. We pay for the audit again next year. — T.B., RIA Principal
