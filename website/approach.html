Objections, answered

What we actually do that the alternatives do not.

Objection 01

Why not just use Concur or Navan?

Concur and Navan are policy-enforcement and expense-capture tools wrapped around an OTA. They are excellent at making sure a 28-year-old account executive books a refundable economy seat under $400. They are not advisors.

They cannot upgrade you at a Four Seasons. They cannot get you a suite at the Aman New York at sold-out rates. They cannot rebook a transatlantic itinerary at 2am when there are no seats showing in the GDS but a partner desk can find them. The platforms exist to standardize the bottom half of your travel program — not to elevate the top.

Objection 02

Why not just have my EA book it?

Your EA is excellent. Your EA is also being paid $90K–$140K and spending six to twelve hours a week on travel logistics. That is $14,000–$28,000 of EA time a year on a function we charge $3,600 for — and your EA still cannot access Virtuoso rates, Privé benefits, or a 2am rebooking desk staffed by someone with twenty years in the GDS.

The right model is partnership. Your EA holds your calendar and your preferences. We hold the bookings, the supplier relationships, and the after-hours pager. EAs who work with us describe it as getting a teammate back.

Objection 03

Why not just use Amex Travel or my Platinum Concierge?

Amex Travel is a fine consumer travel agency. The Platinum Concierge is a call center. Neither knows your name, your preferences, your tolerance for connections, your hotel allergies, or the meeting you are flying into. Every call starts at zero.

We do have access to Fine Hotels & Resorts. We also have access to Virtuoso, STARS, Privé, Bellini, Elite, and Impresario — and we know which of them gets you the better deal at any specific property, on any specific night. That is the work.

Objection 04

Don't you cost more than booking direct?

No. The hotel rate you book through us is, in nearly every case, the rate the property publishes on its own site — sometimes lower. The added value comes in the form of benefits the hotel attaches when we book it through a preferred program: room upgrade at check-in, daily breakfast for two, a $100 property credit, an early arrival or late departure. The hotel pays the program. You do not.

On air, we charge a $50 ticketing fee — waived for Atlas members. Compare that to the time you would otherwise spend on a 90-minute United Airlines reissue call.

Objection 05

What if I only travel six times a year?

Then Atlas is probably not for you, and we will tell you that on the intro call. We charge non-members $250 for a domestic complex itinerary and $500 for an international complex one — the math only works above roughly fifteen trips. We would rather lose a small fee than sell you something you do not need.

Objection 06

I already have status with airlines and hotels. What do you add?

Status is the floor — it gets you boarded earlier and acknowledged at check-in. What we add sits on top of status: confirmed room category upgrades booked into the reservation before you arrive, F&B credits on the folio, late checkout written down, and a relationship with the GM that means when something goes wrong, it gets fixed at the property, not on a customer-service line. Status and preferred-partner programs stack. We make sure both run.
