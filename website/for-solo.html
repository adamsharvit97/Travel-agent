Built for · Solo Professionals

The economics of an EA. The bandwidth of an in-house travel team.

For independent consultants, expert witnesses, executive coaches, and senior advisors operating without staff. If your travel year sits between fifteen and forty meaningful trips, Atlas Principal is built for you.

The math

At twenty trips, we cost less than your time.

A solo practitioner who travels twenty times a year spends, on average, three and a half hours per trip on travel logistics — research, booking, calendar coordination, ground arrangement, and the inevitable recovery work when a leg goes sideways. That is seventy hours.

Your hourly value, if you are reading this page, is somewhere north of $250. The arithmetic favors Atlas Principal at $3,600 by a wide margin — and that is before counting the value of room upgrades, breakfast, and a Desk that picks up at 2am.

What Atlas Principal actually does for you.

01.

Your preferences, once

Aisle seats. Quiet floors. A room with a desk. Sparkling water on arrival. A standing order for ground from JFK to midtown. Tell us once. We file it. Every trip after that, it happens.

02.

A five-line email is enough

"Boston Tuesday, dinner with a client, back Thursday." That is the brief. We come back with the routing, the hotel, and the calendar holds in under twenty-four hours. Approve in one reply.

03.

Loyalty stacking

You already have status with Delta and Marriott. We add the preferred-partner layer on top — Virtuoso, STARS, Privé — so your upgrade is in the reservation, not a hope at check-in.

04.

Compliance and expense

Your bookkeeper gets a clean monthly export. Category codes. Vendor names. Business purpose. The work your accountant should not be doing for you.

05.

Real after-hours coverage

You are alone on the road. The Desk is your team. Cancellation at 11pm, taxi that never arrived, hotel walked you — call us first. We will be there.

06.

The occasional indulgence

Anniversary in Mexico City. Long weekend in Lisbon. Atlas applies to personal travel too. The same desk handles it with the same benefits attached.

Mini case study — anonymized

An executive coach's typical week.

Sunday evening

Coach forwards an email from her client's EA — Tuesday session in New York, Wednesday in Greenwich, fly home from White Plains Wednesday evening.

Sunday, 22:00 ET

Itinerary drops in her inbox. Delta into LGA, The Mark for Tuesday night (her preferred property), black car to Greenwich Wednesday morning, Tradewind Aviation back to JFK from Westchester. Total time spent by the coach: zero.

Wednesday, 16:40 ET

Greenwich session runs forty minutes long. The Desk receives a one-word Slack from the coach: "running." Tradewind is rebooked to the next departure. The car is held at the front door. She makes the flight without a thought.

Friday

The Mark $100 credit was applied to her room service breakfast. She did not have to ask. Loyalty points posted to her account the next day. Atlas paid for itself on the upgrade alone.
