    Three phases. Warm first, then compound.

    Months 0 – 6

    The warm network.

    Founder's first-degree network, plus 1:1 outbound to EAs, COOs, and GPs in the US East Coast finance corridor. Goal: first ten paying members, signal proof for the next phase.

    Phase II
    Months 6 – 18

    The Journal & the rooms.

    Latitude Journal on LinkedIn — weekly cadence, advisor-as-author. Partnerships with EA networks (NYCEA, IAAP), and curated events in NYC, Boston, and Greenwich. Goal: thirty members, two anchor accounts.

    Phase III
    Months 18 +

    Referral compound.

    Referral compounds inside PE/VC platforms — one partner becomes the firm, the firm becomes the portfolio. Private aviation cross-sell becomes the natural up-tier. Goal: revenue durability, predictable inbound.

    Section V · Risk
    Risks & mitigations

    What could go wrong.
    And how we hedge it.

    Risk I

    Client concentration.

    Early revenue clusters around the first three to five Atlas Office accounts. Any one of them leaving creates a hole.

    Mitigation

    Deliberate diversification by year four — no single account >20% of revenue. Anchor accounts are required to onboard at least one referral within the first two quarters.

    Risk II

    Founder bandwidth.

    The 60-minute SLA depends on the founder being reachable. That is not a scalable position.

    Mitigation

    Virtual assistant infrastructure by Q2 of year two. Backup advisor + on-call rotation by Y3. Tech leverage (profile system, templated trip briefs) from day one.

    Risk III

    Cross-border regulatory.

    Selling US travel services from a Canadian entity is a structure that needs to hold up under scrutiny.

    Mitigation

    Operating under Fora Travel's US accreditation (IATA, CLIA, TICO-equivalent). No solicitation of Canadian clients. Counsel review of every materials cycle.
