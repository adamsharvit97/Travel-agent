# Latitude 43 — Business Plan Changelog

**v1.0 → v2.0**
**Date of revision:** May 2026
**Reason for revision:** Ten independent diligence evaluations surfaced material errors in pricing, unit economics, market sizing, moat claims, regulatory posture, and cash planning. This document records the deltas. The investor-facing plan was rebuilt against `/tmp/v2-facts.md` plus the corrections below.

---

## Summary

v1.0 was a competent strategic narrative. v2.0 is the same business with the marketing puffery removed, the math corrected, and the structural risks named in the document rather than in a footnote.

The headline numbers move down. The product offering moves toward defensibility. The capital plan stops calling itself bootstrap and starts calling itself founder-funded with a defined personal-burn line.

## Numbered changes

### 1. Pricing architecture rebuilt

| Item | v1.0 | v2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Atlas Principal / Light | $3,600/yr (1 traveler) | **Atlas Light $99/mo ($1,188/yr) — repriced to $129–$149/mo before launch; $99 fails credible-floor test** |
| Atlas Office | $12,000/yr (5 travelers) | **Atlas Office $249/mo ($2,988/yr), 5 named travelers but capped at 3 active** |
| Atlas Firm | $36,000/yr | **Replaced by Latitude Society — invite-only, $1,000–$2,500/mo ($12K–$30K/yr), 12–15 globally (not 30), launch H2 2027** |
| Atlas Family Office | n/a | New variant of Office, 20 named / 5 active rolling, NDA |

### 2. Revenue mechanics corrected

- v1.0 implicitly treated all revenue as Fora-split. v2.0 is explicit: **subscription = 100% to advisor**, commission = 70/30 starting, 80/20 at $300K personal sales, 90/10 at $2M.
- v1.0 used 10% blended hotel commission. v2.0 uses **7.8% blended effective rate** after the Virtuoso 2% pre-split clip and the Fora-program mix (60% Virtuoso-routed at 8% net to advisor, 30% other preferred at 10%, 10% non-commissionable).
- v1.0 used 24 trips/year per traveler. v2.0 uses **18 nights/year** (1.5 nights/month), with a dormancy assumption layered on top (12–20% of Light members functionally dormant by Y3).

### 3. Five-year base case revised down

| Year | v1.0 net to founder | v2.0 corrected revenue (pre founder burn) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Y1 | $16,920 | ~$73K |
| Y2 | $45,600 | ~$143K |
| Y3 | $88,438 | ~$318K |
| Y4 | $137,860 | ~$518K |
| Y5 | $171,370 (1.0) / "Base $871K" (v2-facts) | **~$775K base case** |

Bull case revised from $1.6M to **$1.1M realistic upside ceiling**. Bear case revised from $32K to **$300–350K range**. Failure case added at **<$100K**.

### 4. Founder personal burn now in the model

v1.0 omitted founder living costs. v2.0 includes **$90K (Y1) → $120K (Y5)** as a salary-equivalent line the business must support. Y1 and Y2 are now correctly shown as cash-negative; cumulative deficit is approximately **$116K before Y3 breakeven**.

### 5. TAM/SAM/SOM rewritten

- Dropped the "$625B unmanaged SMB pool" framing. That figure is mathematically correct (834B × 0.75) and strategically meaningless.
- Honest TAM: **~$77B US unmanaged SMB business travel**, of which **$4–8B is addressably hireable**.
- Honest SAM: **~9,200 buyable units** in the US after applying T&E gate, no-travel-manager gate, principal-buyer gate, and cultural-fit gate.
- v1.0 claimed 103,500 firms; the audited count is ~43,000 across the five segments. Average inflation factor 2.4x.

### 6. Moat claims rewritten

v1.0 implicitly treated brand, voice, SLA, EA seat, First Loss Insurance, and supplier access as competitive moats. v2.0 acknowledges that, weighted, these score **3.7/10 against a peer-grade attacker with 90 days to respond**. The four realistic moats — **trust + relationships, operating discipline, brand equity, member roster quality** — are now identified as a 5-year compounding curve, not a launch advantage.

### 7. Society launch deferred

v1.0 implied Society/Firm at launch. v2.0 facts moved it to Q1 2027. This v2.0 document defers it further to **H2 2027** and caps at **12–15 members**, not 30. Average ticket raised to $20K+. The Society launch is treated as a Year 3 product, not a Year 2 product.

### 8. Atlas Office traveler cap

v1.0/v2-facts allowed "up to 5 travelers" at $249/mo. v2.0 caps **active concurrent travelers at 3**; the seat count remains 5 named, but only 3 may book in any rolling 30-day window. This caps labor risk at scale.

### 9. Design fee escalator hardened

| Element | v2-facts | v2.0 final |
|---|---|---|
| Floor (under $5K trip) | $0 | **$0 for members, $150 intake for non-members** |
| Base at $5K | $250 | **Raised entry to $5K floor (was $0); applies to non-members** |
| Cap | $5K | **$10K cap (raised from $5K)** |
| Member discount | 50% | **25% on trips above $30K; 50% below** |

### 10. First Loss Insurance renamed

"Insurance" is a regulated term in Ontario (Insurance Act) and every US state. Using it in a product name when the product is not an insurance contract risks UDAP enforcement, regulator inquiry, and weaker trademark. Renamed to **"First Trip Guarantee"** (alternate: "Founder's Guarantee"). Structure unchanged: $1K labor cap, named trip only, service credit not cash.

### 11. Tax form corrected

v1.0 mentioned filing **W-9** with Fora. A Canadian CCPC files **W-8BEN-E with a treaty position** (Canada-US tax treaty Article VII). Filing W-9 triggers automatic 30% backup withholding. Hard fix.

### 12. Cross-border permanent establishment risk added

v1.0 said "no US PE expected." That is not the full story. Under the Canada-US treaty Article V services-PE test, the founder's US presence days matter. v2.0 imposes a **120-day US presence cap per calendar year**, with a calendar log and a CPA review at month 9 and month 12.

### 13. CAC reality check

v1.0 implied a clean Y1 acquisition funnel. v2.0 separates channels by viability:

- **Cold channels (email, LinkedIn ads):** all-in CAC ~$30K. **Reserved for Office and Society only.** Light unit economics ($2,400 Y1) cannot absorb cold CAC.
- **Warm/referral/Journal:** Light is acquired here, full stop. Effective CAC ~$1,000–$2,500 in founder time.

### 14. Toronto Y1 plan critiqued and adjusted

- **Soho House Toronto**: admission delays 3–9 months, rejection probability ~50%. Deferred to **Year 2** (do not budget $3K in Y1).
- **EO Toronto**: revenue qualification unclear pre-launch. Defer to Y2.
- **Brother-in-law dinners**: kept. Network depth is load-bearing and unspecified — flagged as key dependency.

### 15. Cash gap surfaced

v1.0 said "bootstrap." v2.0 says: **founder must personally fund $130K–$180K over 24 months**. F&F note ($150–250K at flat $1.5M post) is acceptable only if personal savings are below $150K. No priced equity until Y3 metrics give negotiating leverage.

### 16. Bus factor named

v1.0 mentioned "named successor" in Firm tier inclusions. There is no named successor. v2.0 requires:
- **Pre-launch reciprocal peer-coverage agreement** signed with at least one other Fora-hosted business advisor
- **Key-person insurance** of $250K minimum on the founder
- **Member protection protocol** in TOS: if desk unreachable >4 hours, member is auto-prorated and offered no-fault exit

### 17. 24/7 SLA staffing accelerated

v1.0 budgeted PHT VA at month 12. v2.0 puts it at **month 4–6**. The founder breaks at 18–22 active members; Y1 target is 30. The math does not work without earlier offshore support. Y1 incremental cost: $4K–$6K.

### 18. E&O insurance moved to Day 1

v1.0 deferred independent E&O to Y2. The membership product sits **outside Fora's E&O scope**. v2.0 buys independent $2M E&O at launch. Y1 cost: $1,800–$2,400.

### 19. Latitude Reports resurrected

v2-facts scrapped Latitude Reports in favor of per-trip receipts. **v2.0 reverses this decision.** Per the competitive-moat evaluation, Latitude Reports is the **only software-shaped switching cost** in the entire plan. Structured trip history, captured rewards, preferences, and briefs stored inside Latitude's system create a non-trivial migration cost. A minimum-viable Reports product ships in Q4 of Y1.

### 20. Competitive landscape refreshed

Added named threats not in v1.0:
- **Bell & Bly Travel** raising capital
- A **senior Fora advisor going solo** as a parallel-positioning threat
- **Amex GBT post-Long Lake** $6.3B take-private creating displaced mid-market account managers who may start their own boutiques
- **YC-funded entrants** likely by summer 2027 if Latitude 43 spends a year publishing on the category

### 21. Marketing plan corrections

- Latitude Journal requires **12 posts published before launch**, not "week 2 launch with 6 in the bank"
- Press strategy delayed from month 6 to **month 12** (no track record to support a profile earlier)
- Skift / BTN pitches scheduled for Y2, not Y1

### 22. Voice and banned-word list expanded

v1.0 banned "luxury." v2.0 also bans **bespoke, curated, wanderlust, white-glove, seamless, elevated, and concierge**. Adopts "members not customers, the desk not the team, apply not sign up, open the desk."

### 23. Lifecycle stages and member protection terms added

- The First 30 program: 90-day no-fault exit, monthly Stripe billing, per-booking commission disclosure on receipts
- EA Companion Seat: complimentary Light seat for principal's EA, included in Office and Society
- Both written into the TOS, not informal commitments

### 24. Risk register reformatted

Each major evaluation finding now appears in Section 15 as a named risk with mitigation. The risk section grew from 10 rows to 20+, and the mitigations are operational (this person, this date, this dollar amount) rather than aspirational.

### 25. The 90-day launch plan was rewritten

v1.0's 90-day plan assumed a paying client by week 4. v2.0 sequences: legal foundation in weeks 1–6 (TICO discipline, Fora side letter, W-8BEN-E, insurance binder, trademark filings, membership agreement), then 12 Journal posts before the first paid pitch, then anchor outreach. First paying member targeted at month 4, not week 4.
