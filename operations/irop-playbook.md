# IROP Playbook
## Latitude 43 Standard Operating Procedure · Internal

**Version 1.0 · Effective May 2026 · Owner: Principal**
**Classification: Internal. Not for member distribution. Selected templates may be sent verbatim.**

---

## 1. Definition of an IROP

An IROP — irregular operation — is any event that breaks the published itinerary of an Atlas member and requires the Desk to intervene. The Desk treats the following as IROPs without exception:

- Flight cancellation, regardless of cause.
- Flight delay greater than 120 minutes, or any delay that breaks a connection.
- Diversion to an unscheduled airport.
- Hotel walk (hotel cannot honour a confirmed reservation).
- Room category downgrade discovered at check-in.
- Visa rejection at the gate, at immigration, or pre-clearance.
- Ground transport no-show beyond 15 minutes of the scheduled pickup.
- Lost or delayed luggage where the trip purpose depends on the contents (suit for a board meeting, samples for a pitch, equipment for an event).
- Medical incident involving the member or an immediate travel companion.
- Geopolitical event affecting the route, the destination, or the return path.
- Any natural event — weather, seismic, wildfire — that affects the itinerary inside 48 hours.

If the Desk is not certain whether something qualifies, it qualifies. Escalate first, downgrade later.

## 2. The First 15 Minutes

The first 15 minutes determine the member's experience of the entire incident. The objective is not to fix the problem in 15 minutes. The objective is to make the member feel that someone competent is already working on it.

**Minute 0 to 2.** The Desk acknowledges. One message to the member, no more than three sentences, never automated. Template:

> "Eleanor, we have it. AC 704 has cancelled. Sarah is on the airline desk now and Marcus is pulling alternatives. You will hear from us within 20 minutes with options. Stay where you are."

**Minute 2 to 5.** The Desk opens an IROP record in the operations log. Member name, original itinerary, nature of the disruption, timestamp, owner. Every action from this point is logged in that record.

**Minute 5 to 10.** The Desk runs the parallel-track protocol. One advisor works the supplier (airline reservations, hotel front desk, ground vendor). A second advisor works the alternatives independently — never sequentially. If only one advisor is on duty, the founder is paged immediately to take the second track.

Named systems:
- **Sabre / Amadeus** for live availability and rebooking.
- **ExpertFlyer** for award and upgrade space verification.
- **Operations log** (Notion · IROP database) for the timeline of record.

**Minute 10 to 15.** The Desk returns to the member with two or three concrete options, each priced, each timed, each with the trade-off named.

> "Eleanor, three options. One: AC 706 at 11:15, gets you to JFK at 12:50, you miss the morning session. Two: UA 644 through EWR at 09:30, arrives 11:05, full meeting day intact, requires a separate ticket. Three: rebook to Friday and shift the LP dinner. Sarah recommends two. Your call."

The member chooses. The Desk executes. The next message to the member is confirmation, not consultation.

## 3. The First Hour

By the end of the first hour, the Desk has:

1. Confirmed the new itinerary in writing to the member.
2. Re-issued ground transport at origin and destination.
3. Notified the hotel of the revised arrival window.
4. Updated the calendar entries the member has shared with us.
5. Re-priced any difference and noted who absorbs it.
6. Filed the airline disruption claim where eligible (EU 261, APPR, contract-of-carriage).

## 4. The Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3 Decision Tree

The Desk classifies every IROP within five minutes of opening the record. Classification governs who acts, who is called, and how the member is communicated with.

### Tier 1 — Routine
The disruption is contained, the workaround is obvious, the member is inconvenienced but not stranded.

Examples: a 90-minute delay with a comfortable connection buffer; a hotel room category mismatch that the front desk corrects on the spot; a ground vendor running ten minutes late.

- The Desk acts independently.
- One member message at acknowledgement, one at resolution.
- Logged. No escalation.

### Tier 2 — Disruptive
The itinerary is broken and must be rebuilt. The member's day or week is materially affected.

Examples: flight cancellation; missed connection; hotel walk; lost critical luggage; ground no-show during a tight schedule.

- The Desk acts and the principal is notified inside 15 minutes by text. No question asked, just informed.
- Member messages: acknowledgement, options, confirmation, mid-trip check-in, post-trip debrief.
- Logged. Debriefed inside 48 hours.

### Tier 3 — Critical
The member is at risk — physical safety, legal exposure, reputational consequence — or the firm is.

Examples: visa rejection at the gate; medical event abroad; political instability at the destination; arrest, detention, or border refusal; death in the family requiring immediate repatriation; data breach on the member's account.

- The principal takes the desk personally. The advisor on duty supports.
- Member is called, not messaged. Voice contact within 15 minutes.
- Legal counsel and insurance partners are looped where relevant.
- The founder owns the case from open to debrief.
- A full written file is opened and retained for seven years.

## 5. Member Communication Templates

The Desk uses these as starting frames. Every message is rewritten to fit the member, the moment, and the relationship. Voice is restrained, specific, and never apologetic in a performative way.

### Template A · Minor delay (Tier 1)

> Eleanor, AC 704 is showing a 95-minute delay, new departure 09:10. You will still make the 12:30 with 40 minutes to spare. Blacklane will hold at JFK and the hotel has been notified. No action needed on your side. — Sarah

### Template B · Cancellation (Tier 2)

> Eleanor, AC 704 has cancelled. We have it. Two paths.
>
> One: UA 644 via EWR, depart 09:30, arrive JFK 11:05, full day intact. We rebook the ground and notify the hotel. Cost difference $340, absorbed.
>
> Two: AC 706 at 11:15, arrive JFK 12:50, you lose the morning session but keep the original ticket. No cost.
>
> Sarah recommends one. Reply with a number and we move. — The Desk

### Template C · Critical (Tier 3)

Critical events are not opened over text. The principal calls. If the call cannot connect, a single line is sent:

> Eleanor, this is Sarah from Latitude 43. There is a situation with your departure and Avshalom is trying to reach you now. When you can, please call the desk line: +1 416 XXX XXXX. We are already moving.

The written message follows only after voice contact has been established.

## 6. The 2am Frankfurt Scenario

The single most stress-tested scenario in the playbook. A member is in Frankfurt. The 22:40 LH return to YYZ is cancelled while they are at the gate. It is 02:00 in Toronto. The founder is asleep.

**Step 1.** The overnight advisor (Marcus, Berlin time) receives the cancellation alert from the flight monitoring system within 90 seconds. He acknowledges the member in writing inside 4 minutes.

**Step 2.** Marcus pulls three options simultaneously: a same-night re-route via LHR or CDG; a morning rebook on LH 470 at 10:00; a stay-and-recover plan with the Steigenberger Frankfurter Hof or the Villa Kennedy. Each option is priced and timed.

**Step 3.** Marcus contacts the LH HON desk on the priority line. While that call is live, the second track is opened: a WhatsApp message to the founder reading "FRA Tier 2, member rebooking, no action needed unless you wake up." The founder is not called. He sees it in the morning.

**Step 4.** The member chooses. Most often: stay and recover. Marcus books the Frankfurter Hof, a Junior Suite, late arrival flagged, breakfast standing. Blacklane is dispatched to the gate. The morning LH 470 is held. The member is in a bed within 60 minutes of acknowledging the cancellation.

**Step 5.** At 06:00 Toronto time the founder reads the log, calls the member at a civilised hour, and takes the debrief from there.

The principle: the member's experience does not depend on the founder being awake. The system does.

## 7. The Handoff Protocol

The Desk operates across three shifts. Handoffs are scheduled, written, and verified.

**Shift A.** Toronto morning, 07:00 to 15:00 EDT. Founder or Lead Advisor.

**Shift B.** Toronto evening, 15:00 to 23:00 EDT. Lead Advisor.

**Shift C.** Overnight cover, 23:00 to 07:00 EDT. Berlin-based associate advisor on call.

A written handoff goes into the operations log at every shift change: members in motion, open requests, pending IROPs, anything the next shift needs to watch. The incoming advisor reads and acknowledges before the outgoing advisor signs off. No verbal handoffs.

The founder is woken only for Tier 3 events. The threshold is explicit and documented. Anything below Tier 3 waits for the morning. Waking the founder for a Tier 2 erodes the founder's capacity to be sharp for a Tier 3 the next day. Discipline matters.

The VA covers administrative continuity — calendar, billing, correspondence — but never touches a live IROP. The VA's job during an IROP is to keep the rest of the business running so the advisor can focus.

## 8. Post-IROP Debrief

Within 48 hours of resolution, the Desk sends the member a written debrief. Restrained. Factual. Never defensive. The structure:

1. What happened, in two sentences.
2. What we did, in three to five sentences.
3. What it cost, if anything, and what was absorbed.
4. What we filed on the member's behalf — disruption claims, insurance, points reinstatement.
5. What we changed in our procedures as a result, if anything.

The debrief is signed by the advisor who ran the case, copied to the principal. A copy is filed in the member's relationship record.

The debrief is not an apology. An apology is appropriate only when the firm itself failed. When the airline cancelled, when the hotel walked, when the visa was refused — the Desk acted, did not fail, and the debrief reads accordingly.

## 9. The IROP Log

Every incident is logged. The log lives in the operations database and contains, at minimum:

- Date, time of open, time of close.
- Member, trip ID, supplier(s) involved.
- Tier classification.
- Nature of the disruption.
- Actions taken, in timestamped sequence.
- Cost impact: out of pocket, absorbed, recovered.
- Claims filed and outcomes.
- Time to first member acknowledgement.
- Time to resolution.
- Lessons.

The log is reviewed monthly by the principal. Three things are extracted from each review: patterns (is one supplier driving disruptions?), gaps (where did the system slow us down?), and stories (which incidents go into the year-end review with the member's permission).

The annual IROP report goes into the Atlas year-in-review for each member: "Four IROPs handled on your behalf this year, three averted, one rebooked. Average time from disruption to resolution: 38 minutes."

That single line is, more than any marketing the firm will ever produce, the reason members renew.
