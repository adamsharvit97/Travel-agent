# FAM Trip Briefing
## Latitude 43 Standard Operating Procedure · Internal

**Use this template every time a Latitude 43 advisor visits a hotel, airline, ground vendor, or destination supplier on a familiarisation basis. The trip exists to produce intelligence the desk will use for years. Treat it as fieldwork, not as a hotel stay.**

---

## 1. Pre-Trip Prep

### What to know before arrival

Before the wheels are up, the advisor should be able to answer five questions about the property without consulting notes:

1. Who owns it, who manages it, and when did the current management take over.
2. Room count, suite count, the average daily rate, the published rack rate.
3. The named GM and the named director of sales. Their tenure. Where they came from.
4. Which membership programmes the property participates in: Virtuoso, Fora Preferred, Hyatt Privé, FHR, STARS, Bellini, Le Club, Impresario. What each entitles a member to.
5. Three things the property is currently known for, and three weaknesses identified in recent guest reviews and trade press.

If those answers are not on the page before the trip, the trip is not ready.

### What to bring

- A printed copy of the supplier file: rate history, last twelve months of member stays, every amenity our members have received and what was charged.
- A blank scorecard (template in the supplier intelligence database, section 4 of this document).
- A notebook. Paper. Not a phone.
- Business cards, modest, English on one side, the local language on the reverse where relevant.
- A travel uniform that matches the property's register. Brioni navy at the Mark, an unstructured jacket at Soho House, a tie at Claridge's. Read the room before walking into it.

### What to read

- The property's last three years of GM letters and press releases.
- Trade coverage in Hotels Magazine, Travel + Leisure trade edition, Skift Travel Experiences.
- The most recent twenty TripAdvisor reviews — not for sentiment, for pattern.
- Our own internal member feedback file if there are prior stays.

---

## 2. The Objective

A FAM trip is not a holiday. It produces three deliverables, no more, no fewer. State them aloud before departure.

**Deliverable A.** A property briefing for the Latitude Journal: 600 to 900 words, written voice, photograph short-list, room category recommendation matrix.

**Deliverable B.** A supplier scorecard: 22 metrics, all observable, all timed. Filed in the supplier intelligence database within seven days of return.

**Deliverable C.** Three named contacts on property added to the relationship file: a GM or senior representative, a guest relations or concierge lead, a desk or operations contact for IROP escalation.

If the trip does not produce all three, the trip failed. The hotel may have been wonderful. The trip failed.

---

## 3. Conversation Framework

Three conversations matter. Schedule them. Do not let them happen by accident.

### With the General Manager — 45 minutes, scheduled

The GM conversation is strategic. We are not asking what the property serves at breakfast. We are asking what the property is becoming.

- What changed in the last twelve months, and what is changing in the next twelve.
- Where the property's occupancy is coming from now versus three years ago.
- Which competitor properties they watch and what they think those properties do better.
- How they define service recovery. What authority the front desk has at 2am.
- What a Latitude 43 member should expect that a general guest would not.
- What would make our members an ideal source of business for them, and what would make us a difficult partner.
- Who, exactly, do we call when something goes wrong at 03:00 on a Sunday.

End with: "If we sent you ten of our members this year, what would you want to know about them in advance." That answer becomes the brief we send the property before every stay.

### With the Concierge or Guest Relations Lead — 30 minutes

The concierge conversation is operational. This is the person who will execute on our member's behalf.

- How do they prefer to receive amenity instructions: email, a portal, a phone call.
- What is their actual response time to a request received in writing.
- What can they do that the front desk cannot.
- Which restaurants in the city do they have a personal table at, and for which nights.
- Who do they call for the things they cannot do themselves.
- What goes wrong most often, and how do they catch it before the guest does.

### With the Desk Manager or Director of Front Office — 20 minutes

The desk manager conversation is about IROP performance. This is the person whose decision determines whether a tired member at 23:00 gets the suite or the standard king.

- What authority does the night manager have to upgrade, comp, or move.
- How does the property handle a walk: where do they walk to, who calls the guest, what does the guest hear.
- What is the property's protocol when a flagged Fora or Virtuoso member arrives at a less-than-promised room.
- Can the property hold a room past the standard cancellation window for our members in transit.

## 4. What to Observe

Service quality reveals itself in small things, repeatedly. Watch for these. Time them. Note them on the scorecard.

- **Arrival.** From kerb to room key, how many minutes. Was the name used unprompted. Was the reservation found on the first try.
- **The room.** Was the category as confirmed. Was the amenity present. Was the welcome note personal or generic. Was the temperature reasonable. Was the lighting controllable.
- **The first request.** Place a small, specific request inside the first hour: a particular bottled water, a tailoring repair, a printout. Time the response. Note whether the staff returns to confirm completion.
- **Breakfast.** Volume of the room. Speed of coffee. Quality of the eggs cooked to order. Treatment of solo guests.
- **The middle-night moment.** Call the front desk at 02:00 with a benign request — a spare phone charger, a water. Note who answers, in what tone, in how many rings, and how the request is fulfilled.
- **Departure.** Was the bill correct. Were the amenities billed that should not have been. Was the goodbye personal.
- **The non-guest moment.** Walk through the lobby at 16:00 and again at 21:00. The lobby tells the truth.

## 5. The Deliverables, in detail

### Property briefing for the Latitude Journal

Written in the Latitude voice. Restrained. Specific. No adjectives that could attach to any hotel. The brief should be useful to a member who has never stayed there and never plans to read a marketing page about it.

Structure:
1. One paragraph on what the property is and is not.
2. One paragraph on the room categories — which to book, which to avoid, where the value is.
3. One paragraph on the property's actual strengths, named.
4. One paragraph on the property's weaknesses, named.
5. One paragraph on the moments — the bar at 18:00, the breakfast room at 09:30, the rooftop in summer.
6. One paragraph on who this property is for, and who it is not for.

### Supplier scorecard

Twenty-two metrics, scored 1 to 5, with a sentence of evidence each. Categories: arrival experience, room delivery, F&B, service recovery, concierge capability, GM accessibility, after-hours response, value at category, member-programme execution. Total scored out of 110. Anything under 78 is not recommended for Atlas without caveats. Anything over 95 enters the firm's standing recommendation list.

### Named contacts

Three contacts, with full mobile numbers, email addresses, role, tenure, and a one-line personal note from the visit ("daughter at McGill," "moving to a Rosewood property in November"). Saved into the relationship file, set to remind us of birthdays and tenure milestones.

---

## 6. Post-Trip Thank You

Within 72 hours of departure, the advisor sends a handwritten note to the GM. Card stock, navy on ivory. Not an email. The note references one specific moment from the visit and one specific person below the GM by name. The point is to be remembered as a partner who notices.

A second message, by email, goes to the concierge and the desk manager. Shorter. Specific to the conversations had.

A third communication, two weeks later, is the booking. Latitude 43 sends a real member to the property and copies the GM. This is the test of the relationship. Suppliers who notice and act on that booking become standing partners. Suppliers who do not notice it are downgraded in the recommendation order.

The relationship is built in three contacts: the visit, the thank-you, the first booking. After that, the file maintains itself.
