# Brand Voice Guide
## Latitude 43 · Internal · For everyone who writes anything that reaches a member

**Owner: Principal. Apply to every member-facing artefact: emails, briefs, IROP messages, the portal, the Journal, the Reports digest, social, marketing.**

---

## The voice in one sentence

Latitude 43 sounds confident, restrained, specific, and dry. It does not sell. It does not perform. It does not announce. It states.

If a sentence could appear in a private bank's quarterly letter, it is on-brand. If it could appear in a travel agency's Instagram caption, it is not.

---

## Do

- Say **the desk**, not the team, not customer service, not concierge.
- Say **member**, not customer, not client, not guest.
- Say **apply**, not sign up, not join, not enroll.
- Say **open the desk**, not contact us, not get in touch, not reach out.
- Say **the Journal**, not the blog.
- Say **Reports**, not analytics, not dashboard, not insights.
- Say **brief us**, not request, not submit.
- Lead with the noun, not the adjective. "A property in Marrakesh," not "an exquisite Moroccan retreat."
- Name things. Names build trust. "AC 704" not "the morning flight." "Sarah K." not "your advisor."
- Use periods. Short sentences land harder than long ones with caveats.
- Cite numbers with units. "60 minutes." "$340." "Three days."

---

## Don't

- No adverbs of intensity: truly, genuinely, incredibly, deeply, absolutely, completely.
- No exclamation marks. Ever. Not in headlines, not in CTAs, not in messages.
- No emojis. Not in the product. Not in correspondence. Not internally if it might end up in front of a member.
- No leisure-luxe vocabulary: bespoke, curated, luxe, wanderlust, journey (the noun), experience (the noun), unforgettable, magical, dream.
- No startup vocabulary: excited, thrilled, stoked, hyped, vibes, drop, launch.
- No corporate vocabulary: leverage (as verb), synergies, deliverables (in member-facing copy), reach out, circle back, touch base.
- No false intimacy: hey, hi there, friend, family.
- No urgency theatre: act now, limited time, don't miss out.
- No apologies for things that are not our fault. State what we did. Move on.

## Ten sentences that are on-brand

1. The Mark, Park Suite, three nights. Confirmed.
2. AC 704 cancelled. Two paths. UA 644 keeps your day intact. AC 706 keeps your ticket. Sarah recommends one.
3. The hotel walked one of our members last September in Geneva. The Mandarin took them at midnight at no charge. We remember.
4. Your year in review is attached. Fourteen trips. Forty-two nights. Three IROPs handled. One averted.
5. The desk is open. Sarah K. is on. Response time is under fifteen minutes.
6. We do not book restaurants under our own name. We will tell you who does it well.
7. Berlin, 12 to 14 November. We are looking at Hotel de Rome and Soho House. You will have a recommendation Thursday.
8. The fee is $4,800 a year. It buys you a standing right to call.
9. We declined two of every five applications last year. This is intentional.
10. Your file remains with us for thirty-six months. If your circumstances change, we pick up where we left off.

## Ten sentences that are off-brand

1. We're so excited to welcome you to the Latitude family.
2. Our curated luxury travel experiences are truly unforgettable.
3. Don't miss out on this amazing opportunity to elevate your travel game.
4. Hey there, just circling back on that Berlin trip you mentioned.
5. We're absolutely thrilled to share that the desk is open 24/7.
6. Let our team of expert travel concierges craft your bespoke journey.
7. Reach out anytime — we're here for you, day or night.
8. The Mark is a magical property and you're going to fall in love with it.
9. We've got you covered with all your travel needs and more.
10. Click here to sign up and start your incredible Latitude journey today.

The two lists are not stylistic preferences. They are the difference between a firm a CFO trusts and a firm a CFO does not.

## Names and capitalisation

- **Latitude 43.** Always with the space and the numeral. Never Lat 43, Latitude43, L43, Latitude Forty-Three.
- **Atlas.** Capitalised when referring to the membership tier. "An Atlas member." Lowercase when referring to the noun in general usage.
- **Desk.** Capitalised when referring to ours. "Open the Desk." Lowercase when referring to a hotel desk or airline desk in passing.
- **Reports.** Capitalised when referring to the product. "Your Reports are ready." Lowercase for the general noun.
- **Journal.** Capitalised. "The Latitude Journal." Italicised when in long-form prose.
- **Member.** Never capitalised, even in headlines.
- **The firm.** Use sparingly. Acceptable in internal documents and the Journal. Avoid in direct member correspondence.

## Place names

For the cognoscenti, use airport codes: TEB, LCY, LAX, JFK, YYZ, ZRH, HND, SIN. Members travel often enough to read these as fluently as city names.

For unfamiliar destinations, use the full place name on first reference and the IATA code thereafter. "Sun Valley Friedman Memorial (SUN)." "Comporta, by way of Lisbon (LIS)."

Never abbreviate without the convention. "NYC" is fine. "TO" for Toronto is not. "The City" is acceptable for Manhattan in the right context, never for any other city.

Use the local form where it serves clarity. Geneva, not Genève. Munich, not München. But Zürich keeps the umlaut in long-form prose.

---

## Numbers

- Spell out one through nine. Numerals from ten upward.
- Always include units. "60 minutes," not "an hour." "Twelve months," not "a year." "$4,800," not "under five thousand."
- Times in 24-hour format internally and in operational documents. 12-hour with AM/PM in member-facing copy where it reads more naturally ("07:35" in a flight context, "7:30am" in a casual line about coffee).
- Currencies always with the unit. "USD 4,800" in cross-border copy. "$4,800" where the audience is North American by default.
- Decimals to one place for percentages and tonnages. "3.2t of CO2." "12.5 percent improvement."
- Spell out percentages in prose ("twelve percent"), use the symbol in tables and data.

## The voice on the worst day

The truest test of voice is the 02:00 message to a member whose flight has just cancelled in Frankfurt. Not the marketing page, not the welcome letter. The midnight text.

That message is calm. It is specific. It names the problem in one clause and the action in the next. It does not apologise for the airline. It does not catastrophise. It does not perform empathy. It demonstrates competence.

> Eleanor, LH 470 cancelled. Marcus is on the HON desk now. You have a room held at the Frankfurter Hof, Junior Suite, late arrival flagged. Blacklane is dispatched to the gate. Morning rebook in progress. Twenty minutes for confirmation.

That is the voice. Everything else is downstream of that.
