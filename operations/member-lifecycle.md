# The Member Relationship Lifecycle
## Latitude 43 Standard Operating Procedure · Internal

**Owner: Principal. Reviewed quarterly. This document defines what the firm does at each stage of a member relationship, from the first conversation to the door we leave open on the way out.**

---

## Discovery · Week minus two to zero

The relationship begins before there is a member. Discovery is where the firm decides whether to extend an invitation and the prospect decides whether to accept.

**The intro call.** Thirty minutes. Conducted by the principal personally. The objective is not to sell. The objective is to understand how the prospect travels, what has failed them in the past, and whether the firm can meaningfully improve their next twelve months. A prospect who needs us only for booking will be poorly served and is referred elsewhere with grace.

**The application.** Sent within 24 hours of the intro call if the fit is right. Eight questions, none cosmetic: annual trip volume, average trip value, two recent disruptions and how they were handled, current advisor or platform, what the prospect would replace and why, what they would not replace and why, an introduction source if any, two references inside our existing membership.

**The fit assessment.** The principal reads the application against a short rubric: travel volume sufficient to justify the relationship, complexity sufficient to need us, expectations within what the firm can credibly deliver, no conflict with an existing member relationship. A second opinion is taken from the lead advisor before invitations are extended.

**The invitation.** A short letter. Signed. Sent. The membership terms are attached as a separate document. The invitation states the fee, the term, what is included, what is not, and the date by which the firm needs a decision. No urgency, no scarcity language. The relationship begins as it will continue: restrained.

The firm declines roughly two of every five applications. This is intentional. A wrong-fit member damages the experience of the right-fit members.

---

## Onboarding · Week one to two

The first two weeks set the operating cadence that lasts the year. Done well, the member feels held inside seven days. Done poorly, the year is recoverable but harder.

**Day 1.** The welcome packet arrives. Physical for members within Toronto, Montreal, New York, San Francisco, London. Digital for the rest. The packet contains the travel profile questionnaire, the desk contact details, the scope statement, and a short letter from the principal.

**Day 2 to 4.** The member completes the travel profile. The desk responds within four hours of receipt with any clarifying questions. Document images — passports, Global Entry cards, loyalty account confirmations — are uploaded to the encrypted member vault.

**Day 5 to 7.** The orientation call. Thirty minutes, scheduled. Conducted jointly by the principal and the lead advisor who will own the relationship day to day. Three things happen: the member meets the people who will pick up the phone, the desk demonstrates the portal and the response-time commitment, and the first trip on the calendar is identified and timed.

**Day 10 to 14.** The first booking. Whatever trip is next on the member's calendar inside 60 days. The first trip exists to teach both sides how to work together. The lead advisor over-communicates deliberately — more confirmations than will be standard, more checkpoints than will be standard. The member learns where the seams are. The desk learns the member's actual preferences as distinct from the questionnaire answers.

**End of week two.** A short internal review. The lead advisor and the principal sit for fifteen minutes and assess: did the onboarding feel proportionate, did the questionnaire surface what we needed, is there anything in the member's profile we should be researching ahead of the next request. The review is logged.

---

## First 90 Days · The trust window

The first ninety days are the calibration period. The member learns whether to trust us with the things that actually matter. The firm learns whether the member relationship will be a multi-year one or a single-term one.

**Touch frequency.** Higher than steady state. The lead advisor proactively reaches out at least once a fortnight even when there is no booking in motion: a flag on a fare change for a known route, a property opening relevant to the member, a status credit opportunity. The point is to demonstrate that the relationship runs whether or not there is a transaction.

**Building the file.** Every booking, every conversation, every preference revealed in passing is added to the member file. By day 90 the file should contain enough operational detail that any advisor on the desk could serve the member competently if the lead advisor were unavailable.

**The first IROP.** Statistically, most members experience their first disruption inside the first 90 days. It is, perversely, the best thing that can happen. A well-handled first IROP is worth more than any onboarding document. The desk treats every first-window disruption as a Tier 2 minimum regardless of severity, and the principal is notified.

**The 90-day check.** A scheduled call, thirty minutes, between the member and the principal. Three questions, asked plainly: what has surprised you, what has disappointed you, what would you like more of. The answers are written down verbatim and reviewed at the next quarterly partner meeting.

---

## Months Three to Twelve · Steady state

By month three, the relationship has settled into a rhythm. The lead advisor knows the member's calendar shape, the member knows how to brief us in a sentence, the desk knows the supplier preferences and the airline status positions cold.

**Monthly cadence.** The first Monday of each month, the member receives the Latitude Reports digest: trips taken, spend by category, rewards captured, status progression, any patterns the desk has noticed. The digest is short. Members read it.

**Quarterly cadence.** A scheduled call, thirty minutes, between the member and the lead advisor. Forward-looking. What is on the calendar for the next 90 days. Anything coming up that would benefit from advance work — visas, status pushes, points consolidation, gear we should think about now rather than the week before departure.

**Annual cadence.** The year-end review. Sent in early December, covering the calendar year. Hero stats, narrative recaps of the notable trips, disruption summary, three recommendations for the coming year. The year-end review is the single most important artefact the firm produces. It is the document a member quotes when explaining to their spouse, their partners, their friends why they keep paying.

**The ambient work.** Behind the visible cadence, the desk maintains an always-on background: monitoring fare changes on known routes, watching for status credit promotions, tracking property renovations that affect known bookings, flagging supplier issues before they reach the member. The member never sees most of this. They feel it as the absence of friction.

---

## Renewal · Sixty days before term end

Renewal is not a sale. It is a conversation between two parties who have spent a year working together about whether to continue.

**Sixty days out.** The lead advisor prepares the renewal packet: the full year of activity, the spend summary, the rewards captured, the IROPs handled, the relationship file as it now stands. The packet is reviewed by the principal before it is shared.

**Forty-five days out.** The renewal conversation. Conducted by the principal personally. Thirty to forty-five minutes. The conversation has a shape: an honest review of the year (what worked, what did not, what we wish we had done differently), the member's plans for the next twelve months, the firm's recommendation for whether to renew at the current tier, upgrade, downsize, or part ways amicably.

**What we ask for.** Renewal at the agreed fee. One introduction to a peer if the member feels the firm has earned it — no pressure, no follow-up if declined. Permission to use one or two anonymised case studies from the year in the firm's Journal.

**What we offer.** A locked fee for an additional year. A single discretionary credit toward an upgrade or amenity for a notable upcoming trip. A small physical gesture — a leather-bound copy of the year-in-review, or for long-term members, something more personal that the file has earned us the right to know about.

**Thirty days out.** Renewal confirmed in writing. The new term begins on the original anniversary date. No gap, no friction.

---

## Off-boarding · When a member leaves

Members leave. Sometimes for reasons we control (a service failure we did not recover from). Sometimes for reasons we do not (a life change, a move, a corporate travel programme that subsumes their personal travel). The firm's discipline is to off-board well regardless of cause.

**The exit conversation.** Conducted by the principal. Twenty to thirty minutes. The objective is to understand, not to retain. If retention is possible, the member will say so. If not, the conversation moves to logistics.

**The handover.** The member receives a clean export of their file: itineraries, preferences, loyalty positions, named supplier contacts, IROP records. Everything we hold on them, returned to them, in a format their next advisor or their assistant can use. This is non-negotiable. A member who leaves us well-equipped is a member who refers us later.

**The final reports.** The year-to-date Latitude Reports digest. The final spend summary. A short note from the principal acknowledging the relationship and naming the door we leave open.

**The door we leave open.** The exact language matters. "Your file remains with us for thirty-six months. If your circumstances change, we will pick up where we left off. No new application, no new onboarding, the same fee tier you held." This is said in writing. The firm honours it.

**The internal debrief.** Within two weeks of departure, the lead advisor and the principal review the relationship from first contact to last. What did we learn. What would we have done differently. Is there a systemic gap the departure has exposed. The debrief is logged. It informs the next intake.

---

*A relationship well-ended is part of how the firm grows. Members who leave well speak well of the firm for years. The door we leave open is not generosity. It is operating discipline.*
