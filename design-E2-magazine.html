— Chapter 01
An arrival, considered.

There is a generation of people hitting their peak travel years — honeymoons, family weeks, milestone celebrations, business trips — who never had a relationship with a travel advisor. Their parents had advisors. They have Expedia.

This is for them.

Hotels publish a rate. Whether you book on Booking.com, Expedia, or through us — the rate is identical. Where it changes is what's already waiting in the room.

An upgrade. Daily breakfast, two people. A $100 hotel credit. A welcome amenity that says your name on the card. None of which you knew to ask for.

Same price, different arrival. That's the entire pitch.

Underneath, the obvious answer: we are paid by the hotel, the way Booking.com is. Except they keep the commission. We hand it back to you in the room.

Most agencies plan resort trips they've never taken.

We've been to Excellence Playa Mujeres and Punta Cana. Le Blanc and Zoetry. Hyatt Inclusive Cap Cana, Rose Hall, Riviera Cancun, Cabo. Plus a dozen more.

We know which property has a kids' club that actually delivers, and which one keeps the same babysitter for twelve children. We know which adults-only is genuinely quiet at 11pm, and which has a DJ until 1am.

We know which $700/night room is worth $700, and which is sold to people who don't know better.

"Sandals is mid. There are three all-inclusives in the Caribbean that aren't, and none of them advertise on TV."

It is, on its surface, a small advantage. Stay where you sell. But it is the difference between booking a brochure and booking a trip.

— Chapter 04 · A recent booking

A honeymoon. Six Senses Fiji, seven nights.

M+S booked through us in March. They paid $8,000 for what they thought was a Pool Villa.

At check-in, the front desk handed them keys to the Premier Beachfront Pool Villa. The room they walked into would have cost $2,400 more on Booking.com.

Daily breakfast was included. A $100 spa credit waited at the concierge desk. A bottle of champagne sat on the table, with a note: "Mr. & Mrs. K. — welcome home."

None of it cost extra. None of it was asked for. The hotel paid Fora the commission they would have paid Booking.com — except Booking.com would have kept it.
