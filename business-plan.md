# Latitude 43

### A travel office for principals who travel for work.

**Prepared:** May 2026
**Operating entity:** Latitude 43 Inc. (Ontario CCPC; dba forthcoming)
**Host agency:** Fora Travel (US ARC/IATA, CLIA, state seller-of-travel registrations)
**Launch market:** United States (TICO Path A — US-only sales)
**Document version:** 3.0 — Model B Unlimited canonical, replaces v2.0

---

## 1. Executive summary

Latitude 43 is a members' travel office for US-based principals — founders, VC/PE partners, RIA principals, family office principals, and solo professionals — who travel for work and have stopped tolerating the system they're using. The product is sold as a flat-fee membership across two active tiers and one invite-only tier, with every dollar of trip design work bundled into the membership. There is no design fee escalator, no surprise invoice, no à la carte schedule. The pitch is one sentence: *"Light $189 a month, one traveler. Office $349 a month, up to five travelers and your EA, everything included."*

The plan is built on three core mechanics:

1. **Subscription is 100% retained.** Membership revenue does not split with Fora.
2. **Commission splits with Fora on a tiered ladder:** 70/30 starting, 80/20 at $300K personal sales, 90/10 at $2M. Virtuoso clips 2% of hotel commissions before the split. The blended effective hotel commission to the advisor is 7.8% at Year 1.
3. **All trip design is included for members.** Design fees survive only as a qualifying mechanism for non-member one-off project work.

The shape of the business is a lifestyle practice — not venture-backable, not built for an exit. Year 5 base case revenue is **~$870K** (60 Light / 40 Office / 20 Society) under modest assumptions and ~$1.6M in the bull case. The founder operates from Toronto under Fora's US registrations, takes US-only sales (TICO Path A), bears no outside capital, and treats the operation as supplemental to a separate primary income for the first 18 months.

This plan supersedes v2.0 entirely. The pricing pivot from v2.0's $99/$249 + design fee escalator → $189/$349 Unlimited is the result of 30 sales simulations across three pricing models, three First Trip Guarantee mechanics, and five sales-cycle stages (Rounds 6 + 7). The data is summarized in §6 and lives in `/sales-kit/r7-sims/` and `/sales-kit/r3-sims/`.

## 2. The pricing and offering

### 2.1 The three tiers

| Tier | Price | Coverage | Design fees | SLA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlas Light Unlimited** | $189/mo ($2,268/yr) | 1 traveler | ZERO. All trip design included, including personal travel. | 60 min, 24/7 |
| **Atlas Office Unlimited** | $349/mo ($4,188/yr) | Up to 5 named, 3 active concurrent. **EA Companion Seat included.** | ZERO. All design included, including the principal's family leisure. | 60 min, 24/7 |
| **Latitude Society** | $1,000–$2,500/mo ($12,000–$30,000/yr) | Invite-only, capped at 12–15 globally. Dedicated principal. 15-min SLA. Quarterly in-person Year-in-Review. | Event design billed à la carte above $30K | 15 min, 24/7 |

Atlas Family Office is a variant of the Office tier: pre-signed NDA, default-off marketing clause, flat-fee event SKU, rolling roster of up to 20 named with 5 active at any time. Same price as Office.

### 2.2 What "everything included" actually means

For Atlas Office at $349/mo, the member receives the following at no incremental cost:

- **All trip design** — itineraries, hotel selection and negotiation, restaurant reservations, ground transfers, status matches, irop handling, rebooking, hotel walks
- **All booking work** — air, hotel, car, rail, restaurant, on-site experiences
- **Personal and family travel** — anniversary trips, family ski week, summer in Europe, spouse-led leisure
- **Virtuoso benefits** — room upgrades when available, daily breakfast, $100 property credit, late check-out, occasionally a fourth night free
- **Four Seasons Preferred Partner, Marriott STARS, Hyatt Privé, Belmond Bellini, Mandarin Oriental Fan Club** — equivalent benefits at brand properties
- **24/7 desk coverage with 60-minute SLA** — founder on evenings/weekends/overnights, PHT VA on US daytime starting month 4
- **EA Companion Seat** — the principal's EA gets their own Light-level access to the desk
- **Standing instructions** — room preferences, dietary restrictions, allergies, seat preferences stored once and applied every trip
- **Per-trip receipts with commission disclosure** — every booking reports the property paid commission and the rate the member received
- **First Trip Guarantee** — see §2.3
- **The First 30** — 90-day no-fault exit, monthly Stripe billing
- **Quarterly trip-pattern review** — 15-minute call to flag overspend, under-utilization, or rotation issues

What is NOT included: airfare itself, hotel rates, restaurant tabs, anything the member would pay direct.

### 2.3 First Trip Guarantee — mechanic depends on the stage, not the tier

After R6+R7 sim data, the FTG is matched to the sales-cycle stage where the guarantee is invoked, not to the pricing tier. Three mechanics are operational:

- **FTG1 — Month + trip-value labor credit:** *"If your first trip fails on our end, we refund the month's membership AND credit the greater of $1,000 or the trip value or three months of dues, whichever is greater, toward the rework."* For cold inbound discovery and live IROP saves. The trip-value-or-three-months clause was added after R7 Sim 8 surfaced that the original $1K labor cap was undersized for Office-tier complex trips.
- **FTG2 — Full refund + free month + service credit:** *"If your first trip fails on our end, we refund the month, give you the next month free, AND issue a $500 service credit for a future trip."* For mid-year retention saves and family-office onboarding. Maximum trust signal where trust has eroded or never existed.
- **FTG3 — 30-day money-back guarantee:** *"30-day money-back on the membership itself. Doesn't matter why. You decide if it was worth it; if not, you get the month back, no questions."* For new-buyer onboarding and price-negotiator closes. The simplest mechanic, deflects most legal-leaning objections.

The FTG is declared on the sales call and named on a single trip ("the named trip"). It is not a perpetual guarantee — it expires once the named trip closes successfully or 90 days, whichever comes first. After that, retention is earned, not insured.

### 2.4 Non-member design work

Non-members can engage Latitude on a project basis. Fees:

- $500 floor on any trip planning engagement (below this, refer out)
- $250 base + $50/$1K from $5K–$15K trip value
- $750 base + $75/$1K from $15K–$30K
- $1,875 base + $100/$1K above $30K
- Capped at $10,000/trip
- Paid in full before design begins

The purpose of non-member design pricing is to qualify in. A prospect who pays $500–$2,000 to use Latitude once converts to Atlas Light at renewal at roughly 30–40% within 12 months. It is a funnel, not a margin product.

## 3. Ideal customer profile

Latitude 43 sells to five overlapping segments. All are US-based. All are repeat travelers. All have either a personal frustration with their current setup or an EA whose travel admin is the limiting factor.

### 3.1 Series A–C founders

The founder of a 20–80 person startup, post-revenue, post-Series A. T&E $80K–$300K. Travels 30–60 nights/year between fundraises, board meetings, customer trips, partner events. Their EA is overworked or non-existent. Their last three trips had at least one avoidable failure: a missed upgrade, the wrong hotel category, a status match that never coded. They would pay $189/mo for Atlas Light because it removes a recurring source of low-grade humiliation and gives them a person to text from a TSA line.

**Target product:** Atlas Light Unlimited ($189) for solo operators; Atlas Office Unlimited ($349) when the EA + co-founder are on the seat. **Acquisition:** warm introduction from other founders, Latitude Journal on LinkedIn, EA-network channels, cold email at 100/day.

### 3.2 VC and PE partners at sub-$1.5B funds

A partner at a $300M–$1.5B fund. Travels for diligence, LP meetings, portfolio support, conferences. T&E $120K–$500K. The fund admin won't (or can't) staff a real travel team. The partner has been doing it themselves or routing through a long-suffering EA. They are sophisticated buyers — they read fee structures, they ask about commission splits, they want to see the math.

**Target product:** Atlas Light Unlimited for the partner individually; Atlas Office Unlimited if two or three partners share. **Acquisition:** direct outreach to sub-$1.5B funds via PitchBook, referrals from existing members, ACG and SBIA presence.

### 3.3 Lower-mid-market PE operating partners

A senior operating partner at a $500M–$2B PE shop. Travels heavy — portfolio company visits, ops reviews, integration work. T&E $200K–$500K. Has used Amex GBT or Concur for years and is tired of phone-tree calls at 11pm. Sophisticated negotiator. Will push on pricing.

**Target product:** Atlas Office Unlimited (3 active travelers cap suits a single senior partner + EA + spouse), or Family Office variant for the senior partner's family-layer needs. **Acquisition:** direct outreach, ACG and SBIA, VC referrals.

### 3.4 RIA principals at sub-$1B AUM firms

A managing partner at a $200M–$1B AUM RIA. Travels for conferences (Schwab IMPACT, Barron's, T3), client meetings, custodian events. T&E $80K–$200K. LinkedIn-dense audience — content marketing works. Spouse often the co-decision-maker on which trips happen.

**Target product:** Atlas Light Unlimited for solo principals, Atlas Office Unlimited when the firm has 2–3 partners. **Acquisition:** Latitude Journal (RIAs read LinkedIn), conference-season outreach, custodian-platform partnerships (Schwab, Fidelity, Altruist), referrals.

### 3.5 Solo professionals — lawyers, surgeons, M&A bankers, family office principals

A solo-practice professional billing $500K+/year. Travels for cases, surgeries, clients. T&E $60K–$250K. No EA. The principal is also the booker.

**Target product:** Atlas Light Unlimited. **Acquisition:** Journal, podcast guesting, direct LinkedIn outreach, referrals from RIA/VC adjacents.

## 4. The buyer's value math

The single most important section of this plan: what the buyer actually receives, dollarized, on top of the membership fee.

For an Atlas Office Unlimited member at $349/mo ($4,188/year):

| Component | Annual realized value |
|---|---:|
| Virtuoso / FSPP / STARS / Privé benefits (room upgrades, breakfast, $100 credit, late check-out) on 20 hotel nights/year at preferred properties | $3,000–$8,000 |
| Trip design work that would cost $300–500/trip at agency rates × 22 trips/year | $6,600–$11,000 |
| 24/7 SLA coverage (Cadence, Indagare, Brownell, Bell & Bly all close at 5–6pm) | Priceless on the one trip that matters |
| EA Companion Seat — 2–4 hours/week of EA labor freed | $5,000–$12,000 |
| Personal travel design (anniversary, family ski, summer trip) | $3,000–$15,000 |
| First Trip Guarantee + 90-day no-fault exit | Risk reduction |
| **Total realized value** | **$16,000–$46,000/year** |
| **Annual fee** | **$4,188** |
| **Value-to-price ratio** | **4× to 11×** |

The point of the Unlimited model is that the Virtuoso benefits and included design alone exceed the fee. Everything else — the EA seat, personal travel, FTG, 24/7 SLA — is upside on top of an already-positive trade. For Light at $189/mo, the same proportional math runs ~2× to 4× value-to-price.

The reason this matters is that "stupid to say no to" is a property of the offering, not the sales pitch. If the math doesn't independently work for the buyer, no amount of confessional voice fixes it.

### 5.2 Comparison vs v2.0 (Model A)

| Tier | v2.0 Y1 (Model A) | v3.0 Y1 (Model B) | Lift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Atlas Light | $2,400–$3,000 | $3,370–$3,820 | +27–40% |

The lift comes from subscription only. Design fees, which under Model A produced $250–$1,000/member/year in net revenue, go to zero on members. The subscription uplift more than offsets the lost design fee.

### 5.3 The labor-margin risk this model carries

Model B's marginal cost on every "free" design is the founder's hourly labor. A high-usage Office member doing 40+ designed trips/year against a single $349/mo seat could become labor-negative.

Three structural protections:
1. **3 active travelers concurrent cap** on Office bounds the load
2. **PHT VA hire at month 4–6** absorbs the routine portion of trip work
3. **Quarterly trip-pattern review** flags overuse before it becomes a margin problem and either (a) moves the member to Society, (b) adjusts coverage, or (c) initiates an off-board

The risk is real but bounded. In the bear case (5% of members go labor-negative), the dollar impact at Y5 scale is ~$15–25K, manageable.

## 7. The sales motion

### 7.1 The pitch in one paragraph

> "Latitude 43 is a small members' travel office. There are two ways in: Atlas Light at $189 a month for one traveler, or Atlas Office at $349 a month for up to five travelers and your EA. Both include all trip design — no per-trip fees, no surprise invoices. You get the desk on text, sixty minutes, twenty-four hours a day. You get Virtuoso benefits at hotels. Your personal travel is included. If we screw up your first trip, you get the month back, the next month free, and a service credit on the next one. Apply, don't sign up — it's mutual fit."

That is the entire opening. No banned words. No marketing voice. The buyer can repeat it back. The EA can repeat it back to the principal.

### 7.2 The five-stage sales cycle

The R6+R7 sims tested behavior at five distinct stages. Each stage has a matched FTG mechanic.

| Stage | Persona | FTG | Typical close | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold inbound discovery | Series A founder, replied to cold email | **FTG1** | Office or Light | The FTG is offered early, before objection |
| Onboarding + EA in room | RIA principal, just signed | **FTG3** | Already closed | FTG3 reduces buyer's regret in week 1 |
| Live IROP at 2am | Series B founder, second trip | **FTG1** | Already a member | FTG re-papered to trip-value-or-three-months |
| Mid-year retention save | Sarah-equivalent, low usage | **FTG2** | At risk | FTG2 is the maximum trust signal |
| Price objection close | SMB founder pushed back on $189/$349 | **FTG3** | Hold price | 30-day money-back deflects to "you decide" |

### 7.3 The 25 lessons of the playbook (compressed)

Carried forward from R1–R5, validated under R6+R7 cold + warm conditions:

1. Persona-band fork at diagnostic (frugal Series A vs PE MD vs CFO vs delegated buyer)
2. "No real problems" rebuttal: *"You absorbed the failures as background noise."*
3. Top-tier anchor neutralization in opening 30 sec for HNW
4. EA buyer/user split: *"The member is you. The user, most days, is your COS."*
5. First Trip Guarantee — offered EARLY, not as save
6. The First 30 program for burned-before — 90-day no-fault
7. 90-second elevator script for CFOs and time-poor
8. Force-a-Decision close: *"Is this a yes, a no, or a need-more-information?"*
9. Holding Price — never discount; three rehearsed holds (multi-year, founder rate, per-head) plus concession menu
10. Sophisticated Buyer Track — branch for data-driven buyers, 90-day measurement framework, ban "typically"
11. AI / ChatGPT objection FAQ — three structural reasons it can't do this
12. 2am Scenarios Library — three named stories to deploy when buyer won't admit pain
13. Reference Bench — 2 per segment, pre-consented, 48-hour SLA
14. No fabricated benchmarks — *"I'm new. I don't have a public benchmark. Two members will take your call this week."*
15. "Layer above" framing for Navan/TMC defenders
16. "Disqualify up" to Society for HNW: *"$349 is the wrong product for you"* flips dynamic
17. Spouse line for bootstrapped founders: *"$189 isn't really for you. It's for Sunday night with [partner]."*
18. Multi-EA coordination layer: *"We sell the seam between your two EAs."*
19. Family-first reframe for married buyers: *"The person who has to be happy in 6 months is not you, it's [spouse]."*
20. Multilingual backup principal — pre-staged for Latam/Iberian/Asian families
21. "Service contract on a scientific instrument" for technical buyers (biotech, engineers)
22. Post-yes onboarding hook — book the EA's onboarding call before ending the founder's call
23. Atlas Family Office variant — rolling roster of 5 active from a pool of up to 20
24. The "concierge" word is banned — founders never use it; marketers do
25. Confessional register, not triumphalist

### 7.4 The Model B–specific moves from R6+R7

Five lessons that emerged specifically from testing the Unlimited offering:

26. **The "everything included" anchor** — when asked "what does it cost?" the answer is one number. Never let the buyer think there is a second number coming.
27. **The 10:47pm anchor** — extract the buyer's own dollar value from a specific failure. *"What did the missed upgrade actually cost you?"* They quote their own number; you charge less.
28. **The "personal travel" reframe** — when a buyer balks at $349, walk them through their last anniversary trip and price it at agency rates. The membership pays for itself on one well-planned personal trip.
29. **The EA-as-buyer flip** — sometimes the EA is the actual decision-maker. Speak to her directly. *"This frees you, not him."*
30. **The disqualification close** — for true sub-$80K-T&E solos, gently steer to a referral and the Journal newsletter. They are not customers. They are future referrers.

## 8. Operations

### 8.1 Service-level agreement

- **60-minute SLA on all member inquiries, 24/7**
- **Founder coverage:** 7am–11pm ET, all days
- **PHT VA coverage:** 11pm–7am ET (US daytime is PHT evening), hired month 4–6
- **Backup principal:** pre-staged at Fora for vacation, illness, founder offline
- **Society SLA:** 15 minutes, founder-only, with documented escalation

### 8.2 The desk

Single channel for members: text/SMS to a dedicated line, with email as redundancy. No app to download. No portal to log into for routine requests. The member-portal HTML mockup (`/operations/member-portal-mockup.html`) is the exception case for itineraries and standing instructions.

### 8.3 IROP playbook

Documented in `/operations/irop-playbook.md`. Covers: flight cancel, missed connection, hotel walk, lost bag, medical, weather. Each scenario has a documented escalation tree, a script, and an expected response time.

### 8.4 Onboarding

A new member's first 72 hours are documented in `/operations/member-lifecycle.md`. The principal and EA each get a 30-minute onboarding call within 5 business days of signing. Standing instructions are captured on that call. The First Trip Guarantee is named on the call.

### 8.5 Off-boarding

The First 30 program gives any new member a 90-day no-fault exit. After that, off-boarding is annual, on the renewal date, with no commitment beyond the month. Monthly Stripe billing. No multi-year contracts. The off-boarding script is documented and protects the relationship for future re-entry and referral.

- **Stack:** Smartlead + Apollo, with Crunchbase and LinkedIn Sales Navigator as list sources

Newsletter-first. Social as distribution. Latitude Journal (`/sales-kit/journal-12-posts.md`) is the long-form bench. The 43rd Parallel is the monthly identity piece. Desk Diary is the short, frequent operational voice.

## 11. TICO and tax structure

### 11.1 TICO three-path framework

Cannot sell travel to Ontario residents without TICO registration. Three paths:

- **Path A (Year 1 recommendation):** US-only sales, Toronto network as referral engine. No TICO required. Sales contracts and Stripe accounts US-routed.
- **Path B:** Get TICO (~$5–15K + bonding). Defer to Year 3 if Canadian referral demand emerges.
- **Path C:** Partner with TICO-licensed Canadian agency. Optional.

### 11.2 Tax structure

- Operating entity: **Latitude 43 Inc.** (Ontario CCPC)
- Founder receives salary + dividends; dividends pre-eligible
- US treaty position via **W-8BEN-E** filed with Fora to avoid 30% withholding
- US sales sourced as services delivered from Canada (no US trade or business)
- Fora handles state-level seller-of-travel registrations on the advisor's behalf
- No US permanent establishment — written approval workflow for any planned on-site engagement on US soil (e.g., Society Year-in-Review hosted in NYC)

## 15. The decision this plan represents

The pivot from v2.0 (Model A, $99/$249 + design fee escalator) to v3.0 (Model B Unlimited, $189/$349, all design included) is the most consequential strategic call in the plan. It was made after 30 sales simulations established four findings:

1. **Same buyer, higher tier under Model B.** Vanessa Coyne closed Light $99 under Model A and Office $349 under Model B with no extra friction — a 253% revenue lift on the same person.
2. **The escalator was the single biggest unanswerable question in cold sales.** No rep could answer *"what's the all-in?"* with one number under Model A. Every rep could under Model B.
3. **The Virtuoso + included design + 24/7 SLA stack alone exceeds the fee.** This makes "stupid to say no to" a property of the offering, not a property of the sales pitch.
4. **The buyer's EA can sell Model B internally.** No EA in 30 sims successfully sold the Model A escalator internally; multiple EAs in Model B sims explained the membership to the principal in one sentence.

The cost of the change is acceptance of one structural risk: marginal labor cost on every "free" design is the founder's hour. The risk is bounded by the 3-active cap, the PHT VA hire, and the quarterly trip-pattern review.

This document is the operating bible. Subsequent revisions will track the labor-margin reality at the end of each quarter and revise pricing if and only if a Year 2 quarterly review shows labor-negative members exceeding 5% of the book.
