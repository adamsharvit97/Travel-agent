# PHASE 5 — Year 3: Small Team / Scale Entry
*"You hit max solo throughput. Now choose: cap and run, or build."*

### Objective
Year 3 is the **fork**. You'll have hit the realistic personal ceiling of a solo advisor (~$200–280K personal commissions + fees). To go higher, you build a team.

### Path A — Stay solo (the lifestyle path)
- Personal output: $200–300K commissions, $150–220K take-home
- 35-hour weeks
- Premium clients only
- VA + part-time admin
- **No team override revenue**
- Cap reached at ~year 4, plateau forever
- Sale value: limited (personal brand doesn't transfer easily)

### Path B — Build a team (the exit path) ← what you said you want
- You're now a "team leader" under Fora's Team structure (or you've gone fully independent and become your own host)
- **2–4 junior advisors under your brand**, each producing $50–100K in commissions
- You take a **20–30% override** on their bookings
- Personal output: ~$200K commissions + $50–100K override = $250–300K total
- Plus equity in the brand for eventual sale

### Hard deliverables — Y3 build path
- 2–4 advisors recruited and onboarded under Preface (your sub-team within Fora, or your own independent agency)
- **$400K–$1M total team commission revenue**
- Personal income $250–400K
- Brand recognizable in Toronto for your demographic
- Newsletter at 10–25K subscribers
- 25–40 partnership relationships, with a top 10 sending consistent volume
- First profitability tracking and tax structure overhaul (consider incorporating by Y2 if you haven't)

### How you find and pay advisors

The Fora team-leader model lets you bring on advisors under your tier. They get your training + your inbound leads; you get a 20–30% override on their commissions. Talent pool:
- **Your peer network** — friends in adjacent industries (PR, hospitality, sales) who want side income
- **Wedding planners' employees** — adjacent skill, hate their hours
- **Former Fora advisors who flamed out** — they have the license; they need leads. You have the leads.

Typical first-advisor profile: someone you know personally, sales-aptitude, who wants $30–60K supplemental annual income from a side advisor career. They sell ~$300–500K in trips for you → ~$36–60K in commission → you keep $11–18K override per advisor + they keep the rest. With 3 advisors, that's $40–55K passive override on top of your personal output.

### What unlocks Y3 hires
You can hire when:
1. **Lead volume exceeds capacity** (you're turning away clients or letting them get cold)
2. **Brand has gravitational pull** (people apply to you, not the other way)
3. **Process is documented** (intake, booking, follow-up SOPs in writing)
4. **You can pay them** ($30K+ override pool + tools)

Realistic earliest: **month 18**. Plan for Y3 having 2–4.

### The structural decision: Fora team vs. independent host
At ~year 2–3 you may want to leave Fora and become your own TICO-registered host agency. Pros: keep 100% of commission (no 30% to Fora), full brand control, sellable as an asset. Cons: $10K Travel Compensation Fund deposit + ongoing TICO compliance + you take on the back-office (commissions tracking, supplier negotiations, E&O insurance, etc).

**Default recommendation:** stay on Fora through Y3 unless you have specific reason to leave. The 30% you pay them is genuinely cheap for the infrastructure. The right time to leave is when team override revenue alone justifies the back-office hire ($60K+/yr just in override).

# PHASE 6 — Year 5: Exit-Ready
*"$200K isn't enough for a family. The exit is."*

### Objective
By year 5, the business is either generating personal income at the level that "raises a family on one income" — **or** it's a salable asset that converts equity into the multi-million-dollar outcome that does.

### Two end-state scenarios

**Scenario 1 — The lifestyle agency (you stay, you operate)**
- 5–8 advisors
- $1.5–3M total annual commission revenue
- Personal income: **$400–600K/yr** (your personal book + override)
- You work 30–40 hrs/wk
- No exit; recurring income for 30+ years
- This is the **right answer if your priority is income + family time**

**Scenario 2 — The acquisition target (you build to sell)**
- 12–20 advisors
- $4–8M total annual commission revenue
- EBITDA margins of 25–35% in advisor-network businesses
- **Acquisition multiples are 3–6× EBITDA** for travel advisory businesses
- Likely acquirers: **Internova (Travel Leaders / Altour / Protravel / Frosch / Tzell), Travel Edge (Internova's Canadian arm), Virtuoso member firms looking to expand demographics, or a PE roll-up**
- Realistic exit value at Y5: **$4–15M** for the business; your take depends on equity retained
- Pre-exit personal income: $300–500K
- Post-exit (with 3-year earnout): another $2–8M
- This is the **right answer if your priority is generational wealth**

### How to position for Scenario 2
- **Build your own brand** distinct from Fora's. Acquirers buy brands, customer lists, and books — not "an above-average Fora advisor."
- **Document everything** by Y3. SOPs, training, supplier relationships in writing.
- **Defensible niche.** "We're the Gen X/millennial luxury agency" is the kind of position an acquirer values.
- **Recurring + repeatable revenue.** Newsletter ARR (if you ever monetize a paid tier), retainer-based corporate clients, etc. The closer the revenue is to ARR-like, the higher the multiple.
- **Tax structure clean.** Incorporate by year 2. Clean books, clean cap table.

### What would have to be true to hit Y5

- **Y3 hires producing** — at least 3 advisors generating $100K+ each in commission
- **Recognizable brand** — Toronto + nationally for your demographic
- **Capital deployed responsibly** — paid marketing, brand investment, maybe a small office
- **Founder ego in check** — at some point, you stop being the salesperson and start being the operator. Many advisor businesses fail to scale because the founder can't let go of being the rainmaker.

# RISKS + DECISION GATES

These are the realistic things that could break the plan. Watch each one explicitly.

| Risk | Watch for | What to do if it happens |
|---|---|---|
| **TICO study deprioritized** | Past week 2 with <5 hrs studied | Block 3 evenings/wk, no exceptions. Pay $50 rewrite if needed but get it done before week 6. |
| **No bookings month 1** | End of month 1, 0 bookings | Your warm network outreach was passive. Send 1-to-1 messages, not group blasts. 50 personal messages = 5 bookings minimum. |
| **Cold email deliverability tanks** | Spam folder placement >40% | Add second sending domain. Warm up via Mailwarm. Reduce volume per inbox. |
| **No partnerships by month 6** | 0–1 partners after 12 weeks of outreach | Your pitch isn't compelling. Try the 25% commission share line + actual case study. Coffees in-person beat emails. |
| **Day job conflict** | Manager notices your sideline | Be discrete. Personal devices only. No moonlighting policy violations. If it surfaces, prepare to quit on your terms with 1-month notice + runway. |
| **Burnout at month 9** | You hate doing this and it's not making $2K/wk yet | Common at this stage. Either niche harder (drop 2 of 5 segments) or extend the Y1 timeline by 3–6 months. Don't quit at month 9. |
| **Wrong segment dominating** | Your bookings are 80% bach parties, you're miserable | Tilt outreach to other segments. The brand is built to be flexible; don't lock in. |
| **HST surprise** | You cross $30K revenue in 4 rolling quarters | Register voluntarily at month 4–5, not retroactively. Talk to an accountant by month 6. |

### Major decision gates

**Month 6: Day-job decision.** Quit if business is producing $1K+/wk for 8 weeks and you have 4+ months runway. Otherwise stay 3–6 more months.

**Month 12: Incorporate decision.** If Y1 net is $60K+, talk to an accountant about CCPC incorporation. Tax deferral + lifetime capital gains exemption ($1M+) for future sale.

**Month 18: First hire decision.** Hire a junior advisor only when leads are exceeding your bandwidth AND you've documented your booking process. Premature hires destroy quality.

**Year 3: Independent host vs Fora team decision.** Stay on Fora unless your team override is exceeding $80K/yr; at that level, the back-office cost of becoming an independent TICO-registered host is justified.

**Year 4–5: Sell vs. operate decision.** Begin conversations with M&A advisors and potential acquirers at year 4. Don't commit to a sale; understand the market. The right time to sell is when you've had 18 consecutive months of growth and a clean operating year.
