# Five-Segment Market Analysis
### An independent travel advisor positioning study

**Prepared for:** [Founder], independent travel advisor, Toronto
**Date:** May 2026
**Scope:** Five priority customer segments, sized and unpacked. Conversion strategy, channel mix, and website architecture follow.

---

## Executive Summary

This analysis evaluates five customer segments that share one defining trait: **they are not currently being served by a human travel advisor**, despite being the highest-volume travel buyers in the North American market. They book Expedia, Booking.com, Costco Travel, credit-card travel desks, or — increasingly — TikTok. They are HENRYs (High Earners Not Rich Yet), aspirational professionals, young parents, and the social organizers around them. They spend $4K–$25K per trip, take 2–4 trips a year, and have a 25-year buying horizon ahead of them.

The legacy luxury-advisor industry (Virtuoso, Brownell, Indagare, Embark Beyond, etc.) ignores them because individual trip economics don't justify white-glove hours. The OTA industry serves them transactionally but adds no value beyond price comparison. **The opening is to be the human advisor for the 95% of trips that nobody is advising on — at a price point and service model the legacy world refuses to staff.**

Five segments stack into this thesis cleanly. They are not five separate businesses; they are **five doors into the same 30-year client relationship.**

| # | Segment | Avg trip value | Avg commission + fee per trip | Repeat / yr | 5-yr client value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Young professional couple, annual vacation | $4,000–$8,000 | $500–$1,000 | 1–2 | $3,000–$7,500 |
| 2 | Young family, school-break trip | $5,000–$12,000 | $700–$1,500 | 2–3 | $7,000–$18,000 |
| 3 | Multi-gen milestone birthday | $25,000–$80,000 | $3,000–$9,000 | once · referrals | $5,000–$20,000+ |
| 4 | Honeymoon | $6,500–$20,000 | $900–$2,500 | once · gateway | $8,000–$30,000 (lifetime) |
| 5 | Bachelor/bachelorette party | $8,000–$18,000 (group) | $1,000–$2,500 | repeat-format | $4,000–$12,000 |

**Strategic recommendation:** Lead acquisition with **Segments 4 and 5 (honeymoon + bach)** — they have the strongest emotional intensity, highest social-sharing frequency, and most predictable trigger events. They feed Segments 1 and 2 over the following 3–7 years as those clients pair off, marry, have children, and become annual-vacation clients. Segment 3 is the long-tail referral monster — every couple in Segments 1–4 has parents who turn 60.

## SEGMENT 1 — The Young Professional Couple
### *The "we earn good money now, we don't know what to do with it" segment*

#### Persona snapshot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Age | 28–36 |
| Status | Married or cohabitating, no kids yet (or pre-kids) |
| Household income | $180,000–$400,000 combined |
| Net worth | Modest. House (often mortgaged), retirement starting, not much liquid wealth. |
| City | Toronto, NYC, SF, LA, Chicago, Vancouver, Austin, etc. |
| Job profile | Senior IC at tech / consulting / finance / law / medicine |
| Trip frequency | 1 major (10–14 days) + 1–2 long weekends per year |
| Booking horizon | 8–14 weeks out (compressing) |

#### The trip

A two-week September trip to Italy, a 10-day Japan in spring, a week in Greece, a long weekend in Mexico City, a four-night Tulum reset. Mid-tier-to-premium hotels ($350–$700/night). Business class only sometimes (points-funded). Restaurants matter more than nightlife. They want it "right" but they don't want to overthink it. They have ChatGPT'd their itinerary, watched 20 TikToks, and saved 11 Instagram reels — but they're still uncertain.

#### Emotional drivers

1. **The need to "do it right" without becoming the project manager.** They have demanding jobs. The trip planning starts as fun and turns into 18 hours of unfun by week three.
2. **Mild status anxiety.** They want the trip to look good in the camera roll and the recap. Not Instagram-thirsty — quietly proud.
3. **The "we work hard, we deserve this" frame.** Pleasure permission is unlocked by spend justification.
4. **Loss aversion on time.** A bad hotel choice or wasted day is more painful than a $200 overspend.

#### Friction (pain points)

- **Decision fatigue.** 14 hotel options on Booking, 6 saved Instagram posts, 4 friends' contradictory recs. They freeze.
- **The "is this the right hotel?" anxiety.** They can't tell from photos if it's actually nice.
- **Restaurant booking insanity.** The places they want need a 30-day reservation; they didn't know.
- **The bumped-flight nightmare.** Air Canada cancels Lisbon and they're holding for 90 minutes on a layover.

#### Status quo (where they currently book)

- **Hotels:** Booking.com, hotel website direct, Amex Travel
- **Flights:** Google Flights, Skyscanner, direct
- **Activities:** Viator, GetYourGuide, Reddit
- **Restaurants:** OpenTable, Resy, Instagram
- **Advice:** TikTok, friends, ChatGPT, Reddit

#### Why they don't use a travel advisor today

> *"That's for older people / rich people / honeymoons."*

They literally don't know it's an option for them, don't know it's free at point of purchase, and assume it would cost extra or push them upmarket. Cultural unfamiliarity is 90% of the objection.

#### The unlock (winning message)

> *"Same hotel, same nightly rate as Booking.com — except you arrive to a room upgrade, free breakfast, a $100 credit, and 4pm checkout. And we made the dinner reservations."*

This is one of the cleanest value propositions in any service category. The "same rate" framing removes the price objection entirely; the perks are essentially free margin from the supplier.

#### Channels (where to reach them)

- **Instagram** — carousels and Reels showing "what a Virtuoso booking looks like" with side-by-side receipts
- **LinkedIn** — surprisingly effective for this demo; professional-credibility halo
- **Newsletter (Substack-style)** — long-form trust building, drives high-LTV signups
- **Referrals from peers** — Segment 4/5 clients refer Segment 1 clients within 18 months
- **B2B partnerships** — wealth advisors (Wealthsimple advisors, RBC PHN, etc.), CPAs, mortgage brokers

#### Conversion levers

| Lever | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Free 20-min consult, no commitment | Removes both price and time risk |
| Specific perks listed in dollar terms | "$100 credit + breakfast = $300/stay value" beats vague "amenities" |
| Side-by-side OTA comparison | They've been using OTAs; show the gap explicitly |
| Booking the trip they were *already going to take* | Don't try to upsell — match their intent, add value |

#### LTV / repeat opportunity

This segment is the **slow-burn jewel**. They take 1–2 trips a year for the next 10 years. They will become honeymoon clients (Segment 4), then family clients (Segment 2), then milestone clients (Segment 3). One acquired Segment 1 customer = $25K–$60K in lifetime commission and at minimum 3 referrals.

## SEGMENT 2 — The Young Family
### *The "I don't have time for this, but I want the points" segment*

#### Persona snapshot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Age | 32–42 |
| Status | Married with 1–3 kids under 10 |
| Household income | $150,000–$350,000 combined |
| Net worth | Tied up in mortgage + retirement; mid-five-figure liquid |
| Trip frequency | 1 winter break (Caribbean/Mexico) + 1 summer (cottage/road trip/Europe) + 1 spring break maybe |
| Booking horizon | 4–9 months out (school-calendar-locked) |
| Cost sensitivity | Higher than Segment 1; points-aware |

#### The trip

Christmas week at a Beaches resort in Turks & Caicos. March break at an all-inclusive in Punta Cana. Summer week at a Disney property or two weeks renting a villa in Italy. Air Canada Aeroplan + Amex MR points are deployed strategically. The trip is for the kids but the parents desperately need it too.

#### Emotional drivers

1. **Exhaustion.** They are tired. They want the trip to happen *to* them, not *because* of them.
2. **The points obsession.** This demo over-indexes hard on Aeroplan, Marriott Bonvoy, Hyatt, Chase Sapphire / Amex Plat. Earning and redeeming is a sport.
3. **Risk aversion on the kids' experience.** A bad pool, a bad kids' club, a bad room view = trip ruined.
4. **Family-photo currency.** The trip lives forever in the family photo book. Aesthetic + memory > price.

#### Friction (pain points)

- **Coordinating connecting rooms.** Two queen beds + a king isn't bookable on OTAs.
- **Knowing which resort isn't terrible.** All-inclusive review sites are useless; staff turnover changes everything.
- **Kids' club hours and ages.** Marketing pages lie.
- **Airport-to-resort logistics with car seats.** Disaster waiting.
- **Trip insurance and "what if a kid gets sick" anxiety.**

#### Status quo (where they currently book)

- **OTAs and packages:** Expedia, Costco Travel, Air Canada Vacations, WestJet Vacations, Sunwing
- **Direct:** Marriott.com, Disney, Sandals
- **Advice:** Mom-blogs, Instagram parent influencers, Facebook groups (huge), word-of-mouth at the school gate

#### Why they don't use a travel advisor today

Three reasons stacked:
1. **They think they're already getting "best price" through Costco Travel** (often true on rack rate, false on perks).
2. **They think "travel advisor" = expensive and slow.** They want SAME-DAY answers.
3. **They want the points to accrue to their card.** Some advisors do break this; the right host agency does not — bookings on their cards still earn points.

#### The unlock (winning message)

> *"Costco-level prices, but I sit on hold with Sandals for you, get connecting rooms guaranteed, pre-book the kids' club, and you keep all your Aeroplan points."*

Critical: this message lives or dies on **"you keep your points."** Lose that, you lose the family demo entirely.

#### Channels (where to reach them)

- **Instagram (parent influencer adjacency)** — comment on parent-influencer travel posts with genuinely useful intel
- **Facebook neighbourhood/parent groups** — Toronto Mom Boss, etc. Massive untapped channel
- **School parent WhatsApp groups** — direct referral velocity once you have one client
- **B2B: pediatric dental offices, family therapists, school PA committees, kids' programs**
- **Aeroplan / points-enthusiast subreddits and forums** — credibility loop

#### Conversion levers

| Lever | Why it works |
|---|---|
| "I'll match the Costco rate or beat it" | Eliminates the comparison anxiety |
| Trip insurance handled, defaults to "yes" | Family demo loves insurance — frame it as included thinking |
| One-text booking changes ("kid sick, need to leave a day late") | Beats Sunwing's call centre infinitely |
| Pre-booked restaurant + transfers | Reduces day-1 chaos for stressed parents |

#### LTV / repeat opportunity

**This is the bread-and-butter recurring segment.** Two trips a year for 12 years = 24 transactions, $700/avg net = ~$17K per client. Plus extended family referral (their parents become Segment 3, their friends become Segments 1/4/5).

## SEGMENT 3 — The Milestone Birthday Group Trip
### *The "Mom is turning 70 and Dad is paying for everyone" segment*

#### Persona snapshot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Milestone birthday (50, 60, 65, 70, 75, 80) |
| Decision maker | Usually the celebrant's adult child (35–55), with payment by celebrant or spouse |
| Group size | 6–25 people across 2–3 generations |
| Household income (of payor) | $400K–$2M+, often retired with assets |
| Trip budget | $25,000–$150,000 total |
| Trip length | 5–10 nights |
| Booking horizon | 9–14 months out |

#### The trip

A villa rental in Tuscany for 14. A Disney Cruise for 18 grandchildren and grandparents. A buy-out of a small Caribbean resort for 22. Multiple rooms, multiple dietary requirements, multiple boomers' worth of mobility considerations, multiple group dinners, transfers, excursions, and a celebration moment built in.

#### Emotional drivers

1. **The legacy frame.** This may be the last big trip with all of us. It must be beautiful.
2. **The competence-signaling.** The organizer (often the eldest adult child) wants to look like they pulled it off.
3. **Conflict avoidance.** Two-week trips with extended family = pressure cooker. Logistics that prevent flare-ups are gold.
4. **Honoring the celebrant.** This trip is *about* someone; the planner wants them treated like royalty.

#### Friction (pain points)

- **Coordinating 20 calendars.** Nightmare.
- **Collecting deposits from 6 households.** Worse.
- **Accommodating wildly different budgets within the group.**
- **Mobility limitations on the older generation.**
- **Picky-eater grandkids vs. adventurous-eater adults.**
- **The celebrant doesn't want to be involved in planning but wants everything perfect.**

#### Status quo (where they currently book)

- **DIY (most common):** the adult-child organizer assembles it from VRBO + Booking + spreadsheets. Takes them 60–100 hours. They burn out.
- **Tour operators:** Tauck Bridges, Backroads (active), Adventures by Disney
- **Cruise lines:** Disney, Royal Caribbean, Viking (for adults-only milestones)
- **Local travel agents:** older boomer parent might have an existing relationship

#### Why they don't use a travel advisor today

Often they assume they need to "be rich" to qualify, or they only know the old neighborhood-strip-mall agency that doesn't feel right. They also worry about the markup — they don't realize the commission is supplier-paid.

#### The unlock (winning message)

> *"I handle the spreadsheet, the deposits, the dietary requirements, the dinner reservations, and the day everyone arrives. You and your mom show up."*

This is **labor relief, not luxury access.** The luxury access is the upsell.

#### Channels (where to reach them)

- **B2B referral channels are dominant:** wealth advisors, estate planners, retirement-coach consultants
- **Country clubs and private social clubs**
- **Wedding planners** (multi-gen logistics overlap)
- **Real estate agents** in upper-middle neighborhoods (they know who's downsizing into the milestone-trip life-stage)
- **Direct mail / niche** — the only segment where targeted print mail still works on the celebrant's address

#### Conversion levers

| Lever | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Project-manager framing | Removes the "I'm too busy at work to plan this" excuse |
| Visual itinerary deliverable (PDF) | The organizer can share it with the family group chat instantly |
| Payment collection from group members | The single most-requested feature for this segment |
| Mobility / dietary intake form | Demonstrates seriousness instantly |

#### LTV / repeat opportunity

**Highest single-trip commission of all five segments** ($3K–$9K). Then:
- Every adult attendee (typically 8–15 people) is now a warm lead for Segments 1, 2, 4, 5
- The celebrant's spouse / siblings will turn the same age soon
- Anniversary trips become annual

One closed milestone trip = ~$30K–$80K of downstream revenue over 5 years if you work the referral muscle.

## SEGMENT 4 — The Honeymoon
### *The "this is the one trip we're going to splurge on" segment*

#### Persona snapshot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Age | 28–35 typically |
| Status | Newly married, 2–8 weeks post-wedding |
| Combined income | $150K–$500K |
| Trip budget | $6,500 average (US), 64% spend $10K+, 15% spend $20K+ |
| Trip length | 8–14 nights |
| Booking horizon | 6–10 months ahead of wedding |

#### The trip

Hawaii (#1 destination for 2026), Italy (#2), Japan (#3, surging hard). Increasingly **multi-destination** (Tokyo + Kyoto + beach; Italy + Greek islands; Paris + Maldives). Premium hotel tier — Four Seasons, Aman, Belmond, Rosewood. Often the largest single trip the couple will take for a decade.

#### Emotional drivers

1. **"This is THE trip."** Permission to overspend is socially granted; the couple is the protagonist of their own movie.
2. **First-time-doing-something-big anxiety.** They've never spent $15K on a trip. They desperately want it to be perfect.
3. **Romantic-narrative pressure.** It needs to look beautiful, feel meaningful, and produce 200 Instagram-worthy photos.
4. **Decision fatigue from wedding planning.** They are done with deciding things by month 8 of engagement.

#### Friction (pain points)

- **The Maldives water-villa is $4,000/night and they don't know if it's actually worth it.**
- **They want to combine Italy + Greece + maybe Croatia and have no idea how to sequence it.**
- **Internal flights between islands, train transfers, transfer cars — too much for an Excel.**
- **Honeymoon-registry contributions need to be applied to bookings without breaking them.**
- **They want VIP treatment they don't know how to ask for.**

#### Status quo (where they currently book)

- **Increasingly using travel advisors** — 54% of Fora advisors report rising honeymoon inquiries year-over-year
- **DIY via Booking / Expedia / hotel direct** — still dominant for under-$10K trips
- **Wedding planner referrals** — the biggest single channel
- **Travel-influencer "honeymoon edits"** — heavy TikTok/IG presence

#### Why they don't use a travel advisor today

Many do now (especially $10K+). The gap is the $5–10K segment where couples assume an advisor is "out of their budget" and DIY a trip that's worse than it should be.

#### The unlock (winning message)

> *"You've made 400 decisions in the last 9 months. Make this be the one where someone else handles it. Same hotel rate. Champagne in the room, room upgrade, late checkout, on the house."*

The exhaustion frame is more powerful than the access frame for this segment.

#### Channels (where to reach them)

- **Wedding planner partnerships** — single highest-ROI channel
- **Wedding venue partnerships** — direct co-marketing
- **Bridal show booths** — old-school but still works in Toronto
- **The Knot / WeddingWire** — paid + organic vendor listings
- **Instagram (engagement-announcement spotting)** — surgically slow but high-LTV
- **Engagement-ring jewellers, bridal boutiques, photographers** — the warm-up funnel that touches the couple 6–12 months before booking

#### Conversion levers

| Lever | Why it works |
|---|---|
| "We do honeymoon registries that don't get lost" | Niche pain point, instantly credible |
| Multi-destination itinerary as a deliverable | DIY can't do this elegantly |
| Photographer / restaurant rec list | Couples crave the local-insider list |
| Surprise touches (champagne, in-room amenities) you arrange | Storytelling currency at the dinner table |

#### LTV / repeat opportunity

**Honeymoon is a gateway, not a one-off.** A couple acquired at honeymoon will book:
- 1-year anniversary trip
- Babymoon (Segment 4-adjacent) 1–3 years later
- Annual vacations (Segment 1) for the next 5–10 years
- Eventually family trips (Segment 2)
- Eventually milestone trips for their parents (Segment 3)

Lifetime value of a well-handled honeymoon client easily clears **$25K–$50K**.

## SEGMENT 5 — The Bachelor / Bachelorette Party
### *The "the best man is one frantic group-chat from a meltdown" segment*

#### Persona snapshot

| Decision maker | Best man, maid of honour, or co-organizer |
| Age | 27–35 |
| Group size | 6–14 typically |
| Group total spend | $8,000–$25,000 |
| Per-person cost | $800–$2,000 |
| Trip length | 3–4 nights |
| Booking horizon | 4–8 weeks (compressed; lots of last-minute) |

#### The trip

Vegas, Nashville, Scottsdale, Cabo, Tulum, Miami, Mykonos, Lisbon, Mexico City. Hotel block (or villa), day-club cabanas, group dinners, ground transport, one excursion (boat day, golf round), one "wild" night. Joint bachelor-bachelorette weekends are now mainstream for 2026.

#### Emotional drivers (organizer)

1. **Pure stress relief.** The best man / MOH is overwhelmed and would pay real money to make this go away.
2. **Performance pressure.** They want to be the hero of the weekend, not the punching bag.
3. **Group politics.** Different budgets, different vibes, different relationships to the bride/groom.
4. **Time scarcity.** Most organizers have full-time jobs and zero hospitality experience.

#### Friction (pain points)

- **Collecting money from 12 people who are bad at responding.**
- **Booking a hotel block when 5 people change dates 3 times.**
- **Getting reservations at the spots that won't take groups over 8.**
- **Coordinating transportation from the airport for arrivals spread across 36 hours.**
- **The plus-one drama.**
- **The "I can't afford that" texts at 11pm.**

#### Status quo (where they currently book)

- **DIY-by-spreadsheet** — overwhelming majority
- **Bach-party planning startups** (Bach.com, Batch app) — handle activities and itinerary, *not* hotel/transport bookings well
- **Generic hotel booking + activity-by-activity OpenTable / TripAdvisor**

#### Why they don't use a travel advisor today

They simply don't think to. There is **no incumbent travel advisor brand in this space at all.** Closest is the Bach.com / similar app ecosystem, which doesn't handle the booking layer.

This is the cleanest blue-ocean of the five segments.

#### The unlock (winning message)

> *"I plan group trips that don't fall apart. Hotel block negotiated, dinners reserved, transport arranged, and I collect everyone's money — so you don't have to. Group of 8+ only."*

The single most powerful word in this pitch is **"I collect."** That alone closes deals.

#### Channels (where to reach them)

- **Instagram + TikTok content** — "how to plan a bach party without losing your mind" content engine
- **Wedding planner partnerships** — wedding planner already has the engaged couple; bach is downstream
- **Engagement-photographer Instagram tagging** — surgical
- **Bridesmaid/groomsmen gift companies** — partnership channel (Bridesmaid for Hire, Bach.com)
- **Direct outreach to recently-engaged couples** — public engagement announcements are findable

#### Conversion levers

| Lever | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Payment-collection link | Single most valuable feature |
| One-pager itinerary the organizer can drop in the group chat | Status currency for the organizer |
| Flat per-person planning fee | Predictable, easy to add into the per-person total |
| Group-friendly hotel-block negotiation | Couldn't be DIY'd; real value |

#### LTV / repeat opportunity

**Highest-velocity referral engine of all five segments.** Every closed bach party = 8–12 future weddings = 8–12 future honeymoons (Segment 4) = 8–12 future couples (Segment 1) = decades of bookings. The math gets silly within 24 months if executed well.

## Cross-Segment Insights

### Insight 1 — The HENRY thread

Four of the five segments (1, 2, 4, 5) are dominated by High Earners Not Rich Yet. They earn $150K–$400K, take 2–4 trips a year, and are systematically underserved. **This is the brand's core demographic.** Segment 3 is the older outlier — but it's frequently *triggered by* and *paid for* people whose adult children are HENRYs.

### Insight 2 — The pain-point overlap

| Pain point | Segments affected |
|---|---|
| "I don't have time to plan this" | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (all five) |
| "I want the perks I see other people getting" | 1, 4 |
| "I want this group thing to not implode" | 2, 3, 5 |
| "I want to keep my points / get good rate" | 1, 2, 4 |
| "I want someone to text when it breaks" | All five |

**Three pain points cover all five segments: time scarcity, perks anxiety, and group logistics.** These should be the three load-bearing messages on the homepage.

### Insight 3 — The "be your travel guy for 30 years" play

Each of these segments is **a stage** in the same person's life:

> Bachelor party (27) → Honeymoon (30) → Couples annual trips (30–37) → First family trip (35) → School-break family vacations (37–48) → Their parents' milestone birthday (45) → Their own milestone (55) → Repeat.

**If you acquire someone at any point in this sequence, you can own the relationship for 25+ years.** The right entry point is whichever stage is easiest to acquire (probably Segment 5 → 4), and the brand strategy is **stay the same brand at every stage** so they don't outgrow you.

### Insight 4 — The points/perks economics are universal

Across all five segments, the single most under-appreciated fact is: **the advisor adds value at zero cost to the customer.** Same rate, free upgrades, breakfast, credits, and the points still go to the client's credit card. This is the entire commercial moat against OTAs and it's the lead message in every segment.

### Insight 5 — Channel mix is bimodal

| Channel | Segments where it dominates |
|---|---|
| Instagram + TikTok content | 1, 4, 5 |
| Facebook parent groups | 2 |
| Wedding planner B2B referral | 4, 5 (and indirectly 2 via couples) |
| Wealth advisor / CPA B2B referral | 1, 3 |
| Direct neighbourhood / school networks | 2, 3 |
| Newsletter (Substack-style) | 1, 4 |

The "everyone uses Instagram" assumption is half-right. **Wedding-planner B2B partnerships are the single highest-ROI activity** across Segments 4 and 5, and indirectly fuel all the others.

## Strategic Recommendations

### Sequencing — what to do in what order

**Months 1–3 (foundation):** TICO certified, Fora onboarded, brand launched, website live (this document supports that). Book the first 10–15 trips through friends and personal network as a learning lab.

**Months 4–9 (acquisition machine):** Focus 70% of effort on **Segments 4 and 5** via wedding-planner partnerships and direct outreach to recently-engaged couples. These segments have the strongest trigger events, highest emotional intensity, and best referral mechanics.

**Months 9–18 (compound):** Honeymoon clients begin booking first anniversaries (Segment 1). Bachelor clients begin getting married (Segment 4). Newsletter audience starts converting Segments 1 and 2 inbound.

**Months 18–36 (defensive moat):** Segment 3 referrals from happy Segments 1–4 clients start coming in. By month 36, retention + referral covers 60%+ of new business.

### Pricing recommendation

| Segment | Planning fee | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Young couple annual trip | $0–$250 | Commission-only on most; charge for complex multi-destination |
| 2 — Family school-break trip | $0–$150 | Often package-only; rarely warrants a fee |
| 3 — Multi-gen milestone | $1,500–$5,000 flat | High value justifies real fee; reflects real work |
| 4 — Honeymoon | $250–$750 | Standard for multi-destination |
| 5 — Bachelor/ette | $250 base + $50/person | Predictable, scales with effort |

### Naming considerations for the brand

The brand should be **trip-agnostic** so it can serve all 5 segments without retrofitting. Onset (screen-led) and Preface (couples-led) are both too narrow.

Candidate names that work across all five:
- **Preface** — keep, broaden the positioning. *Every trip is a preface to a memory.*
- **Standing** — refers to "a standing reservation," ongoing relationship. Quiet, ownable.
- **Marker** — the marker of a moment.
- **Counsel** — the advisor framing, distinguished, premium.
- **The Address** — implies "the right address for this kind of trip."
- **Cadence** — the rhythm of the trips that mark your life.

**Recommended:** continue with **Preface** as the brand name, but reposition the offering around the 5 segments. "Preface" is short, ownable, trip-agnostic, and the founder already has equity in the name.

## Website Architecture Spec

### The strategic problem

A site that tries to speak to everyone speaks to no one. A site that speaks to one segment loses the other four. **The architecture must allow each segment to self-identify within 5 seconds and feel addressed.**

### Recommended IA

```
/
├── Home — the umbrella + a "who's this for" 5-card picker
├── /couples — Segment 1 landing
├── /family — Segment 2 landing
├── /milestone — Segment 3 landing
├── /honeymoon — Segment 4 landing
├── /bachelor — Segment 5 landing
├── /brief — the newsletter
├── /about
└── /book — first-call booking flow
```

### Home page sections (in order)

1. **Hero** — umbrella message that captures all 5 ("A travel advisor for the trips that actually matter")
2. **Who we plan for** — 5-card grid, one per segment, each a link
3. **What you actually get** — the three universal pain-point relievers (time / perks / group logistics)
4. **The Brief** — newsletter signup (lead capture)
5. **About** — short personal note + credentials
6. **CTA** — book a 20-minute call

### Each segment landing page anatomy

1. **Hero** — segment-specific headline + subhead
2. **"The trip looks like this"** — what's typical (length, group size, budget, destinations referenced generically)
3. **"Here's what's painful about doing it yourself"** — name the friction
4. **"Here's what we do instead"** — 3-bullet value prop
5. **Sample of the deliverable** — what they get
6. **One testimonial** (synthesized for the mockup)
7. **CTA** — book a call, segment-specific copy

### Universal design system (carried from previous work)

- **Palette:** cream paper (#F4EDE0), ink (#1A1A1A), warm brown accent (#8B6F47)
- **Type:** Cormorant Garamond italic for display, Inter for body
- **Voice:** quiet, NYT-travel-section, no exclamation points, specifics over adjectives
- **No place names on the homepage.** Generic on the umbrella, specific on the segment pages where context warrants it.
