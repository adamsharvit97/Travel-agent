— How I'd think about it

The questions I ask before recommending a property. You should be asking these too.

Anyone can read the brochure. The right call depends on what you actually want, not on what marketing photos promise.

Adults-only, or kids in the equation? The biggest fork.
Adults-only properties run quieter, slower, and usually serve better food. Family resorts have kids' clubs and waterslides but adult dinners get loud. There's no in-between; pick a side.
What's your noise tolerance? Yes, really.
Royalton CHIC has DJs at the pool. Zoetry has acoustic guitar at the pool. Some people love one, some need the other. Marketing photos lie about this — I won't.
Mexico, Caribbean, or DR? Which trade-offs do you prefer?
Mexico is fastest flight, sharpest design, best food, occasional sargassum. Caribbean is best beaches but limited supply at the top tier. DR is biggest variety and best value but more uneven service.
Club access or base room? The most common mistake.
Most all-inclusives have a "Club" tier (Excellence Club, Preferred Club, etc.). The price difference is $200-500/wk per couple. The amenity difference is enormous — private pools, premium liquor, butler, better food. Get Club.
— Common mistakes

Four things people get wrong about booking all-inclusives. I see all of them every week.

01

Booking on Booking.com to save money.

You don't save money. The hotel charges the same rate. You just lose the upgrades, club access, breakfast, credits, and human concierge that come with booking through an advisor.

Average value left on the table: ~$1,200 per couple per week.

02

Picking Sandals because it's famous.

Sandals does heavy marketing. They're fine. They're often not the best option at their price point. Excellence Playa Mujeres, Le Blanc, and Royalton's adult-only properties typically deliver more for the same money.

Tell your advisor your priorities first; let them recommend the brand, not the other way around.

03

Booking June through October in Riviera Maya.

Sargassum season. Beaches are covered in brown seaweed for half the year. Hotels can clean it but you'll smell it from the lobby. If you must travel summer, go Caribbean (Turks, Anguilla, BVI) or Pacific Mexico.

Nobody books for the resort. They book for the beach. The beach is the product.

04

Not booking Club / Preferred / Premium tier.

The base room is bait pricing. The actual product is the Club tier. You'll spend $5K either way; the question is whether you arrive to a basic room or to private-pool, premium liquor, butler service.

If your budget is ~$5K, book Club at a mid-tier resort. Better than base room at a top-tier resort.
